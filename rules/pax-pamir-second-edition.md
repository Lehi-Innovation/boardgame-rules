## Overview

Players are Afghan political leaders building courts and shifting between British, Russian, and Afghan coalitions. Purchasing and playing court cards creates tribes, spies, armies, and roads, and supplies actions. Four Dominance Checks award points: if a coalition dominates the map, its loyal players compete on influence; otherwise everyone competes on their personal cylinders in play. A lead of four victory points after a check wins immediately; otherwise the highest final score wins.

This summary follows the [English third-printing rulebook](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/rxcttget9flnuuv12lhlf/PaxPamir-Rules.pdf?rlkey=kx19mzkv0x9x8p7ugg883g3hh&dl=1), linked from [Wehrlegig's resources](https://wehrlegig.com/pages/resources), including its Wakhan opponent. Card-specific abilities and event text can override the ordinary rules. The source identifies its license as [CC BY–NC–SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/); this is a paraphrased rules reference, not a reproduction of its historical essays or card biographies.

## Components

Rulebook pages 2–3 describe:

- Cloth map with six regions—Kabul, Kandahar, Punjab, Herat, Persia, Transcaspia—a victory track and favored-suit spaces.
- 36 coalition blocks: 12 each British, Russian, and Afghan. Upright in a region they are armies; on a border they are roads.
- 55 player cylinders, 11 per color: one score marker and ten usable pieces. A cylinder in a region is a tribe, on a court card a spy, and on the loyalty dial a gift.
- 36 one-rupee coins. Money is not limited by the physical supply; substitute if needed.
- 142 cards: 100 court cards, 16 events including four Dominance Checks, 24 Wakhan AI cards, and two Wakhan aid cards.
- Five player boards and loyalty dials, six ruler tokens, and a favored-suit marker.

Coalition blocks help all players currently loyal to that coalition; placing one does not make it personally yours. Cylinders remain their owner's pieces through loyalty changes.

## Setup

Use rulebook pages 4–5:

1. Set the favored suit to **political**. Place all blocks and coins in supplies and the ruler tokens by their regions.
2. Separate court cards from events. Shuffle court cards and make six piles, each containing **5 + player count** cards. Remove the unused court cards.
3. Put one Dominance Check in each of piles 3–6. Shuffle the other events: add two to pile 2 and one each to piles 3–6; remove the six unused events. Pile 1 has no events.
4. Shuffle each pile separately. Stack them in order with pile 1 on top and pile 6 on the bottom. Do not shuffle the completed deck.
5. Deal a market of two rows and six columns, filling leftmost columns first and the top row before the bottom in each column. Put the deck to the right.
6. Give each player a board, loyalty dial, 11 cylinders, and **four rupees**. Put one cylinder on score 0. Start with no court or hand cards.
7. Starting with a random player, choose coalition loyalties clockwise. The **last player to choose loyalty takes the first turn**; thereafter turns proceed clockwise.

For Wakhan, count her as a player during deck construction, give her a spare color but no loyalty dial, shuffle her 24 AI cards, and use an aid card for gifts. Place her to the right of the last human to choose loyalty; she takes the first turn.

## Turn Structure

On your turn take up to **two ordinary actions** in any order. You may pass either or both. Actions on court cards matching the favored suit are bonus actions and do not consume the two-action allowance. Each individual court card can supply **at most one action per turn**, regardless of how many action icons it has or whether that action is a bonus (pp. 8–9, 12).

You always have Purchase and Play. Other actions require the relevant icon on a card in your own court. A newly played card can supply an action later that same turn if unused. Choose the card used for an action; where rank matters, use that card's one, two, or three stars.

### Cleanup — page 9

1. Discard court cards until court size is at most **3 + total political stars** in your court. Recalculate as cards leave; discarded political cards can reduce your limit further.
2. Discard hand cards until hand size is at most **2 + total intelligence stars** in your court.
3. Discard events in the market's leftmost column, top row first, then bottom. Resolve each event's upper discard effect for everyone. Rupees on the event stay in its market position.
4. Slide each row's remaining cards and their rupees left without changing order. Coins left in vacated positions attach to the card moving there. Refill empty spaces leftmost column first, top before bottom.

**Instability:** if revealing a Dominance Check while another is already in the market, immediately resolve **one** check, discard both cards, and continue refilling. If the fourth/final check was among them, this counts as the final check and scores double. Resolve this interruption immediately, rather than leaving two checks together.

## Actions

### Purchase — page 10

Take a court card from the market into your hand, along with rupees already on it. Prices by column are **0/1/2/3/4/5 rupees**. Pay one rupee onto each market card to its left in that row. If a required space is empty, pay onto the opposite row's card in that column instead. You must afford payment before receiving coins on the purchased card.

When **military** is favored, prices double: pay two per card to the left. Any card on which you placed a rupee for any reason this turn is unavailable for you to purchase for the rest of that turn. The market does not slide/refill between actions.

Purchased events never enter your hand. Resolve their lower purchase effect immediately. Keep a persistent event in the appropriate player area or near the map until its stated expiration, normally the next Dominance Check. A purchased Dominance Check resolves immediately.

### Play — page 11

Reveal and announce a hand card and its region. If another player rules that region, pay them a bribe equal to their **number of tribes there**, not their armies or total ruling pieces. The ruler may waive any part. If you cannot agree/pay, the play does not occur and consumes no action. No bribe is due when you rule the region or nobody rules it.

Add the card to the **left or right end** of your court; you cannot freely rearrange existing cards. If it is a patriot of another coalition, change loyalty as described below. Resolve impact icons **top to bottom**:

| Impact | Effect |
|---|---|
| Tribe | Place one of your cylinders in the card's region |
| Army | Place one block of your coalition in that region |
| Road | Place one block of your coalition on any border of that region |
| Spy | Place one cylinder on any player's court card matching that region, including your own |
| Leveraged | Take two rupees from the bank; remember the repayment on discard |
| Suit change | Move the favored-suit marker to the depicted suit |

There is no regional, border, or card stacking limit, and hostile pieces may coexist.

### Tax — page 13

Take up to the acting card's rank in rupees, divided among any market cards and/or eligible players. A player is eligible if they have at least one court card associated with a region **you rule**. Their economic stars protect that many rupees; only the excess may be taxed. Market money has no tax-shelter restriction and does not need to be associated with a ruled region.

### Gift — page 13

Place one available personal cylinder on an empty gift space of your loyalty dial. Pay the space's marked **2, 4, or 6 rupees** to the market. Each gift adds one influence to your current coalition and is lost on changing loyalty. Each space can hold only its one gift.

### Build — page 13

Place up to **three** coalition blocks, each costing **two rupees**, as armies in regions you rule and/or roads on borders of regions you rule. Any mix is allowed, and placements can be split across eligible regions/borders. The three-unit maximum is independent of acting-card rank.

### Move — page 14

Spend up to the acting card's rank in individual moves, divided among loyal armies and your spies. One piece may move repeatedly.

- An army moves to an adjacent region only across a border containing a road of the **same coalition**.
- A spy moves one card left or right through the players' courts, treating all courts as a single continuous ring. It can cross from one player's end card to the adjacent player's court. Moving spies does not require matching regions.
- Tribes and roads do not move with this action.

### Betray — page 14

Pay **two rupees** to the market to discard one court card on which you have a spy, including a card in your own court. Return every spy on that card to its owner's supply and resolve leveraged/overthrow consequences. If the card has a coalition prize, you may accept it by tucking it behind your loyalty dial. Taking a different coalition's prize changes loyalty; taking the prize is optional.

### Battle — page 14

Choose **one region** or **one court card**. Remove up to the acting card's rank in eligible pieces, also capped by your fighting strength at that location:

- In a region, your fighting strength is loyal armies there. Eligible targets are enemy armies, enemy roads on its adjacent borders, and tribes of players whose coalition differs from yours. Your tribes provide no battle strength.
- On a court card, your fighting strength is your spies there; targets are other players' spies, even if they share your coalition.
- Never remove armies/roads of your own coalition or tribes of coalition partners. Do not spread one battle across multiple regions/cards. This action has no normal money cost.

### Paying card-action costs and hostage bribes — page 12

Gift, Build, and Betray payments go onto the market's **rightmost cards in both rows**, one rupee per card, proceeding left as needed and skipping vacant positions. Excess payment beyond the remaining market cards leaves the game. This placement also makes those paid-on cards unavailable to you to purchase this turn.

If a single other player has more spies on a card in your court than each other player individually, they hold its actions hostage. Before using any action on it, pay that player a bribe equal to their spies there, unless they waive/reduce it. This applies even to favored-suit bonus actions. Passive special abilities are never held hostage.

## Scoring / Victory Conditions

Resolve checks when bought, discarded during cleanup, or triggered through instability (p. 15).

### Successful dominance

A coalition dominates if its total armies **plus roads** exceeds **each** other coalition's total by at least **four**. Compare rivals separately, not their combined total. Thus 8 British against 4 Russian and 4 Afghan succeeds.

Only players loyal to the dominant coalition score. Rank them by influence:

**Influence = 1 + gifts + accepted prizes + patriots in your court.**

Award **5/3/1 victory points** for first/second/third place. Then remove **all coalition blocks of every color** from the map, returning them to supply. Cylinders and court cards remain, subject to applicable card effects.

### Unsuccessful dominance

Rank all players by personal cylinders in play, including spies, tribes, and gifts—cylinders not on their player board. Players with zero still participate. Award **3/1 victory points** for first/second. Leave coalition blocks on the map.

### Ties, final check, and victory

For any tied rank, add the points for all tied places, divide by the number tied, and round down. For example, two tied first on an unsuccessful normal check each earn (3 + 1) / 2 = 2. Unawarded places beyond the scoring range contribute zero.

The final Dominance Check **doubles points before dividing ties**: successful places are 10/6/2; unsuccessful 6/2. Two players tied second on an unsuccessful final check each earn one.

After **any** check, a player leading every opponent by at least **four points** wins immediately. Otherwise the final check ends the game and highest score wins. Ties break by most military stars in court, then most rupees. The rulebook's final tie-break is a playful cooking contest; agree on how to handle a still unresolved tie if needed.

## Special Rules & Edge Cases

### Ruling, loyalty, discards, and limits — pages 6–8

- To rule a region you need at least **one personal tribe** there and strictly more ruling pieces than every rival individually. Your ruling pieces are your tribes plus all armies of your current coalition. Other players of that same coalition count those armies too. Each hostile coalition's armies can block you even if no opposing tribe is present. A tie means nobody rules. Update ruler tokens immediately as the situation changes.
- Economic stars equal tax shelter; political stars increase court capacity; intelligence stars increase hand capacity; military stars break endgame ties. All are totals of ranks in that suit, rather than card counts.
- You always have exactly one coalition loyalty. Changing it requires gaining influence in another coalition through a patriot or accepted betrayal prize; you cannot simply turn your dial whenever desired.
- On a loyalty change, lose **all existing gifts, prizes, and previous patriots**, then set the new loyalty. Your personal spies/tribes remain, but blocks now help according to the new coalition.
- **Overthrow:** losing your last tribe in a region immediately discards every political card of that region from your court. Conversely, losing your last political card associated with a region immediately removes every personal tribe there. This applies during actions and discards; resolve the resulting chain.
- Whenever a court card leaves, every spy on it returns to its supply. If leveraged, its owner owes **two rupees to the bank**. For each unpaid rupee, discard one additional hand or court card, excluding the card already leaving. If no cards remain, no further payment is due. Additional leveraged discards can create further obligations.
- If required to place a block/cylinder with none in supply, take the correct shape/color from elsewhere in play, excluding pieces placed **this turn**, and place it in the new role. This can trigger overthrow. You cannot use the score marker as an ordinary piece.
- Negotiation and coordination are allowed and deals are nonbinding. Cards never transfer between players. Money transfers require a specific rule such as a bribe or tax; promises do not permit arbitrary gifts of money.
- Court abilities operate while their card remains in court. Card/event text overrides ordinary rules. Successful dominance clears blocks, not gifts, prizes, spies, or tribes; most persistent events expire at the stated check.

### Wakhan automated opponent — pages 16–18

Wakhan supports one or two human opponents; this is competitive, with only one winner. She pays ordinary costs and bribes. Treat her as loyal to **all three coalitions** for influence/scoring, keeping mixed patriots and prizes and counting each gift in every coalition. Her temporary **pragmatic loyalty** is used for placing, moving, and fighting with blocks: it is the leftmost coalition on the active AI card not shared by any human. For ruling, count her tribes plus only the most numerous single coalition's armies in that region, rather than all armies combined.

At her turn reveal one AI card to the discard pile, using its face and the next draw-deck card's back for decisions. When the draw deck empties, reshuffle the entire discard, including the card just drawn, and draw again. Follow the AI card's central action list from top to bottom, skipping impossible conditions, looping until two nonbonus actions have been used. Favored-suit actions are still bonuses and each court card remains once per turn. If she can buy a Dominance Check and score the most points and/or win, she prioritizes buying it regardless of the list. After her two actions, use remaining legal favored-card actions from leftmost unused court card, taking its leftmost legal action.

Her usual core action is **Radicalize**, combining a purchase and attempted immediate play into one action:

1. Follow any specific AI instructions, choosing cheapest qualifying card, then highest card number if tied.
2. With a Dominance Check in the market, buy it only if she scores the most points and/or wins. Otherwise favor the cheapest patriot of the dominant coalition, then the cheapest card with the most army/road impact icons. Without a dominant coalition, favor the cheapest card with the most spy/tribe icons. Break remaining ties by highest card number.
3. Otherwise the AI red arrow selects top/bottom row and black arrow the 0–5 column on the next card's back. If that card is invalid, search leftward; if exhausted, try the original column of the other row. She cannot buy cards paid onto this turn.
4. Play if able to pay the regional bribe; otherwise discard the purchased card. Top red arrow adds it to the left end of her court; bottom to the right. Her spies go to highest-priority regional cards where she lacks the most spies. Roads follow the AI's region priority in sequence, repeating if needed. Block placement always uses pragmatic loyalty, including patriot impacts.

For selecting court cards, apply this descending priority list: opponent's card; favored suit; patriot of dominant coalition; prize matching dominant coalition; other patriot; leveraged; higher rank; higher card number. Use only cards legal for the choice in question.

Default actions, unless the AI gives other instructions:

| Action | Wakhan's default |
|---|---|
| Gift | Buy cheapest affordable empty gift space |
| Build | Spend as much as possible, up to normal three blocks, on armies in the first AI-priority region she rules |
| Betray | Highest-priority card with a prize and her spy, including her own; always accept its prize |
| Battle | First priority region with an enemy piece and her army; destroy tribes, armies, then roads. If none, use highest-priority card containing her and another player's spies |
| Tax | Eligible humans before market; most rupees first, red arrow breaks ties. Otherwise leftmost market money, red arrow breaks row ties |
| Move | Armies only; she needs no roads. Move to adjacent regions with human tribes, using regional priorities, seeking no more armies than tribes and never surrendering a ruler token |

When several humans could be targeted, the red arrow breaks the relevant tie. Wakhan chooses the favored suit whenever asked to select a suit and always uses optional special abilities. If unable to repay a leveraged card, she does not discard other court cards for unpaid rupees.

For her court-limit discards, prioritize removal lexicographically: nonpolitical; nonpatriot; nonleveraged; most human spies in excess of her spies; fewest spies; lowest rank; nonfavored suit; lowest card number. She has no normal hand to manage. Award her points and test victory normally, remembering her all-coalition loyalty. Use the publisher-linked [Wakhan FAQ](https://www.boxofdelights.net/wakhan-faq) for additional AI interaction rulings beyond the source rulebook summarized here.
