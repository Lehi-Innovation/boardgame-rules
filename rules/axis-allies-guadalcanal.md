## Overview

It is August 1942, and Japan has occupied much of the Solomon Islands chain to protect the flank of its offensive in New Guinea, form a security barrier for the Japanese base at Rabaul, and interfere with Allied supply lines. The United States has just begun its counterattack with a surprise landing on Guadalcanal, capturing the under-construction airfield there (later renamed Henderson Field). One player controls the Axis (Japan), the other the Allies (United States); a game generally lasts 4 to 6 turns. Each turn, players alternate moving their units one unit type at a time, then resolve combat in contested zones in three stages — air, sea, and then land — in an attempt to seize control of the island zones and their airfields. Finally, players call for reinforcements and build more airfields. The first player to reach 15 victory points wins; victory points are earned by controlling airfields and by destroying capital ships (battleships and aircraft carriers).

## Setup

1. Decide who plays the Axis and who plays the Allies, and sit on the appropriate side of the board.
2. Give the Axis player the first-player token.
3. Put your base card adjacent to your route-to-base zone(s).
4. Put the victory-track markers on the victory tracks.
5. Put 1 Allies airfield marker on Guadalcanal and 1 Axis airfield marker on Bougainville.
6. Put an Allies control marker on Guadalcanal and Axis control markers on each of the other islands.
7. Put units and supply tokens on the board and base cards as indicated by the printed silhouettes, using plastic chips as needed.
8. Put the 12 dice into the battle box.

## Turn Structure

Axis & Allies: Guadalcanal is played in turns. Each turn consists of 3 phases, followed in order:

1. **Phase 1: Movement**
2. **Phase 2: Combat**
3. **Phase 3: Regroup**

The Axis player holds the first-player token at the start of the game and therefore moves first; the token passes to the other player at the end of each Regroup phase, so the first player alternates turn to turn.

## Actions

### Phase 1: Movement

Players alternate moving their units by unit type, following the Movement Sequence below. For each step, the first player completes that step for their own units, then the other player does the same, before moving to the next step.

**Movement Sequence:**
1. Load and Move Transports
2. Move Battleships
3. Move Aircraft Carriers
4. Move Cruisers
5. Load and Move Destroyers
6. Move and Attack with Submarines
7. Move Bombers
8. Move Fighters

General movement rules:
- **Sea unit**: may move 1 sea zone.
- **Air unit**: may move multiple zones (Fighters up to 2, Bombers up to 3), may move into sea and island zones, may move through zones containing enemy units, and is considered "in the air" even if it doesn't move.
- **Land unit**: may only be moved by transport or destroyer.
- The sea zone on a player's base card is one move away from the route-to-base sea zone(s) indicated on the board. Sea and land units cannot move directly between sea zones separated by an island zone; only air units can cross by moving through the island zone itself.

**Step details:**
1. **Load and Move Transports**: load land units and supplies from adjacent island zones onto transports (each transport has 2 spaces; each unit or supply token takes 1 space and may come from different adjacent island zones). Units being loaded are placed in the sea zone next to the transport; if loading multiple transports in the same sea zone, declare exactly how the cargo is split among them. Loaded units move with their transport when it moves. Transports may not load after moving.
2. **Move Battleships**: move 1 sea zone; no special movement rules.
3. **Move Aircraft Carriers**: any fighters loaded on a carrier move with it for free into its new zone.
4. **Move Cruisers**: move 1 sea zone; no special movement rules.
5. **Load and Move Destroyers**: load and move as with transports, except a destroyer has only 1 space for a land unit or supply token.
6. **Move and Attack with Submarines**: after moving (or declining to move), a submarine may make a special 1-die attack on a sea unit of choice in its zone. Roll in the battle box and apply the result of the first die to the chosen target. Casualties from submarine attacks are not removed until all of that player's submarines have attacked, so a damaged unit may be attacked again by another submarine. A battleship that ignores a hit from a submarine may not also ignore a hit during the Combat phase of the same turn (mark it to show it has already done so).
7. **Move Bombers**: a bomber moves up to 3 zones.
8. **Move Fighters**: a fighter moves up to 2 zones.

### Phase 2: Combat

Resolve combat in each zone containing opposing units, following the Combat Sequence. For each step, the first player chooses the order in which zones are resolved.

**Combat Sequence:**
1. Attack Air Units
2. Attack Sea Units
3. Unload Transports and Destroyers
4. Attack Land Units and/or Airfields

1. **Attack Air Units**: the first player attacks opposing air units in a zone of choice; then the other player attacks opposing air units in that zone. After both players have rolled, remove destroyed air units. Repeat for each zone where air units can be fired upon.
2. **Attack Sea Units**: same procedure for each sea zone where sea units can be fired upon. Some sea units are damaged instead of destroyed — move them to the damaged area of their base card when casualties are removed. Each artillery in an island zone may fire into only 1 adjacent sea zone per turn (declared per adjacent sea zone; multiple artillery in the same zone may fire into different sea zones); once declared against a sea zone, that artillery can't attack another sea zone that turn (mark it).
3. **Unload Transports and Destroyers**: the first player may unload units and supply tokens from transports/destroyers onto adjacent island zones (a transport carrying 2 units/tokens may unload them onto different island zones); the other player then does the same.
4. **Attack Land Units and/or Airfields**: same procedure as steps 1–2, for each island zone where land units/airfields can be fired upon. Each participating air or sea unit (not land units) must choose to attack either the enemy's land units or its airfields, not both. When attacking airfields, total the attack power of all units chosen to attack airfields, ignore the unit types printed on the neck of the battle box, and simply check that many dice in a row from the outside tip of the neck; every 2 hits (rolls of 1 or 2) damages one enemy-controlled airfield in the zone (place a damaged marker; an airfield can never carry more than 1). Each cruiser or battleship may fire into only 1 adjacent island zone per turn (declared and marked the same way as artillery above).

**How to Attack** (used for air, sea, and land combat alike):
1. **Determine Total Attack Power**: add up the attack powers of all attacking units — this is how many dice you roll. Each unit has 3 attack numbers, one per category (land, sea, air). A battleship or cruiser may add its land attack power to an attack on 1 adjacent island zone per turn; an artillery in an island zone may add its sea attack power to an attack on 1 adjacent sea zone per turn — either may be done even without friendly units present in the attacked zone. Other units add their attack power only to attacks in their own zone.
2. **Roll Dice in the Battle Box**: load all 12 dice into the neck, shake to randomize, tilt down, and pull the neck out; the dice emerge in a line. If the attack uses more than 12 dice, use the box again until enough have been rolled.
3. **Declare Your Hits**: starting from the first die at the tip of the neck, check a number of dice in a row equal to the attack power; each die showing a 1 or 2 is a hit. Read the unit type for each hit off the neck slot for the category being attacked (air, sea, or land). If a hit lands on a unit type the enemy doesn't have (or has already lost) in that zone, it is instead applied to the first available unit type reading up the neck from slot 1.

**Damage and casualties:**
- **Loss of cargo**: if a transport or destroyer is hit, the units and supply tokens it carried are destroyed. If a carrier is hit, its loaded fighters are not destroyed — they remain in that sea zone and must find a new place to land during Regroup.
- **Choosing which unit is hit**: on a roll of 2, the defender chooses which unit of the indicated type takes the hit; on a roll of 1, the attacker chooses. Exception: with multiple battleships present, each ignores one hit (its Heavy Armor ability) before any hits are assigned to them.
- **Damaging vs. destroying**: most units are destroyed on any hit of 1 or 2. Destroyers, cruisers, battleships, and aircraft carriers can instead sustain damage: a roll of 1 destroys them as normal, but a roll of 2 damages them — move the unit to the damaged area of its home base card instead of the box, to be repaired later with a supply token during Regroup. Hits are assigned to undamaged units of a type before damaged ones; a second hit (of either value) on an already-damaged unit destroys it.

### Phase 3: Regroup

The final phase of the turn, following the Regroup Sequence:

1. **Determine Control of Island Zones**
2. **Land Air Units**
3. **Build Airfields**
4. **Reinforce, Repair, and Deploy**
5. **Score Victory Points and Check for Victory**
6. **Pass the First-Player Marker**

1. **Determine Control of Island Zones**: for each island zone on the board (you can't lose control of your base island zone), total each player's land attack power of land units present to determine who controls it — for example, an artillery has 1 land attack power and an antiaircraft gun has 0 land attack power. Air units and supply tokens do not contribute to control. If you have at least 1 land unit in the island zone, your adjacent cruisers and battleships also add their land attack power to that zone; for control purposes only (unlike firing on island zones), a cruiser or battleship can exert its land attack power on all adjacent island zones at once. Whoever has the highest total land attack power controls the zone; ties keep the current controller. Mark the zone with the appropriate control marker. If no enemy land units remain in an island zone you control after this step, you capture all enemy airfields there (flip them, keeping any damaged markers) and enemy supply tokens (replace with your own), and may spend those supplies or land air units on those airfields (if undamaged) the same turn, scoring their victory points. Because capturing an enemy airfield requires eliminating all opposing units on that island, it's possible to control an island zone without controlling one or both of its airfields.
2. **Land Air Units**: fighters and bombers must land now. Bombers may move up to 3 zones and land only on their home base or a controlled airfield. Fighters may move up to 2 zones and land on their home base, a controlled airfield, or a friendly aircraft carrier. Any air unit that can't land is destroyed; an air unit can never land on a damaged or enemy-controlled airfield, even one it didn't move from this turn. Landing capacities: home base holds any number of air units; an airfield holds 2 air units per airfield marker; an aircraft carrier holds 2 fighters (no bombers).
3. **Build Airfields**: starting with the first player, spend 3 supply tokens on an island zone with an empty airfield slot to place an airfield marker (appropriate side up); you control the airfield you build even if you don't control the island. Guadalcanal, Bougainville, and New Georgia can each hold up to 2 airfields; Santa Isabel, Malaita, and Choiseul can each hold no more than 1. On a 2-slot island, each player can control an airfield there simultaneously.
4. **Reinforce, Repair, and Deploy**:
   - *Reinforce*: each player gets 10 reinforcement points per turn plus 4 points per island zone (not base card) they control — a player controlling 3 islands gets 22 points. Starting with the first player, spend points; new reinforcements go onto the appropriate base card (land units, air units, and supply tokens to the land zone; sea units to the sea zone). Any new or existing transports, destroyers, or carriers already in the sea zone may immediately load new or existing units from the base card. Unspent reinforcement points are lost.
   - *Repair*: spend 1 supply token at your home base to repair each damaged sea unit there (it returns to the sea zone on your base card and may then be loaded as normal); spend 1 supply token on an island zone to repair each damaged airfield you control there (remove its damaged marker).
   - *Deploy*: starting with the first player, deploy any or all sea units from your base card directly onto the board by spending supply tokens at your home base. Each side sea zone has a deployment number; deploying a sea unit there costs that many supply tokens. Units already loaded on a deploying sea unit move with it at no extra cost.
5. **Score Victory Points and Check for Victory**: each player scores 1 victory point for each undamaged airfield they control, adjusting the victory tracks. A player who reaches 15 victory points during this step wins the game. (If a player reaches 15 or more points during the Combat phase by destroying a capital ship, they don't win until this step.) If both players reach 15 victory points on the same turn, the player with more victory points wins; if tied, the game continues until the tie is broken during a later occurrence of this step.
6. **Pass the First-Player Marker**: the current first player passes the marker to the other player, who becomes first player for the next turn.

## Scoring / Victory Conditions

- **The first player to reach 15 victory points wins the game.**
- Victory points come from two sources: 1 victory point per turn for each undamaged airfield controlled (scored during Regroup step 5), and 1 victory point awarded immediately to a player's opponent whenever that player destroys one of the opponent's capital ships (battleships or aircraft carriers).
- Reaching 15+ points during the Combat phase (via a capital ship kill) does not win the game immediately — the win is only finalized during the Regroup phase's Score Victory Points step.
- If both players reach 15 victory points on the same turn, the player with more victory points wins; if they are exactly tied, play continues until the tie is broken on a later Regroup scoring step.

## Special Rules & Edge Cases

- **Transports and destroyers may not load after moving** — all loading must happen before that unit moves for the turn.
- **Submarine Torpedo Attack** happens during the Movement phase (not Combat), so a sea unit damaged or destroyed by it is removed from the zone before it has a chance to fire back during the Combat phase.
- **Ranged fire restrictions**: an artillery may fire into only 1 adjacent sea zone per turn; a cruiser or battleship may fire into only 1 adjacent island zone per turn. Each must be declared per target zone and, once committed to one zone, cannot also fire into another that turn (mark the unit).
- **Airfield attacks** use total attack power as a simple count of dice from the tip of the battle box neck (ignoring the printed unit-type slots); 2 hits damage 1 airfield.
- **Heavy Armor** (Battleship): ignores the first hit against it each turn, whether the die showed a 1 or a 2 — but only once per battleship per turn, and only once total across both the submarine special attack and the Combat phase.
- **Capturing airfields and supplies** requires eliminating every enemy unit in that island zone during the Determine Control step of the same turn; a player can hold an island zone without holding all the airfields on it.
- **Advantage Tokens** (optional rule): 16 tokens, 8 per player. A token's effect is spent once and then gone for the rest of the game; players may use all the advantages or only some, but each player should have the same number of tokens.
  - *Axis Advantages*: **Command Decision** (beginning of Combat, before any attacks: choose a zone and a category — air, sea, or land; after both players attack and take casualties in that category/zone, both players attack again there); **Banzai Charge** (before the Attack Land Units/Airfields step: choose an island zone; each roll of 3 against land units counts as a hit there this turn); **Night Fighting** (before the Attack Sea Units step: choose a sea zone; each roll of 2 against sea units there counts as a destroy hit, like a 1); **Quick Drop** (end of Movement phase: choose a sea zone and unload all your destroyers and transports there onto an adjacent island zone); **Superior Fighters** (during Movement, before moving fighters: your fighters may move up to 3 zones during Movement and Regroup this turn instead of 2).
  - *Allies Advantages*: **Command Decision** (identical effect to the Axis version); **Good Logistics** (during Regroup, before buying reinforcements: buying a supply token or artillery this turn gets you a second one of the same type free); **Amphibious Assault** (between the Unload Transports/Destroyers step and the Attack Land Units step: attack the land units in 1 island zone of your choice using the units you unloaded there this turn, resolved immediately without the enemy returning fire); **PT Boats** (before the Attack Sea Units step: place this token in a sea zone with at least 1 of your units; it counts as a sea unit with 1 sea attack power, can move in the Load and Move Destroyers step, and if the enemy hits a unit type you don't have there, the PT boat takes that hit and is destroyed — it otherwise stays in play until hit); **Coast Watchers** (during Movement, before moving a chosen unit type: that unit type doesn't move until after all your other units have moved this turn).
- **Hidden Movement (optional rule)**: replaces the Movement phase steps with: (1) starting with the first player, each player loads transports and destroyers normally; (2) both players secretly write down each sea unit's origin and destination sea zone (a force can split into two destinations), then reveal and move simultaneously; (3) each player may attack with submarines; (4) each player moves bombers; (5) each player moves fighters.

### Key Numbers

- Victory target: **15 victory points**.
- Airfield capacities: Guadalcanal / Bougainville / New Georgia — up to 2 each; Santa Isabel / Malaita / Choiseul — up to 1 each.
- Reinforcements: 10 points/turn + 4 points per controlled island zone.
- Airfield build cost: 3 supply tokens. Repair cost: 1 supply token (sea unit or airfield).

- Airfield damage: every 2 hits scored damages 1 enemy airfield.
