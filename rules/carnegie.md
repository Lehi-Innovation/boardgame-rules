## Overview

Carnegie is inspired by the life of Andrew Carnegie, the Scottish-born industrialist who rose to become a titan of the American steel industry and one of history's great philanthropists. Players each run a Company, recruiting employees and expanding their business by investing in real estate, producing goods, developing transport technology, and building transport chains across the United States — and, ultimately, by becoming benefactors whose donations contribute to the country's greatness.

The game runs for exactly **20 rounds**, with every player taking one turn each round. On their turn, the active player picks one of four action types (Human Resources, Management, Construction, or Research & Development) by placing the Timeline marker on that action's row; every other player then also gets to use their own Departments of that same type, in turn order. A player's success is measured in victory points (VP), earned throughout the game and at final scoring; the player with the most VP when the game ends wins.

Companies are staffed by employee figurines that can be active (standing, ready to work) or inactive (lying down, "in training"). Employees are assigned to Departments — each Department belongs to one of the four action types and can be used once per round for each active employee it holds — and may be sent out on Missions to the game board's four regional Mission areas to generate money, goods, or other benefits, returning later to generate income.

## Setup

1. Place the main game board in the center of the table.
2. Shuffle the 8 Timeline tiles, draw 4 at random (each is double-sided; pick sides randomly), and arrange them as shown. Frame these 4 with the Start tile and End tile. Return the other 4 Timeline tiles to the box.
3. Place the 4 action markers on their starting positions at the far left of the Timeline.
4. Depending on player count, randomly remove some Department tiles and return them to the box before laying the rest face-up on the table: remove 4 for a 4-player game, 8 for a 3-player game, or 16 for a 2-player game.
5. Create a shared supply of goods cubes and money next to the board.
6. Give each player:
   - **A.** 4 goods cubes and $12.
   - **B.** 1 Company board and their 4 different project tabs, slid under the right side of the board matching colors/icons, leaving each tab's first (rightmost) space exposed.
   - **C.** 10 employee figurines: place 1 standing (active) on each of the Company's 5 starting Departments, and lay the other 5 down (inactive) in the Company's Lobby. Set the player's remaining 5 figurines aside — they are not available at the start of the game.
   - **D.** 30 player disks, of which: 1 goes on the "0" space of the score track; 1 goes on the first space of each of the 4 regional transportation tracks; 1 goes on each of the 3 starting locations on the player's project tabs (Housing, Commerce, Industry — the Public Infrastructure/magenta tab does not start with a disk); the remaining 22 disks form the player's personal supply.
7. The most daring player becomes the first player and takes the first-player marker and the Timeline marker.
8. Action Choice tiles: in a 4-player game, every player gets one; in a 3-player game, only the third player gets one; in a 2-player game, none are used.
9. In a 2-player game, set aside 18 disks of an unused player color and shuffle the solo-game action cards. Draw cards one at a time, and for each: place a disk on the donation chart space indicated at the top of the card, then place a disk on the leftmost unoccupied construction space of each of the 2-4 cities named on the card's 4th row. Repeat until all 18 disks are placed. In a 3-player game, do the same but with only 9 disks. Skip this step entirely in a 4-player game.
10. Beginning with the player to the right of the first player and proceeding counter-clockwise, each player places the disk from their Housing tab onto a Housing space in a medium or major city on the board.
11. Starting with the first player and going clockwise, each player may move employees up to a total of 6 steps around their own Company board (each individual move must be orthogonal, never diagonal; a moved employee ends up lying down/inactive; employees may move onto empty building spaces).
12. Finally, once all movement is complete, each player may activate employees (pay the listed Workstation cost, if any, to stand an employee up), exactly as at the end of a normal round.

## Turn Structure

The game lasts **20 rounds**. Each round has four parts, always in this order:

1. **Select Timeline Row**
2. **Events**
3. **Use Departments**
4. **Activate Employees & End of Round**

### 1. Select Timeline Row
The first player chooses which Timeline row to use this round — Human Resources, Management, Construction, or R&D — and places the Timeline marker (the gear) immediately to the right of that row's action marker. This determines which Departments everyone may use this round and triggers any event on the space the Timeline marker lands on.

A player may choose a row even if its action marker has already reached the end of its Timeline; in that case the first player must still place the Timeline marker to its right, and must also flip face-down the action marker on the row immediately below the chosen one (or on Human Resources, if R&D was chosen). If that marker cannot move forward, flip the marker on the next row down instead, continuing downward as needed. A face-down action marker triggers the event on the space immediately to its right. At the end of the round, that marker is turned back face-up and advanced one space along its row.

### 2. Events
Placing the Timeline marker may trigger one of two events:

- **Take Income:** If the Timeline marker lands on a space showing a Mission area, that region's Mission area becomes active. Starting with the first player and going clockwise, each player with at least one employee in that active Mission area may return one or more of them to their Company's Lobby. Each returned employee generates transport income equal to the player's current position on that region's transportation track (at the start of the game, every region generates $1 in transport income); a player must take exactly the income their transport level dictates and cannot choose otherwise. Any player who returns at least one employee also collects, once per round regardless of how many employees returned, the income shown on any of their already-built projects' revealed tab spaces. A player who cannot or does not want to return an employee receives no income or transport income that round.
- **Make a Donation:** If the Timeline marker lands on a donation symbol, the first player may make a donation, then the other players may do so in clockwise order. To donate, a player places one of their disks on an unoccupied space of the donation chart. A player's first donation costs $5; each subsequent donation by that same player costs $5 more than their last (a 2nd donation costs $10, a 3rd costs $15, and so on). A maximum of 12 VP may be earned from any single donation.
- If the Timeline marker lands on one of the four rightmost Timeline spaces, each player may choose to do one or both of Take Income and Make a Donation.

### 3. Use Departments
The first player uses any of their own Departments matching the chosen action type, in any order, then the other players do the same in clockwise order. Each Department may be used once per active employee it holds. A player is never required to use every active employee, but must finish with one Department before moving to another, and may not return to a Department already used that turn. When a Department offers multiple possible effects, the player chooses which effect applies for each active employee individually. Departments with passive/ongoing effects (see Departments 4, 8, 12, and 16 below) work automatically as long as they hold an active employee — except New Lobby, the only Department that needs no active employee at all to function.

**Action Choice tiles** (3-4 player games only) let a player use a different action type than the one the first player selected; each tile is used once per game (return it to the box to use it), and any Action Choice tile left unused at game end is worth 3 VP.

### 4. Activate Employees & End of Round
After all other actions are finished, players may activate inactive employees in any of their Departments by paying the cost shown under an empty Workstation and standing that employee upright there. Once every player has had a turn, the first player advances the round's action marker one space to the right (displacing the Timeline marker), then passes the Timeline marker and first-player role to the next player clockwise, beginning a new round.

## Actions

Each Company starts with 1 Lobby and 5 Departments, and may add more Departments during play (a Company may never hold two identical Departments). A Department cannot be used unless it holds at least one active (standing) employee, and each Department may be used once per round for each active employee it holds.

### Human Resources
Moves employees within a Company. Steps, in order: (1) calculate the total number of moves available — each active employee in a Human Resources Department provides 3 moves, and since the starting Human Resources Department always has a permanent active employee (no other employee may occupy that Workstation), every player always has at least 3 moves available; (2) move active and/or inactive employees anywhere on the Company board up to that total, one orthogonal step at a time (never diagonal) — moving an active employee lays it down (makes it inactive), and any space or Department may hold any number of inactive employees. The number of available moves is fixed at the start of the turn; moving an active employee off of Human Resources itself does not reduce the total.

### Management
Every Company starts with two different Management Departments:
- **Commerce and Finance:** for each active employee here, choose one of: receive $3; receive a goods cube; send the employee on a Mission and receive $6; or send the employee on a Mission and receive 2 goods cubes.
- **Strategic Planning:** for each active employee here, either pay 1 goods cube to place a new Department on a free space of the Company board that already contains at least one employee, or pay 2 goods cubes to place a new Department on any free space.

### Construction
Builds Housing, Commerce, Industry, and Public Infrastructure projects. When used, a player: sends the employee taking the action to the Mission area of the region where they wish to build; pays 1 or 2 goods cubes depending on the project type; then moves a disk from the matching project tab onto a construction site in that same region. If the project is built in certain small cities (marked on the board), the player immediately receives the transport income shown just below their marker's current position on that region's transportation track. If more than one disk is available on a project tab, the player must build the one farthest to the right. The chosen project must be built in the region where the employee was sent, on an unoccupied board space, and on a space matching that project's type symbol — or in a small town, which accepts any project type.

### Research & Development (R&D)
Improves transportation networks and advances project tabs. Each active employee in an R&D Department generates study points (the starting R&D Department and one example expanded R&D Department shown in the rules each provide different point totals per employee — for instance, 3 points from the starting Department versus 4 points from a particular built R&D Department). Study points may be spent, in any combination and any number of times in a turn, on: moving a project tab one space to the right and placing a new project disk on the newly revealed space (paying that space's listed study-point cost); or moving a transport disk one space to the right on any regional transportation track (paying its listed cost). Unused study points are lost at the end of the turn. Advancing a project tab beyond its build-marked spaces earns the VP printed on the tab at game end; a fully-advanced tab can no longer absorb study points.

### Departments (16 total)
Departments 4, 8, 12, and 16 are the ones with passive/ongoing effects (they function automatically while staffed, rather than requiring the employee to go on a Mission).

| # | Department | Type of use | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Training and Partnerships | Send employee on Mission | Receive any combination of money and employee moves totaling no more than 8 (e.g., $2 & 6 moves, $4 & 4 moves, or $7 & 1 move) |
| 2 | Recruiting | Send employee on Mission | Place a new employee from the player's own supply into their Company's Lobby; has no further effect once all 5 of that supply's employees have been recruited (employees may also enter play via Housing-tab income and the East transportation track) |
| 3 | Safety and Quality | Send employee on Mission | Receive 1 VP for every two active employees in the Company (rounded up); employees on Missions do not count as active |
| 4 | New Lobby | Ongoing (no active employee required) | Once built, adds a second Lobby; new recruits and returning employees may be allocated between the two Lobbies as desired — the only Department that functions with no active employee at all |
| 5 | Purchasing | Send employee on Mission | Take 3 goods cubes from the supply |
| 6 | Sales | Used freely, once per active employee | Pay 1 to 3 goods cubes to the supply; receive $5 for each cube paid |
| 7 | Logistics | Used freely, once per active employee | Pay 1 to 3 goods cubes to the supply; receive $3 and 1 VP for each cube paid |
| 8 | Property Management | Ongoing (requires an active employee) | Whenever the player adds a new Department to their Company, immediately move one employee (active or inactive) from anywhere on the Company board to the new Department, in an inactive state; it may be activated normally at end of round |
| 9 | Construction | Send employee on Mission | Pay only 1 goods cube to build a new project (even an Industry or Public Infrastructure project, which normally costs 2 goods cubes) |
| 10 | Construction Outsourcing | Used freely, without sending an employee on a Mission | Pay $4 plus 1-2 goods cubes (per the project type) to build a new project anywhere on the board |
| 11 | Supply Chain | Used freely, once per active employee | Purchase up to 3 goods cubes at $1 each |
| 12 | Communications | Ongoing (requires an active employee) | The cost of the player's donations is calculated at $3 per donation instead of $5 |
| 13 | Advanced Research | Send employee on Mission | Gain 8 study points |
| 14 | Advanced Design | Used freely, once per active employee | Gain 4 study points for each active employee in the Department |
| 15 | Charitable Giving | Send employee on Mission | After paying the appropriate donation cost, place a donation disk on top of another player's existing donation disk (instead of an empty space), duplicating that donation; a player may not make the same donation twice |
| 16 | Telegraph Operators | Ongoing (requires an active employee) | The player's study-point cost to progress along transportation tracks is reduced by 1, to a minimum of 1 |

## Scoring / Victory Conditions

The game ends after 20 rounds, once all 4 action markers have reached the final space of their Timelines and every player has finished their actions for the round. During the game, players earn some VP immediately (through income or certain Departments); the rest is scored only at the very end.

A player's final score is their accumulated immediate VP, **plus**:
- **3 VP** for each unused Action Choice tile.
- **1 VP** for each active (standing) employee in the Company. Employees currently on a Mission, and the permanent employee that always occupies the starting Human Resources Department, do not count.
- **2 or 3 VP** for each Department built during the game: Departments built in a Company's topmost row score 3 VP each; all other built Departments score 2 VP each (these values are also printed on the Company board to the left of each row).
- VP earned from project tabs, up to a maximum of **6 VP for Housing, 9 VP for Commerce, 12 VP for Industry, and 15 VP for Public Infrastructure**.
- VP earned from connecting major cities (see below), up to a maximum of **36 VP**.
- **0-3 VP** for each Construction project built in a small, medium, or major city, per the value printed below that city on the game board.
- VP earned from donations, up to a maximum of **12 VP per donation**.

**Connections between major cities:** at game end, a player scores VP for linking at least two of the four major cities — New York, Chicago, New Orleans, and San Francisco — via a continuous, unbroken line of their own construction disks. The player adds up the connection points for the cities they've linked, then looks up the total against the *lowest* level of transport development they've reached across all the regions in that connected network (as shown on the scoring chart on the board). If a player has two separate, unconnected networks of major-city links, only the higher-scoring network counts. (Example from the rules: a player connecting San Francisco, Chicago, and New York, having reached "Railroad" level in every one of those regions, scores 27 VP; a player connecting San Francisco, New Orleans, and Chicago but falling short of Railroad level in the West only scores 18 VP.)

**The player with the most total points wins.** There are no tiebreakers: tied players share the victory.

## Special Rules & Edge Cases

**Active vs. inactive employees:** An employee always enters a Company board inactive (lying down). An inactive employee that moves stays inactive; an active (standing) employee that moves must be laid back down. Each Department can hold one active employee per Workstation it shows, plus any number of inactive employees; Lobbies may always hold any number of inactive employees regardless. Employees may only be activated at the very end of a round, after all other actions are complete, by paying the cost shown under an empty Workstation (if a Workstation shows no cost, activation there is free).

**Sending employees on Missions:** to use a Department marked for Missions, move the active employee from its Workstation to one of the four Mission areas on the board — it must be the same employee used to take the action. An employee cannot be sent on a Mission if the player cannot meet that action's requirements (for example, lacking the goods cubes needed to build). Employees on Missions do not count as active. Returning employees are placed inactive in the Company's Lobby, where they generate the income described in "Take Income" above; to make a returned employee active again, it must first be moved out of the Lobby via a Human Resources action, after which it becomes eligible for activation at the next end of round.

**Transportation Tracks:** each region's track starts at "Cart" (South, Midwest, West) or "Stagecoach" (East), and can progress to "Railroad" or beyond. Reaching the very last box of a region's track earns an immediate one-time reward of 3 goods cubes or $10 (depending on the track), and only one player may ever occupy that final box.

**Solo Mode (vs. Andrew):** In a 2-player game, one seat can instead be played by a virtual opponent named Andrew. Setup mirrors the normal game (including the 6-step employee movement and only 16 available Departments), except Andrew has no employees, VP, money, goods cubes, study points, or individual Company board, is assumed to always have enough of everything, and starts with all of his disks available for construction using an unused player color (he does not place a starting disk on the board). Andrew places one disk on each of the 4 regional transportation tracks like a normal player. His 5 VP cards are arranged in order from 0 to 10, and the 20 action cards are shuffled into a draw pile placed to the left of the "0 VP" card; a locomotive token starts at the top-left of the "0 VP" card.

Each round has 5 steps: (1) the human player draws Andrew's top action card, unseen, and places it face-down below the "0 VP" card; (2) whoever the locomotive currently favors chooses the Timeline row for the round — if it's on the left, the human player chooses as normal; if on the right, the symbol on the back of that round's action card dictates Andrew's chosen row; (3) Andrew's turn: the card is flipped face-up, any Mission event does nothing for Andrew, any donation event has Andrew place a disk on the indicated donation space (or, if it's already occupied or he's out of disks, his card instead slides one space right); then his chosen action type resolves mechanically off the card (Human Resources slides his card right by the shown amount; Management has him take a tile from each Department numbered on the card, sliding the card right once per Department he couldn't take; Construction places one of his disks on the leftmost open construction site of each named city, sliding right once per site he couldn't use; R&D moves his transport disk 1-3 spaces as shown, sliding right once per move he couldn't make) — Andrew's card can never advance past the rightmost "10 VP" card; (4) the human player resolves Take Income/Make a Donation and uses matching Departments exactly as in the standard game; (5) end of round: the human player may activate employees as normal, advances the actual action marker one space, and Andrew's action card is turned face-down and filed under whichever VP card it reached (or under "0 VP" if it never moved) before the locomotive flips to the other side of the "0 VP" card.

At game end, Andrew scores: VP per the position of each of his action cards along the VP-card row; 2 VP for each Department tile he acquired; 6 VP for each of his transport disks that reached the last position of a track (he does not score for city connections); the printed VP value (0-3) of each city where he placed a construction disk; and donation VP based on what the human player actually did during the game — for example, if the human built 5 projects in the West and Andrew holds the donation disk awarding 2 VP per project built in that region, Andrew scores 10 VP for it. The higher total between Andrew and the human player wins.

## Player Reference

**Game length:** 20 rounds, one turn per player per round.

**Round structure:** (1) Select Timeline Row -> (2) Events (Take Income and/or Make a Donation) -> (3) Use Departments -> (4) Activate Employees & End of Round.

**Starting resources per player:** $12, 4 goods cubes, 10 employees (5 active on Departments, 5 inactive in the Lobby; 5 more held in reserve), 1 Company board with 4 project tabs, 3 disks already placed on project tabs (Housing/Commerce/Industry), 1 disk on each of the 4 transportation tracks, 1 score-track disk at "0."

**Human Resources moves:** 3 moves per active employee in that Department; the starting Department's permanent employee guarantees a minimum of 3 moves every turn.

**Donation costs:** 1st = $5, 2nd = $10, 3rd = $15, increasing by $5 each time; maximum 12 VP per donation.

**Project-tab VP caps:** Housing 6, Commerce 9, Industry 12, Public Infrastructure 15.

**Major-city connection bonus:** up to 36 VP, based on connection points scaled by the lowest transport level reached across the connected regions.

**Department-building VP:** 3 VP if built in the Company's topmost row, 2 VP for any other row.

**Other end-game VP:** 1 VP per active employee (Missions and the permanent HR employee excluded); 3 VP per unused Action Choice tile; 0-3 VP per Construction project per its city's printed value.

**Transportation track start/end:** South, Midwest, West start at "Cart"; East starts at "Stagecoach"; tracks run up through "Railroad" and beyond; reaching the final box pays out 3 goods cubes or $10 (track-dependent) and can only be occupied by one player.

**Winner:** highest total score; ties are shared (no tiebreaker).
