## Overview

Auf Achse is a trucking and transportation-contract trading game for 2-6 players designed by Wolfgang Kramer and published by Schmidt Spiele. Freight and money dominate the transport trade between Flensburg and Verona: players drive trucks around the board, picking up and delivering goods to complete lucrative transportation contracts. The competition among carriers requires sophisticated route planning, tense price struggles, and optimal use of truck cargo space. The goal is to complete as many lucrative transportation contracts as possible to earn money — at the end of the game, the player with the most cash wins.

## Setup

1. Place the goods cubes on the loading space of the game board.
2. Put the traffic jam sign and construction sign into their plastic bases and place them next to the game board.
3. Shuffle the event cards and put them face-down on the appropriate space on the game board.
4. Give each player a starting capital of €5000. Give the remaining money to one player, who manages it as the banker.
5. Shuffle the contract cards and deal 3 contracts to each player, which they should keep concealed from the other players.
6. Depending on the number of players, a different number of the remaining cards are placed in a face-down pile next to the game board to form a draw pile; place any further remaining cards back in the box:
   - 2 players: 12 cards
   - 3 players: 16 cards
   - 4 players: 20 cards
   - 5-6 players: 24 cards
7. Take the top 4 contract cards from the draw pile and place them face-up onto the four contract card spaces on the game board — these are the "public contracts."
8. Each player chooses a truck and places it on any space of the game board. In selecting a location, players should look at their contract cards — you can place your truck in a city that is the starting point for one of your contracts.

## Turn Structure

1. **Determine the starting player**: the person who has driven the longest route this month goes first.
2. **Roll and move**: on a turn, a player rolls both dice, then moves their truck in any direction according to the number of pips shown on *one* of the two dice (the player picks which die to use for movement).
   - A player must move the full value of the chosen die unless they reach the traffic jam sign, the construction sign, or the starting/ending city of one of their contract cards, in which case they may stop there and forfeit the rest of their movement.
   - If one of the dice shows a 6, the player may instead choose any value from 1 to 6 for that die. This makes it easier to land on a city by exact count in order to acquire public contracts.
   - It is permitted to drive through spaces occupied by trucks. No more than 2 trucks can occupy a highway space at the end of a turn; city spaces have no limit.
   - The two ferries can be used like normal road spaces at no extra cost: Rotterdam-Flensburg counts as 3 spaces, and Flensburg-Rostock counts as 2 spaces.
3. **Resolve the space the truck stopped on** (see Actions):
   - *Initiate or deliver your own contracts*: allowed whenever the truck ends on the contract's start/destination city — any excess rolled movement is ignored, so you may overshoot and still stop there (exact count is not required).
   - *Exact-count-only effects*: drawing an event card and selecting a public contract require arriving by exact count. If the player had to forfeit excess movement to stop on the city, or arrived via an event card, they may not select a public contract that turn.
4. **End of turn**: once a player has finished their turn, the next player clockwise takes their turn. Note that successfully initiating a contract ends a player's turn immediately (see Actions).

## Actions

### Initiate a Contract
Each contract card shows a starting city and a destination. To initiate a contract, a player must end their movement at the start city shown on the card (any excess movement that was rolled can be ignored when initiating a contract). The player places the contract card face-up in front of themselves on the table and loads into their truck the number of goods cubes shown on the card. This ends the player's turn immediately. If a player receives a contract card while their truck is already in the start city for that card, they may immediately initiate that contract, provided they have sufficient space to accommodate the entire load. On a player's next turn, they are not required to drive directly to the destination city — they may instead choose to initiate additional contracts in order to maximize the use of their cargo space. A contract must always be fully executed; it is not possible to load only part of a contract. If a player has insufficient capacity for a contract, they may buy a trailer.

### Deliver a Contract
When a player arrives at the destination for one of their initiated contracts (extra rolled movement is ignored), they unload the goods for the contract, placing them back into the stockpile on the board. The player then receives the contract's payment from the bank and removes the completed card from the game.

### Buy or Sell a Trailer
To transport more than 6 goods, a player may, during their turn, purchase as many trailers as they desire to increase their loading capacity — by 4 goods (small trailer) or 6 goods (large trailer) respectively. Purchased trailer cards are placed on the table in front of the player and can be loaded with additional goods whenever needed; a player keeps purchased trailer cards for the rest of the game. During their turn, a player can also sell any of their trailer cards for €500 each, regardless of the size of the trailer. A player who still has trailer cards at the end of the game receives no money for them — they are worthless. Four large trailers are available (6 additional goods capacity each, price €3000 per trailer) and four small trailers are available (4 additional goods capacity each, price €2000 per trailer).

### Draw an Event Card
Event spaces are marked with a red/white triangle. When a player lands on an event space by exact count, they must draw the top card of the event card deck and follow the instructions on the card. After completing the required action, the event card is removed from the game. If a player cannot pay the cost of an event card, they must place the card face-up beside them until they can pay double the amount due. Note: the event cards are mostly positive.

### Trigger the Construction Sign
When a player rolls and chooses a 1 (or a 6 used as a 1), they first move their truck one space and then must place the construction sign on the board (or move it, if it is already in play). The sign must be placed on an unoccupied highway space — it may not be placed on a city space. The sign disables that section of highway; trucks may not move through it.

### Traffic Jam Sign
The traffic jam sign comes into play through some event cards and is valid for only one round. It closes one area to traffic, forcing trucks to wait or make a detour.

### Select and Bid on a Public Contract
When a player arrives at any city by exact count, they may select one of the four face-up public contract cards on the board and express interest in acquiring it. If the player had to forgo excess movement points in order to stop on the city, or arrived there by an event card, they may not select one of the public contract cards. If a player selects a public contract, other players who are also interested in that contract (or who want to drive up the price for it) may bid for it. All players may, in turn, make a bid as in an auction: the player to the left of the person who selected the contract starts the bidding, followed by the other players in turn (clockwise), who must increase the bid or pass. The player who selected the contract has an advantage — they do not have to bid higher than their competitors to obtain the contract; their bid only has to equal the previous bid. Players who did not pass can continue another round of bidding and either increase the bid or pass. All bids are made in accordance with the bids ("Angebot") numbered 1 to 5 that are printed on the contract card; the table on the card shows the cost and net proceeds for each bid level. The player who wins the auction must immediately pay the purchase price from the cost table to the bank — credit is not granted. If no other player places a bid, the player who selected the contract obtains it for no cost. The winning player then takes the contract into their hand and may initiate it whenever they reach the starting city (or immediately, if already there with enough free cargo space). Whenever there is a gap in the public contracts, slide the remaining contracts in the direction of the arrows to fill the gap, then draw a new contract card from the deck and place it in the opened public contract space in the corner of the board.

**Example:** Four players (Red, Yellow, Blue, Green) are playing. Red chooses the Berlin-Trieste contract. Yellow passes. Blue skips bid level 1 and immediately bids 2. Green is also interested and bids 3. Red bids the same (3) — having selected the contract, Red does not need to increase the bid to match. Blue now passes. Green now increases the bid to 4. Red now passes. Green wins the contract and immediately pays €2800 to the bank. Upon delivery, Green will receive €3900 in freight revenue, for a profit of €1100.

## Scoring / Victory Conditions

The game ends immediately once there are no more public contracts on the board **and** a player has completed all of the contracts in their hand — it does not matter in which order these two conditions occur. The player with the most money wins the game. Unfinished contracts (still in hand or in progress) are worthless at game end, and any unsold trailer cards are also worthless.

## Special Rules & Edge Cases

- **Public contract cycling:** whenever a player arrives at a city by exact count (without forfeiting any movement points) and is not interested in any of the current public contracts, the public contract closest to the loading dock is removed from the game. The remaining contracts then slide in the direction of the arrows, and a new contract card is drawn to fill the freed-up public contract space in the corner. This release mechanic is specifically designed to help end the game and speeds play up.
- A contract must always be fully executed — partial loading is not allowed.
- Initiating a contract ends the acting player's turn immediately, even if movement was still available.
- Landing on a space by exact count is required to draw event cards and to select public contracts, but **not** to initiate or deliver your own contracts: for both initiating and delivering, any excess rolled movement is ignored, so a player may overshoot and still stop at the start/destination city. Being forced to stop early (forfeiting excess movement) or arriving via an event card specifically disqualifies a player from selecting a public contract that turn.
- Rolling a 6 lets a player treat that die as any value from 1 to 6, which is the main tool for landing exactly on a desired city.
- Trailers, once purchased, are kept for the rest of the game (they don't need to be re-bought) and can be sold back at any time during a player's turn for a flat €500, regardless of trailer size.

### Simplified Game Variant (for children from 8 years old)
The basic rules apply, with the following changes:
1. During preparation, each player receives 5 contract cards in their hand (instead of 3, and no separate draw pile).
2. The stack for public procurement contains only 10 cards, of which 4 cards are placed face-up on the appropriate spaces on the board.
3. Trailer cards are not used. Each truck has a fixed capacity of only 6 goods.
4. Whenever the starting city of a public contract is reached, that contract may simply be taken — contracts are not sold at auction. If the player can immediately load the necessary goods into their truck, the contract is placed face-up in front of them. If the goods cannot be loaded, the contract card is taken into their hand and can be loaded later by revisiting the starting city with sufficient empty cargo space. After a contract is taken, a new contract card is laid out to replace it. The arrows and the bid table printed on the contract cards have no meaning in this variant.
