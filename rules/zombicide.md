## Overview

Zombicide is a cooperative game in which 1-6 players control Survivors of a zombie outbreak — one Survivor each in a 4-6 player game, or several Survivors per player in smaller games — working together to complete a Mission's objectives while fighting off hordes of Zombies controlled entirely by the game system (there is no "Zombie player"). Survivors scavenge Zones for weapons and equipment, kill Zombies to gain experience and unlock Skills, and must cooperate — trading gear, sharing wounds, and making tactical sacrifices — to survive an escalating tide of undead. The game is won immediately, for every player, the instant all Mission objectives are accomplished, even if some Survivors died along the way; it is lost only when every Survivor has been killed.

## Components

- 6 dice
- 71 miniatures: 6 Survivors, 40 Walkers, 16 Runners, 8 Fatties, 1 Abomination
- 9 game tiles (double-sided)
- 6 Survivor Identity Cards
- 110 mini-cards: 42 Zombie cards, 62 Equipment cards, 6 Wounded cards
- 4 car tokens (Police Car & Pimpmobile designs)
- 6 Zombie Spawn tokens
- 1 Exit Point token
- 12 Door counters
- 18 Noise counters
- 24 Skill counters
- 1 First Player token
- 10 Objective tokens
- 6 Experience trackers

## Setup

1. Choose a Mission.
2. Place the tiles as shown by the Mission.
3. Place the doors, cars, and Objectives per the Mission.
4. Set aside the pimpmobile cards (Evil Twins and Ma's Shotgun), the Molotov card, the Pan cards, and the Wounded cards. Also set aside a single Fire Axe, Crowbar, and Pistol card.
5. Shuffle the Zombie cards into one deck and the Equipment cards into another; place both face down near the board.
6. Each player chooses Survivors based on player count:
   - 1 player: 4 Survivors.
   - 2 players: 3 Survivors per player.
   - 3 players: 2 Survivors per player.
   - 4-6 players: 1 Survivor per player.
7. Place the chosen Survivors' miniatures on the starting area indicated by the Mission.
8. On each Survivor's Identity Card, place an experience tracker on the first square of the Danger Bar's blue area, and a Skill counter on the Survivor's first (starting) Skill.
9. Randomly deal starting equipment: a Fire Axe, a Crowbar, a Pistol, and enough Pan cards for every Survivor to have one card. If a Survivor's starting Skill lists a starting weapon, that Survivor also receives that Equipment card from the Equipment deck, independent of (and in addition to) the randomly dealt card.
10. Decide who is the first player and give them the First Player token — do not choose randomly, since Zombicide is a cooperative game.

## Turn Structure

Each game turn has three phases, always in this order:

### 1. Players' Phase
Starting with the player holding the First Player token, each player activates their Survivors one at a time, in any order they choose. Each Survivor has 3 Actions at the Blue Danger Level (4 once they reach Yellow); free Actions granted by Skills don't count against this total. Once a player has activated all their Survivors, the player to their left takes their turn the same way. The phase ends once every player has activated all Survivors.

### 2. Zombies' Phase
Every Zombie on the board activates (Attacks resolve first, then Moves — see Special Rules & Edge Cases), then new Zombies appear in the active Spawn Zones.

### 3. End Phase
All Noise tokens are removed from the board, and the First Player token passes to the player on the left. A new game turn then begins.

## Actions

Each Survivor gets 3 Actions per turn (4 at Yellow Danger Level or higher). Unless stated otherwise, any Action may be repeated multiple times in a turn. Available Actions:

- **Move**: Move to an adjacent Zone (sharing an edge — no diagonal movement). Moving between a building and the street requires a door. Leaving a Zone containing Zombies costs one extra Action per Zombie present; entering a Zone with Zombies ends the Move Action immediately, even for a Survivor with a Skill that would normally let them move through multiple Zones.
- **Search**: Indoors only, and only in a Zone with no Zombies present. Draw one card from the Equipment deck; keep it in inventory or discard it immediately. Only one Search Action per turn per Survivor, even if it's an extra/free Action; after searching, the Survivor may freely reorganize their inventory. Cars in the Survivor's (Zombie-free) Zone may also be Searched: a pimpmobile only once per game (yields Ma's Shotgun or the Evil Twins, drawn randomly — once all such weapons across all pimpmobiles in the Mission are taken, none remain to find); a police car any number of times (draw until a weapon is found, discarding non-weapon draws; drawing an "Aaahh!" card spawns a Walker as usual and interrupts the Search). If the Equipment deck runs out, reshuffle all discards — except Wounded cards, Pans, Molotov, and pimpmobile cards — into a new deck.
- **Open a Door**: Doors are usually locked and require a Melee weapon with the "open a door" symbol in hand. Opening a building's door for the first time reveals every Zone of that building at once: draw one Zombie card per Zone (in any order) and place the indicated Zombies, unless the draw is an Extra Activation or Manhole card, which instead resolves its own effect and adds no Zombies to that Zone. The "Lock it down" Skill can re-lock an open door so re-opening it later doesn't trigger another spawn.
- **Reorganize Inventory / Trade**: For one Action, a Survivor may rearrange their own inventory cards freely and may simultaneously exchange any number of cards with one other Survivor in the same Zone (that Survivor then reorganizes their own inventory for free as part of the trade). Independent of this Action, discarding cards to make room in inventory is always free and can be done at any time.
- **Ranged Combat**: Attack with an equipped Ranged weapon (max Range of 1+) at a Zone within its printed Range that the Survivor can see.
- **Melee Combat**: Attack with an equipped Melee weapon (max Range 0) against Zombies in the Survivor's own Zone.
- **Get In/Out of a Car**: Getting in requires an empty seat (driver or one of three passenger seats) and no Zombies in the Zone; there's no restriction on getting out.
- **Change Seats in a Car**: Move to a different empty seat in the same car; impossible if no seat is empty.
- **Drive a Car**: Only if the Mission allows it. The Survivor in the driver's seat moves the car 1-2 street Zones per Action (cars cannot enter buildings). This is not a Move Action, so it ignores Move-related bonuses/penalties, including the extra-Action cost for leaving a Zone with Zombies. The car attacks every Actor in each Zone it starts in, passes through, or ends in (see Special Rules & Edge Cases — Car Attack).
- **Take or Activate an Objective**: Take an Objective token or activate an object in the Survivor's Zone, per the Mission's rules.
- **Make Noise**: Place one Noise token in the Survivor's Zone.
- **Do Nothing**: Ends the Survivor's turn early; any unused Actions are lost.

**Noise**: opening a door or attacking produces one Noise token in the Survivor's Zone only when that specific use (open-a-door or kill) is marked "noisy" on the weapon's card — for example, the Fire Axe is noisy to open a door but silent in Melee, while the Chainsaw is noisy for both uses. A single Action produces at most one Noise token, regardless of dice rolled, hits scored, or Dual-weapon use. Every Survivor's own miniature also counts as a Noise token in its own Zone. All Noise tokens are cleared in the End Phase.

## Scoring / Victory Conditions

- **Win**: Achieved immediately, for all players together, when every objective of the chosen Mission has been accomplished — even if some Survivors died getting there.
- **Loss**: The game is lost when every Survivor, across all players, has been killed and eliminated.
- Specific win objectives are set per Mission (examples from this box: reach an Exit Zone with survivors and/or supply cards, collect specific supply cards, reach the Red Danger Level, neutralize Spawn Zones, cleanse a "bunker" Zone of Zombies, return a set number of Survivors to the starting Zone). This box includes a Tutorial plus 10 Missions — Tutorial, City Blocks, Y-Zone, The 24hrs Race of Zombicity, Drive-by Shooting, Big W, The Escape, Grindhouse, Zombie Police, Might Makes Right, and Small Town — ranging from Easy to Hard difficulty, 20-180 minutes, and typically requiring 4+ or 6+ Survivors (Might Makes Right is built for 1-3 players with one Survivor each). Missions may be played in any order, and a Mission's special rules override the general rules and card text when they conflict.
- Experience (see Player Reference) does not by itself win a Mission — it raises a Survivor's Danger Level and unlocks Skills — though some Missions (e.g., The 24hrs Race of Zombicity) use "reach a given Danger Level" as a win condition.

## Special Rules & Edge Cases

- **Line of Sight**: On streets, Actors (Survivors or Zombies) see in straight lines parallel to the board's edges, covering as many Zones as the line crosses before hitting a wall or the board's edge; diagonal sight is not possible. Inside a building, an Actor sees only into Zones that share an opening with their own Zone, and only 1 Zone away — an open doorway does not block sight, but a closed door does. Sight from a street into a building (or vice versa) can span any number of street Zones in a straight line, but only 1 Zone once inside the building.
- **Zombies' Phase — Activation**: Each Zombie spends its one Action (Runners get a second — see below) on either an Attack or a Move. All Attacks are resolved first, then all Moves. A Zombie sharing a Survivor's Zone always attacks successfully, with no roll: it snatches an Equipment card from that Survivor if the Survivor is carrying any, and the Survivor receives a Wounded card in its place. A Wounded card occupies an inventory slot and cannot be discarded or traded away (only removed by an effect like the Medic Skill). A Survivor holding two Wounded cards is eliminated and their remaining Equipment is discarded. When multiple Zombies share a Survivor's Zone, all of them attack, even past the point of overkill; if multiple Survivors share the Zone, the players decide together how to distribute the resulting Wounds.
- **Zombies' Phase — Movement**: A Zombie with nobody to attack moves one Zone toward the nearest Survivor it can see; if it sees Survivors in different Zones, it moves toward the noisiest group instead (each Survivor counts as Noise); if it sees no one, it moves toward the Zone with the most Noise tokens; if there's no open path, it paths as though every door were open (locked doors still block movement). Zombies always take the shortest route, and split into equal groups (adding Zombies as needed so each new group matches in composition) whenever multiple routes of equal length exist, or multiple target Zones are tied for Noise. The Abomination never splits. A Fatty created by a split does not gain escort Walkers. If there aren't enough Zombies of a type to split evenly, each Zombie of that type gets an extra activation instead.
- **Runners**: Get 2 Actions per Zombies' Phase. After every Zombie (including Runners) resolves its first Action, Runners resolve a second Action — attacking if they now share a Survivor's Zone, otherwise moving.
- **Zombie Spawn**: At the end of the Zombies' Phase, draw one Zombie card per Spawn Zone — always starting from the same Spawn Zone and proceeding clockwise — reading the line matching the highest Danger Level among Survivors still alive. Fatties spawn with 2 Walker escorts (but not when created by a group split). Drawing an Extra Activation card instead gives every Zombie of the indicated type another activation and spawns nothing (no effect at Blue Danger Level). Drawing a Manhole card instead places the indicated Zombies in every Zone with a manhole, but only on tiles where at least one Survivor currently stands. If the Zombie deck runs out, reshuffle all discards into a new deck.
- **Zombie types**: Walker — 1 XP to kill, no special rule. Runner — 1 XP; 2 Actions per activation. Fatty — 1 XP; immune to weapons dealing only 1 Damage, so a 2+ Damage weapon is required; spawns with 2 Walker escorts. Abomination — 5 XP; immune to anything under 3 Damage (a Molotov also kills it); only one Abomination is in play at a time — if a second is drawn, replace it with a Fatty plus 2 Walkers instead.
- **Running out of miniatures**: If a required Zombie type has no miniatures left to place, place as many as are available, then give every Zombie of that type on the board an extra activation.
- **Experience & Danger Levels**: Killing a Zombie grants its killer XP (1 for Walker/Runner/Fatty, 5 for Abomination) and advances that Survivor's Danger Bar. At 7 XP, a Survivor reaches Yellow: they gain a permanent 4th Action plus the fixed Skill shown for Yellow on their Identity Card. At 19 XP, Orange: choose 1 of the 2 Skills shown. At 43 XP, Red: choose 1 of the 3 Skills shown. Skills accumulate across levels, so a Red-level Survivor has 4 Skills total (1 starting + 1 each from Yellow/Orange/Red). A newly gained Skill or Action may be used immediately, the same turn it's earned. The Danger Level used for Zombie Spawning is always that of the most experienced Survivor still alive; if that Survivor is eliminated, it drops to the next-most-experienced Survivor's level.
- **Inventory**: Up to 5 Equipment cards per Survivor, but only 2 may be equipped/"in hand" at once — weapons must be in a hand slot to be used, while non-weapon effect cards (e.g., Goalie Mask, Flashlight, Plenty of Ammo) work even from reserve slots. A Survivor over the 5-card limit must discard down to 5; discarding to make room is always free and can happen any time.
- **Combat resolution**: Roll dice equal to the weapon's Dice value; a matched pair of Dual-symbol weapons fires together (both aimed at the same Zone) for a single Action. Each die meeting or exceeding the weapon's printed Accuracy is a hit, dealing that weapon's Damage to one target; a weapon dealing only 1 Damage cannot harm a Fatty or Abomination at all, no matter how many such hits land. In Melee, the attacking player freely assigns each hit to any target in their Zone. In Ranged Combat, the shooter does not choose targets — hits fill this priority order, one tier fully before the next: (1) Survivors other than the shooter, (2) Walkers, (3) Fatties or Abominations, (4) Runners; Actors in Zones between the shooter and the target are unaffected. A weapon's Range is printed as min-max Zones (0 = Melee only). A weapon that requires reloading (e.g., the Sawed-off) refreshes for free at the End Phase; if fired and then passed to another Survivor without reloading, it must still be reloaded before its new owner can fire it that same turn — one Action reloads both halves of a Dual reload-required pair.
- **Improved weaponry**: Combining component cards from inventory is free and costs no Action, and the resulting weapon takes only one inventory slot; it may be equipped immediately. Sniper Rifle = a Rifle + a Scope (place the Scope under the Rifle card); its wielder may then freely choose Ranged targets instead of following priority order. Molotov = discard a Glass Bottle + a Gasoline card for a Molotov card; using it (a Ranged Combat Action, but with no Dice/Accuracy/Damage roll) kills everything in the target Zone, including Survivors and the Abomination; discard the Molotov after its single use.
- **Car Attack**: Moving a car rolls one die for every miniature in the Zone it leaves, each Zone it passes through, and the Zone it ends in (Survivors riding in that car are exempt). Each die showing 4, 5, or 6 is a hit dealing 1 Damage — never more, so it's impossible to run down a Fatty or Abomination this way. Hits are assigned using the same priority order as Ranged Combat (Survivors except those in the attacking car, then Walkers, then Fatties/Abominations, then Runners); all targeted Survivors must die before hits reach Walkers, and so on. The driver gains all XP from Zombies killed this way. Survivors may still make Melee or Ranged attacks from inside a car normally; a car gives its occupants no protection from Zombie or Survivor attacks.
- **Skills**: Each Survivor has specific Skills (granted at Setup and at each Danger Level) whose rules override the general rules in case of conflict. Representative Skills: Tough (ignore the first Attack from a single Zombie each Zombies' Phase); Ninja (produces no Noise at all, ever, from the Survivor's miniature or equipment use); Medic (once per turn, freely remove a Wounded card from any Survivor — including themself — in the same Zone); Sniper (freely choose all Ranged Combat targets); Lucky / "1 re-roll per turn" (re-roll all dice of one Action; these stack with each other and with reroll Equipment); Born leader (give a free Action to another Survivor, usable only on that Survivor's next turn or it's lost); "Is that all you've got?" (discard one Equipment card to negate one incoming Wounded card); Hoard (carry one extra reserve Equipment card); Ambidextrous/Gunslinger/Swordmaster (treat all Melee and/or Ranged weapons as if Dual).

## Player Reference

### Turn Order (every game turn)
1. **Players' Phase** — First Player activates all their Survivors (any order); play then passes clockwise until every player has activated all Survivors.
2. **Zombies' Phase** — Activation (all Attacks, then all Moves; Runners get a 2nd Action) → Spawn (one Zombie card per Spawn Zone, clockwise, read at the highest Danger Level among living Survivors).
3. **End Phase** — Clear all Noise tokens; pass the First Player token to the left.

### Actions per Survivor
3 Actions at Blue Danger Level → 4 Actions at Yellow, Orange, and Red. Skills can add further free Actions restricted to a specific Action type (e.g., "+1 free Move Action").

### Danger Level / Experience Thresholds
| Danger Level | XP Required | Skill Gained |
|---|---|---|
| Blue | 0 | Starting Skill (from Identity Card) |
| Yellow | 7 | Fixed Skill shown for Yellow, + permanent 4th Action |
| Orange | 19 | Choose 1 of 2 Skills shown |
| Red | 43 | Choose 1 of 3 Skills shown |

### Experience per Kill
| Zombie | XP | Kill Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Walker | 1 | any weapon |
| Runner | 1 | any weapon |
| Fatty | 1 | 2+ Damage weapon |
| Abomination | 5 | 3+ Damage weapon, or a Molotov |

### Ranged Combat / Car Attack Hit-Assignment Priority
1. Survivors (other than the shooter/driver)
2. Walkers
3. Fatties or Abominations
4. Runners

*(Each priority tier is filled completely before hits carry over to the next tier. Melee attacks skip this — the player freely assigns hits instead.)*

### Inventory Limits
5 Equipment cards max per Survivor; only 2 hand slots (weapons must be in-hand to use). Discarding to make room is always free.

### Car Attack Hit Roll
A die result of 4, 5, or 6 is a hit (1 Damage, cannot kill Fatties/Abominations).
