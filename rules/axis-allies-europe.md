## Overview

Axis & Allies: Europe is a World War II strategic wargame for 2 to 4 players, set in the spring of 1941 as Germany prepares to launch Operation Barbarossa against the Soviet Union. Each player controls one (or more) of four world powers — Germany (the Axis), and the Soviet Union, Great Britain, and the United States (the Allies) — fighting over a gameboard depicting Europe, North Africa, the Middle East, and part of North America.

Players win militarily and economically at once: capturing enemy territories increases a power's Industrial Production Certificate (IPC) income, which funds the purchase of more combat units. On a turn, a player works through a seven-part Action Sequence — buying units, moving into combat, resolving battles, repositioning forces, deploying new units, righting submerged/damaged ships, and collecting income — before passing play to the next power.

Victory conditions:
- **Allied powers** (Great Britain, United States, or Soviet Union): occupy Germany and hold it until your next turn, while also controlling your own home country (the Soviet player must specifically retain control of Moscow). The Allies win as a team in this case, not just the player who took Germany. (If an individual "winner" among the Allies is also wanted, it is the Allied country with the greatest increase in IPC income.)
- **Germany**: occupy Great Britain, the United States, or Moscow and hold it until your next turn, while also controlling Germany.

Distinctive mechanics of this version (versus the original Axis & Allies) include destroyer and artillery units, Allied IPCs being vulnerable to German naval raids (the Battle of the Atlantic), a prohibition on moving into or flying over neutral countries, no weapons-development process, no building of new Industrial Complexes, fighter escorts/interceptors for Strategic Bombing Raids, expanded battleship/submarine abilities, direct Allied IPC payments to Germany if it seizes the Middle East, a fixed order of play, a one-time starting cash advance for each side, and special Soviet control over Allied units stationed on Soviet soil.

## Setup

1. **Pick a world power.** Order of play always runs Germany, then Soviet Union, then Great Britain, then United States, and the setup assignment mirrors this:
   - 4-player game: (Germany) / Soviet Union / Great Britain / United States, one power per player.
   - 3-player game: Germany / Soviet Union / Great Britain-and-United-States combined.
   - 2-player game: Germany / Soviet Union-Great Britain-United States combined.
   - If you control more than one world power, keep each power's IPCs and weapon units completely separate.
2. **Distribute National Reference Charts** — one per world power.
3. **Distribute National Control Markers (NCMs)** — punch them from the cardboard sheets; each power has its own.
4. **Set up the National Production Chart** — place one NCM on each power's starting-income space (including convoys); note that the United States and Germany start on the same space. Appoint one player as scorekeeper to move NCMs as territories change hands. (If markers are ever knocked loose, Allied countries recompute by adding up territories and Convoy centers owned — except Middle East countries — while Germany adds up all land territories it controls, plus its Middle East countries tallied separately.)
5. **Prepare plastic chips** — one gray chip = 1 unit, one red chip = 5 units; a stack must always be topped off with the actual unit piece it represents.
6. **Place starting units** on the gameboard per each power's National Reference Chart, using chips to save space where helpful.
7. **Set out the dice** — used for all attack/defense rolls ("firing"), including Strategic Bombing Raids.
8. **Set out the Battle Board** — used to track units and required rolls during combat.
9. **Prepare the money** — the scorekeeper also serves as IPC banker.
10. **Special Cash Advance** — before the game starts (after pieces are on the board), the banker gives Germany 12 IPCs and the Allied players (as a group) 12 IPCs, to spend or save. Uniquely for this one-time disbursement, both sides may buy and place units in any territory or sea zone where they already have at least one unit (normally, units can only be placed where an Industrial Complex is controlled). Germany buys and places first, then the Allies. The Allies may divide their 12 IPCs among themselves however they like. Any IPCs not spent may be kept and added to that power's first-turn starting income.
11. **Starting income** — at the start of the first turn, the banker pays each world power its starting income, based on how many land territories and Convoy centers it controls (place IPCs next to each power's National Reference Chart; the Soviet Union's National Reference Chart lists a starting income of 24 IPCs). Remaining money stays in the bank.

## Turn Structure

**Order of play** is fixed and repeats every round: (1) Germany, (2) Soviet Union, (3) Great Britain, (4) United States.

Each player's turn works through the same seven-part **Action Sequence** (players are not required to complete every part every turn):

1. **Purchase Combat Units** — spend IPCs on new units (not yet placed on the board).
2. **Combat Movement** — move units into enemy-occupied or enemy-owned spaces to create battles, and move units for Amphibious Assaults or Strategic Bombing Raids.
3. **Resolve Combat** — fight out every battle created in step 2, Strategic Bombing Raids first.
4. **Non-Combat Movement** — land returning air units and reposition any remaining units.
5. **Place New Units on Gameboard** — deploy units purchased in step 1.
6. **Submerged submarines resurface; damaged battleships are uprighted.**
7. **Collect Income** — receive IPCs from the bank per the National Production Chart, including any Middle East IPCs owed to Germany.

## Actions

### 1. Purchase Combat Units
Buy units with IPCs on hand; units are not placed on the board until step 5. Prices are listed under COST on the National Reference Charts (for example, a fighter plane costs 12 IPCs). Steps: (1) choose units to buy, (2) pay the bank, (3) place the purchased units in the "Action Sequence 1 Purchases" area at the lower left of the board, where they wait until step 5. If a player runs out of a physical piece, a labeled paper substitute may be used.

### 2. Combat Movement
Move existing units into enemy-occupied and/or enemy-owned land territories and sea zones, creating combat. A player may start as many battles as desired, but each attacking unit may fight in only one battle per turn. Allied powers may never attack each other. Movement allowances are listed on the National Reference Charts.
- Air units flying a Strategic Bombing Raid, and units mounting an Amphibious Assault, move during this sequence.
- A player may move into an empty, adjacent, enemy-controlled territory to take control without a fight.
- Naval units may leave a sea zone containing enemy units (if they started the turn there) even if it prevents combat; land units may not load/unload a transport in a sea zone containing enemy units.
- Armor may "blitz" through an empty, enemy-controlled territory into a further adjacent territory.
- Air units moved this sequence must have a safe place to land; flying over territories with enemy AA guns exposes them to AA fire (once per territory overflown).

### 3. Resolve Combat
All combat movement must be finished first. Combat is resolved by "firing" dice, separately for each battle, in an order the attacker chooses (Strategic Bombing Raids are always resolved first). No reinforcements may be moved in once combat resolution begins.

**Resolving Land Combat:**
1. Both sides place their units on the Battle Board.
2. If the defending territory has an AA gun and the attacker has aircraft, the defender's AA gunner fires first: one die per attacking aircraft; each roll of "1" shoots down a plane immediately (attacking player's choice which), with no chance to fire back. Only one die per aircraft is rolled regardless of how many AA guns are present.
3. The attacker rolls one die per attacking unit; a roll at or below that unit's attack factor scores a hit.
4. The defender chooses casualties for any hits (moved to the Casualties area, but still eligible to fire back this round), then rolls one die per defending unit (including those in the Casualties area, using their normal defense value) to fire back.
5. The attacker removes casualties from the defender's fire (attacker's choice which units); the defender then removes its own Casualties-area units.
6. Repeat steps 3–5 until: (A) the attacker retreats — to an adjacent friendly territory that the owner controlled at the start of the turn and from which the attacking land units came (all units must retreat together to the same territory; aircraft may retreat instead to any friendly territory, island, or carrier within range and controlled since the start of the turn, still subject to AA fire while retreating); (B) the attacker is destroyed; (C) the defender is destroyed (attacker captures the territory only if it has a surviving land unit — surviving aircraft alone do not capture); or (D) both sides are destroyed (defender keeps the territory). Retreating is the attacker's privilege only.

**Resolving Naval Combat:**
1. Both sides place their units on the Battle Board (an attacking transport has no attack value but may still be chosen as a casualty).
2. Attacking submarines may fire a "first-strike" round before anything else: one die per attacking sub, "2" or less hits, and the hit unit is removed immediately with no chance to fire back. An enemy destroyer in the battle cancels this first-strike ability (but not the sub's ability to submerge). Instead of attacking, a sub may submerge — it is turned on its side, may not attack or be attacked again that turn, and stands back up in Action Sequence 6.
3. The attacker rolls one die per remaining attacking unit; a roll at or below the unit's attack factor hits. (A hit transport takes any loaded units down with it — they cannot be chosen as the casualty instead.)
4. The defender chooses casualties, then rolls one die per defending unit (including Casualties-area units) to fire back; defending submarines may submerge instead of firing (only submarines may withdraw from battle this way). Battleships need two hits in the same battle to be destroyed — the first hit turns the ship on its side (it can still fight normally); if it is not hit again before the battle ends, it is "repaired" and stood upright at the end of the turn.
5. Casualties are removed by both sides.
6. Repeat steps 2–5 until: (A) the attacker retreats and/or submerges — to an adjacent friendly sea zone from which the attacking units came (Exception 1: may retreat into a sea zone whose only enemy is a submerged submarine; Exception 2: may NOT retreat from a sea zone containing only submerged submarines — the battle simply ends instead; Exception 3: an attacking sub may submerge in place instead of retreating); (B) the attacker is destroyed/submerged; (C) the defender is destroyed/submerged; (D) both sides are destroyed/submerged; or (E) only submarines and aircraft remain on both sides, which cannot attack each other, ending the battle.

**Combat Results:** In land combat, the successful defender holds the territory (attacker withdraws, attacker destroyed, or both sides destroyed); the attacker captures the territory only if the defender is destroyed and the attacker has a surviving land unit. Naval combat generally diminishes/destroys fleets rather than capturing sea zones, except that Convoy centers can be captured. Air units alone can never capture a territory, and attacking planes may not land in a space where a battle just took place.

**Capturing a Territory:** (1) Remove the defender's control marker and add your own (exception: liberating a former Allied territory returns control to the original owner, not the liberator). (2) Move surviving attacking units into the territory. (3) Immediately adjust the National Production Chart: the attacker's income rises by the captured territory's value and the defender's falls by the same (exception: when liberating, the original ally regains the value unless its capital is still enemy-occupied, in which case the liberator takes the income/Industrial Complex use until the ally's capital is freed, after which the NPC is adjusted again). Any AA gun or Industrial Complex present transfers to the attacker (a captured Industrial Complex cannot be used until the new owner's next turn). No NPC adjustment is needed if the defender holds.

**Capturing an Enemy Capital:** Handled like any other territory capture, except the capturing player also immediately takes all IPCs the losing player has on hand. To win the game this way, the capturing player must still hold both the enemy capital and their own capital at the start of their next turn. A player whose capital is captured stays in the game — they may not purchase units or collect income on their turn, but may still move units and fight; regaining the capital restores income collection that same turn.

**Multi-Player Forces:** Allied units sharing a space may defend together (defenders jointly choose casualties, or the attacker chooses if they can't agree; each defender still rolls its own counterattack separately) but may never attack together — each Ally attacks only with its own units, on its own turn. An Allied transport may carry another Ally's land units, but only via a three-step process: the passing Ally boards on its own turn, the transport's owner moves it (or leaves it in place) on their turn, and the passing Ally unloads on its own next turn — this applies even if the transport never needs to move.

### 4. Non-Combat Movement
- Air units still aloft from Combat Movement must land in a friendly territory within their remaining range.
- Any/all/none of the units not yet moved this turn may reposition. Land units may move into any friendly territory (occupied or not) within range, never into enemy or neutral territory; AA guns may likewise reposition but not on the same turn their territory was captured. Naval units may move into any friendly sea zone within range (never enemy-occupied sea zones or enemy-controlled Convoy centers); transports in or moving to a sea zone next to a friendly coastal territory may load/unload there. Air units may move into friendly territories within range (fighters, but not bombers, may also land on a friendly carrier); they may not land in enemy/neutral/just-captured territory, may not fly over neutral territory, and are not shot at by AA fire during this sequence.

### 5. Place New Units
Land and air units bought this turn are placed in any territory with an Industrial Complex that the player has controlled since the start of the turn (newly bought fighters cannot be placed directly onto a carrier). Naval units are placed in sea zones adjacent to such a territory (if enemy ships already occupy that sea zone, combat occurs on the enemy's next Resolve Combat sequence unless they move away first). Any number of units may be placed in a player's home territory; a captured territory with an Industrial Complex may only receive as many new units as its IPC income value. Great Britain may place some or all of its purchased air/land units in Canada, and naval units in the sea zone next to Canada.

### 6. Submerged Submarines Resurface; Damaged Battleships Are Uprighted
All submarines and battleships that were tipped on their sides during the turn are turned upright again.

### 7. Collect Income
Collect IPCs from the bank per the current level shown on the National Production Chart, including any Middle East payments owed to Germany (see Special Rules below).

## Scoring / Victory Conditions

There is no points-based scoring — the game is won outright by capturing and holding an enemy capital:

- **Allied win:** any Allied player occupies Germany and still holds it at the start of their next turn, while their own home country remains under their control (the Soviet player specifically must retain Moscow). This is a team win for all Allied powers, not an individual one. If players also want to name an individual "winner" among the Allies, it is whichever Allied country achieved the greatest increase in IPC income.
- **Axis win:** Germany occupies Great Britain, the United States, or Moscow and still holds it at the start of Germany's next turn, while Germany itself remains under German control.
- Capturing an enemy capital immediately transfers all of the losing player's on-hand IPCs to the capturing player, in addition to the normal National Production Chart adjustment for the captured territory.
- A player whose own capital falls is not eliminated: on their turn they may still move units and fight (just not purchase units or collect income) and can retake their capital to restore full play, including IPC collection that same turn.
- Liberating a formerly Allied capital or territory returns it to its original owner (the liberator does not keep it), unless that owner's own capital is still in enemy hands, in which case the liberator temporarily keeps the income/Industrial Complex use until the original owner's capital is freed.

## Special Rules & Edge Cases

**Neutral Territories:** Seven white territories on the board are neutral; units may not move into or fly over them (naval units also may not pass through the neutral Turkish sea zone, though air units may fly between the Black Sea and Aegean Sea).

**The Middle East:** The yellow Middle East countries are independent states occupied by the Allies, who enjoy their oil resources; these are captured/liberated like any other land territory, but their IPCs are tracked on a separate Middle East column (to the left of the National Production Chart) with eight positions. Whenever Germany captures a Middle East country, that column is adjusted (the rest of the chart is unaffected), and during Germany's Collect Income step the Allies — not the bank — must pay Germany directly for the accumulated Middle East value (the Allies decide among themselves how to split the cost; if they can't agree, Germany chooses who pays). Losing Middle Eastern countries does not lower the Allied powers' regular income.

**The Soviet Patriotic War:** During his Non-Combat Movement, the Soviet player may inspect any U.S. or British units currently sitting in Soviet territories (red or light-purple) and convert any of them into Soviet-equivalent units at will (no obligation to discuss this with his Allies first, though it's suggested). Converted units may not move until the Soviet player's next turn, and remain Soviet permanently — even if later moved into Allied territory.

**Amphibious Assaults:** An attack on an enemy coastal territory or island by land units carried there by transport during Combat Movement. The attacker must declare the target territory during Combat Movement and cannot redirect it later. Each transport may only unload into one territory (though two different transports in the same sea zone may each choose a different target). Land units from adjacent territories, air units, and any battleships/destroyers in the same sea zone as the unloading transport may also join in. Unlike a normal land battle, all participating land units lose the option to retreat (air units may still retreat normally). Moving into or out of a transport uses a unit's entire move for the turn. A transport that fought this turn may load or unload (not both); one that retreated from a naval battle during the assault may not unload that turn.
  - *Resolution order:* (1) attacking battleships (needing a roll of "4" or less) and destroyers (needing "3" or less) each fire one "one-shot" support round, with defender casualties moved to the Casualties area (still eligible to counter-fire normally); (2) those ships return to the sea zone on the board; (3) defending AA guns fire at any attacking air units as usual; (4) attacking land/air units fire, the defender counterattacks, and this repeats until one or both sides are destroyed.
  - *Naval battle precedes the assault* if enemy naval units share the sea zone: air units assigned to the amphibious assault must fly to the target territory before this naval battle (and cannot also fight in it); all attacking naval units, including transports, must clear the sea zone of enemy naval units before transports may unload (a destroyed transport's cargo is lost too); the attacker cannot hold ships back from the naval battle to save them for the assault. After a naval battle, the attacker may call off the assault entirely (but not redirect it), and battleships/destroyers that fought in the naval battle lose their one-shot support-fire option in the follow-up land battle.

**Strategic Bombing Raids (SBR):** An economic attack against an enemy Industrial Complex, conducted only by bombers (fighters may escort). Sequence: (1) during Combat Movement, move a bomber into a territory with an Industrial Complex and announce the raid (no Battle Board is used); (2) optionally send escort fighters along (aircraft used in an SBR cannot also join a regular land attack on that same territory that turn, and any defending fighters used against the SBR likewise cannot also defend a regular land attack there that turn); (3)–(4) the defender's AA gun fires first (one die per attacking aircraft, "1" hits), then the defender's fighters fire (one die each, "2" or less hits — note fighters defend at "2," not their normal "4," in an SBR), then surviving escort fighters return fire at defending fighters (one die each, "1" hits and removes a defending fighter — escorts attack at "1," not their normal "3," in an SBR); the attacker removes any resulting casualties; this SBR combat lasts only one round; (5) each surviving bomber then rolls one die, and the total rolled is the number of IPCs the defender must immediately surrender to the bank (or as many as they have, if fewer). Surviving bombers and fighters return to a friendly base within range during Non-Combat Movement; a bomber cannot conduct an SBR and a normal attack in the same turn.

**The Battle of the Atlantic (Convoy Centers):** Seven sea zones are Convoy centers (four British, two U.S., one Soviet) where German naval units may raid the Allies' supply lines. They are captured/recaptured like land territory: a ship with an attack value may enter or pass through an unoccupied center to take control (a German marker is placed, or removed if the Allies retake it); an occupied center requires combat, and the attacker takes control only if all defenders are sunk/submerged and at least one attacking warship remains (a submerged sub counts as "gone" for control purposes). Transports cannot claim a Convoy center by themselves (though they may accompany an attacking force that does). When Germany takes a center, the former owner loses its IPC income there and the National Production Chart is adjusted — but Germany does not gain that income itself, since the convoy is considered sunk. The original owner regains the income only once they (or another Ally) retake the center.

**Combat Units — key special rules:**
- *Antiaircraft (AA) Guns:* neutral-colored; taken over automatically if their territory is captured; fire once per enemy aircraft flying over during Combat Movement (only one die per plane no matter how many AA guns are present); cannot attack like other land units and cannot move into enemy-occupied territory; may be transported to friendly coastal territories/islands; cannot move in a territory captured that same turn; an AA gun in Allied-held Middle East territory may be moved by any Ally, but only once per round.
- *Industrial Complexes:* neutral-colored; cannot attack, defend, or move, and cannot be built or destroyed during the game; serve only as the gateway for placing newly purchased units (subject to the IPC-value placement cap described in Actions, step 5).
- *Armor "Blitzing":* a tank may move through an empty, enemy-controlled (but not enemy-occupied) territory into a further adjacent territory: (1) move into the unoccupied enemy territory, (2) place a control marker there, (3) adjust the National Production Chart, (4) continue into the second territory (which may be occupied, controlled, or friendly). A blitzing tank may also move out and back to its starting territory in the same turn. A territory held only by AA guns still counts as occupied and cannot be blitzed through, though a tank moving into it takes control automatically without rolling dice.
- *Fighters and Aircraft Carriers:* a carrier holds up to two fighters; on the carrier owner's own turn, only fighters they own may take off (and must do so before the carrier itself moves, or they're stuck as cargo for the rest of the turn); an Allied fighter aboard cannot take off on a turn that isn't its own. If a carrier is destroyed while defending, any fighters aboard may fly up to one space to a safe landing site they've controlled since the start of the turn, or are lost.
- *Submarines:* attacking subs get a "first-strike" round (unless an enemy destroyer is present) that removes hits immediately with no return fire; only submarines may submerge to withdraw from a battle entirely (turned on their side, cannot act again that turn, stand up in Action Sequence 6); air units cannot attack a submarine unless a friendly destroyer is also in the battle, and submarines can never attack or defend against air units.
- *Transports:* carry either one armor unit alone, or two of any other land unit (including AA guns) in combination; a transport with enemy units in its sea zone at the start of the turn cannot load or unload there until it moves to a friendly or empty zone; a transport must unload all its cargo into a single territory; units aboard a sunk transport are lost with it and cannot be chosen separately as the casualty.

## Player Reference

**Turn checklist (repeat for each of the 7 Action Sequence steps):**
1. Purchase Combat Units
2. Combat Movement
3. Resolve Combat
4. Non-Combat Movement
5. Place New Units on Gameboard
6. Right submerged subs / damaged battleships
7. Collect Income

**Order of play:** Germany → Soviet Union → Great Britain → United States (repeats every round).

**Combat Units Reference:**

**Key numbers:**
- Special one-time cash advance before turn 1: 12 IPCs to Germany, 12 IPCs to the Allies (as a group).
- Soviet Union's National Reference Chart lists a starting income of 24 IPCs.
- Convoy centers: 7 total (4 British, 2 U.S., 1 Soviet).
- Middle East IPC track: 8 positions, paid by the Allies directly to Germany, never from the bank.
- Strategic Bombing Raid combat lasts exactly one round; in an SBR, defending fighters hit on "2" or less (not their normal 4) and escorting fighters hit on "1" (not their normal 3).
- A captured territory with an Industrial Complex may only receive as many newly placed units as its IPC value.
