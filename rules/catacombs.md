## Overview

Catacombs is a cooperative dexterity game: up to four players take control of four heroes (wooden discs) who must fight their way through a series of rooms filled with monsters controlled by one player, the Overseer, in order to reach and defeat a Catacomb Lord in its lair beneath the town of Stormtryne. The Heroic Forces (heroes, allies, and familiars) work as a team; the Catacomb Lord's Forces (the Catacomb Lord and its monsters) are all controlled by the Overseer, who tries to win while keeping the game fun for everyone else. Battles are fought by skillfully flicking (shooting) different types of wooden pieces across game boards that represent rooms of the catacomb. The heroes win by eliminating all of the Catacomb Lord's health while at least one hero survives; the Overseer wins if, at any point, all four heroes are killed and/or unable to act.

**Important terms:**
- **Character**: heroes, allies, familiars, monsters, Catacomb Lords, and Antients.
- **Teammate**: a character fighting on the same side. Heroes, allies, and familiars are teammates; Catacomb Lords and monsters are teammates. Antients have no teammates.
- **Turn**: when a character takes an action.
- **Round**: when all characters on the board have taken a turn. A round starts with all the Heroic Forces taking a turn, then all of the Catacomb Lord's Forces (controlled by the Overseer) take a turn.
- **Phase**: there are four phases for each room — Exploration, Setup, Battle, and Resolution.

Monster pieces are wooden discs across five families, distinguished by color (beige, blue, gray, green, orange), matched to the game's Monster cards. There are six heroes to choose from (Oleira the Elf, Xoric the Barbarian, Elani the Thief, Varesh the Wizard, Marorg the Skeleton Explorer, and Roosan the Chicken Champion), though only four are used in a given game — new players are advised to start with the Elf, Barbarian, Thief, and Wizard. There are four Catacomb Lords, from weakest to strongest: Vasesak the Sorcerer (defends with a magic shield), Shargila the Gorgon (can petrify enemies), Hmotha the Lich Lord (can summon hordes of undead), and Phoshar the Dragon (shoots deadly fireballs) — fighting the Sorcerer is recommended for new players.

## Setup

**Choosing players' roles:** one player must be the Overseer, controlling the Catacomb Lord and its monsters. The remaining players split control of the four heroes as follows:

| Total players | Breakdown |
|---|---|
| 5 | Overseer + 4 players controlling 1 hero each |
| 4 | Overseer + 2 players controlling 1 hero each + 1 player controlling 2 heroes |
| 3 | Overseer + 2 players controlling 2 heroes each |
| 2 | Overseer + 1 player controlling 4 heroes |

**Preparing for a first game:**
1. As a group, choose which Catacomb Lord to face (recommended: Vasesak the Sorcerer for a first game) and which four heroes to use (recommended: Barbarian, Elf, Thief, and Wizard).
2. The Overseer takes the Catacomb Lord player mat, the chosen Catacomb Lord's piece, and its three cards: Portrait, Setup, and Rules.
3. The Overseer shuffles the deck of Item cards and sets it aside.
4. Set the Poison card deck aside — it's recommended not to use Poison while learning the game.
5. Place the Devoured! and Teleported! cards face-up off to one side; they are not used during a first game.
6. Place the monster pieces near the Overseer to form the Monster Pool.
7. Place the gold coins beside the Monster Pool to form the Treasury; it's recommended that inexperienced players start with five gold coins per hero character.
8. Each player sets up a hero player mat for each hero they control. At minimum, every hero has a Portrait card and a Rules card; the Rules card shows starting health, base shot sequence, any starting Ability/Spell/Item cards, and whether the hero can use weapons, magic items, or both. Place the health tracker on the blood drop matching the hero's starting health (health can never exceed this maximum). Heroes must start with all listed Ability and Spell cards; if there aren't enough Spell cards to go around (with expansions), players may distribute them among spell-capable heroes as they see fit. Players may choose whether to start with any listed Item cards.
9. Missile, shield, fireball, and ice shot pieces are shared by all players.
10. Arrange the Room cards in a vertical column. For a first game, use this order: Bholoth Cemetery (difficulty 0), The Outer Bailey (difficulty 0), Merchant, Stormtryne Hall (difficulty 1), Moghath (difficulty 1), Healer, Catacomb Lord's Lair.
11. If the Sorcerer is the chosen Catacomb Lord, his hired mercenaries are Skeleton Warriors: substitute a Skeleton Warrior wherever a "question mark" (Mercenary Monster) icon appears on a Room card during setup.

**Preparing the play area:** the Overseer chooses the first game board and places it in the center of the table, then inserts the dark brown extra-large obstacle pieces into the board's holes. Each board has a Hero Start Zone (in front of the players controlling heroes) and a larger Monster Start Zone (in front of the Overseer). To assemble the barrier walls, unfold all four wall pieces and interconnect the notches at each end to form a continuous barrier around the board; player mats and all other components stay outside the walls. The game board must be centered within the barrier walls — the space between the board and the walls is the Shadow Realm.

## Turn Structure

Each room the heroes encounter proceeds through four Game Phases, always in this order:

1. **Exploration Phase**: the Overseer flips the next unrevealed Room card, starting from the one closest to the heroes. If the heroes have reached the Catacomb Lord's Lair Special Room card, the final battle begins instead of a normal room.
2. **Setup Phase**: the Overseer chooses a game board (the same board side cannot be used for two consecutive rooms, except the Catacomb Lord's board specified on its own Setup card) and centers it within the barrier walls; fills the board's circular holes with the extra-large obstacle pieces; finds the Monster cards for each monster shown on the Room card; gathers the appropriate monster pieces from the Monster Pool; and places them anywhere legal within the Monster Start Zone. The players then place their hero, ally, or familiar pieces anywhere behind the Hero Start Zone line.
3. **Battle Phase**: consists of at least one round. In each round, every hero takes a turn (in any order the players agree on among themselves), then the Overseer takes a turn for every monster in the room. Once all monsters have acted, the round ends; players resolve any end-of-round effects (such as recovering from being stunned, or losing health to a bite shot), and a new round begins. Rounds continue until all the monsters in the room have been destroyed or frozen (or a combination of the two) or all the heroes are dead.
4. **Resolution Phase** (only reached if at least one hero survived): each surviving hero receives gold equal to the total value (printed on the Monster cards) of every monster they personally destroyed that Battle Phase; the monster pieces return to the Monster Pool. Play then returns to the Exploration Phase for the next Room card.

## Actions

### A Hero's Turn

On their turn during the Battle Phase, a hero performs exactly one of the following (not both, unless a card explicitly overrides this or the hero is using a Combo item):
1. Perform the basic shot sequence printed on their Hero Rules card (in most cases, one melee shot).
2. Play a card — typically an Ability, Item, or Spell card.

If a shot sequence calls for a "Then" step the character cannot perform, that part is skipped and the rest of the sequence continues; a player must complete every part of a sequence they are able to perform. An "Or" icon between shots means the player must declare which option they're taking before shooting.

### Basic and Ranged Shot Types

| Shot | Damage | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Melee | 1 | Shoot the character's own piece with a finger; the most basic attack. |
| Rush | 0 | Performed like a melee shot but inflicts no damage; used to reposition quickly. |
| Missile | 1 | A separate missile piece is placed within 2.5 cm (1") of the character and shot; removed from the board after use. |
| Fireball | 1 | Functions like a missile shot but uses the fireball piece instead. |
| Roll | 2 | The character's own piece is laid on its side and rolled; it must make contact with an enemy to deal damage. If it hits two characters, only one (shooter's choice) is affected. |
| Target | 1 | Like a missile shot, but if the first shot misses, a second shot may be taken from where the first one ended (the target must be declared before the first shot). Damage applies only to the declared target. |
| Giant Fireball | 2 | Uses the large orange piece; functions like a missile shot. |
| Boulder | 3 | The reverse side of the Giant Fireball piece; functions like a missile shot. |
| Bite (Overseer only) | 1 | See Special Rules & Edge Cases. |
| Devour (Overseer only) | 0 (initial hit) | See Special Rules & Edge Cases. |
| Ice (ranged) | 0 | Freezes rather than damages — see below. |
| Summon | 1 (to the summoned character) | Brings a new character into play — see below. |

If any shot piece leaves the board, it is placed back on the board edge at the point where it left. No shot may end with a piece on top of another piece (including obstacles); the shooting player must move it to the nearest legal spot where it lies flat.

A single shot can damage multiple targets if it hits them directly (e.g., a melee shot ricocheting from one monster into another, or hitting several adjacent characters at once); however, a shot that pushes one character into another does not damage the second character, since it wasn't hit directly.

**Shield shots**: function like a missile shot (no damage) using a shield piece; after the shooter's turn is complete, the shield piece is repositioned to any legal spot within 2.5 cm (1") of its owner and stays under the owner's control only if they perform another shield shot. Shield pieces are removed when their owner dies or the Battle Phase ends. Only certain characters (in the base game: the Wizard hero, the Swordmaster Ghoul monster, and the Sorcerer Catacomb Lord) can perform shield shots.

**Ice shot**: when a character is struck, it takes no damage but becomes frozen — the ice piece is placed on top of it. A frozen character cannot act, takes no damage, and has its defensive properties suspended; it stays in place until the ice piece is knocked off (directly or indirectly) by another shot. If multiple targets are hit, only one (shooter's choice) is frozen. Catacomb Lords and Antients are unaffected by ice shots. If all other monsters in a room are dead and one remains frozen, the room is still considered complete; freezing a monster earns gold the same as destroying it.

**Bite shot (Overseer only)**: inflicts 1 damage immediately to all hit targets; for surviving targets, the Overseer chooses one to place the bite piece on. A bitten character cannot act and is freed only when the bite piece is knocked off (directly or indirectly). If the piece is still in place at the end of the next round, the target loses another point of health, and again at the end of every further round it remains. Stunned heroes cannot be bitten.

**Devour shot (Overseer only)**: the hit hero, ally, or familiar takes 1 damage immediately; if it survives, it is removed from the board and placed on the Devoured! card, unable to act and treated as removed from the game. If multiple characters are hit, all take damage but only one (Overseer's choice) is devoured. Devoured characters are released once the room is cleared or the devouring monster is destroyed — in the latter case, returning to the spot where the monster died, ready to act normally. Being devoured cancels an existing stun or bite effect. Devoured characters cannot be teleported off the Devoured! card.

**Summon shot**: performed like any ranged shot, but using the piece of the monster or Antient specified in the summoner's shot sequence. It always inflicts 1 damage as a standard ranged shot (regardless of the summoned character's own shot sequence); the summoned character can be controlled immediately afterward. A summoned monster's piece must be available in the Monster Pool.

### Shot Modifiers

A modified shot changes the icon color to show its modifier; modifiers can be applied even to rush shots (which still inflict no damage, but the modifier's effect still applies).

| Modifier | Effect |
|---|---|
| Chain (yellow) | The same target cannot be damaged twice consecutively in a single shot sequence; a second consecutive hit on it is ignored. Ranged shots with Chain always originate from the shooting character's location. Never combined with another modifier. |
| Corrosion (brown) | The hit hero shuffles their Item cards and randomly discards one from the game (with any associated board pieces removed). No effect if the hero has no items. |
| Critical (red) | The base shot's damage is increased by 1. |
| Fear (white) | The attacker may immediately perform a single, unmodifiable rush shot with the hit character (even if stunned). |
| Petrify (gray) | The hit character is turned to stone and dies immediately, regardless of remaining health. If multiple characters are hit, only one (shooter's choice) is petrified, though all still take damage. |
| Poison (green) | The hit hero first loses 1 health (if the shot deals damage), then draws the indicated number of Poison cards. See Special Rules & Edge Cases. |
| Regeneration (pink) | The attacker restores 1 health after damaging an enemy (only 1 point even if multiple targets are hit; cannot exceed maximum health). Has no effect against Antients or shadow monsters. |
| Stun (blue) | The hit hero's piece flips to its stunned side and cannot act for a full round, returning to normal at the end of the next round. A stunned hero can still be damaged but not stunned again. Only heroes can be stunned. |
| Transform (purple) | In the base game, only the Fire Spirit has this. After it damages a target, its piece flips to become a Fire Wall — treated as an obstacle for the rest of the room, no longer a monster — which deals 1 damage to any Heroic Forces piece that contacts it, directly or indirectly. |

Critical, corrosion, fear, poison, regeneration, and stun affect every target a modified shot hits; chain and petrify affect only one target even on a multi-hit shot.

### Defensive Properties

Some characters can nullify or reflect specific shots and/or modifiers, listed in a Nullifies or Reflects box on their card.
- **Nullifying**: the listed basic shot (and all its modified versions) inflicts no damage and its modifier has no effect.
- **Reflecting**: works like nullifying, but 1 point of damage is reflected back at the attacking character.

If a Nullifies/Reflects box is on a permanent-frequency card (an Antient, Catacomb Lord, Hero, Monster, or "permanent" Item card), it is automatic. If it's on a once-per-room Ability or Item card, the affected player chooses when to use it (refreshing during the next room's Setup Phase). If it's on a once-per-game Spell or Item card, the affected player chooses when to use it, and the card is then discarded.

### Playing Ability, Item, and Spell Cards

- **Spell cards**: can only be used by spell casters (in the base game, only the Wizard starts with Spell cards). A Spell card is played once per game, then discarded permanently.
- **Ability cards**: specific to a hero, used once per room during the Battle Phase, then flipped face down; they refresh (flip face up) during the Setup Phase of the next room.
- **Item cards**: purchased from Izchak the Merchant or won at the Alewife Tavern. Each is marked once per game, once per room, or permanent, using the same rules as Spell and Ability cards above.

When played, a card's effect applies only to its owner unless the card shows a hero icon, in which case the controlling player may apply it to the owner or to another hero or ally instead.

**Healing**: each heal icon on a played card lets the owner restore 1 health to themselves, or (with a hero icon) to any chosen living hero or ally. A Heal All icon restores 1 health to every living hero and ally, including the card's owner. Health can never exceed a character's starting maximum.

**Combo items** (advanced): a Combo item is an Item card that can be played immediately before (pre-Combo) or immediately after (post-Combo) a hero's normal turn — shown by a "..." icon before or after the "Then" icon in its shot sequence. Only one Combo item may be played per turn, either before or after the hero's action.

### Familiars

Familiars are magic items any spell-capable hero may purchase and use (maximum one per hero). During the Setup Phase, the familiar is placed within 2.5 cm (1") of its controlling hero; the player chooses whether the hero or familiar acts first each round. The familiar performs its own shot sequence every round until it dies or the room clears, then returns to its owner's side at the start of the next room's Setup Phase. Familiars cannot earn gold for destroying monsters (their owner doesn't either), and cannot be poisoned or stunned, though they can be devoured or teleported. If the controlling hero is stunned, bitten, or devoured, the familiar can still act on its own. If the hero dies, the familiar is removed from the board and out of the game along with the hero's other items; if the familiar itself dies during a Battle Phase, it is removed from the board but rejoins its owner at the next room's Setup Phase.

### Teleportation

A hero (or other character) targeted by a Teleport effect is removed from the board and placed on the Teleported! card, adjacent to the outside of the barrier walls. On the character's next turn (the following round), it returns by being placed anywhere around the board's edge and performing a single melee shot, which ends that turn; a post-Combo item may follow this melee shot. While on the Teleported! card, a character cannot take damage or die, and existing stun, bite, and ice effects are immediately removed. A character must return to play on its very next turn — it cannot remain on the card for multiple turns. If all other heroes are dead and one hero remains on the Teleported! card, it may teleport back and keep fighting; the same applies to monsters and Catacomb Lords. Stunned, bitten, or frozen characters cannot teleport themselves but can be teleported by another character. No combat occurs between any characters that are on the Teleported! card at the same time.

### Magic Portals

During the Battle Phase, a hero or Catacomb Lord with the ability can open a magic portal to switch the board positions of any two characters (including itself), which may include allies and familiars. No pieces leave the board — only their positions (and any conditions on them) are swapped. A player may only switch two of their own teammates: a hero cannot swap with a monster, and a Catacomb Lord cannot swap with a hero.

### Special Room Actions

When the Exploration Phase reveals a Special Room card, the heroes may spend gold there instead of fighting:

**Althea the Healer**: cures one Poison card for free (the group decides which hero). Additional services, purchasable by pooling gold:

| Service | Cost | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Heal Hero or Ally | 3 gold | Restores 1 health point. |
| Cure | 2 gold | Cures one Poison card. |
| Resurrect Hero | 10 gold | A dead hero returns to life with 2 health, cured of all poison, keeping all gold and unused Item/Spell cards; may then pay for further healing; starts playing in the next room. |

Multiple services may be purchased in one visit. Allies can be healed by the Healer but not resurrected.

**Izchak the Merchant**: the Overseer shuffles the Item deck and flips 6 cards for the group to examine. Any hero may purchase any number of revealed items they qualify for (players may pool gold); paying 2 gold reveals the next card from the top of the deck instead, with no limit on repeats. Purchased gold returns to the Treasury, and the Item card goes to the buying hero's mat. Any hero may also pay 2 gold for a map, which flips over every Room card in the catacomb, revealing the path to the Catacomb Lord. Items are restricted by symbol: available to any hero, to a specific hero, to heroes meeting a specific condition, to heroes who can wield weapons, or (for magic items) to heroes who can cast spells; a hero's Rules card shows which categories they qualify for. There is no limit on how many items a hero can carry; items cannot be used while stunned (except familiars); only the owning hero may use their own items, and items cannot be given away or traded. Allies cannot buy or use items.

**Amaranth Inn**: any hero not currently in the game may be recruited as an ally for 10 gold coins (pooled if needed). Shuffle the unused Hero Portrait cards face down and draw one at random — that hero becomes available as an ally. One player controls the ally and assigns it to help one of their own heroes; the ally uses its own printed shot sequence and (unless stated otherwise) has a maximum health of 4, tracked on the assisting hero's mat. An ally cannot start with, buy, or use Item or Spell cards, and starts with one fewer Ability card than normal (minimum one), chosen by the recruiting player. During Setup, the ally is placed in the Hero Start Zone with the other heroes; its controller takes a turn for both the hero and the ally each round, in either order. Allies can be teleported and devoured, and can damage or destroy monsters and Catacomb Lords, but cannot buy or use items, use spells, be stunned, be poisoned, or be resurrected, and earn no gold themselves (all gold from its kills goes to the hero it assists). If that hero dies while the ally survives, control passes to the hero with the lowest current health (ties broken first by least gold, then by fewest items). If all heroes are dead, the game ends in an Overseer win even if an ally remains on the board.

**Alewife Tavern**: paying 2 gold coins lets a player place a bet. The Overseer puts all the Orcs, Fire Spirits, Centaurs, and the four hero pieces into an opaque bag; the player draws three pieces and checks the reward table from top to bottom for the best match:

| Pieces drawn | Reward |
|---|---|
| 3 Heroes | Gain full health for the chosen hero and their ally (if any), OR cure all Poison cards from one hero. |
| 3 of a kind | Take any Item card of your choice from the Item deck. |
| 2 Hero pieces | Blindly draw 1 Item card (must be the first one you can use). |
| 3 different colored pieces | Gain 1 health point OR cure 1 Poison card. |
| 1 Hero piece | Gain 1 gold coin from the Treasury. |

Pieces are returned to the bag before another wager. A player may bet repeatedly if they have the gold (pooling allowed); only the hero named as gambling for a given wager receives its reward, and a winner may choose a lower reward than the one they qualified for. All wagered gold is returned to the Treasury regardless of outcome.

## Scoring / Victory Conditions

- **Heroes win** if they eliminate all of the Catacomb Lord's health while at least one hero in the party remains alive. Unlike normal battle rooms, it is not necessary to destroy all of the Catacomb Lord's minions — only the Catacomb Lord itself must be destroyed.
- **The Overseer wins** if, at any time during any Battle Phase, all four heroes are killed and/or unable to take an action (for example, affected by a bite shot or trapped on the Devoured! card) — even if an ally is still active on the board.
- A hero is dead once their health track reaches the skull icon ("0"): their piece, any shield pieces, and any familiar pieces are removed from the board, their Poison cards shuffle back into the Poison deck, and any monsters they've killed return to the Monster Pool. A dead hero stays out of the game — retaining their collected spells, gold, and items — unless the party reaches Althea the Healer and pays to resurrect them; a dead hero's gold cannot be used or offered in a special room until they are resurrected.

## Special Rules & Edge Cases

- **No friendly fire**: heroes, allies, and familiars can never damage each other; likewise, monsters and Catacomb Lords can never damage each other.
- **Piece placement**: a shot may never end with a piece on top of another piece, including obstacles; the shooter repositions it to the nearest legal flat spot. If an obstacle is displaced during play, it is immediately put back in place.
- **Hard component limits**: the supply of pieces and cards is a hard limit. If the Treasury runs out of coins, heroes collect no gold in the Resolution Phase; if the Monster Pool runs out of a monster type, the Overseer simply cannot use that monster.
- **Suppression**: a Battle Room card's Suppresses box lists shot types and/or modifiers that neither side may use in that room. If an unmodified shot is listed, that shot and all of its modified versions are suppressed. If a specific modified shot is listed, only that modifier is suppressed — the base shot can still be used unmodified.
- **Mercenary Monsters**: each Catacomb Lord's Setup card specifies a level 1 "Mercenary Monster" (e.g., Skeleton Warrior for the Sorcerer, Zombie for the Lich Lord) that is substituted for any Mercenary Monster icon appearing on a Room card during Setup.
- **Monster Metre icons**: give the Overseer freedom to choose which monster (of the specified level, any family) populates that quadrant, and how many of its available pieces to deploy — usually all of them, though holding some back in the Monster Pool keeps them available for later summon shots. As an alternative, the monster for each icon can be determined randomly by shuffling all Monster cards with a matching Monster Metre level printed on the back and revealing the top card.
- **Shadow monsters** (such as the Phantom) originate from the Shadow Realm and have a dotted (rather than solid) health icon on their Monster card. They cannot be killed by normal damage — they must be pushed by a direct shot completely off the board, returning them to the Shadow Realm.
- **Antients**: powerful, immortal beings (in the base game, Urthoth the Liquid Antient, summoned via the "Vaiel's Urn" Item card) that enter play via a summon shot and remain for the rest of the room. They take no damage and cannot be destroyed, and are immune to all shots and shot modifiers — they cannot be feared, bitten, devoured, frozen, petrified, poisoned, teleported, or suppressed, and have no health for regeneration to restore. Control of an Antient starts with the hero who summoned it and then passes to the Overseer once that hero's turn ends; it passes back and forth between the Heroic Forces and the Overseer (each side using it on their own turns) until the room is cleared or all heroes are dead, at which point it leaves the game during the Resolution Phase. More than one Antient can be in play at once.
- **Variant monsters**: alternate forms of a monster, marked with a "V" icon on their Monster card and sharing the same wooden piece as the original. The Overseer decides whether to use the variant instead of the original, and that choice applies for the entire game.
- **The Gelatinous Cube** (a level 4 Dungeon-family monster) performs a different shot sequence each round of the Battle Phase, cycling through a fixed sequence before repeating: Round 1 is a melee shot followed by a melee shot with the corrosion modifier; Round 2 is a melee shot followed by a melee shot with the fear modifier (revealed by rotating the Cube piece); the cycle continues through further rounds — including a shot modified with critical — before restarting at Round 5.
- **Monster family vulnerabilities** (optional, for new players): Dungeon family (green) monsters treat any unmodified missile shot against them as having the critical modifier. Infernal family (orange) monsters take one extra point of damage from an ice shot, in addition to being frozen. Mythological family (blue) monsters treat any unmodified melee shot against them as having the regeneration modifier. Undead family (gray) monsters treat any unmodified fireball shot against them as having the critical modifier, and take 1 damage from contact with fire walls or fire obstacles. Extra-small Vermin family (beige) monsters — Sewer Rats, Armoured Beetles, Pit Vipers, Vampire Bats — treat any unmodified melee or missile shot against them as having the fear modifier, unless that monster already has a fear-modified shot in its own sequence (such as the Blood Skull).
- **Poison** (advanced, Overseer-only): recommended only for experienced players. When a hero is hit by a poison-modified shot, they lose 1 health first if the shot deals damage, then draw the indicated number of Poison cards (each showing a numeric dose) onto their player mat. If the total value of all of a hero's accumulated Poison cards ever equals or exceeds that hero's starting health, they are overwhelmed and die — their Poison cards are removed and shuffled back into the deck. If no Poison cards remain to draw, the affected hero is treated as hit with a lethal dose and dies immediately. Stunned heroes can still be poisoned. Poison can be cured by playing a Cure Poison Spell card (Wizard only, in the base game), by visiting Althea the Healer, or by testing luck at the Alewife Tavern; curing lets the hero discard one Poison card of their choice back into the deck.

### Game Phase Checklist (per room)

1. Exploration — reveal the next Room card (or trigger the final battle at the Lord's Lair).
2. Setup — place board, obstacles, and monsters; heroes/allies/familiars enter the Hero Start Zone.
3. Battle — heroes act, then the Overseer's monsters act, each round, until the room clears or the party is wiped out.
4. Resolution — surviving heroes collect gold for kills; monster pieces return to the Monster Pool.

### Card Frequency Types

| Frequency | Refreshes |
|---|---|
| Once per game | Never — discarded after use |
| Once per room | Setup Phase of the next room |
| Permanent | Always active, no action required |
