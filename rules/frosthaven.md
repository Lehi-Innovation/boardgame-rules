## Overview

Frosthaven is a cooperative, campaign-driven tactical game and the follow-up to Gloomhaven. Your goal is to keep the outpost of Frosthaven safe from all threats: a party of mercenaries ventures into the wilderness in self-contained scenarios (hex-map, card-driven combat), and completing scenarios unlocks new ones along a branching narrative (the scenario book contains 138 scenarios; you will not play them all, and not in order). Between scenarios the party returns to Frosthaven to resolve events, acquire new items and abilities, and interact with buildings.

The rulebook is written for two to four players, each playing a separate character; solo mode lets a single player control two or more characters at increased difficulty. There are six starting character classes, and 11 more classes unlock during the campaign. The campaign alternates **Scenario Phases** (three steps: Beginning a Scenario, Playing a Scenario, Ending a Scenario) with **Outpost Phases** (five steps: Passage of Time, Outpost Event, Building Operations, Downtime, Construction). The campaign narrative has an eventual ending, but play may continue past it. A universal rule: the party decides ambiguity.

## Setup

### Campaign / Character Creation
- Each player picks a class and takes its tuck boxes. Mark level 1 on a character sheet; initial characters start with 30 gold. The character mat shows the class's hand size, traits, and maximum hit points by level.
- Take the class's ability cards. At level 1 you may use the level 1 cards (and optionally the trickier level X cards, which count as level 1); you can only bring a number of ability cards into a scenario equal to your hand size.
- Take a standard 20-card attack modifier deck. Advanced class modifier cards are added only through perks.
- Draw two personal quests, keep one, shuffle the other back. Personal quests can be secret or public, as the party decides.
- Spend the 30 starting gold on items in the available purchasable supply (items 120–128; certain Gloomhaven items if you own that game). Unspent remainder is lost.
- Campaign start: the building deck contains Barracks, Craftsman, Alchemist, and Workshop (all level 1); apply their one-time effects. Scenarios 0 (optional practice scenario) and 1 are unlocked; there is no Outpost Phase between scenarios 0 and 1.

### Beginning a Scenario (in order)
1. Select an unlocked scenario whose requirements the party meets (previously completed scenarios may be replayed).
2. Resolve a road event if applicable. Road events are skipped: for scenarios 0 and 1, when replaying a lost scenario without returning to Frosthaven, when moving directly to a linked scenario, for scenarios inside Frosthaven, and in casual mode. Draw from the summer or winter road event deck per the calendar; read the front, collectively choose an option, read and resolve only the chosen outcome on the back (if a requirement — e.g., a character trait or a collective resource — is not met, use the "otherwise" outcome), then remove the card from the game (cards with the return icon go to the bottom of their deck instead).
3. Set up all map tiles for the scenario; retrieve monster materials (shuffle ability decks; insert stat cards in stat sleeves showing the scenario level) and overlay tiles; set up the first room only.
4. Retrieve and shuffle all needed attack modifier decks (character, monster, ally) and the extra bless/curse/other modifier cards.
5. Read the scenario goals, introduction, and special rules.
6. **Deal three battle goals to each character; each secretly keeps one** and discards the others face down. Battle goals stay secret until the scenario is over.
7. Place the token tray; build the scenario's loot deck as directed by its loot table.
8. Each character chooses which items to bring (applying any negative modifier cards and start-of-scenario item effects) and selects ability cards equal to their hand size; these start in hand.
9. Set each hit point dial to the character's maximum, experience dials to zero; apply any road event and scenario effects.
10. Place character figures on any starting hexes (never more than one figure per hex). Monster placement bars in the map layout depend on player count (top = 2 characters, middle = 3, bottom = 4; black = none, white = normal, yellow = elite); randomize standee numbers.

### Scenario Level
At the start of any scenario the level can be set to any number 0–7. Recommended difficulty = **average character level ÷ 2, rounded up**. (Solo mode and the open-information variant: add 1 to the average level before dividing by 2 and rounding up.) Scenario level determines monster level, trap damage (2 + scenario level), hazardous terrain damage (1 plus one-third the scenario level, rounded up), the gold conversion rate for looted coins, and bonus experience for completing the scenario.

## Turn Structure

A scenario is played in rounds. Before each round's steps, apply any start-of-round effects from the scenario rules (all spawning happens at the same time).

### 1. Card Selection
Each character secretly selects two ability cards from hand to play, choosing one as the **initiative card** (its central initiative value sets their position in the order). Alternatively, a character with **at least two cards in their discard pile** may declare a **long rest** (performed on initiative 99 as their entire turn; they play no cards). Players may not show cards or give specific numeric values or card titles, but may discuss general strategy ("I'm going in the first quarter of the round").

### 2. Ordering of Initiative
Reveal selected cards (initiative card on top). Reveal one monster ability card **for each monster set** with at least one figure on the map (several monster types can share one set and deck). Order all initiative tokens lowest to highest; long-resting characters are 99. Ties: between characters, compare second played cards, then the party decides; between a character and a monster set, the character acts first; multiple monster types of the same set are ordered by the scenario's special rules; all other ties, the party decides.

### 3. Character and Monster Turns
Every figure on the map takes a turn in initiative order.

**Character turns:** Perform the **top action of one played card and the bottom action of the other** — never two tops or two bottoms — in either order (the initiative choice no longer matters). Any card can instead be used as a basic "Attack 2" (always a top action) or "Move 2" (always a bottom action), discarding it with no other icons or abilities activated. A character may decline to perform an action (discarding the card with no effect) and may skip abilities within an action unless they are mandatory (marked "!": negative abilities, elemental infusions, experience, lost icons, active icons). Abilities in an action resolve in written order; you cannot start a new ability while resolving another (except abilities from attack modifier cards). Played cards go to the active area (active bonuses), the discard pile, or the lost pile (lost icon). Items can be used per their own timing. **At the end of a character's turn:** they **must loot any loot tokens or treasure tiles in their hex** (end-of-turn looting — characters only; monsters and summons never do this), elemental infusions from their action occur, and end-of-turn condition removals happen only after all other end-of-turn effects resolve.

**Character summons:** A character summon's turn is always **directly before their summoner's turn** in the initiative order and is a separate turn. Summons are not controlled by the summoner: they obey automated monster rules (performing "Move +0, Attack +0"), using the summoner's attack modifier deck. If a summon cannot find a focus, the summoner may choose to have it move toward them. Multiple summons act in the order they were summoned. **Summons never take a turn during the round in which they were summoned.**

**Monster turns:** All monsters of a type act at their set's card initiative: elites first in ascending standee-number order, then normals in ascending order (named monsters act before elites); each monster completes its turn before the next acts. Each monster finds a **focus**: the enemy it can perform its attack on using the fewest movement points (shortest path, counting difficult/icy terrain; paths triggering fewer negative hexes are preferred even over maximizing targets; the path need not be completable this turn; focus does not require line-of-sight). Ties: enemy closest by range, then earliest in initiative order. A monster with no attack (or disarmed) focuses as if for a single-target melee attack; a stunned monster cannot find a focus and ignores all abilities; an immobilized monster ignores move abilities. If no focus exists, the monster does not move or attack but performs its other abilities. A monster only moves if its ability card lists a move, using the fewest movement points needed to maximize its attacks this turn (if it cannot reach an attack this turn, it moves only if that shortens the path). Before a ranged attack on an adjacent target it moves away if possible to avoid disadvantage. It only attacks if its card lists an attack, and only against its focus/foci. Monsters use the shared monster attack modifier deck.

### 4. End of Round
In order: (1) trigger end-of-round effects from scenario rules, items, or ability cards; (2) if any drawn attack modifier card or monster ability card showed a shuffle icon, shuffle that discard pile back into its deck; (3) move round-bonus cards from active areas to discard/lost piles; (4) any character with at least two cards in their discard pile may perform a **short rest**; (5) all infused elements wane one column (strong → waning, waning → inert). **Conditions are NOT removed at end of round** — timed conditions expire at the end of the affected figure's next turn (see Conditions).

## Actions

### Move
"Move X" grants X movement points; 1 point moves 1 hex. You can move through allies, but not through enemies, obstacles, objectives, or walls, and must end in an unoccupied hex. Entering a hex triggers its effects (traps, etc.). Unspent points are lost.
- **Jump** (added effect of a move): ignore all enemies, obstacles, objectives, traps, and hazardous terrain except in the last hex; difficult and icy terrain are ignored completely. Cannot jump through walls.
- **Flying** (active bonus): completely ignore enemies, obstacles, objectives, traps, and overlay terrain, including the last hex, but never end movement in an occupied hex. A figure that loses flying is treated as entering its hex normally.
- **Teleport X**: transport to any valid hex within X, ignoring everything in between (even walls). Not a move ability and not movement; entering the destination hex still triggers its effects. Cannot teleport into an unrevealed room; teleporting onto a closed door opens it.

### Attack
"Attack X" deals a base X damage to one enemy in range. No printed range = melee, range 1. **Attack modification order:** (1) attack bonuses/penalties (party picks order if several); (2) the drawn attack modifier card; (3) the target's shield bonus; (4) ward and brittle (if the target has both, they negate each other and both are removed). Then the target suffers the result and may apply damage-negation effects. Repeat per target — one modifier card is drawn per target, and different targets can suffer different damage.
- **Target X**: attack up to X different figures in range (never the same figure twice with one ability, unless stated). Attack, conditions, heal, forced movement, commanding figures, and manipulating tiles are the only targeted abilities; targeted abilities need line-of-sight (only walls and closed doors block it) and cannot be performed with no valid target.
- **Area of effect**: rotate/mirror the pattern freely; gray hex = attacker's hex (an AoE including a gray hex is always melee); red hexes = targetable hexes (only one red hex must be in range); blue hex = must contain an ally (who is not targeted).
- **Modifier cards**: null reduces the final attack value to zero; x2 doubles the current value. Rolling modifiers chain: keep drawing until a non-rolling card, then apply all drawn modifiers in any order. Shuffle-icon cards trigger an end-of-round reshuffle (immediate if the deck is ever empty). Bless and curse cards return to the supply when resolved instead of the discard pile, and all bless/curse/extra cards are removed from decks at scenario end. Characters can always choose whether to apply a drawn modifier's added effects.
- **Advantage / Disadvantage**: draw two modifiers — with advantage a monster always uses the better one, but **a character may use either one**; with disadvantage the attacker uses the worse one (if ambiguous, the first drawn). If the first card is rolling, draw until non-rolling, then one more (ignoring its rolling icon): advantage keeps all initial rolling modifiers plus the chosen one of the last two; disadvantage ignores the rolling modifiers and uses the worse of the last two. Advantage and disadvantage don't stack and cancel to neither. **Any ranged attack on an adjacent enemy automatically has disadvantage.**
- **Pierce X**: reduces the target's shield bonus by X for that attack (multiple pierce effects add).
- **Attack effects** (conditions, push/pull, added +Attack, etc.): decide whether to apply them before drawing the modifier card; they apply even if the attack deals no damage (including a null), but not if the attack killed or exhausted the target. All attack effects except "+X Target" apply before retaliate. Elemental infusions happen at end of turn.

### Conditions
General rules: a condition remains until its removal requirement is met; **neither positive nor negative conditions can be removed voluntarily**; a figure can't have multiple instances of one condition, but regaining it resets its duration. A condition gained during your own turn that is removed "at the end of the figure's next turn" takes effect immediately and lasts until the end of your next turn; end-of-turn removals happen after all other end-of-turn effects. Conditions attached to an attack land even if the attack deals no damage.

**Positive:**
- **Regenerate**: perform "Heal 1, self" at the start of each of your turns (even while stunned). Removed when the figure suffers damage. With wound: regenerate applies first, removing wound before wound triggers.
- **Ward**: **the next time** the figure suffers damage from any source, it suffers half that amount (rounded down), **and ward is then removed** (single use, not ongoing). Ward + brittle negate each other and are both removed.
- **Invisible**: cannot be focused on or targeted by any enemy (non-targeted abilities still work; allies unaffected; enemies can move through but not end in the hex). Removed at the end of the figure's next turn.
- **Strengthen**: advantage on all attacks. Removed at the end of the figure's next turn.
- **Bless**: shuffle a bless card into the figure's attack modifier deck; when drawn it acts as x2 and returns to the supply. 10 bless cards exist; if none available (or the figure uses no deck), no effect.

**Negative:**
- **Wound**: suffer 1 damage at the start of each of your turns. Removed when healed.
- **Brittle**: the next damage the figure would suffer from any source is doubled, then brittle is removed. Also removed if healed.
- **Bane**: the figure suffers 10 damage at the end of its next turn, then bane is removed. Also removed if healed (which prevents the damage).
- **Poison**: all attacks targeting the figure gain +1 Attack. Removed when the figure is healed, but poison prevents that heal from increasing hit points.
- **Immobilize**: cannot perform move abilities. Removed at the end of the figure's next turn.
- **Disarm**: cannot perform attack abilities. Removed at the end of the figure's next turn.
- **Impair** (characters only): **the character cannot use or trigger any items** (bonuses already gained from items stay active). Removed at the end of the character's next turn.
- **Stun**: cannot perform any abilities or use/trigger items (previously gained bonuses stay active). Removed at the end of the figure's next turn. Stunned characters still select two cards (discarded with no effect if stun isn't removed before their turn ends) or may long rest normally.
- **Muddle**: disadvantage on all attacks. Removed at the end of the figure's next turn.
- **Curse**: shuffle a curse card into the figure's deck; when drawn it acts as a null and returns to the supply. 10 curse cards exist per deck type (character/ally vs. monster). Immunity to curse prevents adding the card but not a drawn curse from taking effect.

### Heal
"Heal X" restores X hit points to the target (never above maximum; healing at full is allowed). A single heal can remove any combination of wound, brittle, bane, and poison — but if poison is present, the hit point increase is prevented. Heal effects on modifier cards work like heal abilities.

### Active Bonuses, Shield, Retaliate, Summon
Cards granting ongoing bonuses go to the character's active area (they are not in hand). **Round bonuses** expire at end of round; **persistent bonuses** expire when their condition is fulfilled (use slots track limited uses — the token advances each trigger even if no benefit; experience icons between slots grant XP; leaving the last slot ends the bonus; with no stated removal condition, they last the scenario; "for N rounds/turns" doesn't count the activation round/turn). A persistent (not round) bonus card may be voluntarily removed anytime except during another ability.
- **Shield X**: reduces the attack value of incoming attacks by X, applied after bonuses/penalties and the modifier card. Stacks. Applies to all incoming attacks while active, but only to attacks.
- **Retaliate X**: after an attack against you resolves, the attacker suffers X damage if within the retaliate's range (default: adjacent), evaluated after all attack effects (including push/pull). Not an attack or targeted effect, so shields don't reduce it; it doesn't trigger if the retaliator was killed or exhausted by the attack. Stacks as a single damage source.
- **Summon**: places allied figures in empty hexes adjacent to the summoner (not summoned if no hex is free). A character summon is a persistent bonus; its card sits in the active area, and the summon is removed at zero hit points (card to discard/lost pile). The summoner may voluntarily remove a summon anytime except during another ability. If the summoner is exhausted, all their summons are removed. Summon stats are hit points, move, attack, range ("–" means it doesn't perform that ability unless granted one). See Turn Structure for summon activation.

### Forced Movement
**Push X** moves the target up to X hexes, each hex farther from the acting figure; **Pull X**, each hex closer. Characters choose direction and distance; for monsters the party chooses direction but must move the target as far as possible. Push/pull ignore difficult terrain but follow all other movement rules. Immobilized/stunned figures can still be pushed, pulled, or teleported. As attack effects, they work even on a no-damage attack (not on a killed/exhausted target). Multiple push or pull effects combine.

### Suffer Damage
Non-attack damage ("suffer X damage") is modified by nothing except ward and brittle (shields don't apply). It is not a targeted ability.

### Loot
- **Loot X ability**: loots **all** loot tokens and treasure tiles within range X (including your hex), unaffected by figures or overlay tiles. It cannot be performed if there is nothing to loot in range.
- **End-of-turn looting**: characters **must** loot any loot tokens or treasure tiles in their own hex at the end of their turn (not at the end of each movement). Only characters do this.
- When a character loots a loot token, they draw one loot card (money, materials, herbs, or the once-per-scenario random item). **Money and resources on loot cards are not gained until the scenario ends; random items are gained immediately** (drawn from the random item deck; if it's empty the card counts as three coins). Monsters with loot abilities remove tokens from the map (gaining nothing, and those tokens are not re-dropped); monsters can never loot treasure tiles.
- Treasure tiles: goal treasures relate to scenario completion; numbered treasures use the Treasure Index, are gained by the looting character (except random blueprints/scenarios), and can only be looted once per campaign.
- The loot deck is built per scenario from: 20 money cards (1–3 coins each, converted to gold by scenario level), 8 cards per material (lumber/metal/hide; amounts scale with character count), 2 cards per herb (arrowvine, axenut, corpsecap, flamefruit, rockroot, snowthistle; 1 herb each), and 1 random item card.

### Recover, Command, Manipulate
- **Recover**: the targeted character returns up to the stated number of cards from their discard or lost pile to hand; some recover abilities instead refresh spent or lost items.
- **Commanding figures**: **Grant** lets a figure give an ability to any figure (the commander decides how summons/allies perform it); **Control** lets a figure force an enemy to perform an ability, deciding how, with allies/enemies swapped from the commander's perspective (a controlled move is forced movement). Commanded attacks use the commanded figure's own modifier deck; a flat "Attack/Move X" can be commanded even with no stat, but "±X" cannot.
- **Manipulating tiles**: create, destroy, relocate, move, replace, or spring (destroy a trap, applying its effects to yourself) overlay tiles. Max one overlay tile per hex (except corridors/open doors); traps and obstacles can't go into occupied hexes; you may not wall off part of the map.

### Items
Characters bring a limited loadout into each scenario: **one head, one body, one feet, up to two one-hand items or one two-hand item, and small items up to half their level (rounded up)**. Some items add minus-one cards to your modifier deck for the scenario. Usage types: **spent** (rotate; recovered by your next long rest), **lost** (flip face down; unusable for the scenario unless recovered — items with the persistent-lost icon can't be recovered until the scenario ends), **flip** (alternates sides), and passive (no icon; unlimited). Items with use slots or passive effects **must** be used when their requirements are met; all other item uses are optional. An item affecting an attack must be used before the modifier card is drawn; an item granting an ability can't be used during another ability. All items reset between scenarios — no item is ever permanently lost. Characters may not own duplicate copies, and items cannot be traded between characters.

### Experience
A character gains the XP shown on an action when any part of it is performed (some XP requires conditions such as consuming an element or advancing past a use-slot XP icon). **Characters do not gain experience for killing monsters.**

### Resting
Both rests require **at least two cards in your discard pile**, and always cost one discarded card.
- **Short rest** (during End of Round): shuffle your discard pile and randomly lose one card; return the rest to hand. Once per short rest, you may suffer 1 damage to instead lose a different random card.
- **Long rest** (declared in Card Selection; your whole turn, initiative 99): lose one card **of your choice** from your discard and return the rest to hand (mandatory); perform "Heal 2, self" (optional); recover all spent items (optional — items can be used the same turn they're recovered).

### Lost Actions, Character Damage, Exhaustion
- **Lost actions**: performing any part of an action with the lost icon sends the card to your lost pile for the rest of the scenario (unless recovered); lost active-bonus cards go to the lost pile when the bonus ends. Persistent-lost icon cards (rotated 180°) cannot be recovered until the scenario ends.
- **Character damage**: when you would suffer damage (after ward/brittle), either reduce your hit point dial or negate the damage via (a) an ability/effect that negates it, or (b) losing **one card of your choice from your hand OR two cards of your choice from your discard pile**. Before your turn, your two selected cards are in neither pile and can't be used this way. Negating damage does not prevent conditions or other effects from the same source.
- **Exhaustion**: a character is exhausted (1) at zero hit points, or (2) at the start of a round when they have neither **at least two cards in hand (to play) nor at least two cards in their discard pile (to rest)** — one in each pile is still exhaustion. All their cards (including summons and active cards) go to the lost pile and the figure is removed (possible mid-ability). Exhausted characters cannot participate further, but exhaustion does not reduce the character count for scenario scaling. If all characters are exhausted, the scenario is lost.

## Scoring / Victory Conditions

There is no point scoring; a scenario is either **completed** (its stated goal is achieved) or **lost** (all characters exhausted, or a scenario-specific loss condition). **Once an end trigger fires, play continues to the end of the current round**, then the scenario ends; if it would be both lost and completed in the same round, it is lost.

**Cleanup (always):** every character recovers all discarded/lost ability cards and all spent/lost items, resets to full hit points, and loses all conditions. Bless, curse, and other added modifier cards are removed from all decks; battle goals reshuffle. Loot still on the map is forfeit. Characters bank the XP on their dial (plus, if completed, bonus XP by scenario level) and their looted coins × the scenario level's gold conversion rate (gold is kept even on a loss).

**If lost, the party chooses:**
- **Return to Frosthaven**: gain the resources from loot cards; keep all XP, gold, and treasure; the Outpost Phase occurs; afterward attempt any available scenario (with road event if applicable).
- **Replay the scenario**: forfeit loot-card resources; keep all XP, gold, and treasure; no Outpost Phase and no road event; attempt the same scenario again.

**If completed:** gain loot-card resources; each character achieving their secret battle goal gains its checkmarks (every 3 checkmarks = 1 perk mark, up to 18 checkmarks / 6 perk marks — a lost scenario yields nothing from battle goals); gain one perk mark per newly achieved class mastery (possible even if exhausted, if the requirements were met before exhaustion); read the conclusion and gain scenario rewards (first completion only); gain **inspiration equal to 4 minus the number of characters**; mark the scenario complete. Even exhausted characters keep everything and may achieve battle goals/masteries if the party completed the scenario. The party then returns to Frosthaven for an Outpost Phase — unless a linked scenario is available (may skip the Outpost Phase) or force-linked (must skip it); links are ignored on repeat completions.

**Campaign progression:** characters level up (mandatory when XP meets the next requirement — Downtime only), retire when their personal quest is fulfilled (unlocking buildings/classes), and the outpost itself grows via construction, prosperity, and morale.

## Special Rules & Edge Cases

### Map Features (Overlay Tiles)
- **Doors** start closed; a character entering one flips it open, revealing the adjacent room (new monsters there act this round — see below). Closed doors don't hinder normal character movement but otherwise act as walls; figures can't enter a closed door via forced movement. Open doors act as corridors but still separate rooms.
- **Corridors** and **pressure plates** count as empty hexes; jump and flying still trigger pressure plates.
- **Traps** spring when entered (not by flying/jumping figures unless a jump ends there): apply their effect, then remove the trap. "Damage" traps deal 2 + scenario level.
- **Hazardous terrain** damages any figure entering it (1 plus one-third of the scenario level, rounded up; same jump/fly exception); the tile stays; no damage for starting a turn in it or leaving it.
- **Difficult terrain** costs 2 movement points to enter (flying/jumping ignore it, even if a jump ends there).
- **Icy terrain**: entering it (except by teleport, fly, or jump) forces one free extra hex of movement in the same direction (chains across more ice; stopped by walls, figures, obstacles, objectives).
- **Obstacles** can't be entered (except flying, or jumping if the jump doesn't end there) and do not block line-of-sight.
- **Objectives** (destroy/protect targets) have scenario-set hit points, are immune to all conditions and forced movement, count as figures (initiative 99 for focusing) but never act, and are not obstacles.

### Common Scenario Rules
- **Allies** (scenario allies): follow automated monster rules, use the ally attack modifier deck, and act at the initiative given in the special rules in ascending token/standee order. With two opposing monster sides, one side uses the ally deck, so bless/curse hit each side separately.
- **Equations** for HP etc. use L (scenario level), C (character count), H (elite max HP of the type).
- **Escape scenarios**: a character ending their turn on an escape hex may escape (figure, summons, and tokens removed; they can no longer participate).
- **Linked hexes** are adjacent for movement; no line-of-sight between them, but focus can be found through them.
- **Locked doors** act as walls until their unlock condition is met.
- **Round track** (top of element board) tracks timed scenario effects; advance it at the end of each round.
- **Section links** are read immediately when their condition occurs, even mid-ability.

### Monsters: Adding, Special Classes, Death
- **Revealed monsters** (new room) and **spawned monsters** act in the round they appear (unlike summons): draw their set's ability card if needed; if their initiative already passed, they act immediately after the current figure's turn; otherwise at their normal position. If standees run out, place as many as possible (elites first, by proximity to an enemy); the rest are not placed. Spawns on occupied locations move to the closest empty hex.
- **Named monsters** and **bosses** (red bases) are neither normal nor elite (immune to rank-targeting abilities); named monsters act before elites of their type. Bosses use special stat cards and a boss ability deck; "C" in boss stats = number of characters.
- **Monster damage/death**: a monster dies as soon as accumulated damage reaches its max hit points — remove the standee and **place a loot token in its hex** (even for summoned/spawned monsters; scenario allies drop nothing). No attack effects apply after death; a monster that dies mid-ability stops.
- **Kill credit**: summons pass kills to their summoner. Kills by attack, retaliate, suffer-damage, or a negative hex reached by forced movement credit the causing figure; deaths from wound, bane, another monster, or voluntarily entering a negative hex credit no one.
- **Monsters and elements**: a set's infusion occurs when the last monster of the set ends its turn; a consumption occurs when the first monster starts its turn and benefits the whole set. The party picks wild/mixed elements for monsters.
- Monster heal abilities target the ally in range missing the most hit points; positive conditions go to the closest ally lacking the condition. Monster summons are placed adjacent to the summoner as close to an enemy as possible, never act the round they appear, and remain if their summoner dies.

### Elements
Infusing (from a performed action) moves the element token to **strong at the end of the acting figure's turn**. Consuming requires the element to be strong or waning **at the start of the turn** (before any infusions) and moves it to inert. The same element can't be consumed twice in one turn; multi-element consumption icons need all shown elements; a wild icon is any one element. At the end of every round all infused elements wane one column (strong → waning → inert).

### Outpost Phase (between scenarios)
Mandatory after each Scenario Phase except when replaying a lost scenario or playing a linked scenario. Five steps in order:
1. **Passage of Time**: mark the next calendar box (one week). Boxes may hold section numbers to read immediately (time passed outside the Outpost Phase defers its section to the next Outpost Phase). Every 10 filled boxes flips the season, summer ↔ winter; the season selects the road/outpost event decks. 10 weeks per season, two seasons per year.
2. **Outpost Event**: draw and resolve an event from the current season's outpost deck (resolved exactly like road events), unless instructed to skip it. **Attack events** target buildings (attack value, target value = number of buildings, target priority; each targeted building gets a separate **defense check**: draw a town guard card and add its bonus to Frosthaven's total defense value plus event modifiers — meet or exceed the attack value to defend). While the Barracks stands, the party may spend any number of soldiers before drawing for advantage and an attack-value reduction (per Barracks level); a wrecked Barracks imposes disadvantage. The "Success" card auto-defends; the "Wreck" card auto-wrecks. Failed checks damage (or for some events wreck) the building; resolve those effects only after the whole event. **Damaged** buildings must be repaired immediately (repair cost payable with material resources of any type, from the Frosthaven supply and/or personal supplies) or the party loses 1 morale. **Wrecked** buildings flip to their wrecked side; their wrecked effect runs each Building Operations step until rebuilt, and they can't be upgraded or interacted with.
3. **Building Operations**: resolve every building's normal (or wrecked) effect in reference-number order; gains/losses may use any combination of supplies; optional effects require full payment.
4. **Downtime** (any order; wrecked buildings unavailable): **Level up** — mandatory when XP meets the next level's requirement (XP is not spent or reset): add one new ability card of your class at or below the new level to your pool, raise max hit points per the mat, and gain a perk mark. Optionally, a character below **half the prosperity level (rounded up)** may level up to that cap without XP, setting their XP to the new requirement. Perk marks come from level-ups, every 3 battle-goal checkmarks, masteries, and (for new characters) 1 per character that player previously retired; perks permanently tune your modifier deck and take effect immediately; perk marks can never be lost. **Retire** — mandatory when your personal quest is fulfilled: the party gains 2 prosperity and opens the quest's building envelope (or its alternate; if both are open, gain a random scenario and a random item blueprint, or inspiration if depleted). A retiree may spend 15 inspiration to immediately complete a second drawn personal quest. First retirement of each class: read the section on the mat's back. Retired characters' items return to the supply and their gold is lost. **Create a character** — any class with no active character; draw 2 personal quests keep 1; start with (10 × prosperity + 20) gold that must be spent immediately on available purchasable items; level 1 plus optional prosperity leveling. A not-yet-retired character may be set aside (keeps everything) or abandoned (quest reshuffled, items returned, gold lost). **Craft items** (Craftsman): pay resource/item costs — material resources must come from personal supplies (never the Frosthaven supply), herbs may come from either; a required item owned only by others may be substituted by paying its own cost. **Brew potions** (Alchemist): spend any two herbs (any supplies), revealing the alchemy-chart window for that combination; extra copies become craftable. Alchemist level 2 allows distilling potions back into one component herb; level 3 allows three-herb potions. **Sell items**: purchasable items fetch half their gold cost (rounded down); craftable items fetch 2 gold per resource/item in their cost. A duplicate copy of an owned item must be sold immediately. **Purchase items**: not freely available until building 37 is built (until then only via new-character starting gold or specific effects).
5. **Construction**: build or upgrade **one** building (lose 2 morale for a second), paying costs from the Frosthaven supply, subject to each build/upgrade's prosperity requirement; then rebuild any number of wrecked buildings (rebuild cost). Wrecked buildings can't be upgraded. New/upgraded buildings grant their prosperity bonus and one-time effects now, and function from the next Outpost Phase.

### Campaign Resources & Trackers
- **Resources**: 3 materials (lumber, metal, hide) + 6 herbs. Personal supplies craft items; the Frosthaven supply pays building costs (materials) and crafting/brewing (herbs only, plus collective costs). Resources move one-way: personal → Frosthaven, never back. Collective gains/losses may use any supplies; unpayable collective losses are simply ignored beyond what's available.
- **Inspiration**: party-wide; +（4 − character count) per completed scenario; spend 1:1 in place of material resources for building costs, or 15 on retirement for a bonus quest.
- **Morale** (0–20): gates some event outcomes and modifies total defense (only the current modifier applies); spend 1 to skip a repair cost, 2 for an extra build/upgrade; hitting 0 or 20 triggers a section read. Starting morale is set at the end of scenario 1.
- **Soldiers**: spent on defense checks; the maximum is set by Barracks level.
- **Prosperity**: +X marks X boxes (numbered boxes raise the level); it caps new-character catch-up leveling (half level, rounded up) and building-upgrade levels. When prosperity rises, under-leveled characters may immediately catch up. −X never erases numbered boxes.
- **Town guard deck**: starts as a standard 20-card deck (numeric bonus cards plus one "Success" and one "Wreck" card); town guard perks and campaign effects add/remove cards. Drawn for defense checks like attack modifiers; reshuffled after each attack event (or when depleted).
- **Unlocks**: building envelopes open via retirement (affix the L0 sticker; the building must still be built); 11 new classes unlock via campaign progression. Enhancement marks on ability cards have their purpose revealed later in the campaign (building 44).

### Game Variants
- **Casual mode**: play any unlocked scenario ignoring requirements; no event, no time passing, and no campaign gains of any kind.
- **Solo mode**: one player runs two or more characters; scenario level uses (average level + 1) ÷ 2, rounded up.
- **Open information**: full card sharing allowed; use the solo-mode scenario level formula.
- **Reduced randomness**: null and curse cards are treated as flat negative modifiers, x2 and bless as flat positive modifiers (shuffle icons still apply).
- **Permanent death**: dropping to zero hit points kills the character (removed from the campaign at scenario end; quest reshuffled, items returned, gold lost; replacement created next Outpost Phase). Card-starvation exhaustion still merely exhausts — the figure stays on the map, can be targeted, counts as initiative 99 for focusing, and survives the campaign.
- **Character respeccing**: pay 10 × level gold in Downtime to re-pick all above-level-1 ability-card choices.
- **Random dungeons**: three randomly generated rooms (room + setup cards); clear all monsters to complete; fixed 24-card loot deck; no road event.
- **Crossover classes**: unlocked classes may move between Gloomhaven-system games.

## Player Reference

**Round sequence:** start-of-round effects → 1. Card Selection (2 cards, one as initiative — or long rest if ≥2 in discard) → 2. Ordering of Initiative (reveal; one monster card per set; low goes first; character wins ties vs monsters) → 3. Turns (characters: top of one card + bottom of the other; summons act just before their summoner; monsters: elites then normals) → 4. End of Round (end-of-round effects; shuffle-icon reshuffles; round-bonus cards off; optional short rests; elements wane).
