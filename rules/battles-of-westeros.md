## Overview

BattleLore: Battles of Westeros (BOW) is a scenario-based, two-player wargame that recreates military engagements between the Great Houses of Westeros in the fantasy setting of George R. R. Martin's *A Song of Ice and Fire*. In the core game, players control House Stark or House Lannister. Each scenario, called a battle, has a battle plan (found in the included Westeros Battle Plans book, or online) that dictates the formation of the battlefield map, each House's starting positions and resources, special rules, and the victory conditions for that session — some battles require earning a target number of victory points (VPs), while others require taking and holding strategic positions. A battle is played over a battle-plan-specified number of rounds, with players alternating turns within each round; commanders use order tokens and Leadership cards to move and attack with their units on a hex battlefield, while each side tracks its army's morale, which can force an early loss if it collapses. If the rules text on a card or other component contradicts this rulebook, the card or component takes precedence.

## Setup

1. **Choose a Battle and a House to Play.** Battles are found in the Westeros Battle Plans book, online, or in expansions. First-time players should play "Clash on the Kingsroad." Decide which House each player controls (Stark or Lannister in the core game); if both players want the same House, determine randomly (e.g., a coin flip).
2. **Assemble figures** (before a first battle): determine each figure's base by counting the teeth on its tab — three teeth use a rectangular base, two teeth use a square base — then press the tab into the base slot with the banner hole toward the rear of the figure (a little glue can help if a figure doesn't attach securely).
3. Each player takes the command board, Leadership cards, Commander cards, and Unit Reference cards for their chosen House.
4. Unfold the map board per the battle plan and place it, correct side up, in the center of the table; add any map overlays or tokens the battle plan requires. Each player sits at their House's board edge (shown by the colored border and House symbol on the battlefield map).
5. Take the commander figures and Commander cards specified by the battle plan (placed uncommitted side up), and return unused commanders' figures/cards to the box. Some commanders have multiple versions — check the title under the commander's name to confirm the correct card.
6. **Build the Leadership deck**: set aside each House's 10 basic Leadership cards (no commander's picture in the lower right). Take the five commander-specific Leadership cards for every commander used in this battle, shuffle them thoroughly with the 10 basic cards, and place the resulting deck facedown in your play area.
7. **Place the Round Track and Round Marker**, with the marker on the "1" space; the number beside the battle plan's Round Limit icon shows how many rounds the battle lasts (5, for "Clash on the Kingsroad"). Assemble the morale track by attaching each House's morale segment to the central morale hub, with each player's segment nearest to their own board edge and faceup on the side specified by the battle plan; place the morale marker on the morale hub's center starting space.
8. **Create token stockpiles** (order tokens, command tokens, engagement tokens, and any battle-specific tokens such as strategy tokens) within easy reach of both players. Each player takes command tokens equal to the number beside the Command icon in the battle plan and places them on their command board.
9. **Form and place units**: lay out the punchboard banners required for the battle first (each banner pole holds one banner, gripped by its round indicator), then, for each hex containing a friendly banner, form the indicated unit — find the unit's figure type on the battlefield map (or on a Commander card's upper-right, if a commander), take the number of figures shown as its strength on the Unit Reference card, and place the assigned banner in one figure's base (the commander's base, if a commander is present in that unit). Place the completed unit in its designated hex.
10. **Assign the momentum token** to the House specified by the battle plan.

## Turn Structure

A battle is played over a series of game rounds — the number set by the battle plan's Round Limit — continuing until that many rounds are complete or a player achieves one of the battle's victory conditions. Each round consists of four phases, always performed in this order: **Rally Phase → Marshaling Phase → Command Phase → Regroup Phase**, after which a new round begins with a new Rally Phase.

**1. Rally Phase** (performed simultaneously by both players): determines who has the advantage (acts first) for the rest of the round, refreshes commanders, and rallies units.
- Players compare the number of command tokens currently on each command board; whoever has the most gains the advantage and becomes the "first player" for the round. Ties are won by whoever holds the momentum token.
- Each player returns all command tokens from his Commander cards to his command board (refreshing those commanders so their Leadership cards can be played again).
- Each player rotates his units' banners to match the current round's color (shown by the round marker's position on the round track), making those units active and eligible to be ordered in the coming Command Phase. This rotation is called "rallying" a unit; a banner already showing the correct color is not rotated.

**2. Marshaling Phase**: the first player performs both of the following actions, in order, then the opponent does the same.
- Roll a number of dice equal to the battle plan's Order Rating; take order tokens matching the results rolled from the supply and add them to any token carried over from the previous round to form the player's Order Pool.
- Draw Leadership cards equal to the battle plan's Leadership Rating, adding them to any card carried over from the previous round to form the player's hand. If a player must draw but his Leadership deck is empty, he shuffles his discard pile to form a new deck.

**3. Command Phase**: starting with the first player, each player in turn chooses one action from those listed under Actions below (use an order token, play a Leadership card, or pass) and resolves it completely before the other player acts. This continues, alternating, until both players have passed, at which point the phase ends. A player who has no legal order token or Leadership card play is forced to pass; once a player passes, he can take no further actions that round, though his opponent may continue.

**4. Regroup Phase**: players perform upkeep in general simultaneously (the first player resolves any timing conflicts first).
- Resolve status conditions (in the core game, this means resolving Fire in any hex with a fire token — see Special Rules & Edge Cases).
- Check victory conditions: if this is the last round of the battle (per the Round Limit), the game ends and the battle's victory conditions are consulted to determine the winner; instant victory conditions (such as a Rout) are checked throughout the game, not just here. If the game has not ended, the Regroup Phase completes in full:
  - Each player discards Leadership cards and order tokens of his choice down to a maximum of one Leadership card and one order token carried over to the next round.
  - **Morale recovery**: if a player's morale marker is in a colored area of the track and not already on a morale break (a space with an arrow), he may move it to the nearest morale break matching that area's color; a marker already on a break, or on a grey-bordered space, cannot recover.
  - The round marker advances to the next space on the round track.

## Actions

On his turn during the Command Phase, the active player chooses exactly one of the following:

### Use an Order Token
Declare which active unit is being ordered, then discard a token from your Order Pool that matches the unit's rank (green/blue/red shield) or discard a Valor (purple) token — or discard two tokens of the same type at once to order any unit regardless of rank. A Morale token instead lets you increase your House's morale by one, or decrease it by one to rally one of your units; either way, no unit is ordered by a Morale token. Using an order token is normally the only way to order an uncontrolled unit (one not in any friendly commander's zone-of-control).

### Play a Leadership Card
1. Declare which commander is using the card (he need not be the commander pictured on it).
2. For each Command icon on the card's command box that you wish to resolve, move one command token from your command board onto that commander's Commander card (a card cannot hold more tokens than the commander's Command Limit); pay for all commands you intend to use before resolving any of them, though they may then be resolved in any order, and each command can only be executed once per card play.
3. Declare which units — all of which must be within the chosen commander's zone-of-control (ZOC) — are ordered by the card's commands. In the core game every commander has a ZOC of two hexes, so any unit within two hexes of the chosen commander is under his control, and a unit can be controlled by more than one commander at once. Commands affect only friendly units unless the card states otherwise.
4. If the card's tactic box also has a usable tactic, you may resolve it: check that its "if" requirement is met (an Order Pool requirement checks that the needed tokens are currently in your pool — a single token can only be checked once per card; a morale requirement checks the marker's position as "better" or "worse" than a stated point on the track), then resolve its "then" effect before or after the card's commands, according to its printed timing.
5. Move all ordered units, then attack with all ordered units.

### Pass
A player may choose to pass instead of acting. Once he passes, he can no longer use order tokens or Leadership cards for the remainder of that round; his opponent may keep acting until he also passes.

### Moving an Ordered Unit
Movement (if the unit chooses to move) must be completed before that unit attacks, and all of a player's ordered units must finish moving before any of them attack. An ordered unit is never forced to move or attack — it may do either, both, or neither.
- A unit moves only into whole hexes (half-hexes on the battlefield's outer edge count as off the battlefield) and only as far as its rank/class allow on the Order Table; some terrain affects movement further.
- Only one unit may occupy a hex; a unit cannot move into or through an occupied hex, and a unit's figures can never split up or combine with another unit.
- If a unit will not attack after moving, rotate its banner to the inactive side once movement is complete.
- An engaged unit that moves must first disengage, which may provoke a Parting Blow from the unit it was engaged with (see Special Rules & Edge Cases).

### Attacking with an Ordered Unit
After all of a player's ordered units have finished moving, he attacks with them one at a time, in any order, following this Combat Sequence:
1. Declare the attacking (ordered) unit and declare an enemy unit as its target.
2. Verify Range and Line of Sight (LOS): count hexes from the attacker to the target (excluding the attacker's hex, including the target's), which must be within the attacker's Attack Range (1, for a Melee icon; otherwise the range printed beside the Ranged Attack icon on its Unit Reference card). For a ranged attack, an imaginary line from the center of the attacker's hex to the center of the target's hex must not cross any hex with an LOS-blocking obstacle (terrain, or any unit, friendly or enemy); if the line runs exactly along a hex edge, shift it slightly in either direction — if both shifted lines are blocked, there is no LOS. Units always have LOS to adjacent units.
3. Place an Engagement Token: if this is a melee attack on a currently unengaged target, place one of the attacker's engagement tokens on the hex border between attacker and target (replacing any other token there first). If the target is already engaged with a different unit, this attack becomes a flank attack (see Special Rules & Edge Cases).
4. Roll attack dice: green rank units roll two dice, blue rank roll three, red rank roll four, before modifiers from terrain, card text, unit abilities, or tokens.
5. Resolve hits: a rolled shield result scores a hit only if its color matches the target's rank (a mismatched shield color is a miss); a Valor result scores a hit unless the attacking unit's Order Table shows the "no Valor hit" icon, and (in the core game) a non-cavalry unit's Valor result cannot hit a cavalry target. The target removes one figure per hit (the banner-bearing figure is always removed last); if the banner figure is removed, the unit is eliminated and its owner suffers the morale loss for that unit's rank. Commander figures never take these hits directly — see Capturing Commanders below.
6. Resolve retreats: if the target was not eliminated, it must retreat one hex (of the attacker's choice) toward its own board edge for each Morale (flag) result rolled — see Retreating below.
7. Rotate the attacking unit's banner to its inactive side.

An attacking unit with the Advance or Pursue X keyword may then move into (and, for Pursue, attack again from) the target's now-vacated hex — see Special Rules & Edge Cases.

### Counterattacking
A target unit with the Stalwart keyword may counterattack any melee attacker, provided the target was not eliminated and remains in its hex (a unit forced to retreat cannot counterattack, and the triggering attack must be fully resolved first). A counterattack rolls the target's normal number of attack dice as a standard attack, but: it does not disengage the counterattacking unit; it does not trigger a Parting Blow even if the counterattacker is engaged to a different unit; the counterattacking unit cannot itself be counterattacked; it cannot advance or pursue afterward; and modifiers that apply only "when attacking" do not apply to it.

### Retreating
A unit forced to retreat moves one hex per Morale result toward its own board edge, always into one of the two hexes that lead further from its original hex (no zigzagging); other terrain effects are ignored while retreating, though impassable terrain still blocks it. A unit cannot retreat into a hex with a friendly or enemy unit, and cannot retreat off the board. If it cannot complete its retreat, or can only partially complete it, each hex of retreat it cannot make is converted into a hit, applied immediately.

### Capturing Commanders
Once every other figure in a commander's unit has been eliminated, the commander himself is subject to capture: the Capture Rating printed on his Commander card's shield is the number of hits that must be scored against him in a single attack to capture him (e.g., a Capture Rating of 3 requires 3 hits in one attack, after his other figures are already gone). A captured commander's figure moves to the capturing player's play area. A commander figure cannot itself take hits to trigger card effects, though figures elsewhere in his unit can.

## Scoring / Victory Conditions

Victory conditions are specific to each battle plan — some battles are won by reaching a target number of victory points (VPs), others by taking and holding strategic positions — and are checked as part of the Regroup Phase once the battle's last round (per its Round Limit) has been completed. In addition, the core rules define these general conditions that apply across battles:

- **Rout (instant victory)**: if a House's morale marker reaches the Rout space on that House's side of the morale track, that House immediately loses and its opponent wins. Instant victory conditions are checked throughout the game (not only during Regroup) and always take precedence over normal victory conditions; the Rout condition in turn takes precedence over any other instant victory condition in a battle.
- **Scoring timing**: most battles award victory points for objectives every round, for objectives only on the battle's last round, or a mix of both, as specified by the battle plan. On any round but the last, only the "every round" objectives are scored; on the last round, both categories are scored.
- **Ties**: in a battle played to a VP target, if the game ends tied, the player with the higher morale wins; if morale is tied at the starting space, the player holding the momentum token wins.

## Special Rules & Edge Cases

### Engagement, Flanking, and Parting Blows
A unit is **engaged** when an engagement token sits on the hex border between it and an adjacent unit. An unengaged melee attacker that targets an already-engaged unit is **flanking**: after rolling his battle dice, the flanking player may choose one die symbol and reroll all dice showing it, accepting the new results (he need not reroll if he doesn't want to). An engaged unit can only attack the unit it's engaged with unless it disengages; disengaging (by moving, or by attacking a different unit) removes the engagement token and lets the unit being disengaged from make a **Parting Blow** — a free attack with normal attack dice, ignoring Morale results — before the disengaging unit can move or attack; if it survives, it continues normally. Disengagements caused by other means (card effects, retreats) do not trigger a Parting Blow. A ranged attacker cannot disengage except by moving away. Any engagement token is removed automatically if the engaged target retreats or is eliminated.

### Morale
A shift of the morale marker toward a player's opponent increases that player's morale; a shift toward the player decreases it (since morale is one shared marker, one side's gain is the other's loss). Morale shifts occur when: a green rank enemy unit is eliminated (+1), a blue rank enemy unit is eliminated (+2), a red rank enemy unit is eliminated (+3), a Morale order token is spent to increase morale (+1), a Morale order token is spent to rally a unit instead (−1), or a card effect specifies a shift. If the morale marker cannot move further but hasn't reached Rout, it simply stays in place until it next shifts the other way.

### Traits and Keywords
Traits (such as a unit's class — infantry, ranged, or cavalry) are printed in bold italic and by themselves do nothing, but can be referenced by cards or abilities. The core game's keywords are:

| Keyword | Effect |
|---|---|
| Advance | If this attacking unit's target is eliminated or forced to retreat, it may move into the hex the target vacated. Once per turn. |
| Cover X / Toughness X | Ignore X hits before any figures are removed — Cover comes from terrain, Toughness from the unit itself; both can stack. |
| Dogs of War | Instead of moving and attacking normally, this unit may make a special ranged attack from 2–3 hexes away (never adjacent). Roll one die first: Green Shield = attacks as green rank, Blue Shield = blue rank, Red Shield = red rank, Morale = no attack roll, target retreats one hex (cannot be ignored), Valor = no attack roll, target automatically takes a hit (cannot be ignored). For this attack, friendly units don't block LOS, but rivers do. |
| Heavy Armor | Defends as if one rank higher (e.g., green defends as blue). A red rank unit with this keyword ignores Valor results; Morale results still affect it normally. |
| Off-balance | If this unit moves before attacking in the same turn, it rolls one fewer attack die. |
| Pursue X | If this attacking unit's target is eliminated or forced to retreat, it may move up to X hexes (its first hex must be the one the target vacated) and attack again, against the same or a different unit. Once per turn; the unit must move at least one hex to make the extra attack, though it may pursue without attacking. |
| Scorch the Earth | Instead of attacking, roll attack dice at an unoccupied adjacent hex with a Burn Limit; each Green Shield rolled adds one fire level there (replacing any existing fire token with one showing the combined level). |
| Stalwart | May ignore the first Morale result rolled against it, and may counterattack (see Actions). Any unit automatically gains Stalwart (until no longer true) while adjacent to two friendly units; some commanders grant it permanently via their Commander card. |

### Terrain
The BOW core game uses 8 basic terrain hex types — Plains, Hills, Forest, River, Forest River, Building, Road, and Forest Road — plus man-made terrain tokens (Wooden Bridge, Stone Bridge, Palisade, Tent, Siege Tower, Ford, Fire, and Devastation) that are placed on top of a hex and, unless noted, override that hex's own effects.

| Terrain | Notes |
|---|---|
| Plains | Burn 4; no burn effect. |
| Hills | Max 3 combat dice may be rolled against a defender here (before modifiers) unless the attacker is also in a hills hex; a ranged unit here ignores LOS-blocking from units. |
| Forest | A unit must stop upon entering; max 2 combat dice rolled against a defender here (before modifiers); Burn 3 — at 3 fire levels the forest is destroyed and becomes a devastation token (treated as plains thereafter). |
| River / Forest River | River hexes don't block LOS; forest river hexes do. |
| Building | A unit must stop upon entering; units here have Cover 1 unless engaged with an engagement token of their own House's symbol; ranged attackers here roll a maximum of 2 combat dice; Burn 4 — at 4 fire levels the building is destroyed and becomes a devastation token. |
| Road / Forest Road | A unit moving only through road hexes may move one additional hex (which need not itself be a road hex); units may advance or pursue one extra hex (up to 2 for advance, 3 for pursue) as long as all of that movement stays on road hexes; Burn 4 for road, 3 for forest road (follows normal forest burn effects). |
| Wooden Bridge | Treated as a road hex for movement; Burn 3 — destroyed (and removed, with its fire tokens) at 3 levels; no advance or pursuit onto it. |
| Stone Bridge | Treated as a road hex for movement; no advance or pursuit onto it. |
| Palisade | Burn 4 — destroyed and removed at 4 fire levels. |
| Tent / Siege Tower | Burn 1 — destroyed and removed at 1 or more fire levels; other effects vary by battle plan. |
| Ford | Infantry must stop upon entering; cavalry entering reduce their remaining movement by one hex; counterattacks from a unit here are capped at 2 dice (before modifiers). |
| Fire | Units cannot enter; a unit already there when a fire token is placed cannot move, and cannot attack. |
| Devastation | Treated as a plains hex for both movement and combat; negates whatever terrain hex it's placed on. |

### Fire (Status Condition)
Every hex with a fire token resolves during each Regroup Phase: burnable terrain checks whether its fire level has reached its printed Burn Limit ("Burn X") and, if so, applies that terrain's burn effect (only some terrain types can burn at all); separately, any unit in a hex with a fire token must eliminate one figure per level of fire present (a commander eliminated this way is considered captured rather than killed). A hex's fire token can never exceed its terrain's Burn Limit in level. Units can neither enter nor leave a hex containing a fire token — except that an ordered unit may spend its entire turn (moving or attacking not at all) to extinguish and remove all fire tokens in its own hex. Terrain with a "Burn: None" limit (such as stone bridges) cannot have fire tokens placed on it at all. A level-four fire spreads during the Regroup Phase: draw a random directional token, consult the battle plan's directional indicator, and increase the fire level of the adjacent hex in that direction by one (placing a new level-one token there if it had none, or doing nothing further if it's already at four). Instead of attacking, any unit may attempt to remove all fire tokens in an adjacent hex by rolling one die: success requires a Green Shield for a 1-level fire, a Blue Shield for 2 levels, a Red Shield for 3 levels, or a Valor symbol for 4 levels.

### Skirmish (Optional Rule)
Instead of a battle plan, players can generate a random battle using a Skirmish Summary card (two are included: "Raid in the Riverlands" and "Westerlands Bounty") together with each House's deck of Skirmish Setup cards. Setup follows the Summary card's "Placement" side: choose a skirmish and Houses (any House pictured on the card, though not the same House twice); orient the board per its Board Indicator; select commanders (up to the card's stated maximum total Value Rating) simultaneously and secretly, then reveal; place the Round Track, token stockpiles, and momentum token as usual (per the Summary card); draw Setup cards equal to the Unit Rating to determine units, and equal to the Terrain Rating to determine terrain overlays, then place terrain, commanders, and remaining units within the distances the card specifies. After setup, flip the Summary card to its "Skirmish Details" side, which gives the Order Rating, Command Rating, Leadership Rating, and victory conditions for that skirmish, and leave it faceup for both players to reference.

### Combat Sequence Checklist
1. Declare attacker and target.
2. Verify range and LOS.
3. Place/replace engagement token if needed.
4. Roll attack dice; resolve shield hits (color must match target rank) and Valor hits (unless restricted).
5. Remove hit figures (banner bearer last); apply morale loss if the unit is eliminated.
6. Resolve retreats (1 hex per Morale result).
7. Rotate the attacker's banner to inactive.

### Steps of Battle Setup
1. Choose a Battle and a House to Play.
2. Assemble figures (first time only).
3. Take House components; place map board.
4. Take commanders; build the Leadership deck.
5. Place Round Track and Round Marker; assemble the morale track.
6. Create token stockpiles; place command tokens.
7. Form and place units (banners, then figures).
8. Assign the momentum token.

### Key Numbers
- Starting unit strength: 4 figures for infantry/ranged units; 3 for cavalry and Stark Kennelmasters.
- Commander zone-of-control (core game): 2 hexes.
- Attack dice by rank: green 2, blue 3, red 4 (before modifiers).
- Morale gain for eliminating an enemy unit: green +1, blue +2, red +3.
- 8 basic terrain hex types; 8 terrain-token types layered on top of hexes.
- Glossary highlights: **Active** = banner shows the current round's color, eligible to be ordered; **Controlled** = within a friendly commander's ZOC; **Engaged** = has an engagement token on a hex border; **Capture Rating** = hits needed in one attack to capture a lone commander.
