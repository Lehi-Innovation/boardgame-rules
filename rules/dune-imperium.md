## Overview

Dune: Imperium is a deck-building worker-placement game. Each player leads a Great House of the Landsraad: you play cards to send Agents to board spaces, use Persuasion to acquire new cards for your deck, recruit and deploy troops to fight over each round's Conflict card, and climb the Influence tracks of four Factions (Emperor, Spacing Guild, Bene Gesserit, Fremen), earning Alliances. Successes are measured in Victory Points. At the end of any round in which a player has reached 10 or more VP (or the Conflict Deck is empty), the game ends and whoever has the most Victory Points wins.

Each Leader has two unique abilities: one used during play as described on the Leader, and a Signet Ring ability activated when you play your Signet Ring card on one of your Agent turns. Note: cards of any type can override the usual rules of the game.

Solo and two-player games use House Hagal cards to run automated Rival opponents (see Special Rules & Edge Cases).

## Components

- Game board
- 8 Leaders (two each from four Great Houses), with a Turn Sequence guide on their backs. Leaders have 1-3 icons after their names; more icons = more strategically complex.
- 67 Imperium Deck cards
- 24 Reserve cards: 8 Arrakis Liaison, 10 The Spice Must Flow, 6 Foldspace
- 40 Intrigue cards
- 18 Conflict cards: 4 Conflict I, 10 Conflict II, 4 Conflict III
- Solari tokens: 4 large (worth 5), 20 small (worth 1)
- Spice tokens: 3 large (worth 5), 20 small (worth 1)
- 15 water tokens
- 4 Alliance tokens, First Player marker, Mentat (a freelance Agent pawn)
- 4 Baron Harkonnen tokens (used only with the Baron Vladimir Harkonnen Leader)
- Board Space Guide sheet; House Hagal rules sheet and 31 House Hagal cards (used only in solo and two-player games)
- Per player, in their color: 3 Agents, 3 Control markers, 1 Combat marker, a 10-card starting deck, 16 cubes (4 become Influence markers; the other 12 are your troops), 2 discs — 1 Score marker and 1 Councilor token

**Starting deck (10 cards, identical for all players):** Convincing Argument x2, Dagger x2, Dune the Desert Planet x2, Diplomacy, Reconnaissance, Seek Allies, Signet Ring.

## Setup

1. Place the game board. Place the Mentat on its side in the Mentat space, and the 4 Alliance tokens on the marked areas of the four Factions' Influence tracks.
2. Build a 10-card Conflict Deck on the board: shuffle the 4 Conflict III cards and place all of them face down; shuffle the 10 Conflict II cards and deal 5 face down on top of them; shuffle the 4 Conflict I cards and deal 1 face down on top. (Top to bottom: 1x Conflict I, 5x Conflict II, 4x Conflict III.) Return the unused Conflict cards to the box without looking at them.
3. Along the board edge: shuffle the Intrigue Deck and place it face down. Shuffle the Imperium Deck, place it face down, and deal 5 cards face up from it to form the Imperium Row. Place the Reserve cards in three stacks: Arrakis Liaison, The Spice Must Flow, and Foldspace.
4. Each player takes a Leader (chosen or random) and all components of one color. First-game recommendation: everyone uses a one-icon Leader (Paul Atreides, Glossu "The Beast" Rabban, Earl Memnon Thorvald, or Count Ilban Richese).
5. Each player places 2 Agents on their Leader and sets their third Agent (their Swordmaster) next to the game board. Shuffle your 10-card starting deck and place it face down in your supply, to the left of your Leader.
6. Each player places one of their two discs on the Score track: on the **1** space in a 4-player game, otherwise on the **0** space. Place your Combat marker (correct side face up) on the 0 space of the Combat track.
7. Each player places 4 cubes, one each, on the bottom spaces of the four Factions' Influence tracks. The other 12 cubes are your troops: place 3 in the circular garrison on the board closest to you, and the rest in your supply along with your remaining components, in clear view of all players.
8. Each player takes 1 water. Create a bank next to the board with the Solari, spice, and remaining water tokens (these are not limited; substitute if you run out).
9. Randomly determine a first player, who takes the First Player marker. Solo and two-player games require additional setup from the House Hagal rules sheet.

## Turn Structure

The game is played in rounds, each with five phases in this order: **1. Round Start, 2. Player Turns, 3. Combat, 4. Makers, 5. Recall.**

### Phase 1: Round Start
- Reveal a new Conflict card from the top of the Conflict Deck and place it face up next to the deck (on top of Conflict cards from previous rounds).
- Each player draws 5 cards from their own deck to form their hand for the round.

### Phase 2: Player Turns
Starting with the holder of the First Player marker and continuing clockwise, players take **one turn at a time**. On your turn, take either an **Agent turn** or a **Reveal turn**. Generally you take Agent turns until you run out of Agents, then take a Reveal turn — but Agent turns are optional: you may take your Reveal turn while you still have Agents left. Once you have taken a Reveal turn, your turns are skipped for the rest of the phase; the phase ends when all players have taken a Reveal turn.

You may play Plot Intrigue cards during this phase, at any point during one of your own Agent or Reveal turns.

**Agent turn** — play one card from your hand face up, using it to send one Agent from your Leader to an unoccupied board space:
- The board space must show (in its upper-left corner) an icon matching one of the Agent icons on the card. You must choose only one Agent icon on the card; one card can't send multiple Agents. A card with no Agent icons can't be played on an Agent turn — it can only be revealed during a Reveal turn.
- You can't send an Agent to a board space that already has an Agent on it.
- You must pay any costs and meet any requirements of the space. Costs are paid immediately, before resolving any effects of the space or the played card; if you can't pay, you can't send your Agent there.
- Gain the effects of the board space plus the effects in the card's **Agent box**, in any order (ignore the card's Reveal box). If the space belongs to a Faction, also advance your cube one space on that Faction's Influence track.
- Arrows on cards and spaces mean cost (left of / above the arrow) then effect (right of / below it). You are never forced to pay such a cost, but if you don't, you don't get the effect.
- **Recruiting:** each troop icon recruits one troop — take it from your supply and place it in your garrison. (If your supply is out of troops, you can't recruit more until some return.)
- **Deploying:** troops sitting in your garrison can't earn rewards. Only when you send an Agent to a **Combat space** may you deploy troops to the Conflict area (the center of the garrisons): any or all troops you recruited this turn (from both the board space and the played card), **plus up to two more troops from your garrison**. Recruited troops you don't deploy go to your garrison as usual.

**Reveal turn** — taken when you have no Agents left (or choose not to use remaining Agents). Four steps, in order:
1. **Reveal Cards:** reveal all cards remaining in your hand, face up, kept separate from cards you played on Agent turns.
2. **Resolve Reveal Effects:** gain the effects in the **Reveal boxes** of the cards you just revealed (not of cards played during Agent turns earlier this round), in any order. You may spend Persuasion to acquire new cards before, between, or after your Reveal effects (see Actions).
3. **Set Strength:** total your strength for this round's Combat — each of your troops in the Conflict is worth **2 strength**; each sword you revealed during your Reveal turn is worth **1 strength**. Troops in your garrison or supply contribute nothing. You must have at least one troop in the Conflict to have any strength: if your last troop is ever removed, your strength becomes 0 and can't be increased by any means, including swords. Announce your total and move your Combat marker to that space of the Combat track (if over 20, flip the marker to its +20 side and start again from the beginning of the track).
4. **Clean Up:** put all the cards in front of you (from your Agent and Reveal turns) into your discard pile.

Note: a Reveal turn has no troop-deployment step. Deployment happens on Agent turns at Combat spaces (or via specific card/Control effects).

### Phase 3: Combat
- **Combat Intrigue cards:** starting with the First Player and continuing clockwise, each player **with at least one troop in the Conflict** may play any number of Combat Intrigue cards, or pass. You are not required to pass just because you passed earlier in the phase. Once all players involved in Combat pass consecutively, resolve the Combat. If a card changes a player's troops in the Conflict (or otherwise alters strength), adjust their Combat marker — remember, with no troops in the Conflict your strength is 0. A few Combat Intrigue cards trigger "when you win a Conflict": play those only after you've won, before moving to the next phase.
- **Resolve Combat:** rewards from the Conflict card are given by strength as shown on the Combat track. Highest strength wins the Conflict and gains the top reward; second-highest gains the second reward; **in a 4-player game only**, third-highest gains the third reward. A player with 0 strength receives no reward.
- **Ties:** tied for first place — no one wins the Conflict; each tied player receives the **second** reward (in a 4-player game, if two players tie for first, the other two still compete for the third reward). Tied for second — each receives the third reward. Tied for third — nothing.
- Once all rewards are given, each player takes their troops from the Conflict and puts them in their **supply (not their garrison)**. Reset all Combat markers to 0.

### Phase 4: Makers
Check each of the three board spaces with a Maker icon: The Great Flat, Hagga Basin, and Imperial Basin. If a space has no Agent on it, place 1 spice from the bank on it as bonus spice, adding to any bonus spice already accumulated there from previous rounds.

### Phase 5: Recall
- If any player is at **10 or more Victory Points** on the Score track, or the **Conflict Deck is empty**, the Endgame is triggered (see Scoring / Victory Conditions).
- If no one has won, prepare the next round: return the Mentat to its designated space in the Landsraad (if it isn't already there); players recall their Agents, returning them to their Leaders; pass the First Player marker clockwise; begin a new round with Phase 1. (Exception: a player who took the Mentat as a Conflict reward keeps it as an Agent for the next round instead of returning it.)

## Actions

### Agent icons
Seven Agent icons appear on cards and board spaces: **Emperor, Spacing Guild, Bene Gesserit, Fremen, Landsraad, City, Spice Trade**. A card's icons determine where it can send your Agent — e.g., Sardaukar Legion's Emperor icon reaches either of the two Emperor spaces, while its Landsraad icon instead reaches one of the five Landsraad board spaces (the Mentat's designated space is in the Landsraad). Each Faction has one board space with a cost and one without.

### Board spaces
Sending an Agent to a Faction's space also gains 1 Influence with that Faction (included below).

Spaces without a Faction icon carry the Landsraad, City, or Spice Trade icon printed on the board. **Combat spaces** are the spaces featuring a desert illustration and crossed swords. Most are on the planet Dune itself; three appear with the Factions: Heighliner (Spacing Guild) and Hardy Warriors and Stillsuits (Fremen). The rulebook's examples also show Carthag and Imperial Basin as Combat spaces; Rally Troops is not one.

### Acquiring cards
Persuasion gained during a round is used during your **Reveal turn** to acquire new cards: any of the five cards in the Imperium Row, or Arrakis Liaison or The Spice Must Flow from the Reserve. (Foldspace is acquired only at the Foldspace board space.) A card's cost is shown at its top right. Acquire as many cards as you can afford; you may pool Persuasion from multiple sources (cards and board spaces) for one card, and may split Persuasion from a single source across different cards. Persuasion can never be saved — whatever you don't use during your Reveal turn is lost. Acquired cards go to your discard pile (face up, to the right of your Leader); you don't use them right away. The Imperium Row must always have five cards: refill it immediately from the Imperium Deck, so after acquiring a card you may also acquire its replacement if you can afford it. Some cards have an **Acquire box** beneath their cost: gain that effect one time, at the moment you acquire the card (not later when you play it).

### Trashing cards
Trashing permanently removes a card — from your hand, your discard pile, or in play — to the game box for the rest of the game (Reserve cards are returned to their Reserve stack instead). Trashing is optional unless it's paying a cost or a card directs you to trash itself.

### Intrigue cards
Intrigue cards can provide resources, Influence, or even Victory Points. You receive them primarily from three board spaces — Carthag, Conspire, and Secrets — plus card effects showing the Intrigue card icon. Keep them face down, separate from your deck; you may look at them at any time and reveal them only when played. After a played Intrigue card resolves, discard it face up next to the Intrigue Deck. Three types:
- **Plot** — play any time during one of your own Agent or Reveal turns.
- **Combat** — play only during Combat.
- **Endgame** — play only at the end of the game.

## Scoring / Victory Conditions

- Whenever you gain or lose a Victory Point, move your Score marker up or down one space on the Score track.
- **Game end trigger:** at the end of a round (Phase 5: Recall), if any player has reached **10 or more Victory Points**, or the **Conflict Deck is empty**, the game ends.
- **Endgame:** first, players may play and resolve any Endgame Intrigue cards they have. Then whoever has the most Victory Points is declared the winner.
- **Tiebreakers, in order:** amount of **spice**, then **Solari**, then **water**, then **garrisoned troops** (troops in your garrison on the board — not troops in your supply).
- **VP sources include:** Conflict card rewards; reaching 2 Influence with a Faction (1 VP each — lost again if you drop back below 2); Alliance tokens (worth the VP shown on the token, and transferable — see below); and card effects, notably Intrigue cards (including Endgame Intrigue at game end).

## Special Rules & Edge Cases

- **Faction Influence:** your cube starts at the bottom of each track. Sending an Agent to a Faction's board space gains 1 Influence with that Faction; other effects can move you up (or occasionally down). At **2 Influence** you gain a VP (lost if you drop back below 2). At **4 Influence** you earn the bonus shown on that space of the track; you do *not* give the bonus back if you later drop below 4, and you can even earn it again by dropping back and re-advancing.
- **Alliances:** the **first** player to reach 4 Influence with a Faction takes its Alliance token and gains the VP shown on it. If an opponent ever passes them by rising to a **higher** space on that track, the token (and its VP) transfers to that opponent.
- **Mentat:** a freelance Agent. Take it only from its space on the board (you can't take it from another player or another space), place it on your Leader, and use it as one of your Agents this round; return it to its Landsraad space during Recall. If you gain the Mentat as a Conflict reward, keep it as an Agent for the next round instead of returning it at the end of this one.
- **Swordmaster & High Council** are once-per-game spaces for each player (8 Solari for your third Agent; 5 Solari for a Council seat granting 2 Persuasion every Reveal turn).
- **Control:** some Conflict cards (titled for Arrakeen, Carthag, or Imperial Basin) reward control of that space. If you win such a Conflict, place a Control marker on the flag below that space (replacing any opponent's marker). While it's there, you receive the bonus whenever **any** player, including yourself, sends an Agent there: 1 Solari for Arrakeen or Carthag, 1 spice for Imperial Basin. **Defensive bonus:** when a Conflict card is revealed for a space you already control, you may deploy one troop **from your supply** to the Conflict.
- **Deck exhaustion:** any time you're unable to draw because your deck is empty, reshuffle your discard pile to form a new deck, then continue drawing. You can't reshuffle early — e.g., Paul Atreides' ability or Poison Snooper can't look at the top card of an empty deck.
- **Losing vs. retreating troops:** a **lost** troop returns to your supply (not your garrison); a **retreated** troop moves from the Conflict back to your garrison.
- **In play / Fremen Bond:** cards you play on Agent turns and reveal on your Reveal turn stay face up "in play" until your Clean Up (unless trashed first). Fremen Bond effects work if you have at least one *other* Fremen card in play; two Fremen Bond cards activate one another regardless of the order played.
- **Conditional icons:** Alliance-icon effects require holding that Faction's Alliance token; Influence-requirement icons need at least the shown Influence with that Faction. "Gain two Influence" effects can't be split between two Factions.
- **Steal Intrigue:** each opponent with four or more Intrigue cards must give you one, selected at random.
- **Rulebook clarifications:**
  - *Bindu Suspension:* play at the start of your Agent turn, before anything else; draw a card, then immediately end your turn.
  - *Calculated Hire:* can only take the Mentat from its starting position in the Landsraad.
  - *Demand Respect:* if you win a Conflict that rewards spice, you may take that spice first and use it to play this card (To the Victor… can likewise fund it).
  - *Kwisatz Haderach:* lets you take an Agent turn with no Agents left by sending an Agent already on a board space (even back to the same space). It can't reuse once-per-game spaces (High Council, Swordmaster) and can't overcome The Voice.
  - *Maker spaces:* if an Agent moves away from a Maker space and it's still empty in Phase 4, bonus spice does accumulate there.
  - *Test of Humanity:* opponents choose starting from your left, clockwise; each must choose something they can actually do (no discarding from an empty hand, no losing a deployed troop they don't have); a discarding opponent chooses the card — it isn't random.
  - *The Voice:* mark the chosen board space with one of your available Control markers and remove it on your next turn; it stops opponents (including via Kwisatz Haderach) from sending Agents there for one turn.
  - *Baron Vladimir Harkonnen (Masterstroke):* secretly choose two of the four Baron Harkonnen tokens and place them Faction-side down on your Leader; return the other two to the box unseen; reveal later as described to gain your reward.
- **Solo & two-player (House Hagal):** automated Rivals take **Agent turns only** (never Reveal turns). On a Rival's turn, reveal the top House Hagal card: the Rival sends an Agent to the revealed space if it's unoccupied (keep revealing past occupied spaces); reshuffle the House Hagal deck when it's empty or the Reshuffle card appears. Rivals ignore a space's normal costs and effects, resolving only their card's icons: Influence icons advance them on that track (no track bonuses, but they take an Alliance if able); each troop icon recruits 1 troop (deployed to the Conflict if the revealed space is a Combat space, otherwise garrisoned); the Signet Ring icon (solo only) uses their Leader's Signet ability. Whenever a Rival's Agent goes to a Combat space, it also deploys up to two troops from its garrison, even if the card recruited none. In Combat, each Rival with a troop in the Conflict reveals a House Hagal card and adds only its sword icons to its strength; you may then play Combat Intrigue cards before resolving. Harvest Spice cards follow their own instructions (removing bonus spice from the space — to the bank in two-player; into the Rival's supply, along with the base spice, in solo). **Two-player:** House Hagal is a third, spoiler Rival acting after each of the First Player's Agent turns while it has Agents; it doesn't collect resources, build a deck, earn rewards, or score VP, and can't claim Control — but if it wins a Conflict for a space another player controls, that Control marker is removed. Setup: remove the three Arrakeen cards marked "1P"; its cubes start at the bottoms of the Influence tracks; it starts with no garrisoned troops. **Solo:** you face two Rivals (they use only their Leaders' Signet abilities and can't be Paul Atreides or Helena Richese; remove House Hagal cards marked "2P"). They *do* take first- and second-place Conflict rewards — VP, Influence, resources and Intrigue cards (kept in their supply), Control (with its defensive bonus), and the Mentat — and trigger the game end if they reach 10+ VP. Whenever a Rival holds the resources indicated (per the House Hagal sheet), it spends them to earn a VP. The difficulty level sets your and the Rivals' extra starting resources, the Rivals' starting garrisoned troops, how many Conflict cards sit above the Rival Swordmasters in the Conflict Deck (when the Conflict card above them is revealed, both Rivals immediately gain their third Agents), and whether a 5 Solari token goes on the Mentat space (making it cost 5 Solari instead of 2; at Kwisatz Haderach difficulty you also can't gain a Swordmaster). The Rival on your left takes the First Player marker. When a Rival chooses Influence, it picks the Faction where it has the least (you decide ties and other choices). At Expert difficulty, Rivals won't deploy to a Conflict I or II they already lead by two or more troops, but deploy everything for a Conflict III.

## Player Reference

**Round:** 1. Round Start (reveal Conflict card; draw 5) → 2. Player Turns (Agent turns, then your Reveal turn) → 3. Combat → 4. Makers (1 bonus spice on each Agent-free Maker space) → 5. Recall (check 10 VP / empty Conflict Deck; return Mentat; recall Agents; pass First Player marker).

**Agent turn:** play 1 card with a matching icon → send 1 Agent to an unoccupied space → pay costs first → resolve space + Agent box in any order (+1 Influence at Faction spaces) → at a Combat space, may deploy this turn's recruits + up to 2 garrison troops.

**Reveal turn:** reveal remaining hand → resolve Reveal boxes + spend Persuasion on cards → set strength → discard all cards in play. (No troop deployment on a Reveal turn.)

**Strength:** troop in the Conflict = 2; revealed sword = 1; no troops in the Conflict = 0, no matter what.

**Combat rewards:** 1st = top reward, 2nd = second, 3rd = third (4-player games only); 0 strength = nothing. First-place tie: no winner, tied players take the second reward. Second-place tie: third reward each. Third-place tie: nothing. Troops then return to **supply**; Combat markers to 0.

**Influence:** 2 = 1 VP (lost if you drop below); 4 = track bonus (kept) + Alliance token for the first player there (transfers if someone rises higher).

**Costs:** Conspire 4 spice · Heighliner 6 spice · Selective Breeding 2 spice · Hardy Warriors 1 water · Hagga Basin 1 water · The Great Flat 2 water · Research Station 2 water · Sell Melange 2-5 spice · Rally Troops 4 Solari · High Council 5 Solari (once/game) · Swordmaster 8 Solari (once/game) · Mentat 2 Solari · Sietch Tabr requires 2+ Fremen Influence.

**Game end:** at end of round with any player at 10+ VP, or Conflict Deck empty → Endgame Intrigue cards → most VP wins. Ties: spice → Solari → water → garrisoned troops.

**Starting deck (10):** Convincing Argument x2, Dagger x2, Dune the Desert Planet x2, Diplomacy, Reconnaissance, Seek Allies, Signet Ring.
