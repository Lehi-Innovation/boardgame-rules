(1.0) INTRODUCTION

Devil’s Den is a company/battalion level simulation of the
Confederate assaults against the Union positions at Devil’s Den
and Little Round Top on the second day of the battle of Gettys-
burg. Basically, the Players will alternate moving pieces repre-
senting the forces involved over a map of the area in which the
battle took place. Officers will issue commands to the units of
their regiments to move and fight through small arms fire and
melee (hand-to-hand) combat. As units take losses, their combat
strengths will decline and their morale decrease. In addition,
units may run out of ammunition, become ineffective or fatigued,
etc. At the end of a certain number of turns, the game is over and
the winner is determined by comparing losses and the number of
geographical objectives occupied.

(2.0) EQUIPMENT
(2.1) Game Map

The game map pictures Devil’s Den, Little Round Top, Big
Round Top, and the surrounding areas on the left flank of the
Union “fishhook” position at Gettysburg. A hexagonal grid
pattern is printed on the game map to regulate the movement and
location of the pieces. There are many different types of terrain
on the map, including wooded hillsides, stone walls, boulders,
streams, and so forth (see Terrain Key on Map). The terrain will
affect the way units move and have combat.

(22.0) SCENARIOS

(2.11) The most striking feature on the map are the contour
lines which indicate the height of the ground through out the
battlefield. Every 20 feet in height is indicated by a colored
contour line running from dark brown (the lowest) to bright
yellow (the highest). All of the hexes between contour lines are
considered to be at the height of the Jower one. For example, a
hex such as 1336 between contour lines 520 and 540 is at height
level (elevation) 520. In order to give the map a more natural
appearance contour lines only roughly follow the hexsides to
which they are drawn.

(2.12) A hex containing both a stream (Plum Run) and a
rough symbol is treated as a rough hex.

(2.13) A hex that contains both a woods and rocky (boulder)
symbols is treated as a rocky hex for movement, Line of Sight,
stacking and melee purposes. Units defending against fire
combat in such a hex have their combat resolved on the woods
line of the Fire Combat Results Table, shifting one column to the
right for the boulders.

(2.2) Playing Pieces

Most of the playing pieces (“units”) represent battalions of
two companies each, organized into regiments. Other units
represent brigade commanders, regimental officers, cannons (in
two-gun sections) and artillery crews. Additional pieces (“mar-
kers’’) record information about the status of combat units
(disorganized, fixed bayonets, in column formation, etc.).

The Combat Strength of each unit is a single number, shown
on the unit’s counter. The Combat Strength is based on the
manpower of the unit, with each Strength Point representing 15
men for infantry and one gun for artillery. Thus an infantry unit
with a Combat Strength of 6 has approximately 90 men, while an
artillery section would have two guns. The Combat Strength also
determines the Fire and Melee Strength of a given unit, which
may be modified by many things. When the Combat Strength of
a unit is reduced in strength place a strength marker under
the unit with a value corresponding to the unit’s reduced
strength. For example, when the First unit of the 4th Maine
Regiment loses a Strength Point its Combat Strength is reduced
4, a “4” marker would be placed under the unit to indicate this.

--- Page 4 ---
As units take losses they may also become ineffective, at which
point they are flipped over to their reverse side. The First unit of
the 4th Maine, for example, would become ineffective when its
Strength was reduced to 3 or less.

(2.3) Game Scale

Each hex equals 30 yards of actual terrain, and the elevation
contours represent changes in height of 20 feet. A game turn
equals about eight minutes of real time. Each Strength Point
represents about 15 men.

(3.0) SEQUENCE OF PLAY

The game is played in sequenced turns called Game-Turns;
each Game-Turn consists of first a Confederate and then a Union
Player-Turn. A Player whose turn is in progress is called the
Phasing Player. All action takes place in the exact sequence
outlined.

1) CONFEDERATE PLAYER TURN

A) Command Phase

1) Replacement officers are placed in play.

2) All disorganized units may attempt to rally through
Spontaneous Rally: each ceases to be disorganized on a die roll
of “1.”

3) Each regimental officer checks to see which units of his
regiment are within his Command Radius. Units which are “‘out
of command” will be subject to severe movement restrictions and
other penalties.

4) Each brigade commander announces which regimental
officers or “out of command” units that are in his Command
Influence will receive a Command Bonus.

B) Activation Phase

During this Phase, each regimental officer tells the units of
his regiment that are In Command what he wants them to do by
issuing orders. Each order “costs” the officer a number of his
Command Points (C.P.). Commands should be carried out in the
following order:

1) RALLY. All disorganized units in command which did
not rally successfully through Spontaneous Rally in the Com-
mand Phase may do so now by having their officer expend 5 C.P.
per unit.

2) FORMATION CHANGES. Units may change for-
mation from column to line or vice versa at a cost of 3 C.P. each.
Artillery units may change formation according to Special rules.

3) ACTIVITY ORDERS. During this subphase a variety of
activities may be ordered at the cost of 3 C.P. each per unit. “TO

ARMS” orders are: remove or fix bayonets, gather ammunition,
volley fire or construct breastworks. “INCENTIVE ORDERS”
are. melee, charge, and force march. The appropriate marker
should be placed on each unit when it is ordered to perform an
activity.

4)INITIATION OF MOVEMENT. A unit may be ordered
to “move out” at a cost of 6 C.P. (Actual movement is done during
the Movement Phase). Units which have not been ordered to

move may change facing within their hex, or change order of
stacking, at a cost of 2 C.P.
C) Movement Phase

1) Each unit which has been ordered to move during the
Activation Phase now conducts its movement.

2) After all other movement has been completed, “Out of
Command” units may attempt to move by Random Movement.

D) Fire Combat Phase

1) DEFENSIVE FIRE: The non-Phasing Player may fire
his infantry and artillery fire against Enemy units. No volley fire
is permitted.

2) OFFENSIVE FIRE: The Phasing Player may fire his
artillery, infantry and sharpshooters against Enemy units. Units
which were ordered to volley fire during the Activity Phase do so.

E) Melee Phase

Hand-to-hand combat may be initiated by all of the Phasing
Player’s units which received an order to melee during the
Activity Phase. All units involved in a continuous melee must
counterattack.

2) UNION PLAYER TURN

The Union Player now exchanges roles with Confederate
Player and becomes the Phasing Player, carrying out Phases A
through E in the same sequence for his own units:

3) TURN MARKER ADVANCED

At the end of the Union player’s Game-Turn, the Game-Turn
marker should be advanced one space on the Turn Record Track
(located on the map), signalling the start of a new Game-Turn.
When the proper number of turns has been completed for the
scenario being played, the game is over and the victor is
determined. Players should keep track of which Player-Turn is in
progress by flipping the Game-Turn Marker over to the appro-
priate side as each Player-Turn begins.

(4.0) OFFICERS AND
COMMAND POINTS

General Rule:

The importance of leadership in battle is represented by the
Command Points (C.P.) given to the officers of each regiment. All
formation changes, activity orders, and activation require the
use of C.P. Regimental officers may only influence units of their
own regiment; brigade officers only those of their brigade, and
special officers may influence any Friendly Leader. A unit that
receives no Command Points from an officer may not perform
any of the functions listed on the Command Chart, but may
attempt to move by Random Movement.

(4.1) How Command Works

There are three types of officers in the game: regimental
officers, brigade officers, and special (divisional or higher)
officers. Regimental officers direct and guide the actual combat
units. Brigade officers increase the Command Point Total of
regimental officers and may also give orders directly to combat
units that are outside of the effective range of their officers.
Special officers may likewise increase the abilities of regimental
officers.

(4.11) Regimental officers have two ratings which determine
their ability to command; their Command Radius and Com-
mand Point Total.

Command Radius:

This is the maximum number of hexes that a unit may be
from its regimental officer and still receive his orders. For
example, if Union Colonel Walker (with a Command Radius of 5)
were in the hex 1031 he could command a unit of the 4th Maine

--- Page 5 ---
Regiment in hex 1530 but not one in hex 1629. Command Radius
may not be traced through hexes containing Enemy units or
Zones of Control, or hexes containing a Continuous Melee.

Command Point Total:

This is the maximum number of Command Points the officer
may expend during an Activity Phase. This total may be
increased by the presence of brigadeor special officers, however.

(4.12) Brigade officers have a Command Influence Allow-
ance through which they may give a “bonus” of either 1), eight
Command Points to any regimental officer of that brigade that is
within three hexes of the officer; or 2), expend up to eight
Command Points directly on any unit of that brigade that is
outside of the Command Radius of its regimental officer and
stacked with or adjacent to the brigade officer. The brigade
officer’s Command Influence Allowance is the number of regi-
mental officers and/or units which he may assist in a given
Player-Turn. (Command Influence simulates the increased
effectiveness a brigade commander could bring to his regimental
officers by conferring with them, as well as the occasional
practice of brigade commanders to personally lead small bodies if
troops.)

(4.13) Each army has a special officer who is present in
certain scenarios—Major General Warren, Chief of Engineers of
the Army of the Potomac, and Major General John B. Hood,
commander of the attacking Confederate division. A special
officer may give a Command Influence Bonus to any number of
regimental officers who are within three hexes of him during the
Command Phasé. Special officers may not directly command
combat units—only regimental officers.

(4.14) A regimental officer may never have his Command
Point Total increased by more than eight Command Points in a
given Player-Turn.

(4.15) Once a unit is determined to be “In Command” during
the Command Phase, it remains so until the beginning of the
next Friendly Command Phase.

(4.16) Units which are Out of Command have an “Out of
Command” marker placed on them and remain so until the
beginning on the next Friendly Command Phase. Out of
Command units may attempt to receive Movement Points
through Random Movement (see 5.8).

(4.17) Certain units do not function under the command of
regimental officers, but instead have their own Command Point
values. Artillery crews have a Command Point value of eight (8),
while units of the 2nd U.S. Sharpshooter regiment have a
Command Point value of ten (10). Confederate sharpshooters (see
19.1) function entirely without Command Points. All such units
may never be Out of Command.

(4.2) Rules for Officers

(4.21) Each officer has a Movement Allowance of 12 when
activated. Regimental officers must expend six of their Com-
mand Points in order to activate themselves; brigade and special
officers are always activated and do not need to expend
Command Points for this purpose.

(4.22) Officers do not count for stacking, nor do they have
Zones of Control, facing or formations. When alone in a hex they
do not block Line of Sight and may not be a target for Off-Map
artillery.

(4.3) Officers and Morale

All units stacked with an officer have one subtracted from
all die rolls when checking Morale. (There is no additional effect
for having more than one officer in the hex.)

(4.4) Officers and Melee

(4.41) When present in a hex where melee is being resolved
most officers will add one Strength Point to the total Melee
Strength of all units in the hex. The following officers are the
only ones who do not add such a bonus to their units: Lt. Col.
Welch of the Union 16th Michigan Regiment, Col. Jackson of the
Confederate 47th Alabama Regiment (both were incompetent),

and all replacement Captains rated 30-3). There is no additional
effect for having more than one Friendly officer in a hex.

(4.42) When a regimental officer is involved in Continouos
Melee his Command Radius is reduced by one hex and his
Command Point Total is halved (round fractions down). A
brigade or special officer involved in a Continuous Melee may
only influence units in the same hex as himself.

(4.5) Officers and Rally

(4.51) During each Friendly Activity Phase a regimental
officer may rally any disorganzed unit of his command that is
within three hexes of him at the cost of five Command Points per
unit. Brigade and special officers may rally any unit of their
command that they are stacked with or adjacent to, at a cost of
one Command Influence Point.

(4.52) Officers involved in Continuous Melee may only rally
units with which they are stacked.

(4.6) Officers and Ineffective Units

An ineffective unit must be stacked with either its regimen-
tal, brigade, or special officer in order to enter an Enemy Zone of
Control without undergoing a Morale check, or in order to
initiate a melee attack.

(4.7) Officer Casualties

Any time an officer is stacked with a unit that suffers a
Strength Point loss from Enemy melee or fire combat, there is a
possibility that the officer will become a casualty. When this
occurs the owning Player should immediately roll two dice and
consult the appropriate Officer Casualty Table.

(4.71) If an officer is alone in a hex he may be fired on as if
he were an infantry unit. Any result of 1 or 2 means that the
officer must roll on the Casualty Tables.

(4.72) At the end of any Melee Phase an officer that is alone
in a hex containing an Enemy infantry unit is automatically
considered to be captured. Note that you must still initiate melee
to enter the hex of an Enemy officer, even if no other Enemy units
are in the hex. Officers may also become casualties as a result of
normal melee combat (see 4.73).

(4.8) Battlefield Promotions

When an officer becomes a casualty he is immediately
removed from the map and his loss is noted for purposes of
Victory Points. A replacement officer is placed on the map during
the next Friendly Command Phase. The first replacement of a
regimental officer is on the flip side of the original officer counter.
If this officer in turn becomes a casualty, an anonymous
replacement officer is used. The last available replacement is an
anonymous 30-3 Captain counter; if this captain also becomes a
casualty he is replaced by another captain with equal command
values. It is suggested that Players keep careful track of leader
casualties, especially if it becomes necessary to “recycle” replace-
ment 30-3 Captain counters.

(4.81) A replacement officer must be placed with any unit of
his command that is not currently in an Enemy Zone of Control
or involved in a Continuous Melee. Replacement officers must be
placed on units within the following priorities:

1) Any combat unit that is effective & organized

2) Any disorganized unit

3) Any ineffective unit

4) Any unit that is both disorganized and ineffective

Within the above priorities, replacement officers may be
placed as the owning Player desires. If for some reason a
replacement officer is not able to appear as scheduled, he appears
at the beginning of the next Friendly Command Phase in which
he can be placed on the map.

(4.82) A brigade officer who becomes a casualty has his
counter flipped over to the “Brigade Staff” side. In the next
Friendly Command Phase the Brigade Staff Counter is placed in

--- Page 6 ---
the hex occupied by the replacement brigade officer. The new
brigade commander will be the highest rated (in Command
Points) surviving original regimental officer; if all of the original
regimental officers are also casualties there will be no brigade
commander replacement and the Brigade Staff unit is removed
from the game. Note that a regimental officer who assumed
command of a brigade must be replaced by a new regimental
officer.

Example: If Brigadier Gen. Weed became a casualty, he
would be replaced by Col. O’Rorke of the 140th N.Y. Regiment
(the highest rated regimental officer of the brigade); the 140th
N.Y. would then be commanded by Lt. Col. Ernst. If two or more
regimental officers have equally high command ratings the
owning Player may choose which one he wishes to the new
brigade commander.

(4.83) A Brigade Staff unit functions in the same manner as
a brigade officer, but has a reduced Command Influence Allow-
ance. Should the Brigade Staff unit become a casualty it is again
replaced, following the procedure in 4.82. The identity of the new
brigade officer should be noted on a piece of paper to note which
regimental officer was promoted and is represented by the Staff.

(4.84) There is no replacement for Major-General Warren if
he becomes a casualty. If Major-General Hood becomes a
casualty the Hood Staff Unit enters the game, in the same
manner as a Brigade Staff unit. The replacements for Hood (in
order of seniority) are Law, Robertson and Benning. The senior
surviving Brigade officer should assume command of the divi-
sion; if Law, Benning and Robertson are all casualties there will
be no replacement division commander and the Hood Staff unit
is removed from play. Note that a replacement “chain reaction”
will occur “through the ranks” as a brigade commander assumes
divisional command and in turn is replaced as in 4.82. The Hood
Staff unit has the same powers as the Hood officer counter.

(5.0) MOVEMENT

General Rule:

During each Movement Phase the Phasing Player may move
as many or as few units as he wishes. In order to move, all units—
except those with independent command—must first be acti-
vated by their officers during the Activity Phase. Movement is
then carried out during the Movement Phase by having each
activated unit expend Movement Points from its Movement
Allowance. The number of Movement Points expended for each
hex entered or hexside crossed depends on the moving unit’s
current formation and the nature of the terrain. Units which are
Out of Command may also attempt to move by Random
Movement.

Procedure:

Units are moved one at a time, tracing a path hex-by-hex
across the map. As a unit enters a hex or crosses a hexside it must
expend the Movement Points (M.P.) cost listed for that action in
the Movement Chart. NOTE: For ease of play, Players should
complete all movement for one unit before going on to the next,
and complete all the movement for one regiment before moving
units of another regiment. Units are always moved one at a time;
however, officers may be moved along with any unit that they
are stacked with, and artillery units may be moved along with
the units that are moving them.

(5.1) Movement Point Allowance

Once activated, each unit has a number of Movement Points
that it may expend in the following Movement Phase. This
Movement Point Allowance is not printed on the counters, but
varies with the type of unit, as follows:

Officers and Artillery Crew 12 MP.
Infantry 10 M.-P.
2nd U.S. Sharpshooters 12 MP.

Under certain conditions a unit may increase its Movement
Allowance through Forced March. Confederate sharpshooters
and units which are Out of Command have their own special
movement rules (see 5.75 and 5.8).

(5.11) Movement Point Cost Chart
(See Charts and Tables)

(5.2) Movement Restrictions

(5.21) Movement may never take place out of sequence. A
Player’s units may only be moved during his own Movement
Phase. A unit may be forced to move involuntarily (retreat) as a
result of fire or melee combat. This is not considered movement
and does not require the use of any Movement or Command
Points.

(5.22) A unit may enter a hex containing an enemy unit only
during a Friendly Melee Phase.

(5.23) Units must stop upon entering a hex containing a
rigid Zone of Control (see 9.0).

(5.24) Movement from hex to hex must be consecutive; units
may not “skip” hexes.

(5.25) Movement Points may not be accumulated from Phase
to Phase or be transferred from unit to unit. Any Movement
Points not used during a given Movement Phase are perman-
ently lost.

(5.26) Units that are forced to exit the map because of retreat
or other causes are simply removed from play. The cost to exit the
map is equal to the cost to enter the mapedge hex that the unit is
currently occupying. Units that exit the map do not count for
Victory Point purposes. Players may voluntarily exit their units
from the map, but such units may not again enter play.

(5.3) Effects of Facing

Unless moving backwards (see 5.4) units may only enter
their center front hex (see 7.0) and thus may have to adjust their
facing accordingly.

(5.4) Moving Backwards

Under certain conditions units may “back out” of a hex; that
is, move directly into their center rear hex instead of their
center front hex.

(5.41) Units may not move backwards into a hex containing
an Enemy Zone of Control (of any type), regardless of the
presence of other Friendly units in the hex.

(5.42) A unit may not move backwards into a rocky hex, or
across stonewall, fence or breastwork hexsides.

(5.43) Artillery units may back out of a hex only if they are

(5.44) A unit in an Enemy Zone of Control (of any type) may

The unit would still be vulnerable to Withdrawal Fire (see 9.2).

(5.5) Charge

A unit in Line Formation (see 6.1) that receives a Melee order
may increase its melee potential (see 11.6) by also receiving a
Charge order during a Friendly Activity Phase. Place a Charge
marker on such units to indicate both orders.

(5.51) A unit may charge an Enemy unit only if the target
unit is in the Line of Sight of the officer who ordered the charge at
the moment the required Command Points were expended.

(5.52) A charging unit must conduct a Melee attack at the
end of its movement, regardless of the actual Melee odds at the
moment of combat. If the target Enemy unit is no longer in the
hex in which it began the Movement Phase the charging unit
must still advance into the vacant hex during the Melee Phase.

(5.53) A charging unit may only fire through its center front
hex, and has its Fire Strength halved (round fractions down).

(5.54) A charging unit may not conduct Volley Fire.

(5.55) Charging units have their strength for melee incre-
ased by one, and must undergo a special morale check after the

(5.56) A charging unit does not add the strength bonus for

(5.57) Units may not charge in two consecutive Game-Turns.

(5.58) A unit that charges has the option of Pursuit (see 11.6).

(5.6) Forced March

A unit that is in column formation (see 6.1) may have its
Movement Allowance increased by four (4) Movement Points by
conducting a forced march. In order to force march, a unit must

units using forced march must check morale, adding one to their

(5.61) Units using forced march may not enter rocky,
woods/rough or stream hexes; cross stonewall, fence or breast-

(5.62) A unit may force march for any number of consecutive

(5.63) Units of the 2nd U.S. Sharpshooter Regiment (see

(5.7) Independent Units

Certain units in the game may function without receiving

the 2nd US Sharpshooters have their own automatic C.P.
Allowance per Turn. They may use these C.P. to initiate
movement or for any other purpose requiring C.P. expenditure,
but they may not “loan” C.P. to other units.

(5.71) Each Union artillery crew unit automatically
receives 12 Movement Points per Movement Phase. (Confederate
artillery units and crews are off-map and do not move.) Union

artillery crews move as infantry units. They may not force march
or initiate melee.

(5.72) Artillery units (also called “cannons” and “guns”)
may only be moved when stacked with crew or infantry units.
Any crew or infantry unit can drag a gun by hand by paying the

units being dragged by hand may not enter rocky hexes, nor

more than one hex per Phase when moving into or through
woods/rough hexes.

(5.73) Limbered artillery has no Movement Allowance of its
own and may only move when stacked with a crew unit.
Limbered artillery moves at the cost listed for it on the Movement
Point Cost Chart. It may not move backwards, nor into rocky or
woods/rough hexes, nor across stonewall, fence or breastwork
hexsides.

(5.74) The troops of the 2nd U.S. Sharpshooter regiment
were specially trained to function independently as advanced
skirmishers. When operating outside of Command Radius of

capable of force marching while in line formation (see 5.63).

(5.75) The Confederate Player may bring into play four

up to a maximum of 6 hexes, regardless of terrain. Whe outside

(5.8) Random Movement

Units that are Out of Command may attempt to move
through the Random Movement procedure. This will result in the
unit advancing, holding its ground, or retreating.

PROCEDURE:

After all other movement has been completed, a Player may
attempt to move units through Random Movement. He should
roll one die for each unit in question and consult the Random
Movement Chart. A unit that receives a result of advance has a
Movement Allowance of eight (8) Movement Points and may be
moved normally. A hold result means that no movement may be
attempted, although the unit is permitted to adjust its facing. A
retreat result means that the unit must retreat according to the
priorities of Case (15.0), until eight Movement Points (or as close
as possible) have been expended. After this movement is
completed the unit is not disorganized.

(5.81) A unit that begins the Friendly Command Phase “In

Command” but is not activated to move during the Activity

(6.0) FORMATIONS
GENERAL RULE:

All combat units may be in one of two different formations.
Infantry units may be in either line or column; artillery units are
either limbered or unlimbered. Officers and CSA sharpshooters
have no formations and are not affected by the following rules.

PROCEDURE:

A unit may be in only one formation at any given time. Units
may change formation during any Friendly Activity Phase, ata
cost of three (3) Command Points.

(6.1) Infantry

Infantry units must be in one of two possible formations:
either Line or Column. A unit’s formation affects its ability to
move and engage as follows:

LINE: .

Units in line are in regular battle formation. They may
conduct fire combat, initiate melee and/or charge normally.
Unless otherwise marked, infantry units are considered to be in
line formation.

COLUMN:

Units in column are in marching formation. They move
faster than when in line, but may not conduct fire combat,
initiate melee, or charge. They may force march, however. If any
combat result other than no effect is achieved against a
defending unit in column, it is immediately disorganized and
placed in line formation, regardless of any other combat result.
Units that are in column are noted by placing an In Column
marker on them. (In cases where there are long lines of troops in
column formation it is often necessary only to put a “column”
marker on the lead unit.)

(6.2) Artillery

Artillery units must be in either limbered or unlimbered
formation. An artillery unit may change its facing only when
stacked with a crew unit. An artillery unit attempting to change
formation may be prevented from doing so by Enemy fire (see
6.22). An artillery unit’s formation affects its ability to move and
engage in combat as follows:

LIMBERED:

Limbered units are hitched to horses. They may move
normally, but may not fire. Flip the unit over to its limbered side
to indicate this.

UNLIMBERED:
Unlimbered units are in position to fire. They may fire
normally, but may only move if dragged by hand.

(6.21) Artillery crews move and function as infantry, and
must either be in line or column formation. They may only fire
their artillery while in line formation.

(6.22) Any artillery unit that attempts to change formation
while in the Line of Sight of Enemy units within six hexes may
immediately undergo fire from those units prior to changing
formation. The Enemy units may combine their fire normally. If
the attack is successful (i.e. achieves any result other than no
effect) the indicated combat result is applied and the artillery
may not change formation. Any other result and the artillery
may change formation and move or fire normally.

(6.23) An artillery unit may not be moved in a Player-Turn in
which it unsuccessfully attempted to change formation.

(8.0) STACKING

(More than one Unit in a Hex)

A Friendly unit may never move through Enemy units nor
stack with Enemy unit(s) unless to Melee. Units from different

unit in one type of formation may not be stacked with an

are an exception to this and may stack with either infantry or
other artillery in different formations.

No more than three Friendly units may be stacked in a hex at
the end of any Phase. There is an additional restriction in the
number of Strength Points that may be stacked in one hex; this
varies with the terrain in the hex and is detailed in 8.2. Officers,
Confederate Sharpshooters, artillery guns without crews, and

All friendly units in the same hex must end a Phase faced

(8.4) Effects On Combat

(8.41) Stacked infantry have their total combat strengths
treated as one unit. The units may combine their fire.

(8.43) Normally only the top unit in the hex takes losses

(8.44) Officers in a hex suffering combat losses will always

(8.5) Order of Stacking

When a Friendly unit enters a hex already containing one or
more Friendly units, the owning Player may place any of the

during a Friendly Activation Phase by having an officer expend

(9.0) ZONES OF CONTROL (zoo

Infantry units in line formation and unlimbered artillery
stacked with crews extend a Zone of Control into the three hexes
adjacent to their frontal hexsides. Being in an Enemy ZOC does
not force combat; all combat, except for continuous melee, is
voluntary. Officers, units that are out of ammunition, crewless

artillery, units in column formation, and information counters do
not have a ZOC.



(9.1) ZOC(s) And Movement

(9.11) All infantry units and artillery units capable of fire
combat exert a rigid ZOC; an enemy unit must stop all
movement upon entering such a hex.

(9.12) Ineffective infantry units, infantry in column for-
mation, and artillery units unable to fire exert a non-rigid
ZOC; an enemy unit may move into and through a non-rigid
ZOC but may suffer withdrawal fire for doing so (see 9.2).

(9.13) A unit that begins a Movement Phase in an Enemy
ZOC (of any kind) may leave that hex, but may suffer with-
drawal fire for doing so (see 9.2).

(9.2) Withdrawal Fire

Ifa unit is in the ZOC of an Enemy unit, and wishes to leave
that hex, the enemy unit may fire at the moving unit before it
leaves.

(9.21) Withdrawal fire is in addition to any normal defensive
or offensive fire. Units may fire withdrawal fire any number of
times, subject to the ammunition depletion rules. A unit may not
fire volley fire during Withdrawal Fire.

(9.22) If there are two or more Friendly infantry units in a
hex that is in an Enemy ZOC, any one unit may leave the hex
without suffering withdrawal fire. The ensuing withdrawal fire
will then be conducted against the unit that remains in the hex.
However, if the Enemy unit that moves out of the hex exits
directly into another Enemy ZOC, the unit that moved would
receive the Withdrawal Fire.

(9.23) If an officer leaves a hex containing one or more
Friendly units that is in an enemy ZOC, he does not undergo
Withdrawal Fire. If a leader is alone in a hex that is in an Enemy
ZOC and he leaves that hex, he does suffer withdrawal fire (see
Case 4.7).

(9.24) A unit that suffers casualties during withdrawal fire
acts as if it had suffered casualties in any other Phase; it may be
required to check morale, or roll for officer casualties, etc.

(9.25) If a unit suffers a disorganized result in withdrawing
it may move one hex and then becomes disorganized.

(9.26) Withdrawal fire occurs only during the Movement
Phase. A unit that is in the ZOC of an Enemy unit in the Fire
Combat Phase and is forced to retreat from that hex does not
undergo withdrawal fire. Units that advance to melee ormove in
pursuit after a charge, do not undergo withdrawal fire, even
if they leave or pass through an Enemy ZOC.

(9.3) Retreat Fire

A unit that is forced to retreat into the Zone of Control of an
Enemy unit other than the unit which caused the retreat may
undergo retreat fire. The procedure is the same as for With-
drawal Fire (9.2) except that units entering the Zone of Control
trigger the fire. After undergoing the fire the retreating unit will
continue moving until its retreat requirements are fulfilled.
Depending on the combat result the unit may be forced to
undergo further morale checks and possibly have to retreat further.

(9.4) Effects on Command Radius

An officer who is attempting to trace a Command Radius
from himself to one of the units under his command may not
trace this path through an Enemy ZOC unless that hex is also
occupied by a Friendly combat unit.

(10.0) FIRE COMBAT
GENERAL RULE:

All infantry and artillery units may fire their weapons at
Enemy units in accordance with the rules of range, Fire Combat,
Line of Sight and Ammunition. There is both offensive and
defensive fire within each Player-Turn, so that each side gets an
opportunity to fire at least once per Player-Turn. The results of all
fire combat are determined on the Fire Combat Results Table
(CRT). Confederate sharpshooters and Off-Map artillery are an
exception to this, and conduct their fire combat according to
their own special rules (see 18.0 and 19.1).

PROCEDURE:

First check the Range Chart to determine the Fire Strength
of the type of weapon being used at the range in hexes the unit
will fire. The total number of Strength Points being fired at the
defending unit should then be found on the Fire Combat Results
Table, and cross-indexed with the type of terrain the defending
unit is in. It is possible that this column may be adjusted left or
right depending on the type of fire, terrain features, position and
formation of the defending unit, etc. After all column adjust-
ments are made, the Attacking Player rolls two dice, consults the
proper line of the CRT and applies the result. Fire Combat may
cause the defending unit to lose Strength Points (and so be
forced to undergo a morale check), or to become Disorganized.
Off-Map artillery units conduct combat according to a completely
different procedure (see 18.2). Except for Disorganization results
and the situation given in Case 8.43 only the top unit in a stack
suffers fire casualties.

(10.1) Restrictions

(10.11) In order to fire, a unit does not have to be activated,
receive Command Points, or be in the Command Radius of its
officer. A unit may fire at any Enemy unit that is within its Line
of Sight, weapons range and Field of Fire.

(10.12) Each unit has a maximum range at which it may fire
at an Enemy unit. This range is found on the Range Chart for
the appropriate unit type (small arms or artillery). No unit may
fire at an Enemy unit that is outside this maximum range. Range
is calculated by counting the distance in hexes from the firing
hex to the target hex. The firing unit’s hex is not counted as part
of this range, but the target hex is counted. Range also helps
determine how effective a unit’s fire will be, depending on the
weapon type of the firing unit. All infantry units, with the
exception of the 2nd U.S. Sharpshooters, are armed with rifles.
The U.S. Sharpshooters have Carbines. Artillery crews and
officers have no weapons, and so are not permitted to conduct
small arms fire combat.

(10.13) Units may fire only through their frontal hexsides.
Units in column formation, units that are out of ammunition,
and units with fixed bayonets may not fire.

(10.14) Each unit (or stack of units) may be fired on only once
per Combat Phase by each type of weapon (infantry small arms
and artillery). The order in which the separate “types” of fire are
resolved is determined by the firing Player. Confederate sharp-
shooters may fire at any Union unit, regardless of the number of
times it has already been fired on by other weapon types (see
Case 19.1).

(10.15) No unit may fire more than its allotted strength in
any single Fire Combat Phase. A unit may fire only once per
Phase, unless conducting Withdrawal Fire.

(10.16) A unit that is adjacent to any Enemy unit is free to
fire at the adjacent Enemy unit, or at a target farther away, as
the owning Player desires (within the limits of Range, Line of
Sight, Fields of Fire and Split Fire).

(10.17) A unit may fire its full strength only through its
center front hex. It may fire no more than half of its strength
through either remaining frontal hex (round fractions down).

(10.2) Split Fire (Firing at
More Than One Hex)

A unit or stack of units is permitted to “split” its fire so that
the fire issues from more than one of its frontal hexsides; each
fire is resolved separately. Thus it is possible for a unit (or stack
of units) to divide its fire into a maximum of three separate
attacks on three separate target hexes.

(10.21) A unit may not split fire through the same hexside.

(10.22) None of the target units of split fire may be more
than six hexes away from the firing unit.

(10.23) Split fire with an adjusted Fire Strength of less than
one has no effect.

(10.3) Multiple Unit Fire

Fire by one or more infantry units against the same target
hex is permitted only in the cases outlined below. Do not confuse
multiple unit fire (more than one firing unit combining their Fire
Strengths to attack the same target unit) with Split Fire (one
firing unit attacking more than one defending unit in separate
attacks). Within the restrictions of 10.2 and 10.3 split fire may be
combined with multiple unit fire.

(10.31) Firing Units of the Same Regiment:

Units of the same regiment may combine their fire against
the same Enemy target unit under the following conditions:

(1) All of the firing units are stacked together and/or in
adjacent hexes a hex adjacent to at least one of the other firing
units; or

(2) All of the firing units are adjacent to the target Enemy
unit; or

(3) All of the firing units are currently within the Command
Radius of their regimental officer and conducting volley fire.

(10.32)Units From Different Regiments

Units from different regiments may combine their fire under
the following conditions:

(1) All the firing units are adjacent to the target unit; or

(2) Units of one regiment are firing at the frontal hex(es) of
the target unit, while the units of the other regiment are firing at
the rear hex(es) of the target unit, and the range of all the fire is
six hexes or less.

(10.33) Units that are ineffective and/or disorganized may
only combine fire with other units that they are stacked with.
They may not combine with units in other hexes.

(10.4) Volley Fire

Volley Fire permits a unit to increase its Fire Strength at the
risk of running out of ammunition.

PROCEDURE:

A unit that intends to conduct volley fire must be ordered to
do so during the Friendly Activation Phase at a cost of 3C.P. per
unit. Each unit so ordered should be marked with a Volley Fire
Marker. The volley fire is then conducted during the Fire Combat
Phase. Use of volley fire gives the firing unit a one column shift
to the right on the Fire Combat Results Table when its combat is
resolved. However, a unit that conducts volley fire increases its
chances of running out of ammunition (see 16.0).

(10.41) Units using volley fire may not combine fire with
units that are not using volley fire. They may combine with units
using Rapid Fire.

(10.42) Units that are Disorganized and/or ineffective
may not conduct volley fire. A unit that had received a Volley
Fire order and them became disorganized may not use Volley
Fire (it may fire normally, however).

(10.43) If units are stacked together and not all of them
receive an order to conduct volley fire, only those units using
volley fire may fire that Phase; the units in the stack that are not
conducting volley fire may not fire.

(10.44) When a unit has been ordered to conduct volley fire,
it must carry out the order during the next Offensive Fire Phase.
When the volley fire has been completed, the volley fire marker

(10.45) Volley fire may not be conducted as part of Defensive

(10.5) Line of Sight (LOS)

The Line of Sight represents a unit’s ability to see a potential
target for fire combat. The heavily wooded terrain around Big
and Little Round Tops allows a maximum LOS of two hexes (60
yards); while the cleared area from Devil’s Den to the western
slopes of Little Round Top allows a Line of Sight limited only by
blocking terrain. The Line of Sight is considered to be a
straight line drawn from the center of the viewing unit’s hex to
the center of the target units’s hex.

(10.51) General Rules:

(1) A unit always has a LOS to any Enemy unit that is
adjacent to one of its frontal hexsides.

(2) The LOS is always blocked by an intervening hex that is
at a higher elevation than both the target and firing unit.
Example: A unit in hex 1033 may not see a unit in hex 1031.

(3) The Line of Sight may be traced into, but not through, a
rocky hex.

(4) Stonewall, fence and breastwork hexsides have no effect
on the Line of Sight.

(5) Blocking terrain consists of any combat unit or rocky hex,
along the Line of Sight. When viewing along the same height
level the LOS may be traced into but not through such a hex.
When viewing “downhill” (i.e. to a hex at a lower level) a
blocking terrain hex interrupts the Line of Sight if it lies half-way
or closer to the target unit. When viewing “uphill” (i.e. to a hex at
a higher level) a blocking terrain hex interrupts the LOS if it lies
half-way or closer to the firing unit.

(6) Crest hexsides completely block the Line of Sight.
However units adjacent to a crest hexside may see and be seen
normally.

(7) If a Line of Sight runs along a “blocking” hexside (e.g.
from hex 1924 to hex 1724) the LOS is not blocked. Likewise, if
the LOS runs along a hexside where a change in height occurs
(e.g. from hex 1520 to hex 1720) use the height that does not block
the Line of Sight.

(10.52) LOS and Woods

The maximum LOS for any unit whose first hex viewed is a
woods hex is two hexes (60 yards). Therefore a unit in a clear
hex adjacent to a woods hex can see only two hexes; likewise, a
unit whose first hex viewed is woods can only see two hexes, even
if all the more distant hexes in the LOS are clear. When viewing
from a non-woods hex into a woods hex from a hex not adjacent
to the woods (e.g. from hex 2537 to 2535) the LOS extends only
into the first woods hex.

(10.53) Woods and Elevations:
Elevation changes in the woods affect the LOS as follows:

(1) A unit may always trace a LOS normally (see 10.52) toa
unit across one contour line (i.e. to the next height level uphill or
down);

(2) May trace a LOS normally to any unit across two contour
lines, but have their Fire Combat Strength halved (round
fractions down) when firing at a such a unit;

(3) May always see and fire normally to units at the same
height level.

(10.54) LOS and Clear Hexes:

In viewing the portions of the map without woods Players
should visualize two basic areas:

(1) The area from the West mapedge to the crest hexsides on
top of Houck’s Ridge near the Devil’s Den; and

(2) The “Valley of Death” between the crest and hilltop near
the Devil’s Den to the crest hexsides and top of Little Round Top.
Except for the hexes around hex 1831 units may not see “from
one side of the hill to another,” i.e. across crest hexsides or the
highest (560) level of Houck’s Ridge. For example, a unit in hex
1333 could not see (or be seen) by any unit East of the 0032
hexrow, except those adjacent to the crest hexsides of Houck’s
Ridge.

(10.6) LOS & Fire Combat

A unit’s potential Field of Fire is the 180° arc traced from
its three frontal hexsides. When firing, the actual Field of Fire is
the 60° arc traced from the actual firing frontal hexside through
which the fire is coming. To determine if a target unit can be fired
upon, a Line of Fire is used. This line is drawn from the center
of the firing frontal hexside to the target unit’s hex and must
pass through one of the target’s frontal hexsides (within the 60°
Arc). This may not occur in hexes bisected by the 60° Arc,

depending on the facing of the target unit.

(11.0) MELEE COMBAT

Melee combat represents hand-to-hand fighting at the closest
possible range—within a single hex. Melee combat takes place
during the Melee Phase, between units stacked in the same hex.

PROCEDURE: .

In order to conduct a Melee, a unit must receive a Melee
Command during a Friendly Activation Phase (at a cost of 3
C.P.). The unit is then eligible to conduct melee combat with any
enemy unit(s) that are in its center front hex at the beginning of
the Melee Phase. During the Melee Phase the unit moves into the
hex that contains the Enemy unit. The Melee Strength of both
sides is then compared, and the resulting combat differential is
used to resolve the combat by means of the Melee Combat Results
Table. For example, if the attacking unit has two more Strength
Points than the defending unit, it attacks with a differential of
+2. As a result of the Melee, units may lose Strength Points,
become disorganized, undergo morale check, retreat, or simply

11

--- Page 12 ---
12

remain in the hex. Officers may become casualties and artillery
guns may be captured. Melee may only occur during the Melee
Phase, and not in any other part of the Game-Turn. After the
Melee is resolved, any units remaining in the hex may change
their facing and order of stacking freely as desired.

(11.1) Determining the Combat
Differential

The Melee Strength of an infantry unit or artillery crew is
equal to its current Combat Strength. Units that are disorganized
have their Combat Strength reduced by one Strength Point when
they Melee. The Melee Combat Results Table (CRT) is “differ-
ential” in nature. On it, the combat odds are expressed as the
difference between the attacker (the Phasing Player) and the
total Melee Strength of the Defender (the non-Phasing Player).
Both of these totals may adjusted due to presence of officers, the
terrain in the hex, and other causes (see 11.6). However, no unit or
stack of units may more than double their strength, or have their
strength reduced to less than one Strength Point, through the
additiun or substraction of Melee Strength Bonuses. In deter-
mining the Melee Strength Point Differential, the defender’s
strength value is always subtracted from the attacker’s regard-
less of who has the greater strength. For example, an attacker
with an adjusted Melee Strength of +5 who attacks a defender
with an adjusted strength of +8 would conduct the attack at a
differential of -3. Differnentials of lower than -4 will be resolved
on the -4 column of the Melee CRT, and differentials of higher
than +6 on the +6 column.

(11.2) Restrictions

(11.21) A unit may initiate Melee only if it has received an
order to do so. This Command may be given only during the
Friendly Activation Phase. After giving a Melee Command,
immediately place a Melee marker on the affected unit(s).

(11.22) In order to initiate Melee, the attacking unit must
begin the Melee Phase in a hex adjacent to the Enemy unit to be
attacked. The attacking unit may conduct Melee only through its
center front hexside.

(11.23) There is no Movement Point cost for an attacking
unit to enter the defender’s hex to conduct melee; any such “cost”
is covered by the Command Point cost to initiate Melee.

(11.24) If a unit has been ordered to initiate Melee and is in
position to conduct the Melee during the Melee Phase, but the
Enemy unit that was supposed to be the target of the attack has
retreated or has been eliminated, the attacking unit still has the
option to enter the hex it would have normally entered had it
conducted the melee attack.

(11.25) An attacking unit which was ordered to “Melee” and
“Charge” (at a total cost of 6 C.P.) is under certain conditions
permitted to ‘“‘pursue” enemy units forced to retreat from the
Melee (see Section 11.6).

(11.26) Regular Melee is not mandatory once ordered—a unit
which was ordered to perform Melee in the Activation Phase, and
advanced to Melee Position in the Movement Phase, may change
its mind and not conduct Melee in the Melee Phase. This is
especially useful if, because of the results of fire combat, the
attacking unit will not have the favorable Melee Differential he
anticipated. However, a unit that has been ordered to “charge”
must complete its Melee attack regardless of the actual differ-
ential at the moment of combat.

(11.27) If units of only one Player remain in the hex after a
Melee combat has been resolved, the victorious Player may
adjust the facing and/or stacking order of his surviving units as
he desires at no cost.

(11.28) Units that intend to Melee may conduct normal
fire in the Offensive Fire Phase preceeding their Melee Attack;
including Volley Fire, if that is so ordered. Such units are
permitted to fire at Enemy units other than the ones they intend
to Melee. within normal restrictions. Units that have received a
“Charge” command may fire only through their center front
hexside, and must fire at half strength. Units that are involved in
a continuous Melee may not conduct fire combat, nor may they
be fired on, before the Melee is resolved.

(11.29) The bottom unit(s) in a stack may initiate Melee only
if the top unit does. Remember that each unit conducting Melee
must receive a Melee Command.

(11.3) Results

(11.31) If the result of the Melee combat requires a Strength
Point loss, the owning Player will determine which of his unit(s)
will be affected. A “D,” “R” result will affect all of that
Player’s units involved in the Melee.

(11.32) If an artillery gun is alone in a hex with an Enemy
unit at the end of a Melee or Movement Phase, the gun is
considered ‘‘captured.” Captured guns remain in the game, and
may be “recaptured” by the original owning Player.

(11.4) Continuous Melee

When Melee is resolved, any result that permits opposing
units to remain in the hex at the end of the Phase means that the
fight is still raging. This is known as “Continuous Melee.”
Combat between these remaining units will continue in the next
player’s Melee Phase, and will continue from turn to turn until
all units of one side or the other have left the hex. The Player’s
should place a “Melee” marker in the hex to indicate that a
Continuous Melee is occuring.

(11.41) Units involved in a Continuous Melee may neither
fire nor move, nor may they be fired on by other units.
Furthermore, other units may not move through the contested
hex, although they may move into it under certain circumstances
(see “B” below). In the Player-Turn following the one in which
the Melee was initiated the former defending Player has three
choices:

A) He may.fight another Melee in his Melee Phase with the
same units recalculating the differential as necessary; or

B) He may, within the stacking restrictions, bring in
additional units and fight another Melee as in “A;” or

C) He may choose to have all his units retreat two hexes, at
the conclusion of which the units must check Morale. The
retreating units would be subject to Retreat Fire from Enemy
units in the hex they were leaving.

(11.42) Units involved in Continuous Melee do not require
any Command Point expenditure to continue the combat. the
Command Point Cost is only to initiate the Melee.

(11.43) Officers involved in Continuous Melee have their
Command Radius reduced by one hex and their Command Point
total halved (see 4.0). Such leaders may rally only those units
they are stacked with in the Continuous Melee.

(11.44) Any combat strength bonuses given for terrain,
bayonets, etc., do not reapply to each following round of
Continuous Melee.

(11.5) Bayonets

Under certain circumstances, units may increase their Melee
Strength by fixing bayonets. Bayonets may be fixed (or removed)
only during the Activation Phase at a cost of 3 C.P. per unit. A
unit that has fixed bayonets is indicated by placing the
appropriate information marker on the unit (fixed bayonet
markers are provided for units both with and without ammun-
ition). Units that have fixed bayonets may not conduct Fire
Combat. They may gather ammunition. A unit that has fixed
bayonets receives a Melee Strength Point bonus when it initiates
Melee Combat (see 11.7).

(11.6) Charge Effects

Each unit that charges into Melee (see 5.5) has one added to
its Combat Strength when resolving the Melee. Charge also has
the following effects on Melee combat.

(11.6) If the defending (non-charging) side retreats from the
hex as a result of the melee combat (including any required
morale checks) the charging unit(s) have the option to “pursue”
the enemy one hex along their path of retreat. This option must
be taken immediately after the retreat is carried out, before the
special morale check described in 11.62.

(11.62) After the Melee combat (and any pursuit) is carried
out the charging units must immediately undergo an extra
morale check to reflect the effort required by the charge. This
would be in addition to any morale check normally required by
the combat results.

(11.63) In order to pursue, charging units may not be
ineffective or disorganized. Likewise, only charging units that

(11.64) Charging units may enter a continuous melee. The

(12.0) DISORGANIZATION
AND RALLY

GENERAL RULE:

Infantry units may become disorganized from either small
arms fire, melee combat, or morale checks. Disorganization is
indicated by placing a Disorganized marker on the affected unit.

DISORGANIZED UNITS

(1) May not move (or change facing or order of stacking);

(2) Have their Fire Strength reduced by 1 Strength Point

(3) May not initiate melee attacks; if involved in continuous
melee or defending against a melee attack, their Melee Strength
is reduced by one Strength Point;

(4) May not gather ammunition, remove fences, or construct
breastworks;

(5) May not conduct volley fire or rapid fire;

(6) May not attempt random movement;

(7) Must be in line formation;

(8) Disorganized artillery crews may not move, or change the
facing or formation of their guns, or fire them in any way.

NOTE: The Fire or Melee Strength of a disorganized unit may
never be reduced below one. Officers, artillery and Confederate
sharpshooters may never become disorganized.

(12.11) A unit that is both disorganized and ineffective may
use Offensive or Withdrawal Fire. It may still use Defensive Fire.

(12.12) If the top unit in a stack becomes disorganized all
other unit in the stack are also disorganized.

(12.13) If a disorganized unit suffers a second disorgani-
zation result due to fire or melee combat, it will lose 1 Strength
Point and remain disorganized.

(12.14) If a disorganized unit suffers a second disorgani-
zation result from a morale check it does not lose a Strength
Point and simply remains disorganized.

(12.15) If a disorganized unit receives a second disorganized
result because it is in a stack whose top unit was disorganized
(see 12.12), it does not lose a Strength Point but must immediately
check morale.

(12.2) Rally

Disorganized units may rally (recover from disorganization)
during a Friendly Command Phase. This may occur in three
different ways:

(1) Spontaneous Rally:

Disorganized units may automatically attempt to rally
during Friendly Command Phases. The owning Player should
make a separate die roll (one die) for each of his disorganized
units. On a result of one the unit is rallied.

(2) Rally by Regimental Officer:

During the rally portion of the Activation Phase a disorg-
anized unit that is within three hexes of its regimental officer
may be rallied at a cost of one Command Influence Point.

(3) Rally by Brigade Officer:

During the Rally Portion of the Activation Phase a disorga-
nized unit that is stacked with or adjacent to its brigade officer
may be rallied at a cost of one Common Influence Point.

Disorganized units that rally have their disorganized marker
removed. Newly rallied units retain the same facing they had in
their disorganized state. There is no limit to the number of times

a unit may become disorganized, rally, and become disorganized
again.

(13.0) MORALE

When a unit is required to check morale, the owning Player
must immediately make a die roll for that unit and cross index
the result with the appropriate column on the Morale Table.
Units that receive a “D” result become disorganized. Units
receiving a “R” result must immediately retreat and become
disorganized at the end of the retreat. Units receiving a “S” result
are “shattered” and removed from play.

(13.1) When Morale Checks Occur:

A unit must immediately check Morale in the following
circumstances:

1) When it suffers a Strength Point loss from Fire or Melee
combat.

2) When it receives an “M” result in a Melee combat.

3) When a unit it is stacked with retreats.

4) If it is already disorganized, and in a stack whose top unit
become disorganized.

5) When a retreating unit enters or goes through its hex.

6) When it withdraws from a Continuous Melee.

7) After completing a charge.

8) After completing a forced march.

(14.0) INEFFECTIVE UNITS

Infantry units and artillery crews that suffer losses may
become ineffective. When a unit’s Combat Strength drops to less
than equal to the value printed on its reverse side the unit is
ineffective. (Flip the counter over to this side to indicate this.)
Exactly when a unit becomes ineffective varies with the indi-
vidual unit. Once a unit becomes ineffective it remains so for the
rest of the game.

EXAMPLE: The first unit of the Confederate 59th Georgia
Regiment begins the game with a Combat Strength of 6 and a
Morale Rating of C. Once the unit loses two Strength Points (has
its strength reduced to 4) it would become ineffective and have a
Morale Rating of E.

(14.1) Results of Being Ineffective

Ineffective Units:

1) Have a lower Morale Rating, as printed on the ineffective
(reverse) side of the unit;

2) May enter an Enemy ZOC only when currently stacked
with an officer;

3) May not attempt spontaneous rally;

4) May combine fire only with other units stacked in the
same hex;

5) Do not have a rigid ZOC (see 9.0). However, they may
conduct withdrawal fire normally;

(15.0) RETREAT

Retreat is a form of involuntary movement that occurs either
as a result of a Morale Check or Melee Combat. Retreat is not
considered to be movement and does not require Command or
Movement Points. Retreating units may not move “backwards,”
they may only enter their front center hexes when retreating, and
may have to adjust their facing accordingly.

(15.1) Priorities

Units that retreat must attempt to move exactly two hexes
from the hex in which the combat occured. Retreating units may
not enter Enemy Occupied hexes but may move through Enemy
Zones of Control (see 9.3). A unit that is unable to retreat at least
one hex is completely eliminated (and all officers with the unit
are captured). Within these restrictions, retreating units must
obey the following priorities in determing the path of retreat:

1) Units may enter an Enemy Zone of Control only as a last
resort;

2) Union units must end their retreat as close as possible to
the nearest Union entry area on the North map edge. Confed-
erate units must end their retreat a close as possible to the
nearest CSA entry hex on the South map edge, or if retreating
from a hex south of the 2300 hexrow, towards Big Round Top
(specifically hex 3319);

3) Units must enter the fewest possible Friendly Occupied |

hexes;
4) Units must make the fewest possible facing changes, i.e.
must attempt to retreat in a “straight line;”

5) Units must enter hexes whose terrain costs the least
number of Movement Points.

If there is a choice between two or more retreat paths the
owning Player determines which one is used.

(15.11) Officers stacked with units that retreat may either
retreat with the unit or remain in the hex, at the owning Player's
choice. Unlimbered artillery guns may never retreat.

(15.12) Units that retreat off the map-edge are not consid-
ered eliminated, but are permanently removed from the game.
Officers are replaced as necessary.

(15.13) Units may not adjust their facing after performing a
retreat.

(15.14) Retreating units may move into or through hexes
containing Friendly units (forcing them to check Morale).
However, if a retreated unit would end the Phase in a hex in
excess of the stacking limits or violation of facing requirements,
the unit must continue to retreat one or more additional hexes
until this would no longer be the case.

(16.0) AMMUNITION SUPPLY

A unit which performs volley fire runs the risk of using up all
of its available ammunition. In addition, most Civil War units
during this period tended to run out of ammunition after an hour

or so of steady combat. Different units will be liable to run out of
ammunition at different times, as shown on the Regimental
Depletion Chart. When an ammunition check must be made, a
die is rolled and the Ammunition Depletion Chart consulted.
Each unit that is out of ammunition should be indicated with the
appropriate marker. Units that are out of ammunition may not
“borrow” ammunition from other units, but may gather it from
the cartridge boxes of the dead and wounded.

(16.1) Conditions

1) Fresh Units: When a unit first enters the game, it must
undergo an ammunition depletion check whenever it conducts
volley fire and causes an enemy strength point loss (not a 2nd
“disorganization” result). The owning Player rolls a die for each
unit that volley fired; loss of ammunition supply occurs on a roll
of “1” or “2.”

2) Expended Units: After a unit has been in combat for
about an hour, it has a greatly increased chance of running out of
ammunition (see Regimental Depletion Chart for the exact turn
in which the different units become “Expended”). A unit in this
condition must check for ammunition depletion after any volley
fire (regardless of result) and any time fire combat causes an
enemy strength point loss (not a 2nd disorganization result).
Ammunition depletion will occur on a roll of “1,” “2,” or “3” after
volley fire and “1” or “2” after regular fire (see Ammunition
Depletion Table).

(16.4) Gathering Ammunition

A unit may attempt to “gather” ammunition (from the
cartridge boxes of fallen comrades) if ordered to do so during the
Activity Phase. The owning Player will roll a die for each unit
attempting to gather ammunition. Ammunition is gathered
successfully on a roll of “1” and the “out of ammunition” marker
should be removed from play. Any other die roll has no effect.

(16.41) Only units that are “out of ammunition” may

attempt to gather ammunition. Combat units may not “loan”
ammunition to each other.

(16.42) Disorganized units, infantry in column, and those
involved in a Continuous Melee may not attempt to gather
ammunition.

(16.43) There is no limit to the number of times a unit may
run out of ammunition, gather more, and run out again.

(16.5) Artillery Ammunition

Each artillery section begins play with the specific number of
rounds of ammunition stated in the Scenario Instructions.
Artillery is not supplied with different types of ammunition
(cannister, shot, shell), since for game purposes use of these
ammunition types has already been figured into Fire Combat
Results Table. When each section fires, one round should be
deducted from that section’s total (see also special Rapid Fire
rules below). Players should keep track of ammunition consump-
tion on a separate piece of paper.

(16.51) Union artillery crews may transfer rounds of ammu-
nition from one section to another during any Friendly Move-
ment Phase that the units are stacked together in the same hex.

There is no cost to do this. The units must both have functioning
crews, however. .

(16.52) Confederate artillery units may not transfer artillery
ammunition from one section to another.

(16.53) When Union artillery use rapid fire (see 18.1) they
use two rounds of ammunition instead of the usual one.

(18.0) ARTILLERY SECTIONS
AND CREWS

GENERAL RULE:

In the game, Union artillery is represented by separate
artillery crew and gun units, while Confederate artillery is
represented by combined gun and crew units on an Off-Map
Display. Each artillery battery is divided into a number of
sections of two guns each. For the Union, both the D Battery
and the 4th N.Y. Battery are composed of three sections;

For the Confederates, the Rowan Battery is composed of
three sections, the Branch Battery of two sections, and the
Richmond Battery of a single section.

(18.1) Union Artillery

Each Union artillery section is represented by a gun counter
and a crew counter. Artillery units lose guns only through
artillery fire, while crews may be affected both by artillery and
small arms fire.

(18.11) Artillery guns and crews are independent units
outside of the normal command structure. The manner in which
they move and change formation is described in 5.7.

(18.12) The Union Player may transfer crews from one
section to another. This is especially useful when, for example,
the guns of one section have been destroyed by Enemy artillery
fire while the crew of another section has also been eliminated in
combat. Any crew may operate any artillery section as its own.

(18.13) Each gun in an artillery section may be operated by
an artillery crew, on a one-per-one basis. For example, a 3
Strength Point crew unit could fire three guns stacked in the sme
hex.

(18.14) Artillery units are automatically captured at the end
of any Melee Phase in which they are alone in a hex with Enemy
combat units. Captured guns may be moved by infantry units,
but may not be fired by them.

(18.15) Disorganized crew units may not fire or move
artillery units. Disorganized infantry may not move artillery
units.

(18.16) Union artillery fire against Confederate units on the
map is resolved in the same manner as small arms fire, but uses
the Artillery Range Chart. Union artillery fire against off-map
Confederate artillery is resolved using the same procedure given
in Case 18.2

(18.17) Artillery units may not combine their fire with
infantry units. They may only combine fire with artillery units
stacked in the same hex.

(18.18) Union Artillery units may increase their Fire
Strength by using rapid fire. Rapid fire uses two rounds of
ammunition instead of the usual one, and may be used in both
Offensive and Defensive Fire. Use the indicated line on the
Artillery Fire Chart.

(18.19) The Fourth N.Y. Battery historically deployed two of
its sections near the rocky southern tip of Houck’s Ridge, with the
third section further back to the Northeast, behind the ridge. The
limbers and horses for all three units were positioned with this
last section. To simulate this, the two sections of the battery
deployed on the southern tip of Houck’s Ridge (sections 1 and 2)
deploy in unlumbered formation and may not become limbered

during the play of the game. They may of course be dragged by
hand.

(18.2) Confederate Artillery

All Confederate artillery is located off the western edge of the
map on Warfield’s Ridge, about % of a mile west of Devil’s Den.
They may be fired on only by Union artillery, not Union
infantry. All fire combat to and from these off-map units is
resolved using the special Off-Map Artillery Scatter Diagram
(printed in the Southeast corner of the map) and the Off-Map
Combat Results Table.

PROCEDURE:

All fire to and from off-map is conducted one section at a
time; no combined fire is permitted. Before any of the fire is
resolved all sections conducting off-map fire must name their
target hex. The firing Player then rolls two dice and consults the
Off-Map Scatter Diagram to see if the target hex was hit, or
whether the fire scattered into some adjacent hex or missed
entirely. (This rule simulates the inaccuracies of long-range
artillery fire during the period). Locate the type of fire determined
by the scatter diagram on the correct line of the Off-Map Combat
Results Table and apply the indicated result to any units in the
target hex.

(18.21) When using off-map fire only artillery sections of the
same battery may fire at the same target hex in any given Phase.

(18.22) Confederate artillery units represent both guns and
crews. When they take a loss, simply flip the counter over to its
reverse side.

(18.23) Confederate artillery units may not move, nor may
they exchange crews or ammunition. They may change facing
normally.

(18.24) Confederate artillery units may not shoot (or be shot
at) by units located in the following locations:

(1) Any hex that is completely surrounded by Woods hexes;

(2) Any hexes between the crest of Houck’s Ridge and the 560
elevation level of Little Round Top that would have the LOS
between the target and the firing unit traced across any crest
hexside of Houck’s Ridge or through hexes 0932 and 1032;

(3) Any hex east of the crest of Little Round Top (the 0012
hexrow);

(4) Any hex that is not within a normal LOS as traced to and
from the hex containing the off-map artillery unit;

(5) Any hex not containing a crew, artillery section or
infantry unit (i.e. an “empty” hex, or one containing an offiver);

(18.25) Additionally, Confederate artillery units may not

(18.3) Scatter Diagram

The Scatter Diagram, which is located in the southeast
corner of the game map, is used to resolve all Fire Combat
involving the Confederate Off-Map artillery. The Player con-
ducting the fire should roll two dice. A roll of “6” or “7” (Union
Player) or “7” (CSA Player) will result in the target hex being hit;
a roll of 2, 8, or 12 (and 7 for CSA Player only) will result in a
complete miss. Any other number will cause the shot to go astray
and hit a hex adjacent to the target indicated by the Scatter
Diagram. For example, if “Branch 2” is firing at a target in hex
1633 and the CSA Player rolls a 10, the shot hits hex 1533.
Similarly, if a section of the 4th NY Battery fires at “Rowan 1”
and the Union Player rolls a 5, the shot would hit “Rowan 2.” The
effect of the “scatter hit” depends on the type of gun firing and
the type of target hit (see 18.9). Note that a result of 1 on an
artillery section results in the loss of 1 gun. (All other results are
as in 10.81).

AND COUNTERS

(19.1) Confederate Sharpshooters

Robertson’s Confederate Texas brigade has available special
sharpshooter units that come into play as indicated on the
Reinforcement Chart. Each indicated regiment has only one
sharpshooter unit. Once these units are eliminated, they may not
be replaced. Sharpshooters are capable of firing at Enemy
officers, artillery crews, and infantry.

(19.11) Sharpshooter fire is resolved using the CSA sharp-
shooter Combat Results Table (19.15). The result of the fire will
depend on the type of target and the type of terrain that the
target is located in. Sharpshooters are equipped with special
telescopic rifles and may fire at any unit in their Line of Sight,
regardless of range or order of stacking. A sharpshooter unit has
a strength of “one,” and is not permitted to combine fire with
other sharpshooter, infantry or artillery units. They may fire
only during a Friendly Offensive Fire Phase. No more than one
sharpshooter is allowed to fire at a given target in a given Game-
Turn. Such fire is always against one specific unit or leader.
Sharpshooter units may not conduct volley fire or rapid fire, nor
do they run out of ammunition.

(19.12) The Union Player may fire at only those sharp-
shooter units that have been “spotted” (see below). An “un-
spotted” sharpshooter may never be fired at. Fire against
sharpshooters is conducted like fire against officers (see 4.8).

(19.13) When a Texas brigade sharpshooter first appears in
the game, it is “unspotted.” It may not be fired upon, until it
becomes “spotted” by doing one of the following:

1) Fires upon an Enemy unit.
2) Is in an Enemy ZOC as the end of a Phase.

3) Ends a Movement Phase in a clear hex and is not
“behind” (adjacent to) a fence, stonewall, or stacked with
Confederate unit(s) (i.e. it is in the open and in the LOS of the

enemy).

When one of these situations occurs, the sharpshooter unit is
flipped to its “spotted” side. It may be flipped back to its
“unspotted” side at the beginning of any Friendly Movement
Phase in which none of the sighting conditions are met.

(19.14) General Rules:
1) Sharpshooter units have no ZOC or formation.

2) They may not fire withdrawal fire, opportunity fire, volley
fire or rapid fire.

(3) A Sharpshooter unit defends against a melee or fire

combat attack as an officer for casualty purposes (see 4.95).

4) They have no stacking value, and are always “unspotted”
when stacked with other units.

5) Sharpshooter units move under the special rules given in
Case 5.75.

(19.2) 2nd US Sharpshooters

The 2nd US Sharpshooters were specially trained and
equipped troops that use the movement and combat rules listed
below.

(19.21) Units of the 2nd Sharpshooters are equipped with
Carbines, which fire faster than regular infantry weapons and
are more effective at short ranges; though ineffective at longer
ranges. Units armed with carbines may not conduct Volley Fire,
but may conduct rapid fire, both offensively, defensively,a nd
during Withdrawal and Retreat Fire. Rapid Fire gives the firing
unit a one column shift to the right when resolving fire combat,
but has the penalty that it must be followed by an ammunition
check (see 16.0). Since carbines are breech-loading, men armed
with them may fire more easily from the prone position or from
behind protective cover; hence units of the 2nd US Sharpshooters
automatically receive a one column shift to the left benefit when
receiving Enemy fire.

(19.22) US Sharpshooter units move under the special rules
given in 5.7. They may also force march while in line formation
(see 5.63).

(19.3) Breastworks

During the battle troops often built temporary breastworks
for protection by piling stones, brush or trees, or by digging
shallow gunpits (much of the terrain at Gettysburg is too rocky
for regular trenches to be dug, and troops at this point of the war
did not yet have the “dig in” mentality they later developed).

PROCEDURE:

An infantry of artillery crew unit may be ordered to construct
breastworks during any Friendly Activity Phase in which they
are in rough, woods/rough or woods hex. The amount of time
needed for construction and the exact procedure used varies with
the type of terrain and the Command Point Total of the officer or
unit expending the C.P. If the officer has a C.P. Total of 50 or
higher the breastwork is automatically completed at the end of
one or two Game-Turns, as listed on the Breastwork Construction
Chart. If the officer has less than 50 C.P. (or the unit is an
artillery crew) the procedure is.the same but the breastwork isnot
automatically constructed. At the end of the construction period
the owning Player should roll one die and consult the Con-
struction Chart. On the indicated die roll the breastwork is
completed; any other result has no effect.

(19.31) To indicate a breastwork under construction place a
marker of this type on top of the constructing unit. If the
breastwork is not completed after one Game-Turn, place a marker
underneath the unit to indicate a continuing effort at con-
struction.

(19.32) To indicate a completed breastwork, place a breast-
work counter in the hex so that its three hexsides correspond to
the three frontal hexsides of the constructing unit. No more than
one breastwork marker may ever be placed in a hex at any one
time.

(19.33) Breastworks may only be built in woods, woods-
/rough, or rough hexes. They may not be built in any sort of
rocky hex, or along stonewall hexsides.

(19.34) In the Game-Turn that it is constructing a breast-
work a unit may not:

(A) Be activated for movement or expend C.P. for any other
purpose;

--- Page 17 ---
(B) Conduct any type of fire combat;

(C) Be involved in melee;

(D) Receive any combat result other than “no effect;”
(E) Be Out of Command.

(19.35) The effort to build a breastwork that takes more than
one turn must be continuous. The constructing unit must meet all
the requirements on each Game-Turn of construction (including
the C.P. cost) or the effort must begin from “scratch.”

(19.36) Brigade and special officers have no direct influence
on breastwork construction, outside of the normal command
rules.

(19.37) More than one unit may be used to construct a
breastwork requiring a die roll. This does not speed up the
process, but each unit may make a separate die roll for the
breastwork’s completion.

(19.38) Ineffective or Disorganized units may not construct
breastworks.

(19.39) Once completed a breastwork remains in place until
it is removed. They may be in the exact manner as fence hexsides
(see 5.9).

Note: The number of breastworks constructed in the game is
not limited by the counter mix.

(20.0) REINFORCEMENTS

Reinforcements are additional units that enter the map
during the play of the game.

(20.1) How Reinforcements Arrive

Reinforcements are eligible to enter the map during the
Friendly Movement Phase of the Game-Turn indicated on the
Reinforcement Chart. They may arrive in any hex of their listed
entry area, and pay the normal Movement Point costs for their
entry area hex. Confederate entry areas with numbers (1, 2 and 3)
on the West mapedge; Union areas are marked with letters (A, B
and C) on the North mapedge. (All units of each separate
regiment or battery must enter through the same hex within its
entry area. These units are not “staged” back and all units pay
the same cost to enter the entry hex.

(20.11) On the turn they enter reinforcements must be
activated for movement while still “off-map.” All units are
considered to be In Command, and all regimental officers may
receive Command Influence from any brigade or special officer
entering with them.

(20.12) Units may be ordered to force march, volley fire,
melee, etc. while off-map. They may charge any Enemy unit that
has a Line of Sight to their entry hex.

(20.13) Units may not change formation while off-map.

(20.14) Reinforcements may not enter in a hex containing
an Enemy unit but may enter into an Enemy ZOC. If all entry
hexes of an area are blocked the units may instead enter from the
nearest available hex.

(20.15) The exact time of arrival of many reinforcements may
be varied by the owning Player. This will have an effect on the
victory conditions (see 20.2).

(20.16) Confederate units arriving on the West mapedge do
not have to pay the Movement Point cost to cross the contour
line which runs along the mapedge. (It is merely establishes the
height of those hexes).

(20.17) Confederate units may arrive in any hex of their
entry areas, not just those with numbers printed in them. Area 1
runs from hex 3342 to 2542; Area 2 from 2342 to 1542; and Area 3
from 0742 to 0142.

(20.18) Confederate sharpshooters do not enter from the
mapedges. Their procedure is given in the Scenario Instructions.

(21.0) VICTORY CONDITIONS

At the end of each scenario victory is awarded to the Player
who has obtained the most Victory Points (V.P.). These Points
are awarded for the elimination of Enemy troops and officers, the
capture or destruction of Enemy artillery, gaining control of
certain geographical objectives, causing Enemy brigades to
become ineffective, the early use of reinforcements, etc. At the
end of the scenario each Player determines the number of Victory
Points he has gained. The Player with the most Points has won.

(21.1) Victory Point Schedule

Victory Points are awarded for the following in all scenarios:

(21.1) Victory Point (V.P.) Schedule

1 V.P. for each Enemy Strength Point killed or captured.
2 V.P. for each brigade officer eliminated.*

4 V.P. for each brigade commander captured.*

6 V.P. for eliminating or capturing a Special Officer.*

1 V.P. for each Enemy Regimental Officer eliminated.

2 V.P. for each Enemy Regimental Officer captured.

2 V.P. for each Enemy gun captured and held at the end of
the scenario.

1 V.P. for each Enemy gun destroyed.

10 V.P. for each Enemy brigade that has become ineffective
due to casualties (see 21.3).

*Victory points are awarded for leaders are based on their
position held at the beginning of the scenario. Regimental
leaders that are promoted to brigade command are counted as
regimental officers. Similarly, a brigade commander who has
assumed division command is counted as a brigade commander.

All regimental officer replacements are counted as regimental
officers.

(21.2) Control of Geographical
Objectives

A Player in considered to control a hex for victory purposes if
one of his combat units was the last to occupy or pass through the
hex in question. Units that are ineffective, Out of Command, or
Disorganized may not be counted for this. Neither Player
controls a hex containing a continuous melee at the end of the
game. Geographical objectives and the Points awarded for them
will vary from scenario to scenario. The Union Player controls all
Victory hexes at the start of each scenario.

(21.3) Ineffective Brigades

In the Major Battle scenario, Victory Points are awarded
for each Enemy brigade that has reached its Brigade Inef-
fective Level. A brigade reaches the ineffective level when it
has suffered about 30-40% losses, depending on its historic
performance. A brigade will suffer no additional penalty during
the game for becoming ineffective. Each brigade listed will be
considered “ineffective” when it has suffered the specified
number of losses. Independent units and units whose brigade
commander are not present in the game are not covered by this
rule.

(4.13) Clarification

Special Officers may only Command regi-
mental officers. Brigade officers may command
either regimental officers or combat units.

Regimental officers may only Command combat
units.

(4.6) Correction

Rule 4.6 should read “an Ineffective unit
must be stacked with either its regimental,
brigade, or special officer in order to enter an
Enemy Zone of Control.” No morale check is
required.
(4.8) Clarification

All anonymous leader replacements must
have a lower rating then the leader they are
replacing, until the lowest level (the 30-3 Captain)
is reached. Within this restriction, Players may
replace an officer with the highest rated replace-
ment remaining in the counter mix.

officer, a Texas Sharpshooter may move up to a
maximum of 6 hexes, regardless of terrain. When
outside the Command Radius...”

(9.11) Clarification

9.12 is an exception to 9.11. Ineffective
infantry units do not exert a rigid Zone of
Control.

(10.15) Clarification
Units may also fire “Retreat Fire’ more than
once per Phase.

(10.6) Clarification

Enfilade fire is a special case where the fire
first enters the target hex through a rear hexside
of the target unit and exits through a frontal
hexside, both of which must be within the 60°
Field of Fire.

(12.11) Correction

A unit that is both disorganized and inef-
fective may not use Offensive, Withdrawal, or
Retreat Fire.

‘ When a stack of units involved in a melee

takes a loss; all other units in the stack merely
check morale.

(14.0) Correction

When a unit's Combat Strength drops to less
than or equal to the value printed on its reverse
side the unit is ineffective.
