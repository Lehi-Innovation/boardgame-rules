Components
6 Dice Cups , 30 Bullseye Dice,
1 Red Bid Die,1 Neoprene Playboard

Setting Up
Give each player 5 dice and a cup. Place the game mat in the middle

of the table within reach of all players. Have all players roll their 5
dice. The player with the highest total begins the game.

Playing the Game

Liar’s Dice has a long and storied history over hundreds of years with
dozens of rule variations. We've chosen our favorite ruleset, but feel
free to use your own house rules!

Rolling the Dice

All at once players roll their dice in their cups before slamming the
cup, upside-down, onto the playing surface, trapping the dice inside
and keeping their results hidden. Lift a corner of the cup to peek at
your results. If any dice are stacked, you must reroll.

Wild Dice

Ones (sometimes called Aces) are wild dice that can count for
any value.

Bidding

The starting player guesses how many dice (quantity) of one value
(face) were rolled by all players. “Two threes” is a bid that dice
showing a three face were rolled a quantity of two times. Remember!
This is your bid for all the dice on the table.

--- Page 3 ---
When you make a bid, place the bid die on the playboard in the
space whose number matches the quantity of dice with the
corresponding value face up. For Example: For a bid of nine fives,
place the bid die on the 9 space, with the value of 5 face-up.

Once the first bid is made, play continues to the left. Each
subsequent player can either:

+ Raise the bid
+ Challenge the previous bid

Raising the Bid
Thanks to our handy game board, keeping track of the bid is a snap.

To put it simply, after the first bid is in place, your new bid must be in
amore advanced space around the track.

Co raise the bid you can:

+ Increase the quantity of dice by moving the bid to a higher space,
leaving the value the same

+ Increase the face value by placing the new value face-up

+ Increase both the quantity of dice and the face value by moving and
then changing the bid die face

The spaces marked with a compass are for betting on Aces. They're
worth a little more than standard values. On the board you can see
that if you wanted to raise a bid from two ones to two aces, you're
actually moving up 2 spaces, past the 35 quantity space, back into a 2
quantity space.

Challenging a Bid

On your turn, if you don’t wish to raise the bid, you may challenge the
most recent bid instead. After declaring your challenge, all players
reveal their dice.

+ If the bid is greater than the quantity on the table, the bidder was
caught bluffing! They lose the challenge and permanently surrender
one die to the center of the board.

+ If the bid is equal to or less than the quantity on the table, the
bidder was right and the challenger loses! When you lose a challenge,
urrender dice to the center equal to the difference between the bid
amount and the actual amount of bid dice in play.

For example: if you challenge a bid of seven fours, but it turns out
there are nine fours in play, you surrender two dice!

Winning the Game

After the challenge resolves, players round up their remaining dice
and roll again. The player who lost last round has first bid. The game
continues until there’s one last player standing.

Special Rule: Elimination

In the event the last two players only have a single die left, things work a little
differently. Instead of bidding on quantity and value, players bid on the sum of
both dice. The one who takes the inevitable challenge is the winner.
