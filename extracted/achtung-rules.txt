"If you can’t follow the laws, then be sure nobody catches you breaking them."
A Proud Mammal Game

ACHTUNG! is a game about bestowing freedom and justice upon your ungrateful
political opponents before they find a way to tyrannize you with a deluge of their unfair
partisan laws. But you're a good citizen, so no matter how petty, difficult, or absurd their
laws become - you must follow them to the letter.
The Goal
A player wins when they are the last person holding cards in their hand.
Setup and How to Play
Every player will draw 5 cards from the deck before the game begins. The most patriotic
player always goes first.
A player’s turn consists of drawing a single card from the top of the deck, then playing a
single card from their hand by reading its text aloud and laying it on the table. The turn
will then pass to the player on their left (Clockwise turn order).
When a player reads a law and lays it on the table, everyone else in the game has to
follow that law. If any player is breaking the law, shout “ACHTUNG!” at them and tell
them what they were doing wrong. The player that broke the law has to discard a card
from their hand.
Types of Cards
The 5 pillars of legislation in ACHTUNG! are as follows...
Global Laws (White cards) are laws that everyone playing must follow. When a player
chooses a Global Law card to play, they must enact only 1 of the 2 laws written on the
card.
Directed Laws (Green and Red cards) are laws that only affect the player that they are
placed in front of, and consist of both a positive and a negative effect. When a Directed
Law enters the game, the player who played the card must place it in front of any one
player in the game, and it will stay there until it is Abolished.

Amendments (Purple cards) are modifiers that get attached to laws that are in play.
Amendment cards each have 2 options when they are played. Amendment and law card
text should read as one seamless sentence when these cards are attached. Directed Laws
that have Amendments attached to them have both their positive and negative effects
changed simultaneously. Only one Amendment can be attached to a law and they cannot
be displaced except by Abolish cards (by Abolishing either the law it is attached to, or the
Amendment card itself).
Amendments are all “if-then” statements, where if you do the required action on the
amendment then you don’t have to obey the law on the card it is attached to. If an
Amendment requires you to say or do something, then you must do or say it every time
that you want to avoid the law. So long as you are obeying an Amendment, you cannot be
punished for breaking the law that the Amendment is attached to.
Abolishes (Black cards) are cards that allow you take a single law off the table. When an
abolish card is used on a law, put both the Abolish card and the law into the discard pile,
as they are no longer in play.  Abolish cards also have the distinct ability of being able to
be used at any time during the game. They can remove any card from play or stop a
Filibuster from proceeding - even on another player’s turn!
Filibusters (Yellow cards) are cards that present the group of players with a challenge.
Everyone takes part in these challenges but not everyone will come out a winner. See the
following section for how Filibusters work!
Filibuster Mini-Games
Filibusters are mini-games that everyone must participate in. Some will allow players to
gain a card advantage while other will alter the flow of the game. Keep in mind that even
during a filibuster, all laws are in effect.
To play a Filibuster, the player that holds the card must read the text aloud, followed by
that player placing the Filibuster into the discard pile. From this point, the game will
proceed in one of two different ways…
Speed Filibusters – These Filibuster rely on quick reactions.
Tax! – When a Tax! Filibuster is initiated, the moment that the card is read aloud,
everyone must race to discard a card from their hand. The player that is the slowest to
place a card into the discard pile, must discard an additional card (2 cards total).
Welfare! – When a Welfare! Filibuster is initiated, the moment that the card is read
aloud, everyone must race to pick up a card from the deck. The player that is last still
draws a card, but they must also discard one (net gain of 0).

Hands Up! – When a Hands Up! Filibuster is initiated, the moment that the card is read
aloud, everyone must race to put their hands above their head and then clap one time. The
player who is slowest to raise their hands and clap must discard a card.
Interruption Filibusters – These Filibusters interrupt the normal flow of
the game.
Bureaucracy! –  When a Bureaucracy! Filibuster is initiated, every player must hand a
card to every other player. The cards and the order doesn't matter, just make sure a card
goes from your hand into every other player's hand.
Vote! – When a Vote! Filibuster is initiated, the player that used the card will draw 4
cards from the top of the deck and lay them out for everyone to inspect. Then players will
vote on which card to add to the game. Once the card is selected by a majority vote, the
player who initiated the Filibuster will read the law that is coming into play and place it
on the table, discarding all cards that lost the vote. If the vote results in a tie, all of the
tied cards will enter into play.
Bribe! – When a Bribe! Filibuster is initiated, every player must give a card of their
choice to the player who used Bribe! The player will then choose one card to add to their
hand, then redistribute the remainder of the cards to the other players, in anyway that they
see fit.
Blacklist! – When a Blacklist! Filibuster is initiated, the player that played Blacklist! can
call out the names of up to half of the people in the game. Every person that is blacklisted
may not draw a card on their next turn, but they may also choose to not play a card on
that same turn.
ADVANCED RULES AND TACTICS
The following sections are more complex laws for more cutthroat players. While using
these rules can add a real edge of competitiveness to the game (and we recommend
playing with all of them), they may be extra difficult for first time players.
Order of Operations
If you ever get into a situation where there is a dispute about when something should
happen, pay attention to the wording on the card. There are 4 phases to every player’s
turn and every card with specific requirements will reference the time when it should be
used.
Beginning of Turn – This is the time between when the last player ended their turn and
before you draw a card.

--- ORIGINAL PDF PAGE 4 ---
Draw a Card – Unless a law states that you may choose not to draw cards, then you
must draw a card during this phase.
Play a Card – Unless a law states that you may choose not to play cards, then you must
play a card during this phase.
End of Turn – This is where you pass the game onto the next player in queue.
One Round is the time it takes from the end of your turn, to the beginning of your next
turn. Some laws will reference this time period. Keep in mind that no one’s rounds will
reset at the same time.
Illegal ACHTUNG!
When any player shouts “ACHTUNG!” at another player who hasn't broken the law, that
is an illegal ACHTUNG! and they may in turn have ACHTUNG! shouted at them for
falsely accusing another player.
Nullifying Laws
When a law contradicts another law, then neither of them are considered to be in effect.
This is called Nullification. If one of them is later Abolished, then the other one will
come back into active play. Anytime a player gets ACHTUNG! shouted at them while
lawfully disobeying nullified laws, that is an illegal ACHTUNG!.
Example: One law states you must hold your right arm motionless, while another says
you must hold both hands above your head when it isn't your turn. Due to the fact that
both of them tell you to do something that is impossible while the other is in effect, you
can do neither and be in the clear. BUT, also notice that the arms above your head law
isn't in effect all the time, so you still must follow the first law while the second one isn't
operating.
Statute of Limitations
A player may not have ACHTUNG! shouted at them if it is more than one round's worth
of time later than when their infraction occurred.
Vetoing
A Veto is an action that will remove a law from the game - before it ever enters play.
Immediately after a player has read a law aloud and placed it on the table, other players
may shout “VETO!”, then the player that is utilizing the veto power must discard a card
from their hand while simultaneously removing the new card from play. In addition to
vetoing new cards, any player may Veto a Veto by shouting “VETO! - VETO!” followed

by discarding a card, which will result in the initially vetoed law remaining on the table
but both vetoing players will still discard. If the turn passes to the next player before a
Veto is initiated, then the card on the table is safe from being Vetoed for the rest of the
game.
Consultants
If someone is in the room but they have no cards in their hand (either a player who has
lost all of their cards, or simply anyone who is watching the game), they can catch
players breaking the law and shout ACHTUNG! at them. They receive no reward for
these instances of shouting ACHTUNG! except the knowledge that they are contributing
to someone's downfall.
