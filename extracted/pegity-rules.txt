Pegity — original Parker Brothers directions

Directions for Playing the Game of PEGITY
Published by Parker Brothers, Inc., Salem, Mass., New York and London.
Image-checked transcription of the complete base-game directions and the two separately labelled variants. Original two-page imposed scans retain all panels and mosaic designs.

BASE GAME
For Two, Three or Four Players. Each player selects all the pegs of one color.
The idea of the game being to get FIVE PEGS OF ONE COLOR IN AN UNINTERRUPTED ROW IN ANY DIRECTION ON THE BOARD, players place their pegs with this idea in mind.
To begin the game, one player puts one of his pegs in ANY HOLE on the board, the other players doing the same in turn.
Players place their pegs in turn, one at a time, IN ANY HOLE WHICH HAS NOT ALREADY A PEG IN IT.
When any player gets three pegs in a row, with an empty hole at each end, the other players should "BLOCK" him by placing their pegs at either end of his row of pegs and thus prevent him from getting FIVE IN A ROW.
The player who first succeeds in getting FIVE PEGS OF HIS OWN COLOR IN A STRAIGHT ROW IN ANY DIRECTION but in DIRECTLY ADJACENT SQUARES (either in a horizontal, vertical or diagonal position) WINS THE GAME.
Each player should try to get five of his own pegs in a straight row, at the same time watching his opponents and "blocking" any attempt on their part to get five in a row.

PARTNERS
Partnership "Pegity" is great fun.
For four players.—Use only two colors of pegs, partners playing with the same color. Play as in the regular game of "Pegity," but partners assist each other in getting 5 in a row and of course must keep a sharp watch to block their opponents, as with four playing partners, the desired row is attained much more quickly than when single players are striving for the same object. It is amusing to arrange a series of signals between partners and thus assist each other in "spotting" openings or necessary "blocks."

4–5–6 — SEPARATE PEG-REMOVAL GAME
This is a novelty application to the game of "Pegity." Pegs of any color are placed anywhere on the board as in the accompanying diagram. Two players sit facing each other with the board between them and the object is for one player to force his opponent to take the last peg. All the pegs or as many as wished, may be taken from any one row at a time, but the player may not take a peg or pegs from more than one row in any single turn. Thus, the first player may take all five pegs in the second row at once, or one, two, three, or four of them. The same applies to the other rows. Remember to so judge the number of pegs you take each time, so that your opponent will be forced to take the last peg.

Diagram reading note: the separate 4–5–6 game diagram shows three starting rows of four, five and six pegs. This is not a removal rule for the base five-in-a-row game.
