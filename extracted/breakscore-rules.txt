--- Publisher FAQ, January26,2009capture ---
Source: https://web.archive.org/web/20090126043301id_/http://breakscore.com:80/bs_faq.html

Card and rule 'interpretations' will inevitably arise from time to time. If the 'interpretation' is disputed consult the rules. If the 'interpretation' is not covered, follow the highest ranked player's ruling. Otherwise 'interpretations' are decided by vote. If the vote is tied, flip a coin to immediately decide and then
email
us for a definitive future ruling!
Game-play
If you zouch after getting a boomer, can you still score the boomer?
No. You lose your entire BreakScore® (boomers included).
Can a player be zapped after he's entered  the enzone?
Sure thing.
When you get a boomer, do you sit or roll on?
Invariably I roll on.
How many boomers can you get in a turn?
There is no limit.
Do you always zap a person back to the start?
Usually but I might not if I were then left exposed as a zap target to more than one other player.
Do you move your counter as soon as you get a boomer?
No. Only move your counter if you decide to sit. Otherwise if you decide to roll on, you can choose to pick a boomer card (most players do) before re-rolling all six dice.
When is a turn, not a turn?
When you have to miss-a-turn!
Can you miss two turns in a row?
Yes. Two examples spring to mind: If you have the boomer card 'Next turn - If you do not take a zouch card, miss one turn' and then you zouch in the same turn as you took the boomer card, and then take the zouch card 'Take a break - Miss your next turn', or you have the same boomer card and land on a miss-a-turn square in the same turn as you took the boomer card.
If you are zapped back to a void, or a miss-a-turn square, do they apply to your next turn?
Yes.
If I am zapped back on to a zouch  square, a back or forward arrow, an extra turn square is that new square activated immediately, when my turn  comes around, or not at all?
It depends. The zouch square, NO, you must land on one in your turn to take a card. The arrows, YES, immediately go back or forward the number of squares indicated. The extra turn square, NO, you must land on one in your turn.
Is it possible to reach the enzone in one turn, and not win the game?
Yes.
*
Dice-play
After getting a boomer, do you get just one more roll of the dice?
No. There is no limit to the number of rolls or the number of boomers you can have in a turn. You can carry on rolling until you sit or you zouch.
Do you have to score all the points you roll, when you are going to sit.  For example say I need 105 points to zap somebody. I roll triple-ones (100 points) and then re-roll the three remaining dice, hoping to get a five. If I get a five and a one, or maybe two fives, do I have to score them both, or can I just score the one five needed to make my BreakScore total 105?
You don't have to score all dice that score. When you roll more than one scoring dice, you can choose which of them it's best to score. I'd probably take the five and zap the other player, but it can depend on other factors like board positions, cards-in-hand etc.
Can you concede?
While there is no rule to say you cannot, it's not in the spirit of the game. If you are way behind keep a positive attitude and, if you're lucky, you might even get the zouch card, "Advance and zap the leading player".
Given the choice to roll on with three dice or two, my friend prefers to score less points and roll three. Is this what you recommend?
Yes. The odds of rolling scoring dice with three dice are better than with two and with luck you might even roll a triple!
If you roll three dice and you get two stars, what do you usually do?
Almost always I would set aside and score both stars, as the odds of getting a boomer are better with one dice rather than two.
Do you always take a one-star in preference to a five-star?
No. Say on my first roll I scored a five-star and on my second I had the choice to score a one-star or a five-star, I could score the five-star to allow for the possibility of getting a woppa but then again scoring the one-star, and not the five-star, keeps the chances of rolling a run alive.
If another player has to miss-a-turn is it okay to try to tempt them by handing them the dice?
Yes. If they are not alert to your cunning plan and decline the dice they will fall have to pay the penalty for being careless!
Am I able to roll as many non-scoring dice as I want, when I want?
No. You must always re-roll all non-scoring dice. If you roll six dice and then set aside and score a star, you must roll all five remaining dice.
If someone rolls a dice and it lands off the table and onto the floor what happens?
The dice are cocked and ALL dice just rolled must be re-rolled.
*
Just because a card doesn't say something, it doesn't mean you can interpret it by what it doesn't say. - Fraser Black.
Card-play
Do you have to take a card?
Taking boomer and zouch cards, is optional that is unless instructed by another card. E.g. "Advance to the next zouch square and take another card." However there are exceptions, for example, "If you zouch on the start or on a void square, you cannot take a card".
I zapped a friend back onto a void. On her next turn, to get out of the void, she used the zouch card "If you land on a void, advance 30 points." and then landed on a zouch square and took a zouch card. Was her turn then over or could she continue it by rolling all six dice?
Yes, she can roll all six dice. NB: Though card play over-rules board play, it isn't meant to be instead of board play!
Do you recommend always taking a card?
Mostly but not always. There are a number of situations where I think carefully first. E.g. with three zouch cards left and I know one of them is a miss-a-turn card. Another is if I'm close to the enzone and I roll a boomer and and there is a risk of overshooting if I take the "Double your BreakScore® card". The trick is to follow which cards have been taken and those that haven't.
Do boomer cards always over-ride zouch cards?
Yes.
Would you ever deliberately sit on less than 50 points if you had the zouch card Next turn - Go straight to the next void if you sit on less than 50 points?
Yes. Assuming that the next void is a good distance away, there are at least two scenarios where I might. 1) If I had the zouch card "Advance 30 points, if you land on a void". 2) If another player is on the next void, and I have a saved card that can be used e.g. "If you zap a player advance 50 points".
What happens if someone stacks a card pack?
This shouldn't happen. Players can cut each pack of cards before the game begins and the player who has first choice to pick a card has the last cut. Remember in BreakScore® you can push the boundaries, particularly with interpretations, but cheating is not on.
If I pick the zouch card, "Next turn - If you zouch return to the start" can I choose not to roll the dice, rather than risk zouching?
No. As you cannot sit on zero points you must face the possibility of zouching by having at least one roll of the dice. Still with six dice to roll, the odds of zouching are very low.
When do you remind another player if they forget to use a card?
Almost immediately if you are playing a beginner in a social game. Otherwise in Champ-Play where rankings are at stake, tell them when the next player has started their turn!
If I zouch, do I have to give back any boomer cards taken on the same turn?
No.

      I had the boomer card "Next turn - If you zouch, keep your BreakScore® and then take a zouch card". On my next turn I took the boomer card "Zouch - Your turn is over, do not take a zouch card".
I can't take a zouch card, but can I keep my BreakScore®?
No If cards of the same kind conflict, the last card taken rules. Though the 2nd boomer card has no instruction in regard to keeping your BreakScore®, a turn over is a turn over! There is no conflict, the 2nd card over-rides the 1st card, so you cannot keep your BreakScore®.
I rolled a boomer and took a zouch bonus boomer card, then I rolled another boomer and took the boomer card "Zouch - Your turn is over, do not take a zouch card". Can I keep my BreakScore®?
Yes. There is no conflict between the two cards: the 1st allows you to keep your BreakScore® and the 2nd doesn't over-ride that instruction. NB: There is conflict between the cards over taking a zouch card: the 1st says take one, the 2nd says don't. In this situation the 2nd card over-rides the 1st, you cannot take a zouch card.
I got the boomer card "If you are zapped outside the enzone, start your next turn on 50 points." What does it mean?
It means just that. Start your next turn on 50 points. You could sit there and then, or you could try to add to the 50 points by rolling all six dice. NB: Sitting might be an attractive option when you have a card with an incentive to zap another player, but not compelling if the player who zapped you, had used a similar card and had moved on .... in that case most likely, but not necessarily, I'd roll on!

      I rolled a boomer and then took the boomer card "Next turn - If you do not take a zouch card, miss one turn" and then later in the same turn zouched and took the zouch card "Take a break - Miss your next turn".
How many turns do I miss?
Two. Firstly the zouch card applies and you miss your next turn, then as you missed that turn you were unable to take a zouch card so the boomer card applies.

--- Publisher Gameplay Guide, March25,2009capture ---
Source: https://web.archive.org/web/20090325095228id_/http://www.breakscore.com:80/boardgameguide.html

Apart from having a good time, the aim in BreakScore® is for players to first get their counter on the game board by scoring 40 points or more, and then win by being on the highest board square at the end of the game.
Players take turns rolling six dice, trying to score on each roll. Ones and fives (called stars) score points, as do three-of-a-kinds rolled in one roll (called triples), and runs (1,2,3,4,5,6).
At any point in their turn players can sit on their score (called a breakscore) and move their counter forward on the board, the number of points that equal their breakscore.
When six dice score (it's a boomer), players can sit  or re-roll all six dice, or they can take a boomer card before deciding to sit or re-roll. Players can roll more than one boomer in their turn, in fact the more boomers the better!
It's almost impossible not to score with six dice, but it can be tough to score rolling one or two of them. If a player cannot score, (it's zouch) their breakscore drops to zero and their turn is over ... that is unless they can take a zouch card.
Game play can be chaotic and exciting with players trying to sabotage each others progress, while pushing their luck to get just a little bit further ahead on the board.
The game board is not just a series of numbers. Voids can freeze players in their tracks,  zouch squares allow them to take zouch cards, other squares can make players miss or gain turns.
Game play can be chaotic and exciting with players trying to sabotage each others progress, while pushing their luck to get just a little further ahead on the board. For example; if a player lands on another player's counter (it's a zap), the victim is  sent back 50 points or, if that's not possible, to the start!
Once a player enters the enzone (the finish area), the remaining players each have one last turn to overtake them which can result in some exciting come-from-behind wins.
The cards are an interesting counter-balance to dice play and can radically change players positions on the game board. There are 60 game cards in total and they are mostly good or great to get. So the odds are the top card will be useful, but it could be also be nasty. Deciding whether to take a card, especially  when the pack is low, gives BreakScore® a cutting edge!

--- Publisher base Game Terms, January27,2009capture ---
Source: https://web.archive.org/web/20090127022044id_/http://breakscore.com:80/gameterms.html

Boomer:
If all six dice score in one or more rolls it's a boomer! - Aim to get several, ideally in every turn!
BreakScore®:
The number of points scored in a turn. - Don't lose track of them!
Sit:
You decide not to roll on. Move your counter the number of squares that equal your BreakScore®. - The key to the game is knowing when to take the risks and when to be happy with what you have!
Zap:
No board square can have more than one counter on it. When you land on another player's counter, return it to the start if it's not past square 90. Otherwise move it back 10 squares. NB: Zapping is a key part of the game, usually doesn't pay to hesitate!
Zouch:
No scoring dice are rolled. Your entire BreakScore® for the turn is lost and you cannot move your counter. If you are on the start or a void square, your turn is over. Otherwise, you can take a zouch card. - Not too worry, hopefully you'll be another chance to shine!
