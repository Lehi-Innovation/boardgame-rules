CITY OF SORCERORS

This is a fantasy boardgame for 2-4
players, each an apprentice sorceror
learning his trade before being tested. It is
a well-produced offering in an attractive
box, and includes: 78 Knowledge, 77
Fate, 25 College of the Arch-Mage and 11
Artifact cards; 4 different Arena maps,
each 8 x 11 inches; a 16 x 22 inch City
Map (essentially a glorified playing-aid
sheet); 25 large hex-shaped terrain chits
(used for altering terrain in the Arena); 66
counters representing havens, monsters,
artifacts and spells (spoilt by being rather
too thin); dice; players’ notes sheets; an
information sheet and rules book (whew!).

The game is divided into two parts. In the
first, the players are fledgelings studying
‘elements of magic’ — artifacts, spells
(monster conjuring, monster control and
several others), potions, psychic potential,
mystery levels and ancient tomes. Each
turn the players move on the City Map,
enrolling in one of the eight colleges,
such as those of the Alchemist or the
School of Magic, which specialise in one
or more of these elements. After enrolling,
cards are generally drawn from one of the
decks and players may attempt to keep
the cards of their choice. Much luck is
involved, but certain rules devices do
enable players to make decisions and
choices so that, to an extent, they can
dabble in the elements they prefer.

Magic research can be hazardous, and
players may draw ‘malediction and afflict-
ion’ cards which vary in their detrimental
effects. All afflictions can be cured, but it
is up to the player to decide whether it is
in his best interests to spend time on the
cure or suffer the problem, for there is
something of the race element in part 1.
After 18 turns Research ceases and
players are put to the test in the arena,
whether they are ready or not.

In part 2, an arena is randomly selected
from the four. Each depicts a wilderness
with different terrain. Players secretly
note the location hex of their ‘haven’ — a
stronghold within the arena — and then,
by various magical means, must detect
and eliminate opponents’ havens to win.
To this end artifacts and spells may be
utilised, monsters conjured and control-
led, terrain altered and psychic points
expended. Players are not represented by
counters themselves, and may not leave
their havens; they are Saruman-like
sorcerors, working magic and manipulat-
ing monsters from their towers. As there
are only 20 hexes in the arena, havens
are quickly discovered, so action moves
fast and furiously; this second part should
rarely last more than 6 turns. Compared
to part 1 luck is less influential and the
winner will usually be the player who had
the most success with magical research.

ORIGINAL BEGINNER’S GUIDE — SELECTED COMPLETE VISIBLE PASSAGES
Image-checked transcription from original photographs5 and14, not automatic OCR.
Only complete visible paragraphs are transcribed. The lower guide is partly obscured;
no hidden or cut-off words have been reconstructed. Original photos remain authoritative.

CITY OF SORCERERS is a fantasy boardgame for two to four players aged 12 to adult. Each player is an
apprentice magician striving to become a Sorcerer by winning a magical battle in the Arena of Ei-eh-Kar, the
Wizard.

In the box you will find 4 Arena boards, each with a different terrain layout. The boards are numbered one to four.
Each time you play the game, one player will roll a die, the result giving the board to be used (if 5 or 6 is rolled, roll
again until you roll a 1, 2, 3, or 4). Each player will then select and secretly note down the hex number of the
location of his or her invisible haven.

When battling in the Arena, the players do not take it in turns to do things, as in most games. Instead each game
turn is divided into 8 phases or sections, and all the players carry out one particular different action during each
phase. For instance during the Movement phase each player may move those monsters he or she controls, and
then in the Combat phase each player may attack opposing monsters which are in the same hex as his or her own
controlled monsters.

The cards you have removed from the sheets give details of the spells and other magic items that you may use in
the Arena for the Introductory Scenario. You may play a card (i.e. cast a spell) by showing it to the other players.
Most of the cards may be played in just one phase in a turn, a few at any time. Some of the cards are discarded
after use, some are retained and a small number may be reused. These details and the magical effects produced
are described on the cards, and a handy list of the cards is also included in the game for easy reference.

ORIGINAL INFORMATION AND TABLE CHART — SELECTED IMAGE-CHECKED DETAILS
Complete unchanged original chart retained as supporting component; these are excerpts, not a full table retyping.
Terrain Alteration Table: numbers are Psychic points expended; dash means no alteration; x means not permitted.
The Plains row gives Desert2, Hills2, Mountains4, Swamp2, Lake3, River2.
River costs cover creation or drying up; the same expenditure changes river direction by turning the marker.
Rivers may flow only between opposite hexsides.
Terrain Effects Table: Lakes admit only water monsters and Air and Water Elementals, though flying monsters
may cross without landing; Fire Elemental entirely prohibited. Adjacent lake hexes permit water-monster movement.
Rivers permit water monsters along them; Goblin, Dwarf, Troll, Earth and Fire Elementals may not cross.
