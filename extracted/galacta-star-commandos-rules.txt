GALACTA — ADVENTURE GAME RULES
Copyright ©1980 Heritage USA; original front,3050–3060.

INTRODUCTION [selected complete procedural paragraphs; story and die-painting advice omitted]
To play the game, you need the figures, rules and die provided here, a table for your battlefield (a cardtable, kitchen table, or coffee table will do), a ruler or yardstick, and some coffee cups and small books to represent buildings, walls, hills, etc.
This game includes a special12-sided gaming die, marked1to12.
The die is used to resolve shooting and fighting. It is not used in movement. To hit a target, you must roll a certain number or higher. For example, if you need an "8+" to hit, that means a die roll of8or higher is a hit (8,9,10,11or12). In some cases, you add or subtract to your die roll. Procedure is simple: roll the die, add or subtract as required, and then see if the final number is at least what you need to hit. For example, if you add one ("+1") to your die roll, and need an "8+" to hit, an original die roll of7, with the+1, equals an8, and you have scored a hit.
These rules are used in both the Star Commando and Galactic Rebellion sets. The Commando set does not include any Squog, Robot, or Octopoid figures; the Rebellion set has no Imperial Knight.

HOW TO PLAY
You play the game in turns. Each turn has four "phases", or steps. First one player moves, then he shoots, then the other player moves, and then he shoots. The player who always moves first is called "Player A", and the player who always moves second is called "Player B." When you make up your own game scenarios (situations), whoever sets up his figures first moves first (is Player A).
The four phases of a turn are outlined in more detail below:
(1) Player A moves his figures.
(2) Player A shoots his figures:
- only figures which did not move (in phase1) may shoot,
- any figures may attack with close combat, even those who did move; if Player B has any figures hit by shooting or close combat, he makes saving throws now.
(3) Player B moves his figures.
(4) Player B shoots his figures:
- only figures which did not move (in phase3) may shoot,
- any figures may attack with close combat, even those who did move; if Player A has any figures hit by shooting or close combat, he makes any saving throws now.

MOVEMENT RULES
You can only move your own figures, and then only in your own movement phase.
You can move as many figures as you want, including all or none. You do not have to move a figure.
Each figure moves separately. It has a movement rate, or speed, in inches (") per turn. You move simply by measuring with a ruler, and moving the figures the proper distance. You can spin and turn figures while they move, they need not travel in a straight line. You don't have to move top speed, you can move slower if you want. Figures can go in a variety of directions, provided the overall distance doesn't exceed top speed. You can't move through enemy figures, but must stop when you touch them.
Movement speeds depend on what the figure is, or what it wears:
6" — human types, in body armor
7" — human types, in light suit
8" — human types, no armor
Octopoid aliens —5"
all robots —6"
all other aliens —8"
