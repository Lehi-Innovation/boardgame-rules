‘Colony Delta’ is a new — and original — Science
Fiction game from Fantasy Games Unlimited. It
deals with the war between Terran colonists and
the insect-people of Sigma Draconis over who
should have control of the mineral rich planet
Delta (the fourth such planet to have been dis-
covered by the Terrans). The conflict depicted in
‘CD’ is one which has occurred many times on
Earth — the wars in the ‘Fertile Crescent’, the
colonial expansion in Africa and the Americas —
it is the struggle for resources, and with them
power. There is no direct political conflict between
Earth and the Draconians — except the need to
keep their home planets alive and the resultant
colonial expansion which brought them both to
Delta in the 23rd Century.
The game comes in a book-case box and the
physical components are very impressive. The map
is mounted and printed in blue, green, fawn and
brown. The 17x22” surface represents the whole
of the planet Delta; terrain is fairly simple — Sea,
Forest, Mountain, Badlands, and Plains. There are
also several black stars dotted around the map and
these give the location of commercially viable ore
deposits which may be mined by the players.
Movement is ostensibly permissible from each side
of the board to the other over the poles and
around the equator. [Reviewer’s suggested alternative map-wrapping procedure omitted.]

There are 532 die-cut counters representing the
forces of Earth, Sigma Draconis and Capella; the
native Deltans; Ore Point markers; and the Victory
Point/Game Turn markers.
The Rule-book is well illustrated, concise and clear
to follow and includes Designer’s Notes. A card
sheet is provided with various Tables printed on it
for easy reference. There are two ‘grades’ of game
— Advanced and Basic — but | would not hesitate
to advise all but the novice gamer to start with the
Advanced game as it plays much better at little
cost in complication.
There are no fixed set-ups in ‘CD’, the players are
given 100 VPs and are free to begin the game with
one Spaceport on Delta and one Shuttle/Domecraft
and whatever other units they choose on the home
planets ...
The system of ‘CD’ is not an over complex one,
although the Turn Sequence involves 22 different
steps. There are the basic elements of movement
and combat — but incorporated into the system are
the specialised features of weather, reinforcement,
air support, and the all-important mining/farming
which keeps each side in the game, There is alsoa
second (Space) Movement phase in which the
players transport Ore and units between the home
planets and Delta — using Terran Shuttles (devel-
oped from the late 20th century Space Shuttles) or
the Draconian equivalent known as Domecraft.
The primary aim of both players in ‘CD’ is to pro-
duce as many VPs as possible which are then trans-
ported back to the home planets. There are two
ways of doing this —

1) Mining: by placing colonist/worker units in ore
hexes on the map the player may mine that ore
deposit at the rate of 1 Ore Point per colonist unit
per Game Turn. If the player establishes a perm-
anent colony on the ore deposit he receives 5
OPs per turn, Advanced rules allow for increased
ore production in mountain and sea hexes, and for
the development of processing techniques to yield
2.5 VPs per OP rather than the normal 2VPs/OP.
Having mined the ore the player must now trans-
port it to a Spaceport (the name given to perm-
anent colonies with Shuttle/Domecraft loading
facilities) for its eventual transport to the home
planet. Only when ore reaches the planet does the
player receive VPs for it, so any ore left on the
planet at the end of the game is of no use,
Transport of ore on Delta can be carried out by
Terran Hovercraft and Submarine units and by
Draconian ‘Turtles’ (the all-purpose tank employed
by the insects for land/sea movement). Military
units too can transport ore but this is neither
economical nor strategically sound bearing in mind
the small amounts carried and the relative cost of
these units.
2) Agriculture: less profitable than mining, but
more widely applicable, this involves the pro-
duction of food to keep the colonists self-sufficient
in vital supplies. All Plains hexes can be farmed —
an Advanced Rule allows for the cultivation of
forests at lower productivity rate and the cul-
tivation of lake hexes (Izaak Walton, Fishmonger
of Mars?) at double the normal rate. As with
mining, use of the Technological Development
Table — at a cost in VPs — may lead to better
farming methods and a resultant increase in yields
per hex ‘under the plough’. The VPs produced by
farming appear directly on the home planet as they
represent the decline of food being sent from the
home planet as the colonists produce more them-
selves.

Mining and Agriculture are not allowed to coexist
in a hex — so it is usually more profitable to mine a
Plains hex than to farm it.
That, then, is the basis of the game — exploitation
of Delta’s natural resources by the colonists, It is
quite possible for the game to proceed in peace
with both sides concentrating on Economic
activity within their own areas. It is, however,
unfortunately rare for this to last an entire game —
eventually one of the players will be faced with the
situation described in the opening section, or alter-
natively will be doing so we// that he must expand.
However it is reached the result will always be the
same — war with the other side.
In peace-time the capabilities of Terran and
Draconian colonists are identical and their develop-
ment will be disrupted only by the Random Events
Table which will give one side an advantage. In
war, however, the two sides are quite markedly
different:
1) Terrans: the fighting strength of the Terrans lies
in the Peacekeepers, the 23rd century equivalent of
Marines. These units are armed with laser rifles and,
using jet-packs, can move on land and in the air.
The Peacekeepers, while using their jet-packs over
short distances, use Hovercraft to travel through
the air over a much wider range. The Hovercraft
Possess some strength of their own, but their main
advantage lies in their transport capacity — 10 OPs
or 1 Peacekeeper unit.
Air support for the Terran forces comes from
Aircraft equipped with powerful weaponry and
able to operate over a wide range. The movement
of aircraft is distinct from that of other units,
occurring in two phases before combat (Base-
Attack) and after combat (Return to Base).

The larger part of Delta’s surface is an ocean— so
Submarines are very useful for attacking enemy
mining installations which are exploiting the rich
off-shore deposits. These units may also be used to
transport OPs or units at sea.
In addition the colonists themselves have a limited
defence capability if attacked — as do Spaceports,
although the latter’s cost (25 VPs) usually means
that they are well protected with other units.
2) Sigma Draconians: the main forces of the
Draconian army are made up of Soldiers, the
genetically aggressive 1/3 of all ‘Siggies’ born. Their
biological make-up makes them totally oblivious to
fear and therefore they attack with no thought for
personal safety. Being insects the Soldier units can
move through the air but not on land/sea.
Turtles are the only ‘fighting machines’ employed
by the Draconians — able to move in both land
and sea hexes they enable the Draconians to reach
any part of Delta’s surface whether for mining or
to attack the Terrans. Like Terran Colonists the
Draconian equivalents (Workers) have self-defence
capability, as do the Draconian Spaceports.
Combat is by Odds-Ratio CRT with results ranging
from D/A Elim. to Exchange and losses of % and %
of the forces involved. To attack a hex it is neces-
sary to have sufficient Movement Points to enter
the hex — which precludes the immobile Worker/
Colonist units from attacking. Advance/Retreat
after combat occurs in a separate phase after the
Terran has returned his air units to base (the
Draconian does not have this Basing phase as he
has no air units), which means that the traditional
movement around a unit by attacking its flanks is
not possible, and retreats are made easier by the
very fluid ZOCs. Terrain effects on combat are
reduced because most of the combat occurs above
the terrain concerned — Forest and Mountain do
convey a defensive advantage to ground units.
In addition to the Draconian and Terran forces
there are also two other groups of potential com-
batants —
1) Deltans: these are the native inhabitants of
Delta and resent (understandably) the presence of
aliens on their planet — strange, isn’t it, to think of
man as an alien? In the Basic Game the effect of
Deltans was represented by a die-roll which could
cause losses of Colonist/Worker units. In the
Advanced Game there are Deltan units which can
be used by either side to harass the enemy.
2) Capellans: these are large feline creatures who
will fight for either side ... at a price. They are, for
want of a better phrase, ‘Space Mercenaries’ and
have their own military hardware — including the
Aircar which can transport the Capellans at high
speed, The troops themselves are largely similar to
Terran Peacekeepers. Capellans may be withheld
from one side by the Diplomatic Events Table, but
their constant availability in the face of opposition
on the home planet (which may prevent the
purchase of other units) means that they are a use-
ful asset in the event of full-scale conflict.
The Outside Events Table is divided into three
sections —
1) Political — this can be used at no cost to the
player, although at his discretion, and reflects
feeling towards the Colony on the home planets.
The possible effects include opposition to the
Colony project (making it harder to purchase
military units — hence the usefulness of Capelians,
who are not affected), and Government grants to
the Colonist/Workers which make it easier to
purchase units.
2) Diplomatic: available at a cost of 10 VPs, this
table reflects the relations between Earth, Sigma
Draconis and the other forces involved. Hence,
there may be a truce between the two sides; more
lenient policy towards the Deltans — giving the
affected side more Deltan units; Capellan neutral-
ity — preventing the purchase of Capellans by one
side.

3) Technological: this costs 30 VPs, and reflects
the diversion of resources on the home planet into
research leading to developments in farming/
mining or in weaponry. The effects of all these
results are good so it is worthwhile trying the
Tech. Table as the result will usually offset the
costs.
