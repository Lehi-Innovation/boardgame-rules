The text of Company Fraud Blackmail card is:
[q]Play anytime on a turn before Company expenses are paid.  Roll one die for each of your officeholders.  Lower the Company Balance equal to twice highest roll and take that much from the bank.[/q]

As the card doesn't say, 'Play immediately before Company expenses are paid', I assume that it can be paid before the Revenue Phase for the turn, at a time when the holder might need some extra cash.  What is the position if the die roll would result in the Company Balance being lowered by an amount that exceeds the current Company Balance?  (For example, a 6 is rolled (doubled to 12), but the current Company Balance is 10 pounds.) The track doesn't contain 'negative spaces.'

I can envisage 3 possible possibilities.
1.  The Company Balance is reduced to zero and the player takes 12 pounds from the Bank.
2. The Company Balance is reduced to zero and the player takes 10 pounds from the Bank.
3. The *[-]Company[/-] [I]Player[/I] takes 12 pounds from the Bank and the Company Balance is notionally reduced to -2 pounds, meaning that the Chairman needs to take debt in the Company Operation Phase (if the card were played before that phase) or the Company needs to take an Emergency Loan (if the card were played in the period between the Chairman acting in the Company Operating Phase and the payment of company expenses in the  Revenue Phase).

Reading the card literally and noting there is no negative space of the Company Balance Track, I'd guess that the answer would be #1.  But I'm not sure and might very well be overlooking something.

Rules as written on the card I'd agree it could be case #1. I think the thematic intent is that you can't take more out of the company than it has in its coffers so I'd be inclined to rule in the case of #2, personally.

I think ruling for #3 would be an absolutely fascinating move. I'm inclined to think Emergency Loans are a sub-rule of Expenses and this isn't, technically, an expense so I really don't think that would be the case.

The way we have always played this in playtests is #2 (can’t steal money that’s not there), but I agree this is an edge case that has strictly speaking not been covered.

Making the rules such that it can cause an immediate emergency loan would make the card too powerful. You could play it right after the Chairman had allocated funds to push the Company into bankruptcy.

Conversely, option #1 would allow you to “defraud” the Company at the Chairman phase without any consequences to the Company at all.

(BTW interesting to note that the card is, I think,  the only place suggesting a clean equivalence of scale between family money and Company money, whereas most of the game (the stock market mechanism) suggests they are on different scales)

Does the Company Balance reflect cash on hand, or just the accounting position reflecting assets over liabilities?  If the latter, then the argument that you can’t embezzle money that isn’t there isn’t necessarily fatal.  There might be cash (or other readily realisable asset) physically present that is notionally earmarked for the discharge of a liability but which is diverted to the fraudster instead, meaning that the fraudster pockets the funds leaving the company indebted in an amount that would have otherwise been discharged.

This is not to say that my proposed alternatives #1 and #3 are correct, just that they are conceptually plausible (at least to me!) depending on what Company Balance represents.  But I am too inexperienced with the game to impute any particular intent to the designer or to express an opinion on how the alternatives might unbalance the game.
