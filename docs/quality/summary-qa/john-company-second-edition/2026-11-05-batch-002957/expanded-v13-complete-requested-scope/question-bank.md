## Q001

During attrition, how does an office roll resolve, what extra modifier does the Chairman have, and where do a retiring member, card and fatigue go?

Roll separately per office card, +1 per fatigue and an additional +1 for Chairman. 1–2 nothing, 3–4 add fatigue, 5+ retire: member to Pensioners, card flipped into Vacant Offices, fatigue returned to supply.

Source: Rules p11; extraction lines 898–916

Required facts:

- Roll separately for each office card
- Add one per fatigue
- Result 1–2 changes nothing
- Result 3–4 adds fatigue
- Result 5+ retires the member
- Chairman adds an additional one
- Member goes to Pensioners
- Flip card into Vacant Offices
- Return fatigue to supply

## Q112

Who chooses whether and how many ordinary Company dividends are paid, what limits their affordability, which funds pay them, and what does each dividend cost and pay per share, including the Chairman? May an individual shareholder forgo payment as in a firm, and exactly when does total dividend payout increase Standing?

The Chairman may choose to pay no dividends or multiple dividends within the available Company Balance. Each dividend lowers Company Balance by the total number of Company shares and pays each owner £1 per share; the Chairman counts as a share for both cost and payout. Individual shareholders cannot use the firm-only waiver permission for Company dividends. If the total cash paid in dividends is strictly greater than Expectations, advance Company Standing one space; equality does not qualify.

## Q113

Where is the opportunity marker at the start of a game, and can the first chosen main Family action be taken twice under the marker’s normal repeat rule?

At game start the opportunity marker is not on any Family action. Therefore the first chosen main Family action cannot be doubled by the marker’s normal repeat rule, which requires the marker already to have been on that action at the start of the phase.

## Q114

## Q116

Who chooses whether an ordinary non-Hobnobbing firm pays dividends and how many? May the manager choose none even when India-treasury cash is available?

The firm manager chooses whether to pay dividends and how many, using available cash in the firm’s India treasury. The manager may choose zero even when cash is available; cash availability does not force a payout.
