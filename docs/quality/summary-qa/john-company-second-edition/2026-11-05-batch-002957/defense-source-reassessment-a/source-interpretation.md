# Independent source interpretation A — John Company Second Edition

The strength-1 Crisis fails. The Commander must exhaust the one active strength-2 local alliance by moving the entire piece to the bottom half of its Army box. Its two strength meets the defense requirement; no roll is made and no residual strength remains available. Remove unrest and award the Commander a trophy under p33. This is a reasoned application of the published sources, not a quotation of a scenario-specific FAQ.

## Application and meaning of “pieces”

A local alliance remains one physical piece for exhaustion. Its printed strength is its contribution to the defense comparison. Reading “pieces” in p33 as an unweighted count would disregard p22's express statement that alliance **strength** is usable for defense. More decisively, p29 calls two officers, two regiments, and Jagat Seths “collective strength of 6”: five physical pieces yield six defense strength. The illustration actually shows the alliance marked 2. Thus the rules themselves distinguish physical pieces exhausted from the strength they provide.

For Deploy, p22 expressly converts an exhausted alliance's printing into that many dice, whereas each officer/regiment provides one die. The player aid p1 calls this alliance printing “strength.” Defense is deterministic: use that strength in the comparison, with whole-piece exhaustion; there is no instruction to roll those dice in Mounting Defense. The general success-check rules on p17 concern checks, and do not turn this defense into a check. Company-controlled regions' zero strength when targeted in Deploy (p22) does not erase the Army's defense against a Crisis: p7 says its strength depends on the Army and p33 provides the defense procedure.

P33's isolated wording says “equal,” without explicitly writing “at least” or “weighted sum.” Read literally as an exact total of weighted strength, it would make the whole strength-2 alliance lose to strength 1. The player aid's Crisis table instead gives the shared heading “Successful (defender wins ties)” and expressly includes exhausted alliances as Company defender strength. Its table supplies the comparison: a stronger attacker succeeds; a defender that meets or exceeds it stops the attack. P32's Invasion and Rebellion text uses the same greater-than comparison, though those paragraphs alone are not a replacement for p33's Company procedure. Reading “equal” as satisfying the required amount reconciles the sources. This threshold application is an inference from their combined instructions, and should not be presented as a verbatim “excess strength is allowed” sentence.

The mandatory p33 instruction therefore requires using the sole available alliance, which commits two defense strength to the strength-1 Crisis. The Crisis fails. The piece is exhausted once as a whole, so it cannot be used again that turn. Its unused capacity is not stored. P22's “strength cannot be split among more than one purpose” bars dividing its strength between Deploy and defense; the separate “each piece can only be used once” also bars splitting the piece across two defenses. Describing both defenses as one general purpose does not create a second permitted use.

## Alternative readings and limits

- **One physical alliance always equals one defense:** rejected by p22 and the p29 strength-six example. Although it produces a successful strength-1 defense, it cannot explain the published weighting or a strength-2 defense.
- **Exact weighted equality only, so 2 loses to 1:** rejected as the combined-source application. It treats p33's compressed wording as an exact ceiling despite the aid's comparison and makes indivisible surplus defense harmful.
- **Use one pip now and keep the other:** rejected. The rule exhausts the whole piece, permits one use, and supplies no partial-exhaustion or retained-strength mechanism.
- **Use the whole alliance and waste surplus:** retained; it implements the strength and exhaustion rules together.

The same sole strength-2 alliance also stops a strength-2 Crisis (an exact tie). It cannot stop a strength-3-or-greater Crisis: exhaust it as all available defense, then resolve Region Loss. The same threshold interpretation allows a single indivisible alliance to exceed an attack's requirement; it does not authorize preserving its surplus for later.

The sources do not explicitly state a minimum-excess selection algorithm for every possible mixture of differently valued available pieces. This review does not decide whether gratuitous additional exhaustion is permitted when another subset already meets a defense. That adjacent selection question is unnecessary here. There is an editorial imprecision in p33's “equal” and “pieces,” but the supplied published rules and aid resolve the specified single-alliance outcome. A scenario-specific FAQ is not necessary for this conclusion.
