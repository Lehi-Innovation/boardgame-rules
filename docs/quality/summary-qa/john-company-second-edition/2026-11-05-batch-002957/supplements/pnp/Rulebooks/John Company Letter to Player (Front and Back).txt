Dear Player,
John Company is the result of nearly a decade of research, design, and development. 
It was a true labor of love and one that was only made possible through your support. 
We cannot thank you enough for making this game possible.
There’s no disguising the fact that this is a big game, and it is best digested slowly. We 
would suggest first reading the introduction on the cover of the rules. Then, punch 
out all of the components and organize them in the trays as described in the image 
below. As you punch the pieces, take a moment to cross-reference them with the first 
few pages of the rules, which will tell you a little about what they are. 
The top two trays are designed to be used during play. You can set them near the 
area of play to keep your table tidy. The bottom tray should remain in the box and is 
generally only used during setup. 
Once you have a handle on the components, I’d suggest making a cup of coffee or 
tea and setting aside an hour or two to read through the rules. While you read, you 
should ideally unfold the board and take a look at the back page of this letter to get 
your bearings. Treat the process like learning a new roleplaying game. Though the 
individual elements of the game are straightforward, there are a lot of them. Take your 
time.
Cole & Drew
When introducing John Company to new players, consider using a “rolling teach.” In this 
method, players will learn the game while playing. Before players arrive, complete the first 9 
setup steps and choose the 1710 scenario. Remove the Governor General from the law deck. 
When everyone is ready, spend the first five to ten minutes offering a general overview. You 
should describe the British East India Company and its three Presidencies. Then explain the 
game’s objective, the sequence of play, and the difference between victory points and power.
Once you’ve done that, deal the setup cards and walk each player through setup, identifying 
what the various symbols on the setup cards mean. I suggest giving yourself the setup cards 
with the Chairman and Prime Minister as these can be confusing roles for new players. 
Now, just start playing the game. As you get to each office, first describe its general function 
and then walk each player through their office card. Often it’s helpful to offer to take their 
first action for them. Players will find that concepts build upon each other, and by the second 
turn they will often be ready to take off the training wheels. For now, don’t worry about the 
finer points of negotiation. Players will figure those out on their own and you can offer some 
suggestions for deals during the second turn. Lastly, don’t attempt to explain the Events in 
India phase during your first game. Instead, resolve it yourself while narrating what happens.
