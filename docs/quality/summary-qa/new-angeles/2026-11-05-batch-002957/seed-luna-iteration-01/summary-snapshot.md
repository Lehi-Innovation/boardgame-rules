# New Angeles

## Overview

Four to six corporations negotiate to keep New Angeles producing resources while accumulating capital. Each player has a secret rival: ordinarily, beating that rival's capital is enough to win, and several players can win together. A possible Federalist instead needs the city to reach 25 threat while holding at least 25 capital.

This summary covers the complete base game and normal complete setup. The Learn to Play's simplified first-game setup and preset starting hands are excluded. No optional variants or expansions are included. Official FAQ v1.0 corrections apply throughout, including the prohibition on trading investments. The Rules Reference controls conflicts with Learn to Play; an asset ability takes precedence when it contradicts the Rules Reference, but obey both whenever possible.

## Components

The board has ten numbered districts in three tiers, five resource tracks in the shared supply, capital/threat/round tracks, and a five-slot turn track. The Root is outside the districts. The resources are energy, consumables, tech, entertainment and credits. Credits in the supply are distinct from players' capital.

There are six double-sided corporation sheets, six matching emergency action cards and six capital tokens; 100 action cards (20 each of construction, biotech, labor, media and security); 34 assets; 16 events; nine demands; 12 investments; seven rivals; and six setup cards. Plastic units comprise nine Human First, nine orgcrime and six prisec. Tokens comprise 14 each of outage, unrest, illness and development; five resource and five target tokens; plus threat, round and active-player markers. Four android tokens are supplied; the fourth is a spare replacement, not an additional android to put into play.

Components are limited to those supplied. If the reserve has no copy of a required component, do not place it. Removed components return to the reserve and can be reused.

## Setup

Use the complete setup below, rather than the introductory fixed city and preset hands. Resolve the steps in this order.

1. **Decks:** Shuffle assets, demands, events and investments separately. Place events in their board box and the other decks beside their matching slots. Separate actions into their five types and shuffle each into its own deck beside its matching slot.
2. **City and turn track:** Shuffle setup cards and draw one. For every component listed, place one copy in each listed district. Take the number of assets printed in the setup card's upper-right corner from the asset deck and place them facedown, one per turn-track slot, starting at slot 1. Return all setup cards to the box. Unrest/outage go in condition boxes, illness/development in their matching frames, units on district art.
3. **Tracks:** Put the round marker at 1 and each resource at 0. Reveal the top demand. Set each target token to the printed amount on its resource track.
4. **Corporations:** Each player chooses a corporation, taking its sheet and matching emergency action faceup. Use the side marked with the four-player icon at four players; otherwise use the five/six-player side. Melange is unchanged between sizes.
5. **First player and capital:** Randomly select one participating capital token. That corporation takes the active-player token. Set threat to 0. The active player starts at 10 capital; proceeding clockwise, the others start at 11, 12, 13, and so on.
6. **Hands:** Each player draws the number/types on their sheet, using its printed top-to-bottom order and the General rule below.
7. **Rivals:** Shuffle the participating corporations' rival cards together with the Federalist. Deal one facedown to each player; place the extra facedown and partly under the board. Secretly inspect your own rival without revealing or discussing it. Return unused corporations' rival cards to the box.
8. **Investments:** Each player draws two investments, keeps one facedown in their play area, and returns the other facedown to the top of the investment deck. Do not reveal the rejected card.
9. **Reserve:** Sort unused android, outage, unrest, illness and development tokens and Human First, orgcrime and prisec units into piles beside the board.

### Corporation draws and contracts

Draw panels below are in their operative top-to-bottom order. Each General entry is a separate draw: choose one action type not already drawn during this Refresh step, before seeing its cards. A multiple-card General entry would all come from one chosen type. Melange's three separate General entries therefore use three different types.

Except for Melange, a contract triggers only through resolving an action card; it does not matter which player resolves it. Movement out of a district does not count as removal. Returning something to the reserve at the Root does not count as removing it from a district, and triggers no district-removal contract.

## Turn Structure

There are six standard rounds and three demand rounds. The board's round track determines their sequence, with a demand round after each pair of standard rounds. Advance its marker at the end of each round. If threat reaches 25, the game ends immediately, even during a procedure.

### Standard round and active turn

A standard round has **Action → Production → Event** phases. The active-player token carries on clockwise between turns and across rounds; a new round does not reset first player. The setup card sets the first Action Phase's turn count; subsequent events set later turn counts. Some players may get no turn in a particular round.

During the Action Phase, the active player takes one turn:

1. Resolve assets that trigger **at the start of your turn**, before Refresh.
2. **Refresh:** Ready all your exhausted assets, then draw your sheet's action cards in printed top-to-bottom order. Apply the General restrictions above.
3. **Resolve one deal**, as described under Actions.
4. **End of Turn:** You may use each of your End of Turn asset abilities once, in your chosen order. If you receive another asset with that header during this step, you may use its ability this turn too. Then discard action cards until your hand is no larger than your hand size (normally five). Emergencies are outside the hand and do not count. Pass the active-player token left.

If no assets remain on the turn track at the end of the turn, the Action Phase ends. Otherwise, the new active player takes another turn. The last deal still has its full end-of-turn step before Production begins.

### Production Phase

Exploit every district containing an android, in ascending district-number order. A district without an android is not exploited in normal Production. Exploiting has two parts in order:

1. Produce its resources, applying the existing production modifiers below and increasing the matching supply tracks.
2. Increase its unrest by one stage, or two if it contains Human First. This still happens when it produced nothing. Apply the special Human First outage rule below.

Production does not trigger illness. After all android districts are exploited, proceed to Event.

### Event Phase

Reveal the top event and resolve it from top to bottom: its upper effect, then one copy of each listed component in every district beside that component, then the indicated next turn count. Place that many assets from the top of their deck facedown on the turn track, starting with slot 1, one per slot. The event's lower-right number is the turn count. Put the resolved event on the bottom of the event deck and advance the round marker.

Event backs warn of a relevant component; location and tier can affect the printed threat result. They are warnings, not a separate automatic threat rule: resolve the actual front's instructions. Components can spill along paths during placement and cause additional Root threat. Illness's action-triggered effect does not trigger from events. Melange checks the total threat increase during the entire event, including its placements. The turn track need not be reset after the sixth standard round because there is no subsequent Action Phase.

### Demand round

Resolve these steps in order:

1. **Score Investment:** Reveal each player's facedown investment and award its printed capital. If an investment requires a resource to meet demand and that resource is below its target, it yields zero. Apply its printed formula and cap when eligible.
2. **Pay Demand:** If every resource's supply equals or exceeds its target, demand is met and no threat is added. If even one is short, demand fails: increase threat once by the demand card's printed failure amount. Investments were already scored before this check. If this is the third demand round, the game ends immediately after this step; omit steps 3–4. Threat reaching 25 still ends the game immediately and uses the Federalist ending.
3. **Reveal Demand:** Reset every resource to 0, put the old demand under its deck, reveal the new top demand and set each target to the new printed value. This reset happens whether the previous demand succeeded or failed; surplus is not carried over.
4. **Choose Investment:** Return all investments to their deck and shuffle. Each player draws two, keeps one facedown, and returns the other facedown to the top without revealing it. Advance to the next standard round.

Sources: RR §§3–4, 6.6, 16, 18, 22, 24–27, 30, 33, 44, 51, 55, 67; LTP pp6–10; FAQ Investments, Illness, Contracts.

## Actions

### Resolving a deal

There are five steps. Cards offered and cards supporting an offer have different roles. Only the winning offer's instructions resolve; support cards count as cards, regardless of their text/type.

1. **Asset:** Reveal the rightmost remaining asset on the turn track (highest-numbered occupied slot), read it aloud and put it in Revealed Asset.
2. **Main Offer:** The active player must place one action from hand or a faceup emergency faceup in Main Offer. They cannot pass.
3. **Counteroffer:** Starting left of the active player, each other player gets exactly one clockwise chance to pass or propose one hand action or faceup emergency faceup in Counteroffer. Before adding it, discard hand actions equal to the faceup cards already in that box: first counteroffer costs 0 additional cards, second 1, third 2, etc. Place the new offer on top. Only the topmost is the current counteroffer; previous offers only affect this escalation cost. Its proposer becomes the countering player, replacing the prior one. Another copy of an already offered action is legal. The active player cannot counter unless an ability explicitly permits it. There is no second circuit around the table.
4. **Support:** With no counteroffer, skip this step. Otherwise, everyone except the active player and current countering player is a supporting player, including a displaced earlier counterer. Starting with the first supporter left of the active player, each gets exactly one clockwise chance to support or abstain. Support uses one or more hand actions facedown beside either offer. A player cannot support both offers or get a later second opportunity to add cards. Active/countering players cannot support without explicit ability permission. Emergencies cannot support.
5. **Resolution:** The offer with more facedown support cards wins. A tie, including 0–0, goes to the active player's main offer. The winner resolves their offer and **then** claims the revealed asset faceup in their play area. All played ordinary actions, including superseded/losing offers and support, go facedown to the shared discard. Return every proposed emergency to its original logo corporation: face down if it won, face up otherwise.

### Negotiation and trading

Players can trade capital, assets and promises at any time; gifts of capital/assets without return are legal. Players **cannot trade action cards, emergency cards, investments, rivals or corporation sheets** (FAQ-corrected RR65.5). An ability explicitly giving, stealing or swapping a normally untradeable card is distinct from freely trading it.

An agreed trade that all participants can immediately and completely fulfill is binding and must be completed. Trades involving three or more players are binding when every part can resolve immediately and simultaneously. A future promise is nonbinding; either party may renege. If players cannot agree before the trade whether it is binding, it is nonbinding. For example, buying support at the moment that supporter must choose can be binding; buying a promise before that player's support opportunity is nonbinding. Trading an exhausted asset does not ready it, and receiving it does not count as claiming it.

### Emergency actions

An emergency is kept outside your hand, faceup initially. Only a faceup emergency may be offered as main offer or counteroffer. Winning uses it: it returns facedown and cannot be offered again unless an effect flips it faceup. Losing or superseded emergencies return faceup. Always return it to the original corporation, even if another player stole and offered it. It cannot be used as support, counted toward hand size, or taken/discarded by an ordinary effect targeting actions in hand.

Resolve the printed instructions in order. Quantities expressed as “up to” are optional within that limit; later “then” consequences still belong to the action. Ordinary placement, unrest, production, illness and component-limit rules apply unless the card explicitly overrides them.

| Corporation / emergency | Effect |
|---|---|
| Globalsec — Ruthless Tactics | Remove up to 4 enemy units from the city. Then increase unrest by 1 stage in 2 districts not in strike or outage. |
| Haas-Bioroid — Underpaid, Overclocked | Choose up to 2 resources and increase each chosen supply quantity by 1. Then gain 4 capital and place 2 Human First in the city. |
| Jinteki — Mandatory Quarantine | Remove up to 4 illness tokens from the city. Then place 2 outages in the city. Illness tokens do not increase threat during this action. |
| Melange Mining — Energy For All | Starting with the active player and proceeding clockwise, each player draws 4 actions of a type of their own choice. Then increase threat by 1. |
| NBN — Sweeps Week | Reduce unrest by up to 4 stages in any districts. Then place 2 illnesses in any districts. |
| Weyland Consortium — Massive Reconstruction | Remove up to 3 outages from the city. Then place 2 orgcrime in the city. |

Sources: RR §§1–2, 14–16, 21, 34, 37, 59–60, 65; LTP pp10, 14; printed emergency faces; FAQ Deals and Trading.

## Scoring / Victory Conditions

Capital is personal wealth, tracked on the capital track. If it rises above 39, flip the token to its “40” side and wrap to 0; the current space then represents 40 plus its number. Flip back when capital falls below 40. Bright multiples of five are only visual aids.

The game ends either immediately when threat reaches 25 or after Pay Demand in the third demand round. Reveal rivals at the ending.

| Ending / rival | Result |
|---|---|
| Third demand finishes with threat below 25; rival names another corporation | Win if your capital is strictly higher than that corporation's. A tie loses. |
| Third demand finishes below 25; rival is your own corporation | Win if you strictly exceed at least 2 other players in a four-player game, or at least 3 in a five/six-player game. Tied players do not count as beaten. |
| Third demand finishes below 25; Federalist | Federalist loses, regardless of capital. |
| Threat reaches 25 at any time, including failure of the final demand | Every ordinary corporation loses. Only the Federalist can win, and only with at least 25 capital at that moment; otherwise everyone loses. |

Each ordinary player checks their own victory independently; highest overall capital is not required. There may be several winners. There may be no Federalist: their rival card might be the spare under the board. A Federalist reaching 25 threat before a demand's investment step cannot wait to score an investment; immediate ending applies. If final demand failure causes the threshold, that demand's investment has already scored.

Sources: RR §§7, 18.1–18.2, 28, 50, 63, 69; LTP pp9, 15.

## Special Rules & Edge Cases

### Production, unrest and outages

The primary resource is the leftmost, circled icon; a second uncircled icon is secondary. A district with just one resource has a primary and no secondary. Produce the quantities printed below those icons, with these modifiers whenever a district produces, including card-directed production:

| Condition | Production / exploitation effect |
|---|---|
| Stable or protest | Can produce normally. |
| Strike | Produces no resources. Ordinary unrest increases cannot go beyond strike. |
| Outage | Produces no resources. Treat unrest as stable and unable to increase while the outage remains. |
| Development | Add 1 to the primary quantity only; never changes secondary. |
| Orgcrime | Produces no primary, even if developed. Secondary is still available. A one-resource district with orgcrime produces nothing. |
| Human First | Changes the unrest increase upon exploitation from 1 to 2 stages; does not itself prevent that exploitation's initial production. |

Supply tracks cap at their last numbered space, and resources at 0 cannot be reduced further. Production is resolved before exploitation's unrest increase: a district starting in protest can produce and then go on strike. A district with an android is still exploited if strike, outage or orgcrime prevents production.

Unrest progresses **stable → protest → strike**, and decreases in reverse order. Stable has no unrest token; protest uses its protest side; strike its strike side. A decrease from protest to stable removes the token. Stable cannot decrease; strike cannot normally increase. Unrest and development tokens never move along paths.

Human First is a specific exception upon exploitation. From stable, its two-stage increase reaches strike. From protest, strike or outage, unrest cannot increase by two, so place an outage. A newly placed outage removes any unrest token and leaves the district stable; this is **not** an NBN contract decrease. If already in outage, the new outage must move. No other ordinary capped unrest increase creates an outage. Removing an outage leaves stable unrest.

### Placement, movement and the Root

A district can have at most one each of android, development, illness, outage, prisec, Human First and orgcrime. Different types can coexist except that prisec forces enemy units to move. Human First and orgcrime are enemies; prisec is not. Unrest/outage share the condition box, with outage removing unrest.

Placing means taking a component from the reserve. If a newly placed or arriving enemy, illness or outage would duplicate its type in a district, that incoming component moves along the district's exiting arrow toward its higher-numbered destination. Continue through successive destinations until it can stay or reaches the Root. If an enemy is placed/moved into a prisec district, or prisec is placed into a district with enemies, each affected enemy moves; prisec does not remove the enemy. The path exiting Base de Cayambe (10) leads to the Root.

The Root is not a district and not part of the city of ten districts. Each enemy, illness or outage that reaches it returns to reserve and adds 2 threat. Moving out of a district is not removal, and the Root return triggers no removal contract. By contrast, an effect that removes a token/unit from a district returns it to reserve and can satisfy the appropriate action-triggered contract.

Android movement is different: move to any district, ignoring paths. Move several androids one at a time and never into a district already containing an android. This prevents directly swapping two occupied android positions as though movement were simultaneous.

### Illness and involved districts

When an action resolves and involves at least one ill district, add **2 threat total**, regardless of how many ill districts were involved. A district is involved if it is chosen for the action, or a token/unit is placed there, removed from it, or moved into/out of it. An attempted placement/move still involves the intended destination if prevented by other components or component shortage. Actions increasing/decreasing unrest choose districts and can therefore trigger illness. An explicit action exception that illness does not increase threat overrides this effect.

Exploitation and event cards do not trigger illness's action effect. An illness token entering the Root still causes the separate 2-threat Root increase, just like an enemy or outage.

### Assets, claiming, exhaustion and theft

Gaining an asset puts it faceup in your play area. Winning a deal **claims** its revealed asset; trading, stealing or swapping merely gains it and does not trigger a “when you claim” ability. Discarded assets go facedown to the bottom of the asset deck.

To exhaust an asset, rotate it 90 degrees clockwise. An exhausted asset cannot be exhausted again. Ready your exhausted assets at the start of Refresh, after any start-of-turn triggers. Exhausted assets stay exhausted when traded or stolen. If you control several abilities with the same timing, choose their order. If multiple players use same-timing assets, resolve them clockwise from the active player. Abilities saying “may” and End of Turn abilities are optional; apply the once-per-ability end-of-turn limit in Turn Structure.

Stealing capital makes its original owner lose the amount and the thief gain that amount. Stealing a hand action keeps it secret from the other players. Ordinary action theft cannot take an emergency. Bryan Stinson's explicit ability, when claimed, discards him to steal 2 emergency actions from participating corporations. Those emergencies may be faceup or facedown and keep their state while stolen; they still return to their original owner on discard, facedown if winning and faceup otherwise. Unused corporations' emergencies cannot be stolen.

Alex Westlund can swap assets. If the player using him swaps one of their own assets, including Alex himself, that player loses 2 capital; swapped cards are not claimed.

### Discards, replenishment and information

All ordinary actions are discarded facedown to the shared discard pile; players cannot look at them as discarded or while in that pile. If a requested action type is empty when drawing, shuffle the entire shared discard, reveal and sort its cards by type, then shuffle each type into its respective deck. This replenishes all affected action decks, not merely the requested type. Assets instead go facedown under their own deck. Chosen investments remain facedown until demand; unchosen investments are not revealed. Do not inspect the investment deck after cards are dealt.

Players cannot tell others which actions are in their hands, and cannot lie about hand counts. Actions stolen or given by an effect remain secret from others. Rivals cannot be revealed or discussed. Do not inspect facedown turn-track assets. Event fronts seen with a look/reorder ability must stay secret; event backs, including the back of the deck's top card, are public.

## Player Reference

- **Turn:** Start-of-turn assets → ready/draw → deal → optional End of Turn abilities once each → discard to five → pass left. Empty turn track at turn end starts Production.
- **Deal:** Rightmost asset → mandatory main offer → one counteroffer chance each clockwise, escalating extra discards → one support chance each eligible player → most cards wins, main wins ties → resolve offer, claim asset, discard/return cards.
- **Production:** Android districts lowest first; produce under existing modifiers, then unrest +1 or Human First +2/outage. Illness does not trigger.
- **Event:** Top card top-to-bottom, including placements and next turn count; bottom the event, advance round. Melange checks all threat from the entire event.
- **Demand:** Score investment → check all five demands → reset supply/reveal new demand → choose investment. Third demand stops after the check.
- **Threat:** Illness in an action +2 total; each enemy/illness/outage to Root +2; failed demand printed amount; events as printed. At 25, stop immediately and check Federalist capital.
- **Victory:** Below 25 after final demand, strictly beat your named rival, or enough other players if you drew your own corporation. At 25 threat only a Federalist with at least 25 capital can win.
