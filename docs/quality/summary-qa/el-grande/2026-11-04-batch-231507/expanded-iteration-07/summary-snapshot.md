## Overview

El Grande is a majority game for 2–5 players in 15th-century Spain. Recruit Caballeros from the provinces into your court, then deploy and move them to control regions. Power cards determine turn order and recruitment; action cards give ordinary deployment plus an optional special action. The hidden Castillo is scored and emptied before the other regions during general scoring after rounds **3, 6 and 9**. The game ends **after the third general scoring**, not before the final regions have scored. The player furthest along the success track wins.

This summary covers the **classic 1996 English base game**, including all base Action Card explanations in publisher PDF pages 1–12 (Jay Tummelson translation/editing). Expansions on pages 13–20, later editions and the optional short game are outside its scope. Page references below refer to that publisher PDF.

## Components

- Board: nine regions, Castillo space, success track and nine-round track with three scoring tracks.
- Five Grandes; 155 Caballeros (31 in each color, including each player's scoring marker); King and round marker.
- 65 power cards: values 1–13 per color, each showing a recruitment allowance of 0–6 Caballeros.
- 45 action cards: eleven each in stacks 1–4 and the single King card in stack 5.
- Nine region cards, five secret disks, Castillo and two mobile scoreboards (8/4/0 and 4/0/0).

## Setup

1. Put the Castillo on its board space. Shuffle the nine region cards; reveal the top card and put the King in that region. Each player takes another region card to determine their home region and puts their chosen-color Grande there.
2. Each player takes ten Caballeros: **two** in their home region beside the Grande, **one** on the first space of the success track and **seven** in their court in front of them. All remaining Caballeros form the **provinces**, the reserve on the table.
3. Give each player their color's thirteen power cards and a secret disk. The disk shows the nine board regions, **not the Castillo**.
4. Separate action cards into stacks by the 1–5 Caballeros printed on their backs. Shuffle each stack separately and place it face down beside the board. Place both mobile scoreboards beside the board.
5. Start the round marker at **1** for the standard game. Players **choose** who begins playing power cards in round 1. (Publisher pp.2–3.)

## Turn Structure

Each round:

1. In rounds after the first, advance the round marker one space. Reveal the top card of each of the five action stacks: five face-up choices, including the King card.
2. Play one power card per player face up. In round 1 the chosen starter begins; later, the player who **took the last turn in the preceding round** begins. Others play **clockwise**. No value already played this round may be matched. Played cards remain in each player's face-up used stack with **only the top card visible**, rather than returning to hand. A special action can recover a played card.
3. Take turns in **descending power-card value**. At the start of your turn recruit **up to** the number of your Caballeros pictured on your power card from the provinces into your court; recruiting fewer is allowed. If there are insufficient Caballeros of your color in the provinces, you may take your own from the board to make up the difference. The King's region remains taboo and the Castillo cannot supply pieces outside general scoring.
4. After recruitment, choose an available face-up action card. It supplies two separate activities: ordinary deployment from court and its special action. Do them in **either order**, but complete one activity before starting the other: **neither may be split or interleaved**. You may decline the special action, including choosing a card to prevent anyone else using it.
5. Lay aside the selected action card after your turn; later players cannot choose it. After every player has taken a turn, discard any remaining face-up action cards. The King card is revealed again every round and **never permanently discarded**.

After rounds 3, 6 and 9, perform general scoring before proceeding. (Publisher pp.3–4,7.)

## Actions

### Ordinary deployment and protected locations

The action card's number is a **maximum**: you may deploy fewer, including none. Stacks 1–5 allow up to 1–5 Caballeros respectively. Move them **from your court** to any regions **adjacent to the King's current region** and/or the Castillo, dividing the allowance freely. Special actions have the broader origins, destinations and ownership limits stated in their individual descriptions.

The King's region is **taboo**: no Caballero, Grande or mobile scoreboard may move into or out of it. Special actions do not override this restriction. It may still be scored. Actions that move the King are the exception: moving him changes which region is protected. If you move the King before ordinary deployment, use his **new** position to determine adjacent destinations; if you deploy first, use his old position.

Whenever adding Caballeros to the board or moving them, any or all may go into the **Castillo**, regardless of the King's location. Announce clearly how many you put inside. No action removes Caballeros from the Castillo; they leave **only during general scoring**. Special scoring of the Castillo reveals and scores its contents, then covers them again without redistribution. (Publisher pp.4–5,7.)

### Stack 1 — Intrigue

Each card also allows the normal up-to-one court deployment. The special action is optional. All relocation origins below exclude the **King's region and Castillo**; destinations exclude the King's region but may include the Castillo. Ordinary deployment retains its adjacency restriction.

| Special action | What it permits |
|---|---|
| All your own Caballeros from one region | Take all your own from one region and distribute among other regions and/or Castillo. You may return some to the original region. |
| Two more from court | Up to two additional own Caballeros from court to **any** non-King regions and/or Castillo. With the normal one, up to three enter the board, but only the normal one requires adjacency. |
| Two more from court **or** all your own from one region | Choose **one** of the preceding special actions, not both. Normal deployment still applies. |
| Up to five from one region | Up to five own and/or foreign Caballeros from **one** origin region, distributed to **other** non-King regions and/or Castillo. |
| Any three foreign | Up to three **other players'** Caballeros from any **non-King board regions**, including multiple origins, **never from Castillo**. Destinations: any non-King regions and/or Castillo. |
| Any three | Up to three own and/or foreign Caballeros from any non-King origin regions to **any** non-King regions and/or Castillo. |
| Two own and two foreign | Up to **two of your own plus two foreign**, from any regions; these are separate ownership allowances. |
| Any four of your own | Up to four **own only**, from any non-King origin regions into **other** non-King regions and/or Castillo. |
| Any four | Up to four own and/or foreign, from any non-King origin regions into **other** non-King regions and/or Castillo. The detailed three-piece card says **any regions**, whereas this four-piece card says **other regions**. |

The table preserves the publisher's destination wording. For multi-origin effects, it does not add a universal prohibition on every selected origin region as a destination; the source gives no worked exchange example resolving that extra interpretation. Only the all-own-from-one-region effect expressly permits returning some to the original region; preserve the **other regions** restriction where specified. All table entries remain subject to the taboo and Castillo rules above. (Publisher pp.7–8.)

### Stack 2 — Veto, returns and special scoring

Each card also allows up to **two** ordinary deployments.

- **Veto:** Put the card before you and retain it to stop **one special action** during this round or the next. It cannot stop the ordinary deployment portion. Other players must announce special actions before executing them while a Veto is held. Object as soon as the player starts; you can stop an announced action before execution or prevent the remaining part of an **incomplete** special action. Completed actions cannot be vetoed. You may prevent just part; already executed parts are not undone. Discard after using the veto once; if unused, discard at the **end of the next round**.
- **Decay of Authority — all court:** Every fellow player sends all Caballeros in their court to the provinces; an empty court sends none. The selecting player is unaffected.
- **Decay of Authority — three from court:** Every fellow player sends three court Caballeros to the provinces, or **all they have if fewer than three**. The selecting player is unaffected.
- **The King returns:** The detailed execution paragraph and printed card say **each player**, including the selecting player, sends **three own Caballeros** to the provinces. Start with the player to the selecting player's left, then proceed clockwise, with the selecting player last. Each chooses their own pieces from any regions and/or their court, **never the King's region or Castillo**. The bold heading instead says “fellow players”; this summary follows the detailed execution paragraph and matching card face. The source supplies **no explicit shortfall rule** if a player has fewer than three eligible pieces.
- **Send one from each player:** The selecting player chooses one Caballero from a region for **each player, including themselves**, and returns each to its owner's provinces. No taking from courts, the King's region or Castillo. The source gives no explicit fallback when a player has no eligible piece.
- **Fellow players secretly remove two:** Each affected fellow player secretly chooses **one** region on their disk, with choices simultaneous and revealed together. It must contain at least two of that player's Caballeros; return two to the provinces. The stated singleton fallback applies if the player has **at most one in every region**: choose a region with one and return that one. It does **not supply a verified fallback** for a player with two or more in the protected King's region and only singletons elsewhere. Never take from the King's region or Castillo. The source does not give an explicit procedure for no eligible pieces.
- **Fellow players secretly remove all from one region:** Simultaneous secret disk selection, then reveal together. Each affected fellow player chooses a region with at least one of their Caballeros and returns **all their own there** to the provinces. A player with none in any region is unaffected. No taking from the King's region or Castillo.
- **Pick one region to score:** Choose any region, **including the King's region or Castillo**. Use normal region-scoring rules, applicable rank limits and bonuses. If Castillo is chosen, score it and cover the same pieces again; do not distribute them.

For the two secret-removal cards, the card faces and headings assign removal to **fellow players**. The explanatory paragraphs say **all players** select disks without reconciling the selecting player's participation. They do not explicitly require that player to remove pieces. The source also does not explain legal disk selection if a player's only pieces are in the protected King's region. These are publisher-text gaps; the taboo still prohibits removing those pieces. (Publisher pp.7,9–10.)

### Stack 3 — Special scoring

Each card also allows up to **three** ordinary deployments. All special scorings follow normal region scoring except their specified selection or rank restrictions. Applicable home and King's bonuses apply. A **mobile scoreboard replaces the printed values** for both points and category eligibility.

| Card | Regions/locations scored |
|---|---|
| All 4-value regions | Regions whose current **first** value is 4. Printed group: Galicia 4/2/0, Catalonia 4/2/1, Seville 4/3/1. A mobile 8/4/0 in Galicia removes it from this group; a mobile 4/0/0 in Granada adds Granada. |
| All 5-value regions | Current first value 5. Printed group: Basque Country 5/3/1, Aragon 5/4/1, Valencia 5/3/2. **Castillo is not scored.** |
| All 6- and 7-value regions | Current first value 6 or 7. Printed group: Old Castile 6/4/2, New Castile 7/4/2, Granada 6/3/1. |
| Castillo | Uncover and score it, then put the Castillo over the **same Caballeros**; redistribution waits until general scoring. |
| First position only in all regions | Only sole first places score; other ranks get nothing. **A tie for first scores nothing**, since it would normally pay second place. Excludes Castillo. |
| Region(s) with most Caballeros | Compare total Caballeros in each board region. Score **all** regions tied for the most. Castillo is neither considered nor scored. |
| Region(s) with least Caballeros | Compare nonempty board regions; score **all** tied for the least. Empty regions and Castillo are neither considered nor scored. |
| Choose any region | Any region, including King's region or Castillo. Scoring Castillo does not empty it. |

(Publisher pp.10–11.)

### Stack 4 — Movement, court and scoring

Each card also allows up to **four** ordinary deployments.

- **Mobile scoreboard:** Introduce a scoreboard from beside the board or move one already on it to a different region. Castillo is allowed. No movement into or out of the King's region. The two mobile scoreboards **cannot be stacked on one another**. Place them right side up as **8/4/0 or 4/0/0**, never reversed or rotated to invent other values.
- **Royal Adviser:** Move the King into a **neighboring region**, no more than one region away.
- **Movement:** Choose a region; all fellow players move **all their own Caballeros** out of it. Each affected player secretly chooses another region with their disk; reveal choices simultaneously and move each player's group to their chosen region. Choosing **the King's region or the region being vacated** sends that player's pieces to **their court** instead. The taboo prevents taking pieces out of the King's region; Castillo cannot be emptied with this card.
- **Grande:** Move your Grande to another region except the **King's region or Castillo**. It cannot leave the King's region. A destination may already contain several Grandes. The destination becomes your new home region.
- **Power Cards:** Return any **one** of your played power cards to hand, **including the one played this turn**. You need not reveal which; taking the visible top card will of course be apparent.
- **The Court:** Move **two of your own** Caballeros from provinces to court. The source gives no explicit shortfall procedure if the provinces cannot supply both. This is separate from the card's ordinary up-to-four court-to-board deployment; either complete activity can go first.
- **Each secretly chooses a region to score:** All players choose secretly on disks and reveal simultaneously. The disk permits the **nine board regions, including the King's region**, but **not Castillo**; scoring is allowed in the King's region. Score each **uniquely chosen** region under normal rules. If multiple players chose a region, **it is not scored**.

(Publisher pp.11–12.)

### Stack 5 — King

The sole card allows up to **five** ordinary deployments and the optional special action: put the King in **any region except Castillo**. This is a wider choice than Royal Adviser's neighboring-region move. Use the King’s current location at the time of ordinary deployment. (Publisher p.12.)

## Scoring / Victory Conditions

### General scoring after rounds 3, 6 and 9

Carry out these stages in order:

1. **Secretly select destinations before uncovering the Castillo.** Each player sets their disk to any board region **except the King's region** and places it face down. All that player's Castillo Caballeros must go to **one** region. **Choosing the King's region is prohibited.**
2. **Score the Castillo.** Once all disks are face down, lift it and count the Caballeros. Score it like another region, using its scoreboard and the player-count/tie rules below.
3. **Reveal disks and empty the Castillo.** Move every player's Castillo Caballeros to that player's selected region. If someone **illegally selected the King's region**, their pieces go to **their court** instead. This consequence handles an illegal selection; it does **not** make the King's region a permitted choice. Replace the Castillo empty; none may be retained inside or split between destinations.
4. **Score the nine regions** in the scoring-track order, moving the round marker along it to avoid omissions or double counting. Castillo arrivals therefore affect these majorities. Move success-track markers to record all points.

(Publisher p.5.)

### Region ranks, ties and bonuses

Only **Caballeros** count toward majority. A Grande marks a home region and contributes **zero Caballeros**.

Use the region's current scoreboard: first number for most, second for second most, third for third most. **Two players:** only first place can pay. **Three players:** only first and second can pay. **Four or five:** first three can pay. Positions beyond the payable ranks score zero.

When players tie for a rank, **each gets the next rank's value**, irrespective of how many players are tied. The next lower group takes the rank after the one paid to the tied group: the source's three-way first-place tie pays all three **second**, then the next player **third**. A tie for third drops to fourth and pays **zero**. Apply the player-count cutoff after this rank shift: a two-player tie for first pays neither player.

Publisher scoring examples:

- On **5/3/1**, counts **4,4,4,3** score **3,3,3,1**.
- On **6/4/2**, counts **4,3,2,2** score **6,4,0,0**.

Bonuses apply whenever the relevant region is scored, **including special scorings**:

- **King's bonus:** +2 for the **sole** majority holder in the King's region.
- **Home bonus:** +2 if the **sole** majority holder has their **own Grande** in that region.

Neither bonus is awarded on a tie for first. (Publisher pp.5–6,10.)

### End

Finish all stages of the **third general scoring**, after round 9. Whoever is furthest along the success track wins. The source gives no additional endgame tiebreaker. (Publisher p.6.)

## Special Rules & Edge Cases

- Choose and resolve each activity as a whole; you cannot deploy some, execute a special action, then resume deployment.
- Special-action movement has no general adjacency requirement, but always respects the King's taboo and the prohibition on removing pieces from Castillo before general scoring.
- Special scoring of Castillo reveals its pieces without emptying it. General scoring always empties it **before** the board regions score.
- Source limitations on certain return-action shortfalls and secret-disk participation are identified under Stack 2. The source does not authorize removing protected pieces to resolve those gaps; an official clarification would be needed for a definitive missing procedure. It also does not reconcile the general permission to move Caballeros into Castillo with the forced Movement card's use of disks omitting Castillo, or explicitly resolve all-4 scoring when the 4/0/0 mobile scoreboard is at Castillo. Those cases need clarification rather than an assumed exception.

## Player Reference

**Round:** Reveal five cards → play unique power values clockwise → turns high to low → discard unused face-up actions. Start marker at 1 and advance in subsequent rounds.

**Turn:** Recruit up to power-card allowance → choose action → complete deployment and special action in either order → lay aside card.

**Deployment:** Up to stack number from court → adjacent to current King and/or Castillo. Special action optional; no interleaving.

**General scoring:** After 3,6,9: choose disks secretly → score Castillo → reveal disks/empty Castillo → score regions. Finish third general scoring to end game.
