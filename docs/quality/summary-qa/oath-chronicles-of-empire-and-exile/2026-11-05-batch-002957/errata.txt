Oath Clarifications and Errata
Last updated: November 15, 2022


If you have any questions that are not answered here or in the forums, you can submit a question to us here.


Law Clarifications


1.17. Relic Setup.
Do the four relics that go in the Imperial Reliquary get placed at random, or do I get to choose which relic goes on which space?
They are placed randomly on the Imperial Reliquary spaces.


2.11. Oathkeeper Title. 
Is the defense die added from the Oathkeeper title considered a campaign modifier?
Yes! 


Since players both lose rule (5.5.6) and gain rule (5.5.7) of sites during a Campaign action, when exactly do I transfer the Oathkeeper of Supremacy title?
At the end of the Campaign action. (I may add an errata below, but I’m figuring out whether there’s a better wording.)


3.2. Visionary Win. 
When a Vision says I need the “most sites” or “most relics and banners,” does that include ties?
Nope! In order to qualify for, you need to have more than each other player, not tied.


4.3.3. Refresh Supply.
As a Citizen, do I refresh to the exact space of the Chancellor’s Supply marker, or do I count up the Chancellor’s warbands? Also, what happens with the leftmost space? There’s no Chancellor mask on my leftmost Supply space.
You refresh to the exact space of the Chancellor’s Supply marker, so if their marker is in the leftmost space (good on them), you refresh to the leftmost space. For clarity, that space should have the Chancellor mask.


5.4. Recover.
Can I recover the Banner of the Darkest Secret if the holder’s site has no cards?
Nope! Likewise, you cannot do so if there is only a ruin at the site, since ruins do not have suits.


Can I recover the Banner of the Darkest Secret if there is a suited card at the holder’s site, but the holder has no advisers?
Yes!


5.5.2. Step 2: Declare Targets and Collect Dice Pools (Campaign).
If the defender’s pawn is at my site, does targeting one of their relics fulfill the “you must declare at least one target at your site” requirement?
Yes it does. This use of “at your site” is a little inconsistent with other uses that refer to things that are physically on the map at the site, so I will likely tweak this wording to clarify in future printings.


5.6. Travel.
How exactly do Travel costs get modified?
First, check for any modifications of the Travel cost to a specific number, such as the Shrouded Wood. Then, modify it with any other Travel modifiers, such as the Decadent power on the Imperial Reliquary. Any Travel modifier that says “Spend no Supply” completely overrides the Travel cost (7.6.2).


7.1.3. Use.
Does “as much as possible” imply that I can use a power even if it would have no effect?
Yes! For example, you could put a favor on Sleight of Hand to pay for it even if there was no one around to take a secret from.


7.5.3. Pay Limit. 
The clarification says I can only use a battle plan “once.” What does that mean?
It means you cannot use the same battle plan multiple times in a single Campaign action.


Glossary. “Give” and “Take” entries.
If some power says, for example, that I cannot “give secrets,” does that mean that other players cannot take secrets from me?
Nope! The “give” and “take” keywords are distinct, and something modifying how you give does not modify how you or others take.


Rules Errata and Additions


Law
2.8. Site Cards.
Add the following text “Facedown sites cannot hold cards.”
(Even though the original rules largely prevented cards from being placed at facedown sites, there were a couple sneaky ways to get around this, so it’s best to make this explicit.)


5.4.1. Recover, Spend 1 Supply and Choose Target. Change the following clarification in error: “(Ignore ruined edifices and facedown advisers, since they have no suit. You cannot recover the Darkest Secret from another player if their site has no cards, or if they have no faceup advisers, but you can recover it if they have no faceup advisers.)”


5.5 Campaign. There is some confusion about how to interpret Imperial Allies’ power sharing permissions, and timing. Use this guidance to interpret them:


Interpret uses of “you,” “your enemy,” “the defender,” etc. on powers to include the entire side—the declared defender and any Allies. However, "your site" and "your board" still refer specifically to the site of your pawn and your own board. Here are some examples:
* Mercenaries: “you’re defeated” -> “your side is defeated” 
* True Names: “against you” -> “against your side” 
* Gleaming Armor: “Your enemy’s” -> “The enemy side’s” 
* Specialist: “the defender” -> “the defending side” 
* Jinx: “you roll” -> “your side rolls”
* Oathkeeper Bonus: "the defender" -> "the defending side" (Meaning this bonus is shared among the Chancellor and Imperial Allies on defense in non-Supremacy games.)
* Peace Envoys, Code of Honor: "you cannot use other battle plans" -> "your side cannot use other battle plans"
* Warning Signals: No change. Read as written.


In rare situations where the defender and/or Allies disagree about timing, priority, or order of who can use a battle plan and when, the declared defender chooses the order. 
(For example, if two players want to use the same power, the declared defender chooses who gets to use the power. If a player might or might not choose to use Code of Honor, which would restrict other battle plans from being used, the declared defender can force them to say whether they are using Code of Honor or not before the declared defender chooses which battle plans they are using.)


5.5.2 Step 2: Declare Targets and Collect Dice Pools. Edit the last sentence to end with “in their FORCE (on their board).” (This just helps with interpretation of powers like Captains.)


5.5.7.I Take Rule of Any Targeted Sites. The Empire box in this section should be moved to and combined with the Empire box in 5.5.6. (This should not affect resolution. It’s just a bit out of place.) 


7.6.4 Adviser Limits. Some powers (Assassin, Family Wagon, etc.) change your adviser limit. When you want to play a card that changes your adviser limit, check whether to discard advisers (5.1.4.II) based on your new adviser limit. If you have multiple cards that change your adviser limit, your adviser limit is the lowest among them. (They don’t add up.)


8.2 Offer Citizenship. Add “other” to “any other Exiles” 


8.3.3 Chronicle. Edit “their attached ruin and relic cards” to “their attached ruin, denizen, and relic cards” (This only matters in the rare situation where a ruin ends up ruled in a later game.)


10 Glossary. 
Draw: Edit to “Take cards from the top of the prompted draw source.” (The previous text was causing overspecification issues in other places, and the only card where the previous text mattered is Oracle. When you use Oracle, you should still advance the Visions Drawn track.)


You: Remove this entry. You: You alone, not including any Imperial Allies (5.5.2).
(This is causing some issues with power sharing. My apologies!)


Enemy: Add this text “Bandits are enemies to all players and vice versa.”


Rule: Add “face-up” to “players rule face-up sites” and “Bandits rule face-up sites that have no warbands.”


Chronicle Aid
The Rebuild Deck step says to shuffle 2 Visions and 12 denizens together to form the top stack. This is incorrect—the top stack should contain 2 Visions and 10 denizens for a total stack size of 12. The Law setup, Chronicle rules, and reference on the World Deck space of the map are all correct.


Clockwork Prince Rules
The example in Search and Play One Card should say “Discord card,” not “Discard card.”


Princely Reliquary
There are some minor differences in wording between some of the powers listed on this side of the Reliquary and the Imperial Reliquary side. They are equivalent, but consider the wording on the Imperial Reliquary side to be canon.


The Grand Scepter
The aid text on the back of the Grand Scepter should note that you can also exchange banners in a Citizenship offer, as described in the Law 6.6.1.
