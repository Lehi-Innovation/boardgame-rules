# Lords of Vegas

## Overview

Develop Las Vegas by acquiring lots, building casinos and controlling their highest dice. Matching property-card draws pay money to investors and points to casino bosses. Most points wins when the End of Game card appears; reaching 90+ points wins immediately.

This covers the **Mayfair 2–4-player base game**, including its two-player rules. The original four-page 2010/2013 English rulebook is the baseline, supplemented by applicable base clarifications in the publisher’s previous-Mayfair unified rulebook (© 2020, listed as updated July 2022). **Up!, Underworld, Americana, Con Jobs, the revised six-player edition and the optional Gamble Variant (laying off bets) are excluded.** There are no risers, extra actions or card effects from expansions in this summary.

## Components

- Board with lots grouped in blocks, The Strip, scoring track, wager area and property-card discard spaces.
- **9 usable casino tiles in each of 5 colors:** aqua, brown, gold, purple and silver. A tenth tile of each color is a replacement, not extra supply. Casino colors do not correspond to player colors.
- For each player: **12 dice, 10 translucent lot markers, a scoring chip and an action card**. Player colors are blue, green, red and yellow.
- Money, 49 property cards (including the End of Game card), player card and House card. The deck has **9 cards per casino color and 4 Pay The Strip cards**, including End of Game.

Dollar amounts stand for millions: $1 means $1 million. The amount of money a player has is public.

### Lots, casinos and bosses

A **lot** is a board space without a casino tile. Your marker on a lot makes it yours. Once built, it is a casino space, no longer a lot; your die on its tile represents your ownership/influence there.

A **casino** is one tile or an edge-connected group of same-color tiles on the same block. Same-color edge neighbors merge immediately into one casino, even if their dice belong to different players. Diagonal contact alone does not connect them; casinos cannot be subdivided after joining.

The **boss** is the player owning the **highest individual die** in that casino, not the most dice or the highest sum of pips. Several highest dice belonging to the same player are sufficient to make that player boss. Resolve highest ties between different players immediately as described under **Boss ties**.

## Setup

1. Each player takes an action card and all pieces of one player color. Put every scoring chip on **0**. Choose a banker; put money and tiles within reach.
2. Remove **End of Game** from the property deck and shuffle the remaining cards. Deal **2 cards per player**. With two players, discard and replace any F-lot setup card, so each player receives two valid A–E initial properties.
3. Put one of your markers on each of your two initial lots. Take money from the bank equal to the **sum of the printed money values on your two cards**. These card money values apply **only during setup**. Discard the cards into their corresponding color/Strip spaces with counts easily visible.
4. Divide the remaining shuffled deck into **4 roughly equal stacks**. Put End of Game **on top of the fourth stack**, then put the other three stacks above it. Place the resulting draw pile beside the board.
5. Each player rolls **2 dice**. The highest roller takes the player card and the first turn. After each turn, pass it to the player on the **left**.

## Turn Structure

Each normal turn has **Draw**, then **Play**. Trading is also possible at any time, subject to the restrictions below.

### Step 1 — Draw and pay

1. **Draw a property card and take over its space before payouts.** If it is an empty lot, put your marker there. If it contains another player’s sprawled tile and die, replace their die with your own **at the same current pip value**, not the board’s printed value. If the tile has no die, add your die at the **printed board value**. If your own die already occupies it, leave it unchanged. The casino tile stays in place. Apply the dice/marker supply rules if necessary and settle any boss tie before paying casinos.
2. **All owned lots pay:** every lot with a marker pays its owner **$1 from the bank**, including the newly acquired lot, regardless of the card’s color or whose turn it is. Casino tiles do not also collect lot income.
3. **The indicated casinos pay money and points:** a single-color card pays every casino of that color anywhere on the board. A **Pay The Strip** card instead pays every casino with at least **one edge adjacent to The Strip**, regardless of color. A qualifying casino pays for **all its tiles**, not only the tile touching The Strip.
4. **Money:** each player receives **$1 per pip on each of their dice** in a paying casino, including players who are not its boss. **Points:** only the casino’s boss receives **1 point per tile** in that casino. Follow the scoring-break and separate-casino rules below.
5. Discard the property card in its appropriate color/Strip pile so the number already drawn remains countable. Proceed to Play, except that two-player F cards repeat Draw as described below.

**End of Game is an immediate end trigger:** use the final-payout procedure below instead of continuing normal turn play.

### Step 2 — Play

On your own turn you may **build, sprawl, remodel, reorganize and gamble** in **any order**, or stop taking actions. You may repeat actions except **gamble is limited to once per turn**, and **no die may be reorganized more than once per turn**. You must be able to pay the stated action cost. After each action check for boss ties and settle them before continuing.

No player may take an action on another player’s turn. Trading itself is not an action.

## Actions

### Build

Choose **a lot you own**. Take back its marker, choose an **available** casino tile of any color, pay the **price printed on the lot**, and place the tile there. Put one of your dice on it with the **lot’s printed die value** face up. Edge-connected same-color tiles merge immediately; boss control may change.

### Sprawl

You must be **boss of the casino** you expand. Choose an **empty, unowned lot sharing an edge** with it, pay **twice that lot’s printed price**, and place an available tile of the **same color** there. Put your die on it at the **lot’s printed die value**. You cannot sprawl into another player’s marked lot, your own marked lot, a built space or a merely diagonal neighbor. Without an available tile of the required color, you cannot sprawl.

The new space’s property card remains in the deck. Whoever later draws it takes over that die before payouts, keeping its current value if occupied by another player. Drawing your own sprawled space leaves your die unchanged.

### Remodel

You must be **boss**. Choose a **new color with enough available tiles for the entire casino**. Pay **$5 per tile** and replace **every tile** of that casino with the new color, returning the old tiles to their supply. Dice stay with their holdings and keep their values. You cannot remodel just part of a casino.

The result merges with any edge-connected casino of the new color. The highest individual die in the resulting casino determines the boss, so a remodel may hand control to another player; settle any cross-player highest tie immediately.

### Reorganize

You need **at least one die** in the casino; you **need not be boss** and may reorganize a casino containing only your dice.

1. Pay **$1 per pip on every die in that casino**, including opponents’ dice, using their values before the roll.
2. **Reroll all dice**, not a chosen subset. Each player rolls their own dice.
3. Each player puts their rerolled dice back on **their own original tiles**. If they have multiple dice, they choose how to assign the rolled values among those tiles; they cannot move onto another player’s former tile. Temporary markers can help remember the original holdings.

You may reorganize different casinos, but **no die can be reorganized twice during the same turn**. A casino containing **any die already reorganized that turn cannot be reorganized**, even after a merger into a larger casino. This limit applies to paid reorganization, **not free boss-tie rerolls**, which happen as often as necessary. Settle boss ties after the action.

### Gamble

**Once during your own turn**, choose a casino where **another player is boss**, and give that player the **House card**. If no opponent bosses a casino, you cannot gamble. You may bet no more than **all three limits**: **$5 per tile in that casino**, **your available money**, and **the boss’s available money**.

Roll any **2 dice** and add them. The net transfer is:

| Dice sum | Result |
|---|---|
| 3, 4, 9, 10 or 11 | You win: the boss pays you **the bet amount**. You keep/get back your own stake. |
| 2 or 12 | You win double: the boss pays you **twice the bet**, limited to the money the boss actually has. You keep/get back your own stake. |
| 5, 6, 7 or 8 | You lose: pay **the bet amount** to the boss. |

The bank does not cover an unpaid portion of a doubled win in standard play. Gambling dice can be any dice; the supply restriction for placing ownership dice does not prevent gambling.

## Scoring / Victory Conditions

### Scoring breaks and multiple casinos

Record casino points by moving your chip on the **printed scoring track**. Numeric gaps are **breaks**: you must earn enough **from that individual casino** to reach the next printed value. Stop before a break you cannot cross and lose unusable excess points; do not save them for later.

For example, from **8**: an award of **1** leaves you on 8; **2 or 3** takes you to **10**; **4 or 5** takes you to **12**.

When several casinos pay you points on one turn, score them **individually, from smallest to largest**, choosing the order of equal-size casinos. You cannot combine their awards to cross a break. Several separate one-tile casinos therefore still cannot move a chip from 8 to 10.

### End and winner

As soon as anyone draws **End of Game**, the game ends immediately. Do not take Play actions or initiate trades after that draw. Make the **final payout for all owned lots and all casinos touching The Strip**, using normal money, boss-point and scoring-break rules. Highest score after that payout wins. If tied for most points, the tied player with **most money** wins. The source gives no further tiebreak if both remain equal.

In this base game, a player also **wins immediately at 90+ points**.

## Special Rules & Edge Cases

### Boss ties

After each action, check every casino. Also settle a highest cross-player tie immediately whenever it arises, including from taking over a sprawled space or trading a die.

If **two or more players** tie for the **highest** value in a casino, reroll **all dice currently tied for highest**, **for free**. Leave smaller dice untouched. Reassess the highest value across the whole casino after each roll. If different players still tie for the current highest, reroll the current highest dice again. Continue until only **one player** owns the highest value; several highest dice belonging to that one player need no reroll.

A tie among smaller dice does nothing. If tied bosses roll below a previously smaller die, that unchanged die’s owner becomes boss. For example, Red 5 and Blue 5 reroll to 3 while Green 4 stays: Green becomes boss, without rerolling the 3s further.

No actions or initiated trades may interrupt the tie-resolution sequence. These free rerolls do not use up or reset a die’s paid-reorganization allowance.

### Finite pieces and public resources

- **Casino tiles:** only 9 usable tiles per color. You cannot build or sprawl without the required available tile, or remodel without enough tiles of the chosen new color to replace the whole casino.
- **Ownership dice:** if all 12 of yours are already on the board when you must place one in a casino (including build, sprawl or inheriting a sprawled tile), **remove one of your dice from another tile and use it** at the value required by the new placement. The vacated tile stays, with no owner, and still counts toward its casino’s points. A casino with no dice has no owner/boss. You can trade to free a die. The limit applies to ownership placement; use any dice for gambling.
- **Lot markers:** if all 10 are on the board and you gain a new lot, move one of your markers to it; you cease to own the vacated lot.
- **Money:** the bank’s supply is unlimited. Your money, piece counts and discarded cards are public; anyone may inspect discard piles. A doubled gambling payout is limited by the boss’s available money, as stated above; action costs must be paid.

### Trading

Players may trade **at any time, even off-turn**, except during boss-tie resolution or after End of Game is drawn. Multiple players may participate. Trade any combination of **money, lots, dice in casinos and actions performed on the acting player’s current turn**.

For a transferred lot replace the owner’s marker with the recipient’s marker. For a transferred casino die replace it with the recipient’s die, retaining the **current pip value**, not the board’s printed die. A die trade can therefore change the boss and cause an immediate highest tie.

You **cannot trade points or casino tiles**, or promise actions, exchanges or transfers for **future turns**. **No player can perform an action on another player’s turn.** A current-turn action can be part of a trade only for the player whose turn it is. If something prevents an element of a trade from happening, the subsequent steps of that trade do not occur.

### Two players

**Do not build on F block.** During setup, **discard any F-lot card and draw a replacement**; use the two accepted A–E cards for starting lots and money. Discarded F cards remain discarded.

During a turn, an **F-lot card places no marker**, but still causes the normal **owned-lot money and indicated casino money/points**. Discard it in its appropriate pile, then **repeat Draw** until an A, B, C, D or E card has been drawn and resolved. Only then proceed to Play. Thus multiple payouts can happen on one turn; you do not take actions between these draws. **End of Game still ends the game immediately** if drawn during this sequence.

## Player Reference

- **Draw:** acquire lot/die → settle any boss tie → every marked lot pays $1 → indicated casinos pay each die’s pips to its owner and tile-count points to boss → score each casino separately smallest first → discard.
- **Play:** any order; repeat build, sprawl, remodel or eligible reorganization; gamble at most once. Settle boss ties after actions. Pass player card left.
- **Build:** own lot; printed price; available color; die = printed board value.
- **Sprawl:** boss; empty unowned edge neighbor; double printed price; same-color tile; die = printed board value.
- **Remodel:** boss; whole casino; $5/tile; enough tiles of new color; mergers may change boss.
- **Reorganize:** at least one own die; pay all pips; all players reroll all dice into their own original holdings; no die reorganized twice this turn.
- **Gamble:** opponent’s casino; bet ≤ $5/tile, own cash and boss cash. Sums 3/4/9/10/11 win bet; 2/12 win double subject to boss cash; 5/6/7/8 lose bet.
- **End:** End of Game draw → final lots + Strip payout → most points, then money. Or immediate base-game victory at 90+.
