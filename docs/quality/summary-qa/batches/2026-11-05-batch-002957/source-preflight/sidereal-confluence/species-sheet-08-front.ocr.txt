SHARING BONUS

 

Since the. Yengii aren’t using their
technologies to lay the foundation of the
Confluence, they gain a reduced Sharing
Bonus.

When the Yengii invent a technology, they
should flip it as normal, but not place it
in the center of the play area with the
other invented technologies. Instead,
they should keep them splayed in front of
themselves.

 

 

Invented technologies do not count
against the Yengii for determining their
research team bid tiebreaking.

Licensing

The Yengii may, as part of a trade, let
another player play a card as if they had
a technology the Yengii invented. The
recipient finds their matching card and
puts it into play permanently. The Yengii
can license a technology during the same
Trade Phase they invented it, effectively
“sharing” it faster than is possible for the
other species.

This ability cannot be used to bring back
a card that was hidden under another
card.

Advice

Starting on turn two or so, invent one
to three technologies that consume
resources that are generally plentiful in
the game, produce resources that are
generally in demand, or can be used to
upgrade the other player’s starting cards.
Ideally all three. Then, try to license
those technologies to as many players
as possible, in as few turns as possible.
In general, if you sell each technology to
at least three players for about two small
cubes apiece, you are doing well.

CONFLUENCE

The galaxy is populated by monsters.

Those aliens can act freely in any way
they choose, and are individually
much more powerful than any of
the Yengii. This is utterly terrifying.
In order to convince the Yengii to
work with such people, the Faderan
gave them a weaker version of the
agreement: the Yengii could keep
their new inventions to themselves if
they so chose. Thus, the Yengii only
share their technology with species
that they feel will use it properly.
