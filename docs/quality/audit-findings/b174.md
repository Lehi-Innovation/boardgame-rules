## the-quacks-of-quedlinburg

**Verdict: MINOR**

### Finding 1 — Components table: ingredient book count is internally inconsistent and wrong

Summary (Components): "Ingredient books (green, blue, red, yellow, purple × 2 each; black × 1; orange × 1) | 13"

The summary's own breakdown sums to 2+2+2+2+2+1+1 = **12**, not 13. Source confirms 12: "2 yellow ingredient books / 2 green ingredient books / 2 red ingredient books / 2 blue ingredient books / 1 black ingredient book / 1 orange ingredient book / 2 purple ingredient books" (extracted lines 43-49). The printed total (13) doesn't match either the source or the summary's own listed breakdown. Cosmetic — does not affect play (no rule depends on the total number of books in the box).

### Finding 2 — Minor omission: no explicit "must stop if bag is empty" rule

Source: "If a player has no more chips in their bag they must stop." (line 241). The summary's Potions Phase description (stop-or-draw-again choice) never states this edge case. Trivial in practice (a player with an empty bag physically cannot draw), but it is an unstated rule.

### Victory condition — verified sentence-by-sentence

Summary: "After 9 turns, the player with the most VP on the Scoring Track wins." / "Tiebreaker: The tied player who filled their pot furthest in the final turn wins. If still tied, all share victory."

Source: "The player whose scoring marker is the furthest down the Scoring Track wins. If more than one player shares first place, the one who was able to fill up their pot the most in the last turn wins. If it is still a tie, they can all claim to be the biggest quack of Quedlinburg." (lines 495-500). MATCH — both the win condition and both tiebreak levels are correct and in the right order.

Space-33 max-pot rule: Summary "immediately receive 15 VP and 35 coins... All players at space 33 roll the Bonus Die (unless exploded)" matches source lines 369-375 exactly (15 VP, 35 coins, bonus-die-unless-exploded all confirmed).

### Numbers checked

### Procedural claims verified (5+)

## battle-for-moscow

**Verdict: MAJOR**

*(Batch context: page order in this extraction is scrambled — Sections 10.0-12.0 (Replacements/Reinforcements/Mud) and the tail end of the Combat Results explanation appear at the top of the file, before the Table of Contents and Section 1.0. The whole file was read and cross-referenced accordingly; searches below covered the entire document, not just one region.)*

### Finding 1 — Invented directional constraint on Defender Retreat (DR)

Summary (Actions -> Combat, DR bullet): "DR (Defender Retreat): the defender retreats two hexes **toward its own board edge**, chosen by the attacker; each hex of retreat must take the unit further from the original hex (no zigzagging)."

Source, the complete DR rule: "DR (Defender Retreat): The defending unit is retreated (i.e., moved) two hexes by the attacking player as follows: Two Hexes: The unit must end up two hexes away from the defending hex (i.e., it cannot zigzag; each hex of retreat must take that unit a hex further from the defending hex). Avoid EZOCs... Unavoidable EZOCs... No Units: The unit may never retreat into or through an enemy-occupied hex, nor end its retreat in a friendly-occupied hex. Or Else: If there is no retreat path which satisfies all of these conditions, the unit is eliminated instead." (lines 668-691, and restated in the scrambled block at lines 3-14).

No support found for a "toward its own board edge" requirement anywhere in the document (searched: "board edge," "own edge," "friendly edge," "toward," every occurrence of "retreat" in the file — 19 hits, none mention a home-edge requirement). The source's only directional rule is that each retreat hex must be strictly farther from the original battle hex than the last — which is a materially different (and more permissive) constraint than "toward your own board edge." Since an invalid retreat path leads to elimination ("If there is no retreat path which satisfies all of these conditions, the unit is eliminated instead"), applying the summary's stricter, unsupported rule could cause a table of players to eliminate units that the actual rules would allow to survive by retreating in a valid "away from the battle" direction that isn't specifically toward their home edge. This is a contradicted/invented rule affecting a core combat outcome.

The rest of the DR/Stand-Fast/DRL/DE/EX/AL combat-results block is otherwise an exact match to the source, including the correct EZOC-during-retreat penalty (lose a step; eliminated if forced to end there), the EX step-loss-then-retreat sequencing, and "no attacking units ever retreat" for AL.

EXACT MATCH on both the win/draw conditions and the control definition.
