## Major Findings

### 1. Invented/contradicted rule: contract initiation and delivery wrongly gated on "exact count" arrival

The summary asserts — **three separate times** — that reaching a contract's start or end
city requires landing there by exact dice count, i.e. that forfeiting excess movement
disqualifies a contract action the same way it disqualifies public-contract selection:

- Turn Structure step 3: *"**Resolve exact-count arrivals**: if the truck's final position
  was reached by exact count (not by forfeiting excess movement), resolve any applicable
  space effect — drawing an event card, placing/moving the construction sign,
  **initiating or delivering a contract**, or selecting a public contract (see Actions)."*
- Special Rules & Edge Cases: *"Landing on a space by exact count matters throughout the
  game: it is required to draw event cards, to select public contracts, **and to
  initiate/deliver contracts**."*
- Turn checklist (final line): *"resolve any exact-count space effect (event card,
  construction sign, **contract initiation/delivery**, public contract selection)..."*

This directly contradicts the source, which explicitly carves contracts out of the
exact-count requirement in both directions:

- Initiating: *"To initiate a contract, a player must end his movement at the start city
  shown on the card. **Any excess movement that was rolled can be ignored** when
  initiating a contract."* (extracted lines 123-126)
- Delivering: *"When a player arrives at the destination for one of his initiated
  contracts (**extra rolled movement is ignored**), he unloads the goods..."* (extracted
  lines 136-138)

The summary's own **Actions** section correctly states this ("any excess movement that
was rolled can be ignored when initiating a contract"; "extra rolled movement is
ignored" for delivery) — so the document contradicts itself, and the wrong version is
the one repeated three times (Turn Structure, Special Rules, and the final turn
checklist a reader is most likely to skim at the table). A player relying on those
summary sections would believe they must land exactly on a contract's city to
initiate/deliver it, when the rulebook explicitly allows stopping short. Only the
public-contract-selection exact-count requirement is real (confirmed at extracted
lines 183, 233-234); the summary merges a real rule with two invented extensions of it.

## Minor Findings

- **Game-end wording adds an unsupported "in hand" qualifier.** Summary (Scoring section

  Source: *"The game ends immediately if there are no more public contracts on the board

  "in hand" qualifier. The summary's own next sentence ("Unfinished contracts (still in
  hand **or in progress**) are worthless") shows awareness that in-progress (already
  loaded/initiated) contracts also count, so a careful reader resolves the ambiguity
  correctly, but the trigger-condition sentence itself is imprecise versus source.
- **Simplified Variant point 1 adds an unsupported, self-contradicted detail.** Summary:
  "each player receives 5 contract cards in their hand (instead of 3, **and no separate
  draw pile**)." Source point 1 only says *"During preparation each player receives 5

  count the way the base game's is. No support found (searched: "draw pile", "stack",
  "procurement" in the variant section).

## Major Findings

### 1. Wrong suns-per-player count for 3-player games (numeric setup error, repeated twice)

- Summary Setup step 3: *"Distribute sun token groups based on player count: 2 players:
  4 suns each; **3-4 players: 3 suns each**; 5 players: 3 suns each."*
- Summary Player Reference table: `| 3 | 3 | 8 |` (Player Count | Suns per Player | Ra
  Track Spaces) — again states 3 players receive 3 suns each.

The source's own numbers show 3-player games actually use **4** suns per player, not 3.
Two independent pieces of source evidence confirm this:

1. Prose: *"Sort the Suns - showing values 2 - 9 with two players, **2 - 13 with three
   or four players**, and 2 - 16 with five players into groups..."* (extracted lines
   78-79) — 3-player and 4-player games draw from the *same* pool of sun values 2-13,
   i.e. the same 12 physical tokens, just split into a different number of groups (one
   group per player).
2. The raw sun-groups table (extracted lines 89-107) lists, for each "group" row, one
   value-set per player-count column in order 2p/3p/4p/5p. Every group's 3-player
   value-set has **4** numbers, e.g. group 1: "13 - 8 - 5 - 2" (4 suns), group 2:
   "12 - 9 - 6 - 3" (4 suns), group 3: "11 - 10 - 7 - 4" (4 suns) — three groups × 4 suns
   = 12 tokens, exactly matching the "2-13" pool. The 4-player value-sets for the same
   groups all have 3 numbers each (group 1: "13 - 6 - 2", group 2: "12 - 7 - 3", group 3:
   "11 - 8 - 4", group 4: "10 - 9 - 5" — four groups × 3 suns = 12 tokens, same pool,
   split four ways instead of three). The 2-player sets (group 1: "9-6-5-2", group 2:
   "8-7-4-3") are 4 suns × 2 groups = 8 tokens matching the "2-9" pool, and the 5-player
   sets are 3 suns × 5 groups = 15 tokens matching the "2-16" pool (minus sun #1, which
   is set aside on the board per Setup) — both of which agree with what the summary
   already gets right for 2p and 5p. Only the 3-player figure is wrong.

This is a foundational setup value: a 3-player game set up per the summary would give
each player one fewer sun (and one wrong-sized group) than the rulebook specifies,
changing how many turns/bids each player gets per epoch. It appears twice in the
document (Setup and Player Reference), not as an isolated typo.

## Minor Findings

- **Pharaoh scoring: "all-tied" special case dropped.** Summary: "Most: +5; Fewest: -2
  (ties: all affected)." Source adds: *"If all players have the same number of Pharaoh
  tiles, no points are awarded or lost."* (i.e., a total tie is 0/0, not +5 and -2 applied
  to everyone simultaneously). Does not change the eventual winner (a uniform +5-2=+3 to
  every player shifts nothing relative), but is a real, uncaptured rule.
- **Sun scoring: same "all-tied" case omitted**, though here it's inconsequential even if
  mis-applied, since +5 and -5 to every player cancel to the same net 0 the source's
  explicit rule produces. Source: *"If all players have the same total, no points are
  awarded or lost."*
- **Disaster stacking not addressed.** Source note: *"If a player has more than one
  disaster tile of the same category, he must fulfill them all"* (i.e. two Funerals won
  in the same auction cost 2 Pharaohs each, not 2 total). Summary's Disasters section
  reads as a flat per-category list and doesn't state whether multiple same-category
  disasters compound. Edge case, rare (needs 2+ matching disaster tiles won in one
  auction).
- **Turn order (who starts) omitted.** Source: *"The player with the highest numbered sun
  begins. After his turn, play continues... clockwise"* for epoch 1, and *"The player
  with the highest numbered sun begins the next epoch"* thereafter. Not stated anywhere
  in the summary's Turn Structure or Setup. Doesn't affect scoring or legality of actions
  once underway.
- **VP-token composition simplified.** Summary Setup: "Each player takes VP tokens worth
  10 points (face down)" — correct total, but source specifies this is *"two value 5
  tablets each"* specifically, and the Components table doesn't give the full
  denomination breakdown (source: 10×1, 8×2, 20×5, 10×10 = 48). Not a rules error, just
  lower detail.
- **Random group assignment omitted.** Source: *"The players decide randomly which
  player receives which group."* Summary's Setup step 3 doesn't specify the assignment
  is random.

## Conclusion

Component counts, the entire tile breakdown, the auction/bidding procedure (including
all three pass/must-bid sub-rules), disaster resolution, and the full scoring table
(cross-validated against the source's own worked monument example) are accurate — most
of this summary is careful and correct. But the 3-player sun allocation is wrong in two
places (Setup and Player Reference) and is a basic setup number a table would use
immediately when starting a 3-player game, giving every player the wrong hand of suns
for the entire match. MAJOR.
