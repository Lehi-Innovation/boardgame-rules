and the summary's Components section describes this correctly. No invented reshuffle/replenish
rule.

**Verdict: PASS** — Extremely faithful, detailed summary. Round structure, victory/tie rules,
the Leadership-deck reshuffle exhaustion rule, all keyword definitions, and the full terrain/Burn
table match the source, in most cases almost verbatim.

### Victory / game-end (sentence-by-sentence)

## catacombs — MINOR

**Verdict: MINOR** — Victory/loss conditions, all gold costs, the poison exhaustion rule, ally
control-passing tiebreakers, and Antient/Gelatinous-Cube special rules all match the source
precisely (several passages verbatim). One confirmed omission: the source's specific
damage/modifier immunity for Catacomb Lords (the final-boss unit) is never mentioned anywhere
in the summary.

### Victory / game-end (sentence-by-sentence)
| Summary claim | Source | OK |
|---|---|---|
| Heroes win: Catacomb Lord's health to 0, ≥1 hero alive; minions need not be destroyed | "If the heroes eliminate all of the Catacomb Lord's health and at least one hero in the party remains alive, they are victorious... Unlike the previous battle rooms, it is not necessary to destroy all the Catacomb Lord's minions" (l.1507-1512) | Y |
| Overseer wins: all 4 heroes killed/unable to act at any point in a Battle Phase, even if an ally remains | "If at any time during any Battle Phase, all four heroes are killed and/or unable to take an action... the game is over and the Overseer wins" (l.1517-1520); ally case: "If all the heroes are dead, the game is over, even if there is an ally still on the board during the Battle Phase" (l.1873-1875) | Y |
| Hero death at 0 health; stays out unless resurrected at the Healer | l.766-772 (health-track skull icon effects) and Healer Resurrect service (l.1210-1216) | Y |

### Finding — MINOR: Catacomb Lord damage/modifier immunity omitted
- Summary: describes Petrify as "the hit character is turned to stone and dies immediately,
  regardless of remaining health" (Shot Modifiers table) and separately describes Antients'
  modifier immunity in detail, but never states any equivalent restriction for Catacomb Lords.
- Source: "Catacomb Lords can only be damaged by melee, roll or ranged shots (that inflict
  damage) from the Heroic Forces and/or Antients. They are immune to the effects of all shot
  modifiers except chain, critical and regeneration." (extracted/catacombs-rules.txt, the
  paragraph immediately before "Winning The Game: Heroes").
- Impact: a table relying only on the summary could ratchet a Petrify/Poison/Stun/Bite/Devour/
  Ice shot against the final-boss Catacomb Lord expecting the printed modifier effect (e.g., an
  instant kill from Petrify) to apply, which the source explicitly forbids. This does not corrupt
  the summary's win/loss/scoring statements (those are all independently correct and complete),
  so it is scored as an edge-case omission rather than a contradicted core rule.

### Numbers checked (22/22 supported, aside from the omission above)
Healer costs: Heal 3 gold, Cure 2 gold, Resurrect 10 gold -> 2 health (l.1210-1216). Merchant:
2 gold to flip the next Item card (no repeat limit), 2 gold for a map (l.1291-1300). Amaranth Inn:
recruit for 10 gold, ally max health 4, ally gets 1 fewer Ability card (minimum 1) (l.1826-1852).
Alewife Tavern reward table (3 Heroes / 3-of-a-kind / 2 Hero pieces / 3 different colors / 1 Hero
piece) matches l.1897-1905 exactly. Shot damage quick-reference (Melee 1, Rush 0, Missile 1,
Fireball 1, Roll 2, Target 1, Giant Fireball 2, Boulder 3, Bite 1(+1/round), Devour 1, Summon 1)
all match their respective rule paragraphs. Poison overwhelm rule (total Poison-card value >=
starting health = death) and its exhaustion clause (no cards left to draw = automatic lethal dose)
match l.1560-1590 exactly — not invented.

### Procedural claims checked (>5)
1. Ally control passes on hero death: lowest current health -> least gold -> fewest items — matches
   l.1869-1874 exactly, including tiebreak order.
2. Familiar rules (placed within 1" of controlling hero, owner chooses act order, cannot earn gold,
   cannot be poisoned/stunned but can be devoured/teleported) — matches l.1330-1349, 1411.
3. Antient control alternates hero-who-summoned -> Overseer -> back, until room cleared or all
   heroes dead; leaves at Resolution Phase; multiple Antients possible — matches l.1996-2025.
4. Gelatinous Cube's per-round shot-sequence cycle (Round 1 melee+corrosion, Round 2
   melee+fear, restarts at Round 5) — matches l.1944-1975 (with Round 3 left appropriately

5. Suppression rule (unmodified shot listed = shot and all modified versions suppressed; specific
   modifier listed = only that modifier suppressed) — matches l.1216-1226ish paraphrase of the
   Suppression definition.

### Term of art

terms" box are a word-for-word match of the source's own "Important Terms" glossary (l.213-219).

## carcassonne-wheel-of-fortune — PASS

**Verdict: PASS** — Located the self-contained Wheel of Fortune section inside the fan-compiled
compendium (originally-released-2009 header, "72 land tiles, 19 of which have Wheel of
Fate/Fortune icons"). Every mechanic, scoring formula, and number in the summary traces to
WoF-specific text (not base-game-only content), including two worked scoring examples in the
source that independently confirm the Tax formula.

### Victory / game-end
- Summary: "Ordinary Carcassonne scoring applies throughout... As in the basic game, the player
  with the most points when the game ends wins."
- Source (base-game section, incorporated by explicit reference — WoF's own text states "The
  rules are identical to the basic game, except for those noted below"): "The player with the most
  points wins." (l.873) — supported, not outside knowledge, since WoF explicitly inherits the base
  win condition and never overrides it.
- No new/alternate victory condition is introduced anywhere in the WoF section (l.3821-3985);
  summary does not invent one either.

### Numbers checked (14/14 supported)
72 total tiles, 19 with icon, hub = 16 tiles/4x4 (l.3821-3822); Fortune = active player +3
(l.3898-3900); Tax = 1 pt/pennant + 1 pt/own-knight per knight, confirmed by the worked example
"BLUE receives for each knight 2(knight) + 2 (pennants) = 4 points, or 2x4 = 8 points altogether"
(l.3813-3818, 3902-3906); Storm = +1/follower in supply (l.3915-3917); Inquisition = +2/monk
(l.3919-3921); Plague = return 1 follower from a land tile only, active player first then clockwise
(l.3923-3927); crown-space scoring 3 (lone, 1-space) / 6 (lone, 2-space) / 3-each (two followers,
2-space) (l.3937-3946); 40 followers / 5 colors + scoreboard for stand-alone play (l.3813-3820).

### Procedural claims checked (>5)
1. Turn order: draw tile -> if WoF icon, resolve wheel (pig move -> event -> crown scoring) BEFORE
   placing the tile -> place tile -> optional follower deployment (to the tile or a free crown
   space) — matches the "1a/1b" + "Wheel of Fortune Tiles" 4-step list at l.3833-3884 exactly.
2. Pig setup: placed on the "Fortune" section, nose pointed right — matches l.3833-3835 verbatim.
3. Famine detail (majority-only scoring, castles count double per farmer, pig/castle bonuses
   apply, barns never score since they aren't followers) — matches footnotes 308-310 (l.3949-3958)
   point for point.
4. Dragon interaction: flies around the *outside* of the Wheel treating those tiles normally;
   followers on the Wheel itself are unaffected — matches footnote 304 (l.3847-3850) verbatim,
   and is independently corroborated by the near-identical Dragon-expansion footnote 132
   (l.2166-2168).
5. Crown-space occupancy cap (max 1 follower/space) and the no-scoring-while-pig-passes-over
   rule — both match l.3868-3870, 3935-3936.

### Term of art
"Majority" for Famine farm-scoring and "in play" for Inquisition's monk count are not explicitly
glossed in the WoF section itself, but both follow directly from the base game's own role-based
follower terminology (a "knight"/"monk"/"farmer" is a follower currently deployed in that role,
per Tax's own footnote 307 defining "knight" as "any follower... placed in a city") — treated as
supported, not invented, since it is the same convention the source uses elsewhere in the same
document.

### Minor note (not scored as a finding)
The source's "place the Wheel of Fortune tile to one side instead of as the starting tile" tip
(l.3969-3972) is stated as a general alternative in the source, while the summary frames it more
narrowly as being "when combining... with a large multi-expansion game." This is a small framing
difference that does not change the actual rule (the option itself, and its mechanics, are quoted
correctly) — trivial wording drift, not counted against the verdict.
