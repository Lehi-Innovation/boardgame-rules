### Minor Findings (all pre-existing from b176; independently re-derived, not
newly introduced by the repair; none required fixing since only the MAJOR
finding was in scope for repair)

- **Pharaoh scoring "all-tied" exception still dropped.** Summary: "Most: +5;
  Fewest: -2 (ties: all affected)." Source adds an exception the summary
  doesn't capture: "If all players have the same number of Pharaoh tiles, no
  points are awarded or lost" (lines 355-356). This one is NOT
  self-cancelling like the Suns case below (Pharaoh's +5/-2 are asymmetric):
  mechanically applying "ties: all affected" to an all-tied group gives every
  player +5-2=+3, not the source's 0. A real, if narrow (requires an exact
  full-table tie), scoring miscalculation risk — most likely to bite in
  2-player games where a Pharaoh-count tie is unremarkable.
- **Sun scoring "all-tied" case also omitted, but inconsequential.** Source:
  "If all players have the same total, no points are awarded or lost" (lines
  453-454). Here Suns are scored symmetrically (+5 highest / -5 lowest), so
  applying "ties: all affected" to an all-tied group nets +5-5=0 per player —
  same result the source's explicit rule produces. Not a practical error.
- **Disaster stacking not addressed.** Source: "If a player has more than one
  disaster tile of the same category, he must fulfill them all" (lines
  303-305) — two Funerals won in one auction cost 2 Pharaohs *each* (4 total),
  not 2 total. Summary's Disasters section reads as a flat per-category list
  and doesn't state whether multiple same-category disasters compound. Rare
  edge case (needs 2+ matching disaster tiles in a single auction win).
- **Turn order (who starts each epoch) omitted.** Source: "The player with
  the highest numbered sun begins" (line 136, epoch 1) and "The player with
  the highest numbered sun begins the next epoch" (line 316-317). Confirmed
  via grep this is nowhere in the summary's Setup or Turn Structure. Doesn't
  affect scoring or action legality once play is underway, only who acts
  first.
- **VP-token composition simplified.** Summary Setup: "Each player takes VP
  tokens worth 10 points (face down)" — correct total, but source specifies
  "two value 5 tablets each" (lines 74-76), and the Components table gives
  only the total (48) plus denominations present, not the full breakdown
  (source: 10×1, 8×2, 20×5, 10×10 = 48). Lower detail, not a rules error —
  doesn't change any in-game arithmetic since scoring only cares about the
  sum of a player's tablets, not which denominations compose it.

### Conclusion

The specific b176 MAJOR (3-player suns count) is genuinely fixed and verified

issues — the auction procedure, all three pass sub-rules, disaster resolution,

all-players-tied-on-Pharaohs case. MINOR.

### Re-check of the b176 MAJOR finding: exact-count requirement for own
contracts vs. event cards/public contracts — CONFIRMED FIXED, consistent
across all four locations named in the brief

1. **Turn Structure** (step 3): "*Initiate or deliver your own contracts*:
   allowed whenever the truck ends on the contract's start/destination city —
   any excess rolled movement is ignored, so you may overshoot and still stop
   there (exact count is not required). *Exact-count-only effects*: drawing an
   event card and selecting a public contract require arriving by exact
   count. If the player had to forfeit excess movement to stop on the city, or
   arrived via an event card, they may not select a public contract that
   turn." — correct on both halves.
2. **Actions → Initiate a Contract**: "...any excess movement that was rolled
   can be ignored when initiating a contract..." — correct.
   **Actions → Deliver a Contract**: "...(extra rolled movement is
   ignored)..." — correct.
3. **Special Rules & Edge Cases**: "Landing on a space by exact count is
   required to draw event cards and to select public contracts, but **not**
   to initiate or deliver your own contracts: for both initiating and
   delivering, any excess rolled movement is ignored, so a player may
   overshoot and still stop at the start/destination city. Being forced to

   turn." — correct, and now the section that most clearly states the rule.
4. **Player Reference → Turn checklist**: "...initiate/deliver any of your
   own contracts at the start/destination city (excess movement ignored) → if
   you arrived by exact count, resolve exact-count effects (draw event card,
   select public contract) → ..." — correct.

All four match source: initiating (extracted lines 124-126, "Any excess
movement that was rolled can be ignored when initiating a contract"),
delivering (lines 136-138, "extra rolled movement is ignored"), event cards

### Minor Findings (pre-existing from b176; independently re-derived; both
remain because only the MAJOR exact-count finding was in scope for repair)

- **Game-end wording still carries an unsupported "in hand" qualifier.**
  Summary Scoring section and Player Reference both say "a player has
  completed all of the contracts **in their hand**." Source: "a player has
  completed all of their contracts" (lines 192-194) — no "hand" qualifier,
  and the rulebook's own state model treats "hand" (pre-initiation, e.g. line
  258 "takes the contract into his hand") as distinct from an initiated
  contract "placed... in front of him on the table" (line 127-128). Taken
  fully literally, the summary's phrasing could be read as satisfied even
  while a player still has an initiated-but-undelivered contract on the
  table. Mitigating: the summary's very next sentence ("Unfinished contracts
  — still in hand **or in progress** — are worthless at game end") shows the
  writer was aware in-progress contracts count too, so a reader who takes in
  the whole paragraph resolves it correctly; only the trigger-condition
  clause itself is imprecise. Matches b176's calibration of this as MINOR
  rather than MAJOR for the same reason.
- **Simplified Variant point 1 still carries an unsupported, self-contradicted
  detail.** Summary: "...5 contract cards in their hand (instead of 3, **and
  no separate draw pile**)." Source point 1 only says players receive 5
  contract cards in hand — nothing about a draw pile either way. The
  parenthetical is contradicted by the summary's own point 2 one line below
  ("The stack for public procurement contains only 10 cards..."), which is
  functionally the same kind of face-down reserve stack the base game uses
  (just resized and no longer tied to player count). No support found
  (searched "draw pile", "stack", "procurement" within the variant section,
  lines 209-231 of source).
