# Source-recovery findings — 2026-11-06

Specific source-backed contradictions only; these are not complete summary audits.

## deep-space-navigator

The summary says ships can stop or reverse by applying opposite thrust over multiple turns. TonyWatson’s contemporary feature (SpaceGamer67 January/February1984 printed4/PDF6) explicitly states a minimum speed of one, maximum of eight, and that backwards movement is ruled out. It illustrates that a ship moving at speed one cannot apply four units of reverse maneuver to travel three units in the opposite direction. The summary therefore teaches illegal stopping and reversing under the covered original1983rules. The same page permits combat before or after movement at the player’s option, rather than the summary’s fixed post-navigation combat sequence. Specific MAJOR for these covered movement/timing rules only; the original changes sheet has unresolved shield ambiguity and later revised-edition equivalence remains unproven. No full audit or summary body repair. [Contemporary reference](https://files.spawningpool.net/docs/Tabletop%20Gamez/Vault%202.0/tabletop-gaming-magazines/Space%20Gamer/Space%20Gamer%20%2367%20-%20Interdiction%20Station%20-%20SJG%20-%2002-84.pdf). Verdict: MAJOR; specific finding only.

## colony-delta

The summary gives automatic resource income from controlled territories and presents control of most territory or elimination as the win condition. Hackett’s contemporary reference (Phoenix30 March/April1981 printed20–21/PDF24–25) instead says the primary aim is economic VP production: colonist/worker units must mine ore deposits, normally producing one OrePoint per unit per turn or five for a permanent colony. Ore must reach a Spaceport and then the home planet before it earns VPs; ore left on Delta at game end is worthless. Farming generates home-planet VPs directly, and mining and farming cannot coexist in a hex. The printed28/PDF32 turn table places economic collection after military actions and includes distinct surface and space transport. Replacing these production/transport decisions with generic territory income and a military-control objective changes the core game. Specific MAJOR against covered economic rules only; full ending/tie conditions and numerical CRT/units remain uncertified. The source’s air-return/advance ordering conflict is unresolved, and reviewer house rules are excluded. No full audit or summary body repair. [Contemporary reference](https://www.spigames.net/Phoenix/Phoenix30.pdf). Verdict: MAJOR; specific finding only.

## city-states-of-arklyrell

The summary requires territory-income, paid unit recruitment and construction/development phases. The detailed Vandenbroucke reference (March10,2015 pages3/18) instead describes leaders recruiting adjacent neutral units with a morale-threshold die roll between movement and combat, and explicitly says controlled citadels provide no resources. The original back cover independently describes leaders gathering armies to capture objective citadels. Replacing that recruitment system with an income/building economy invents a central procedure. Specific MAJOR for covered recruitment/economic scope only. The reference’s declared house interpretations and eight-versus-nine-citadel discrepancy are not treated as certified base rules. Full original manual/charts remain missing; no full audit or summary body repair. [Rules reference](https://grognard.com/replayaar/city-states-arklyrell-aar.pdf). Verdict: MAJOR; specific finding only.

## boarding-party

The summary advertises1–2players but gives only the goal of reaching and neutralizing the command center before a timer. DavidLent’s detailed reference distinguishes the two-player scenario: players compete for VPs from controlling the computer at game end and destroying enemy units, and it explicitly says not to destroy the computer. The robot player may directly control three robots each turn while the rest move randomly. Applying the solo computer-destruction goal to that competitive scenario changes its objective. Specific MAJOR for covered two-player rules only; full original weapon/movement tables and exact VP/time values remain missing. No full audit or summary body repair. [Rules reference](https://centurionsreview.com/boarding-party/boarding-party-review/). Verdict: MAJOR; specific finding only.

## calamity

The summary teaches dice movement, an ending after one circuit, and insurance policies that pay the player compensation for losses while charging ongoing premiums. Connor’s contemporary reference (Imagine 12, March 1984, printed 14 / PDF 16) instead makes players the insurers: they receive premiums on policies still in force at year-end and pay £200,000 per policy hit by the two-die risk grid. Movement uses cards numbered 1–5, and the game lasts two circuits, with most cash winning. Reversing the insurance cash flows and replacing both movement and ending changes central procedures. Specific MAJOR for those covered rules only; full manual, all board effects and bankruptcy/loan exceptions remain missing. No full audit or summary body repair. [Contemporary reference](https://archive.org/download/imagine-magazine-02-keith-thomson-editor/Imagine%20Magazine%2012%20-%20Keith%20Thomson%20%28editor%29.pdf). Verdict: MAJOR; specific finding only.

## air-baron

The summary tells a player to purchase/invest before collecting income and defines market share as the percentage of all spokes owned. Köther’s detailed reference instead orders the turn as optional fare-war declaration, two profit-marker draws and then one action. The original designer Evan Davis (The General30-6, printed/PDF23) independently explains two draws and market share awarded by hub dominance/control, with full control worth double dominance. The original board prints different weighted values for different hubs. Buying before the draws changes available cash and profit eligibility; replacing weighted hub values with a spoke percentage changes the central victory calculation. Specific MAJOR for covered turn order/market share only. Full manual, setup and player-count-specific targets remain missing; no full audit or body repair. [German reference](https://www.hall9000.de/html/ksr/pdf/air_baron), [designer article](https://www.vftt.co.uk/files/AH%20The%20General/The%20General%20Vol%2030%20No%206.pdf). Verdict: MAJOR; specific finding only.

## breakscore

The summary’s turn sequence says to roll, move the counter, resolve its square and then update the running breakscore; its Move action says to move the indicated dice number. The original publisher FAQ instead explicitly says counters move only when the player decides to sit. Players set aside selected scoring dice, reroll all remaining dice, accumulate the turn score and risk losing the whole score on zouch; banking that score then determines movement. The publisher guide and base Game Terms independently confirm this sequence. Immediate movement on each roll replaces the central push-your-luck/banking procedure and changes when board effects occur. Specific MAJOR for this covered turn/movement rule only. Source version/card/zap conflicts and missing full scoring/void/setup rules remain explicit; no full audit or summary body repair. [Original publisher FAQ](https://web.archive.org/web/20090126043301id_/http://breakscore.com:80/bs_faq.html). Verdict: MAJOR; specific finding only.

## burosh

The summary says all three wild cards can represent any card. The original maker’s updated challenge sheet instead restricts each: Emperor represents a face card or Ace and is always trump; Magician represents a numeric card including1but not Ace and is always trump; Hermit can represent any card outside trump. The Emperor also wins otherwise equal hands, while the other wilds copy their chosen card without that tie advantage. Unrestricted wild use creates illegal hands and changes challenge winners. Specific MAJOR for this covered updated challenge subsystem only; no full-base-rule or exact2008edition audit. Other setup/turn/ending claims remain uncertified; no summary body repair. [Original maker challenge reference](https://burosh.win/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Hand-Rankings.pdf). Verdict: MAJOR; specific finding only.

## big-adventure

The summary says the first player to reach the final space wins. The original current Russian publisher reference instead requires a compulsory stop on the gum space, makes that player immovable there, and tells them to roll on their turns to try opening the finish-safe. The original box back explicitly says winning requires reaching the gum and opening that safe. Arrival alone therefore does not win; removing the separate safe attempt changes the ending and other players’ opportunities. Specific MAJOR for this covered current publisher ending only. Exact successful-roll target and other board rules remain unavailable; no first-edition/full-summary audit or body repair. [Original publisher reference](https://gaga.ru/gaga/files/pdf/rules/ru/3037.pdf). Verdict: MAJOR; specific finding only.

## galacta-star-commandos

The summary gives each player movement, shooting, close-combat and morale phases and does not restrict moved figures from shooting. The original1980HeritageHowToPlay front explicitly defines four phases for the entire turn: A moves, A shoots, B moves, B shoots. Only figures that did not move in their movement phase may shoot; close combat is allowed even after movement and saving throws are made within that attack step. Replacing this with four phases per player and allowing the implied move-then-fire sequence changes core activation and attack eligibility. Specific MAJOR for this completely visible base-turn procedure only. Reverse-side combat exceptions, full roster/scenarios and optional rules remain missing; no full summary audit or body repair. [Original photographed rules](https://onemoregamingproject.blogspot.com/2015/09/galacta-another-trip-down-memory-lane.html). Verdict: MAJOR; specific finding only.

The same complete procedure appears in the preserved republication. Reusing one roll for both cards changes the pitcher-controlled branching and outcome distribution. Specific MAJOR for covered at-bat resolution only; the reviewer’s limited-testing qualification and missing full charts/rare outcomes remain explicit. ## battle-over-britain

The summary describes each game turn as a portion of a day while presenting the strategic campaign. The original Spanish NAC introduction (section 1.0, PDF4) and section 5.0 (PDF11) state that the strategic campaign consists of nine turns, each representing five days. The introduction distinguishes this from combat scenarios and the advanced game; those are separate systems, not a portion-of-a-day strategic turn. This changes the timing of raids, repair, activity and replacements. Specific MAJOR for strategic turn scale only. The recovered PDF is partial, missing the remaining combat/advanced chapters and screen tables; OCR exact values require the original scan. No full summary audit or body repair. [Original Spanish strategic manual extract](https://levmishkin.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2009/02/bob_estrat_gico_21.pdf). Verdict: MAJOR; specific finding only.

## ace-of-aces-wingleader

The summary explicitly gives Basic play unlimited ammunition and postpones ammo tracking to an Intermediate level, while its setup already has both players select aircraft cards. The readable original Wingleader Errata instead makes its Ammo Rule require subtracting one point from the Aircraft Card for every shot and prohibits shooting at zero; it places Optional Rules under a separate later heading. The collector identifies the aircraft-card game as Basic, with Introductory/Basic/Optional levels. Unlimited aircraft-card firing removes an enforced resource limit and enables shots the corrected rules forbid. Specific MAJOR for errata-covered aircraft-card ammunition only, not a full rulebook audit or a claim about all introductory/expansion variants. Full manuals/tables remain missing; no body repair. ## fun-house

The summary tells each player to roll a die or spin for movement, then draw a challenge card and collect prizes. The original Pressman instructions instead have the selecting team choose a skill game at Start (later the game named by its current space), one player from EVERY team play it, then EVERY team move clockwise according to that game's scores or results. Selection passes clockwise. The invented roll/move/draw procedure changes both turn participation and movement throughout the game. Specific MAJOR established by the complete core paragraphs on the original front; no reliance on unreadable reverse fine print. First-to-finish remains the objective, with source-specific same-round last-game-score/overshoot ties. No full audit, PASS, ID change or summary body repair. The complete printed Jeux Noël rules instead deal seven ELEMENT/HANDICAP cards, require legally discarding the entire hand, visiting both circular spaces for a sickle and potion gourd, and returning to the village with both items and no cards. Normal movement uses a die; rolling1permits any distance from1to6, and movement may go forward, backward or both ways. These are different core objectives and movement procedures. Printed publisher credits and exact seven-card mechanics confirm BGG61798despite the archive file previously being misclassified as Nathan. Verdict: MAJOR; specific finding only.

## gridiron-master

The summary suggests queen movement for the quarterback, but the original publisher rules assign king movement. ## clan-war

The summary also omits the custom thirty-card tactical deck and the orders, additional cavalry movement and reserve movement phases described by the source. These change central turn decisions. This finding covers the early boxed game; it does not certify later Daimyo Edition rules. ## battlelords

The summary gives an immediate win for reaching a victory-point threshold or eliminating every enemy soldier. The matching rules account instead checks Reputation during the Victory phase, combining current Fame with completed missions and requiring the agreed threshold and sole highest score. Elimination depends on having no active Hotspot and zero Fame at the end of the Hotspot phase, not on losing all soldiers. The primary game description independently places the Reputation comparison at round end. This finding covers those ending rules; the main reference is an unofficial Japanese account. [Unofficial Japanese reference](https://card.g1.xrea.com/en/fb06bl.html). Verdict: MAJOR; specific finding only.
