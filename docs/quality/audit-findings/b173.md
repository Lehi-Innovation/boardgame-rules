## Slug/filename mismatches found during prefilter (documented, not a summary defect)

The prefilter (`python -m scripts.audit prefilter`) reported `MISSING_EXTRACTED` or
`SOURCE_MISMATCH` for several games. Investigation showed three of these are
**registry/filename drift, not missing content** — the manifest's slug (produced by
the current `slugify()` on the registry `name`) no longer matches the actual on-disk
filenames used by `rules/`, `extracted/`, `source_pdf`, and the site's own links:

- `hey-that-s-my-fish` → real files are `rules/hey-thats-my-fish.md` /
  `extracted/hey-thats-my-fish-rules.txt` (confirmed same game: bgg_id 8203,
  identical designer/source_pdf/issue-template references). Audited using the
  real files; verdict reported under the manifest's slug per instructions.
- `red-notice` → prefilter's `SOURCE_MISMATCH` is a false positive: the word
  "notice" (and even "Red" as a bare word) never literally appears in
  `extracted/red-notice-rules.txt` because the title is cover artwork, not
  extracted text. Content match is unambiguous (Angela Farbank/Jessah Hope,
  bgg_id 457724, source_pdf `red-notice-rules.pdf`). Audited normally.
- `the-quacks-of-quedlinburg-the-herb-witches` / `...-the-alchemists` → genuinely
  missing. Both `rules/quacks-of-quedlinburg-the-*.md` files carry
  `verification: unverifiable`, no `source_pdf` field, and an explicit banner
  stating "no source rulebook text is available." No extracted file exists
  under any name. Treated as true `MISSING_EXTRACTED` per Step 0.
- `schotten-totten` → also genuinely missing (same `unverifiable` pattern,
  `source_pdf: ""`). True `MISSING_EXTRACTED`.

## rhino-hero — MINOR

`rules/rhino-hero.md` vs `extracted/rhino-hero-rules.txt`. Small, simple game;
victory conditions, setup, turn structure, all five action symbols, and every
component/hand-size number match the source exactly (including the English
text cross-checked against the German/French/Spanish/Chinese translations
present in the same PDF). Two unsupported additions found, both in the
"exhaustion rule" and invented-detail categories the audit specifically checks.

### Findings

1. **UNSUPPORTED — invented provision-pile exhaustion ruling.**
   - Summary (Special Rules & Edge Cases): "Provision Pile Exhaustion: If the
     provision pile runs out, the Additional Card symbol has no further effect
     (no cards remain to draw)."
   - Source: no support found (searched: "run out", "empty", "exhaust", "no
     more", "none left" — the provision pile is only ever mentioned at setup
     and in the Additional Card symbol text, l.36 and l.66-68; the source never
     addresses what happens if it is depleted).
   - Impact: minor — the summary doesn't tell players to do anything
     incorrect (no invented reshuffle/replenish action), it only states that
     nothing further happens, which doesn't change how the game is played even
     if unconfirmed. Flagged per the mandatory exhaustion-rule check.

2. **UNSUPPORTED — invented "first appearance" clarification for Rhino Hero.**
   - Summary: "Note: On the very first appearance of Rhino Hero, the figure is
     simply placed on the indicated mark (it starts off the tower)."
   - Source: no support found (searched: "first time", "initial", "starts
     off", "not yet", "climbing again," l.78-80 and all four translated
     versions of the Rhino Hero symbol text) — the source never describes the
     figure's very first placement, only ongoing moves.
   - Impact: trivial — doesn't misstate a rule, just fills an unaddressed
     corner case.

## chinatown — MAJOR

`rules/chinatown.md` vs `extracted/chinatown-rules.txt` (243 lines, read in
full). Overview, components, setup, all 6 phases, the overflow/business
formation rules, and the income table are all accurately and precisely
supported. However, the "Building Cards per Round" table — a core mechanic
governing how many cards every player deals/keeps in every one of the 6
rounds — is almost entirely unsupported by the extracted text.

### Findings

1. **MAJOR — "Building Cards per Round" table mostly unsupported.**
   - Summary (Actions, Phase 1, and duplicated in Player Reference): a full
     6-round × 3-player-count table of specific values, e.g. "Round 2 | 6/4 |
     5/3 | 5/3", "Round 6 | 6/4 | 5/3 | 4/2".
   - Source: Table 1 is corrupted by PDF-table extraction — lines 100-115
     show only "Round / 3 players / 7/5 / 4 players / 5 players" followed by
     11 blank tab-separated lines before "Table 1". Searched for every cell
     value in multiple formats (`6/4`, `6 / 4`, `5/3`, `5 / 3`, `4/2`, `4 / 2`,
     `7/5`, `7 / 5`) across the whole file: **only "7/5" (l.102) appears**,
     and Round 1's 4-player value (6/4) is separately confirmed only via a
     worked example ("in a 4-player game, each player receives 6 Building
     cards... keep 4 cards," l.124-125). That leaves 16 of the table's 18
     cells (all of Rounds 2-6, and Round 1's 5-player column) with **no
     support found** anywhere in the extracted text.
   - Note: the summary itself demonstrates awareness that this kind of
     data can be missing from the rulebook text — it correctly hedges the
     parallel Shop Tile draw-count table with "printed on the Player Aid
     card only — not in the rulebook. Refer to the physical card" — but
     applies no such hedge to the Building Cards table, instead presenting
     16 unconfirmed cells with full numeric precision.
   - Impact: this table is consulted at the start of every single round to
     determine exactly how many Building cards to deal and keep — if any of
     the 16 unsupported values are wrong, a table of players deals the wrong
     number of cards for 5 of the game's 6 rounds. The table is presented
     twice (Actions section and Player Reference), doubling its apparent
     authority.

2. **MINOR — unsupported "gifts are legal" trading note.**
   - Summary (Special Rules & Edge Cases): "Trading Strategy Note: You may
     pass money, tiles, or buildings during trades with no obligation to
     receive anything in return — gifts are legal."
   - Source: no support found (searched: "gift", "nothing in return",
     "without receiving", "for free", "no obligation"). The source only says
     trades can combine "buildings..., Shop tiles and money, in any
     combination or quantity" (l.140) — it never explicitly addresses
     one-sided giving.
   - Impact: low — plausible extrapolation, doesn't change play if wrong.

## hey-that-s-my-fish — PASS

(Audited as `rules/hey-thats-my-fish.md` vs
`extracted/hey-thats-my-fish-rules.txt` — see slug-mismatch note above.)

Short, precise summary; every claim checked matches the source closely,
including exact wording on the trickiest points (penguins block even your
own color, you collect the tile moved *from* not moved *to*, the two-stage
tiebreak).

### Verification highlights

- Victory/game-end (checked sentence-by-sentence): "game ends when all
  penguins have been removed... most fish wins... tiebreaker: most collected
  tiles... if still tied, share victory" matches source l.67-73 exactly,
  both directions (no source condition is missing from the summary, no
  summary claim is missing from the source).
- All numbers verified: 4 ocean boards, 60 tiles (30/20/10 split), 16
  penguins, penguin counts per player count (4/3/2 for 2/3/4 players).
- 5+ procedural claims verified: setup placement order (youngest-first,
  clockwise, one penguin at a time on 1-fish tiles only), move mechanics
  (straight line, any distance, no direction change, blocked by any penguin
  including your own, can't cross empty water), the two-step turn (move then
  collect the tile moved from), stuck-player elimination (all penguins
  removed, their tiles collected), and the tile-rearrangement setup option.

### Minor note (not counted as a finding)

The "Last tile" bullet ("A penguin standing on an isolated tile... is removed
and its tile added to that player's collection") is ambiguously worded — it
could be misread as a per-penguin removal rule distinct from the correctly-
stated per-player "Stuck Penguins" rule elsewhere in the same document. The
source only ever describes elimination at the player level (when *none* of a
player's penguins can move, l.81-84), not per-individual-penguin. Since the
"Stuck Penguins" section states this correctly and unambiguously, and the
"Last tile" bullet doesn't clearly assert a contradictory rule (it's most
charitably read as the single-penguin special case of the same rule), this
was not counted as a finding — dropped per "an uncertain finding is worse
than no finding."

**Verdict: PASS** — no contradictions or unsupported numbers found.

## red-notice — MINOR

`rules/red-notice.md` vs `extracted/red-notice-rules.txt` (439 lines, read in
full; see slug-mismatch note above re: the prefilter's false-positive
`SOURCE_MISMATCH`). This is an extremely dense, complex ruleset (asymmetric
2-player bluffing game with two full pages of role-specific procedures) and
essentially every setup step, phase procedure, token-resolution rule, all 4
action types, and every Special Rule matched the source precisely, often
verbatim, including easy-to-reverse details (which side moves first in ties,
attacker vs. defender token comparisons, the exact Radar-non-trigger
conditions). Two issues found.

### Findings

1. **UNSUPPORTED — invented explanation for black-side tokens' origin.**
   - Summary (Special Rules & Edge Cases): "Black-side tokens. ... Certain
     Improvements flip a token to its black side, making it persistent —
     Angela cannot remove black-side tokens with Security points, and a
     black-side Radar is not returned to supply when triggered."
   - Source: the *consequences* of black-side tokens are confirmed twice —
     "You can only remove tokens with their white side up, never black side
     up" (l.278) and "They go back into the supply, except the ones with
     their black side showing" (l.328) — but no support found (searched:
     "black", "flip", "persistent" combined with radar/barrier/token) for the
     claim that Improvement cards are what cause a token to become black-side.
     The source never explains how a token gets flipped to black in the first
     place (individual Improvement card texts, e.g. "Precise Radar," are named
     but not detailed in the extracted text).
   - Impact: low-to-moderate — the confirmed consequences are stated
     correctly, so a player handling an existing black-side token would not
     misplay it; only the unconfirmed causal claim (which Improvements create
     them) could mislead if wrong.

2. **NOTED LIMITATION (not counted as a finding) — False Identity tile-to-
   effect mapping cannot be independently verified.**
   - The source's "False Identity Tile Descriptions" section (l.400-439) lists
     the 6 tile names (Lawyer, Businesswoman, Secret Agent, Doctor, Pilot,
     Journalist) as a block, then lists 6 effect paragraphs separately — a
     two-column grid layout that collapsed into two disconnected lists during
     PDF extraction. The correspondence between specific names and specific
     effects is not preserved in the linear text. The summary's specific
     pairings (e.g., "Businesswoman: Destroy one Permanent Improvement...";
     "Pilot: Perform up to 3 Movements") could not be confirmed or refuted
     from the source structure alone; only content-based plausibility
     supports them. Per the "uncertain finding is worse than no finding" rule,
     this is documented as a limitation rather than asserted as an error, but
     is flagged here so a future audit with access to the original PDF layout
     can check it.

### Numbers/procedures checked

Victory conditions checked sentence-by-sentence (Angela: cash ≥$1,000,000;
Jessah: capture 3 times, first two strip a False Identity) — matches l.126-137,
l.372-384 exactly in both directions. Component counts (12 Action cards, 18
Cheque cards, 9 Action tokens ×2, 6 False Identity tiles (2 used), 10
Improvement cards, 6 Radar/4 Barrier tokens) all confirmed (l.39-58).
Procedural claims verified: Programming phase token placement rule (can't
place 2 of your own tokens on both sides of the same card), Action Resolution
0/1/2-token and tie and POW! rules, Bank action's location-match + exposure
requirement, Security marker cap (max 4, excess lost), and all 3 Radar
non-trigger conditions — all match source precisely, in the correct direction.

**Verdict: MINOR** — one unsupported causal claim about a secondary mechanic
in an otherwise exceptionally accurate, thorough summary; no contradictions
of confirmed rules.

## deadwood-1876 — PASS

`rules/deadwood-1876.md` vs `extracted/deadwood-1876-rules.txt` (507 lines,
image-transcribed PDF, read in full). Checked specifically for contamination
from the unrelated base game "Deadwood" (bgg_id 96713, a different product
also in this corpus) — found none; all mechanics (Safes, establishments,
guns/dice, Heists, Badge Round, Final Showdown) are unique to this game and
match its own source exactly.

### Verification highlights

- Victory condition checked sentence-by-sentence: establishment with most
  combined Gold advances → sole advancer wins immediately, OR multiple
  advancers fight to the death with lowest roll flipping their card, losing
  twice = eliminated, last alive keeps all Gold and wins (l.169-200) —
  matches summary exactly, including the correct tiebreak direction
  ("tied establishment with the **fewest** players advances," l.176-177,
  correctly not reversed) and the correct resolution direction in the
  Showdown (**lowest** roll is penalized, unlike Robbery/Duel where **highest**
  wins — summary preserves this distinction correctly).
- All component counts verified, including reconciling an internal source
  inconsistency: the CONTENTS section says "50 Deadwood Cards (10 of each...)"
  while the later CARD COUNTS section gives the precise 10/12/13/15 split for
  Horse/Duster/Stetson/Holster (l.323-328) — the summary correctly uses the
  precise breakdown.
- All optional variant rules independently verified: Mystery Safes (Bad
  Sarsaparilla, Cash, Dagger, Explosives, Poker Deck), Peace-Lovin' and Fair
  Share team victories, Horse Thief, 2-3 player adjustments, and the 2-player
  Stranger rules — every specific number and procedure matches (l.335-459).
- Exhaustion rule (mandatory check): "Whenever the deck runs out, shuffle the
  discard pile and reform the deck" (l.134-135) matches summary exactly.

### Numbers checked (13/13 supported)

3 platforms, 9 stars, 9 character cards, 10 dice, 25 Safe cards (5/5/7/8
split), 50 Deadwood cards (10/12/13/15 split), establishment capacities
(2/3/4 per establishment for 2-5/6-8/9 players), Duster hand math (7→4 normal,
6→3 in 8-9p), 4 turns/player (8 in 2-3p, 3 in 8-9p), 5 Badge types in fixed
reveal order.

### Minor omissions noted (not counted as findings)

A few tertiary details within already-covered optional rules are omitted
(Horse cards are replenished after use; multiple players may Horse Thief the
same Robbery; a Holster retains attachment if its Safe is given away in the
2-3p variant). These don't contradict anything and don't affect how the
core or variant rules are played, so per "do not force findings" they are
noted here rather than scored.

**Verdict: PASS** — no contamination from the unrelated base game "Deadwood"
found; no contradictions, wrong numbers, or invented mechanics found anywhere
in an extensive ruleset including five optional variants.
